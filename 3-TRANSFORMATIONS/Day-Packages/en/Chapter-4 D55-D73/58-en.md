# Day 58 — One being's happiness, countless beings' happiness: why relapse delays the path

**Date:** Sep 1  
**Chapter:** 4  
**Verses covered:** 4-10 to 4-11

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the two verses 10 and 11 from the fourth chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. When two people pull a cart in opposite directions, it does not move forward. Let us think today about how making mistakes and then mending them, over and over by turns, works in just that way.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

The great adept Tilopa was close to reaching the supreme attainment within seven days. At that time he stole a handful of sesame that had not been given to him. He confessed that small mistake and it was made clean. Even so, the time it took him to reach the attainment was put off by seven months.

So even when we confess wrongdoing and make it clean, we still lose real time on the way up to the higher stages. It is far more important to give up downfalls from the start than to count on mending them later.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today I will not destroy the happiness of even one being, and I will do no harm at all to anyone.

**Explanation:** *(Avoiding wrongdoing)* If we destroy the happiness of even one being, we ourselves are harmed. So if we do no harm to the happiness of others and bring up loving-kindness instead, it helps us and them alike. This is what makes our own mind clean.

*(Source: `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Plans/Dalai Lama/Chapter-4 D55-D73/Day-58-Ch4-V10-11.md`)*

#### Root Verse

*Plain English translation (source: `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-translate/BCA-Full-Plain-English.md#^4-10`):*

> If just ruining one person's happiness already causes harm to yourself, imagine the harm caused by ruining the happiness of beings as countless as the whole of space.

<!-- sub:interlinear -->
#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

If one destroys and does away with even a single being's happiness in this present life, one's own happiness is thereby harmed — so what need is there to mention destroying bodhicitta, the cause of happiness for every embodied being throughout the whole of space, without exception?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^4-10]]

---

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Given that destroying even a single being's happiness is taught to be a grave fault, at the time of generating bodhicitta one wishes to establish all beings in the state of a buddha free from every kind of suffering. As it is said, "benefiting others is the nirvāṇa within another's own mindstream," and so one generates just such an intention — to establish others on the ground of their own nirvāṇa. If one then gives up that bodhicitta, it becomes tantamount to destroying the happiness of all beings; and as for destroying the happiness of every embodied being throughout the limitless reaches of space, what need is there to ask what kind of unimaginable karmic result would come from that? He explains that an extremely grave fault arises.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-10]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The reason, or the proof, is this: if the sūtras teach that destroying even the temporary happiness of a single being — let alone many beings — causes oneself to fall from that temporary happiness and be born in a lower realm, then what need is there even to mention that obstructing a bodhisattva's virtue destroys the great cause of happiness for embodied beings pervading the whole of space without exception, so that one falls from a fortunate rebirth and is born endlessly in the lower realms? The reason for this is that if one does not obstruct that being's generation of bodhicitta, buddhahood is accomplished; and once accomplished, the buddha's manifold light-rays cast into the three lower realms and elsewhere gradually establish all those beings in temporary happiness, and ultimately establish them in the great happiness of buddhahood.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-10]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

If destroying even a single being's happiness that sustains life causes oneself to fall from a higher rebirth, then what need is there to mention that destroying and ruining the cause of unsurpassed happiness for every embodied being throughout the whole of space, without exception, results in birth in the lower realms?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-10]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

The reason is this: if destroying even a single being's happiness causes oneself to degenerate, then, needless to say, obstructing a bodhisattva's virtue — which destroys the happiness of every embodied being throughout the whole of space without exception — leads one to the lower realms. *(Note: this commentary's prose treats verses 4-8, 4-9, and 4-10 together in one continuous run after the grouped transclusion of all three — only the material specific to the 4-10 a fortiori argument is drawn on here; the preceding material and the scriptural citation that follows belong to 4-8 and 4-9 respectively.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^4-19]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

As it is said, this is also established by reasoning: if destroying even a single being's small happiness — let alone many beings' — causes the one who destroys it to fall from a fortunate rebirth, then what need is there to mention that obstructing a bodhisattva's virtue, which destroys the great cause of happiness for embodied beings pervading the whole of space without exception, results in falling from a fortunate rebirth and being reborn endlessly in the lower realms?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-10]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

If one asks why this leads to endless lower realms, the reason is this: needless to say for countless beings — if destroying or ruining even a small benefit and happiness belonging to a single being causes the one who ruins it, oneself, to fall from a fortunate higher rebirth, then obstructing a bodhisattva's virtue, which thereby destroys the great, unsurpassed cause of happiness for every embodied being throughout the whole of space, without exception or limit, results — needless to say — in falling from a fortunate rebirth and being born endlessly in the lower realms. It is also said, "if one destroys a single being's happiness that sustains its life." Turning another back from generating bodhicitta and inducing that person to generate the lesser vehicle's aspiration instead is also taught in the *Detailed Exposition* to become a root downfall, similar to this. In some places it is explained that the preceding verse is established by scripture while this one is established by reasoning, and the *Great Commentary* cites the same scriptural passage given above at this point as well.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-10]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

If a person such as this destroys even the mere happiness of a single being's remaining alive in this present life through killing, then the person who is the killer will, in a future rebirth, be born in the hell realms and must experience the ripened result of hot and cold suffering for one intermediate eon, and, as the corresponding result, must repay that being's life five-hundredfold — this being how one's own happiness is harmed. If that is so, then since the root of happiness for every embodied being equal to space, or pervading the very limit of space — that is, every being without exception — is the generation of bodhicitta, cutting off the aspiration and application that establish other beings in benefit and happiness by abandoning that very root — what need is there to mention that this is an extremely grave fault?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-10]]

##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Destroying the Happiness of Countless Beings Is a Far Graver Fault Than Destroying a Single Being's Happiness

If destroying even a single being's happiness causes oneself to fall from a fortunate rebirth or from a higher realm, then obstructing bodhicitta — which destroys the cause of happiness for every embodied being throughout the whole of space without exception — results, needless to say, in being born endlessly in the lower realms. All the commentaries agree on this to the end.

###### 2. This Verse Establishes the Preceding Point Through Reasoning

Sabzang Mati Panchen explains explicitly, "this is also established by reasoning," and Minyak Kunzang Sonam likewise explains that the preceding verse (4-9) is established by scripture while this one (4-10) is established by reasoning — though in some places this ordering is given differently.

###### 3. Bodhicitta Is the Root of Happiness for Boundless Beings

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that the root of happiness for every embodied being equal to space is the generation of bodhicitta itself; abandoning that root cuts off the very aspiration and application that establish other beings in benefit and happiness.

###### 4. Giving Up Bodhicitta Betrays the Very Vow to Establish All Beings in Buddhahood

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that at the time of generating bodhicitta, one intends to establish all beings on the ground of their own nirvāṇa; giving that bodhicitta up thereafter becomes tantamount to destroying the happiness of all beings.

###### 5. An Illustration: Killing a Single Being Ripens as an Intermediate Eon in Hell and a Five-Hundredfold Repayment of Life

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk illustrates the severity of harming even one being's happiness with the teaching on the ripening of killing, to make the gravity of destroying bodhicitta more vivid by comparison.

###### 6. The Interlinear Gloss Confirms the Same Point

Khenpo Zhenga's mchan-'grel likewise explains that if destroying even a single being's happiness in this present life harms one's own happiness, then what need is there to mention destroying bodhicitta, the cause of happiness for every embodied being throughout the whole of space?

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse establishes the preceding point through reasoning: if destroying even a single being's happiness causes oneself to fall from the happiness of a higher rebirth, then obstructing bodhicitta — thereby destroying the cause of happiness for every embodied being throughout the whole of space, without exception — results, needless to say, in being born endlessly in the lower realms. All the commentaries agree on this basic ground.

**Key points.**

- The reasoning demonstrated here: if destroying a single being's happiness alone causes one to fall to a lower realm, then destroying the cause of happiness for boundless beings is needless to say graver still.
- This verse is explained as establishing through reasoning what the preceding verse (4-9) established through scripture, per Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam, though the ordering is given differently in some sources.
- Bodhicitta is the very root of happiness for every embodied being equal to space, as Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains; abandoning it destroys that root directly.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that giving up bodhicitta betrays the vow, made at the time of its generation, to establish all beings in buddhahood free of suffering — making this an extremely grave fault.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk illustrates the gravity of harming even a single being with the canonical teaching on killing's karmic ripening, in order to show by contrast how much graver it is to destroy the happiness of boundless beings.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^4-10]] [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-10]]

<!-- sub:interlinear -->
#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

In this way, if the powerful root downfall and its antidote, powerful bodhicitta, keep alternating with one another as one circles through saṃsāra, then one takes birth throughout saṃsāra for a long time, so that reaching the grounds such as the ground of Great Joy is delayed for a very long while.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^4-11]]

---

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Even if one is struck by the root downfall of giving up bodhicitta, there is still a way to heal that degeneration by taking the vow again — yet reaching the higher grounds is thereby taught to be delayed for a very long time. Unlike a pārājika of individual liberation, there is a way to be restored; but there is nonetheless a difference between never having been stained by the root downfall in the first place, and having degenerated through the downfall and then been healed again. For example, examined closely, there is a difference in quality between a leg that was never injured and remained sound from the start, and a leg that was injured and then recovered through medical treatment. In just this way, it is of great importance not to become stained by the root downfall to begin with. As a method for this, he teaches that guarding one's visualization through practices such as the six-session guru yoga is of great importance.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-11]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

If one thinks that even though a downfall has occurred, since it is unlike prātimokṣa vows, one can confess it to a spiritual friend and heal it by taking up bodhicitta again — as explained above, if the powerful root downfall of giving up bodhicitta and the powerful bodhicitta taken up again keep alternating with one another as one circles through saṃsāra, then, needless to say, reaching the ultimate great enlightenment is delayed, let alone merely reaching the first ground and the rest for the time being. Even if the downfall is purified through the power of confession so that one is not born in a lower realm, it still becomes an obstacle to the arising of new qualities in one's own mindstream, and so the arising of qualities such as reaching the grounds is delayed for a long time — for example, as it is also said: just as Tilopa, who was about to attain the supreme attainment within seven days, delayed the time of his attainment by seven months because he secretly took a handful of sesame seeds.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-11]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

As explained above, if the powerful downfall of giving up bodhicitta and, once again, powerful bodhicitta keep alternating with one another as one circles through saṃsāra, then, through the force of karma and afflictions, one must be born alternately in the lower realms and in higher rebirths, and reaching the grounds such as the ground of Great Joy is delayed for a very long time. So even at the cost of one's life, one should strive not to let bodhicitta degenerate.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-11]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

If the two — that powerful downfall, and powerful bodhicitta — keep alternating with one another as one circles through saṃsāra, then reaching the first ground and the rest is delayed for a very long time.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^4-22]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If one thinks that even though a downfall has occurred, it is enough to confess it and take up bodhicitta again — as explained above, if the powerful root downfall of giving up bodhicitta that occurs at times, and powerful bodhicitta taken up again at other times, keep alternating through the force of abandoning and taking up by turns as one circles through saṃsāra, then reaching a ground such as the ground of Great Joy is delayed for a long time, needless to say reaching the ultimate great enlightenment.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-11]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

If one thinks that even though a downfall has occurred, it is enough to confess it and generate bodhicitta again — as explained above, if the powerful root downfall of giving up the aspirational mind and, again, powerful bodhicitta taken up once more keep alternating with one another as one circles through saṃsāra, this is taught directly to delay reaching a ground such as the ground of Great Joy for a long time, and by implication shows, needless to say, that reaching buddhahood is delayed for even longer than that.

As for the phrase "even reaching a ground," some hold that the particle "even" here gathers in the further sense that this also makes buddhahood, which is difficult to attain, harder still to attain. This is like two men pulling one palanquin — one pulling forward and one pulling backward — so that it cannot reach its destination, as the yogin Candra says. Therefore, one should strive by whatever means to keep bodhicitta from degenerating, even at the cost of one's life.

In some sources, the fault of transgressing aspirational bodhicitta is explained up to this point, with the fault of transgressing engagement bodhicitta beginning from here onward. In this outline, too, it is said that "the intention to guard against bodhicitta's degeneration" applies up to this point — but the sense of that heading is meant primarily to show guarding against degeneration in general, not bodhicitta alone; the passages above and below show that the earlier vow must be guarded without degenerating, which applies not only to bodhicitta but also, since one has vowed to accomplish the trainings, to the trainings as well.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-11]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

As explained above, if the powerful root downfall of giving up the bodhicitta vow occurs, and then, in turn, one takes up powerful bodhicitta again as an antidote to that and guards it — if these two keep alternating with one another as one circles through saṃsāra in the state of the three realms during that time, then one circles through saṃsāra for a long time, so that reaching the bodhisattva grounds such as the first ground, the ground of Great Joy, is delayed and takes long to arrive — this too, he explains, is therefore unsuitable.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-11]]

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **Minyak Kunzang Sonam:** notes that concerning the scope of the outline section covering this passage (spanning 4-1 through 4-11), some major commentaries hold that it covers only the fault of transgressing aspirational bodhicitta, with the fault of transgressing engagement bodhicitta beginning only from this verse onward; Minyak Kunzang Sonam himself, however, explains that the outline heading "guarding the vow from degenerating" covers both aspirational and engagement bodhicitta together. ⚑

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-11]]

<!-- sub:metaphors -->
#### Metaphors and Examples

- **Tilopa's seven-day attainment delayed seven months by stealing a handful of sesame** (ཏེ་ལོ་པས་ཞག་བདུན་གྱིས་དངོས་གྲུབ་ཐོབ་པ་ལས་ཏིལ་སྤར་གང་ལྐོག་པས་ཟླ་བ་བདུན་འགྱངས་པ) → even a small fault can obstruct the arising of new qualities: Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that even once a downfall is purified through confession, it still becomes an obstacle to the arising of qualities such as reaching the grounds, delaying them for a long time — just as Tilopa, on the verge of the supreme attainment within seven days, delayed it by seven months through the minor fault of secretly taking a handful of sesame seeds.
- **Two men pulling one palanquin in opposite directions** (མི་གཉིས་ཀྱིས་འདོ་ལི་གཅིག་མདུན་རྒྱབ་ཏུ་འཐེན་པ) → alternating between the downfall and bodhicitta prevents any progress at all. Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the yogin Candra: just as a palanquin pulled forward by one man and backward by another cannot reach its destination, so alternating between abandoning and reclaiming bodhicitta leaves one stalled, unable to advance on the path.
- **An unbroken leg versus a broken leg healed by treatment** (རྐང་པ་ཐོག་མ་ནས་སྐྱོན་མེད་པ་དང་སྐྱོན་ཤོར་ནས་སྨན་བཅོས་ཀྱིས་གསོས་པ) → restoring a vow after breaking it is never as good as never having broken it. His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that although the bodhisattva vow can be restored by retaking it, there remains, on close examination, a difference in quality between never having been stained by the root downfall and having degenerated and then been healed again — just as an unbroken leg differs in quality from one that broke and was later treated.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Alternating Between the Downfall and Bodhicitta Delays Reaching the Grounds for a Long Time

If the powerful root downfall of giving up bodhicitta occurs, and one then takes up powerful bodhicitta again, and these two keep alternating as one circles through saṃsāra, then, even though the downfall is purified through confession so that one is not born in a lower realm, it becomes an obstacle to new qualities arising in one's own mindstream, delaying the attainment of grounds such as the ground of Great Joy for a very long time. All the commentaries agree on this.

###### 2. The Delay Reaches Beyond the Grounds to Buddhahood Itself

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both explain that the particle "even" in "even reaching a ground" implies, by extension, that buddhahood itself — already difficult to attain — is made harder still to attain by this same alternation.

###### 3. One Should Guard Bodhicitta Even at the Cost of One's Life

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen teaches that one should strive not to let bodhicitta degenerate even at the cost of one's life, and Minyak Kunzang Sonam confirms this, citing the yogin Candra.

###### 4. Healing a Broken Vow Is Never as Good as Never Having Broken It

His Holiness the Dalai Lama teaches that although the bodhisattva root downfall, unlike a pārājika of individual liberation, can be restored by retaking the vow, there remains a real difference in quality between never having incurred the downfall and having degenerated and then been healed — so guarding against the downfall from the outset, through practices such as the six-session guru yoga, is of great importance.

###### 5. The Interlinear Gloss Confirms the Same Point

Khenpo Zhenga's mchan-'grel likewise explains that if the powerful root downfall and its antidote, powerful bodhicitta, keep alternating with one another as one circles through saṃsāra, one takes birth throughout saṃsāra for a long time, delaying the attainment of the grounds.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that if the powerful root downfall of giving up bodhicitta, and powerful bodhicitta taken up again, keep alternating with one another as one circles through saṃsāra, then, even though the downfall may be purified through confession so that one avoids the lower realms, reaching a bodhisattva ground such as the ground of Great Joy is delayed for a very long time. All the commentaries agree on this basic ground.

**Key points.**

- Khenpo Kunzang Pelden illustrates how even a purified fault can delay the arising of new qualities, using Tilopa's seven-day attainment delayed seven months by a minor theft.
- The delay implied by "even reaching a ground" extends, by implication, to buddhahood itself, made harder still to attain.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the yogin Candra's image of two men pulling one palanquin in opposite directions to show that alternating between the downfall and bodhicitta leaves one stalled rather than progressing.
- Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both teach that one should guard bodhicitta from degenerating even at the cost of one's life.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama teaches that healing a broken vow by retaking it is never quite equal to never having broken it in the first place, so guarding against the downfall from the outset is of the greatest importance.
