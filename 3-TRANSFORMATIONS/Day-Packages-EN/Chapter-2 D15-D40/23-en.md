## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on verses 20 and 21 from the second chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. It teaches us how to make mental offerings through the power of aspiration and prayer. We learn to transform any beautiful music or sweet sounds we hear into clouds of offering that relieve the suffering of all living beings. We also pray that a gentle, continuous rain of precious jewels and flowers may always fall upon holy texts, stupas, and Buddha statues everywhere.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

These verses explain the practice of making offerings through the power of prayer and aspiration. Master Shantideva imagines offering melodious music played on flutes, drums, and stringed instruments to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas. He prays that this sweet music transforms into vast offering clouds that remain constantly before the holy beings. The special power of this music is that whenever suffering or distressed living beings hear it, their pain and sorrow are instantly washed away, leaving their minds filled with peace and joy.

Furthermore, he prays that a continuous, uninterrupted shower of precious jewels, fragrant flowers, sandalwood, and fine robes may rain down upon all holy objects — including Buddhist scriptures (books of Dharma), stupas, and statues of the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas.

Spiritual teachers explain that by offering beautiful music with this pure wish, we plant the positive seeds for ourselves and all beings to always hear the wonderful teachings of the Dharma in the future. Mentally showering holy objects with gems and flowers creates immense positive energy (merit) and helps purify our minds.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, whenever I hear pleasant music or songs, I will mentally offer them to the Three Jewels as a blessing to relieve the suffering of all beings.

**Explanation:** *(Generosity Practice)* Normally, when we listen to music or hear a pleasant song, we do it just for our own entertainment and personal pleasure. Today, following the teachings of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*, whenever I hear beautiful music or sweet sounds, I will mentally transform them into an offering cloud presented to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas. As I listen, I will sincerely pray that the positive energy of this music reaches all suffering and stressed beings, taking away their pain, filling their hearts with peace and happiness, and guiding them toward wisdom.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Beyond those mentioned above, a host of offerings: the many kinds of instruments — those sounded by blowing, by striking, and by plucking — endowed with sweet and pleasing melody; a mass of clouds that clears away the suffering of body and mind in beings and so soothes the mind, that is, brings the mind a happy feeling. May all such varied and distinctive arrangements abide, each in its own place. By this one makes offering to the buddhas together with their heirs.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-20]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Third, the offering by the power of aspiration-prayer. Beyond those explained above, there is a specially exalted host of offerings: the music of the three kinds — blown, struck, and plucked — such as cymbals endowed with the eight qualities; and sweet, pleasing songs of praise accompanied by dance, like the melodious voice of the kinnara's lute, at which even one such as Mahākāśyapa could not keep still but joined the dance, and like Pañcaśikha, king of the gandharvas, striking up the strings of his tamburā. Such music, by the distinctive power that hearing it alone dispels the suffering of beings and brings them soothing joy and ease — "clouds," a word carrying the sense of vast pervasion — means measureless melodies pervading the realms of the ten directions. May they arise before the eyes of the victorious ones together with their heirs and abide there always, each in its own place, without interruption: so one prays.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Having thus named some of the kinds of offering-substance, in order to gather them once more into the offering of aspiration-prayer and set them out: beyond those earlier ones there is a host of offerings surpassing and specially exalted above them — the many kinds of instruments such as the great tabor, kettle-drum, conch and lute, as when it is said that among the sixty-four arts there are eighteen kinds of music; many of them sweet-sounding and captivating; and, accompanied by the beautiful dance of goddesses and the rest, endowed with the chanting of sweet, pleasing song. Their distinctive function is that by the mere hearing of those melodies in the ear, the suffering of beings who suffer is dispelled and their minds are soothed or satisfied with joy and ease. May the stream of those measureless masses of clouds of offering — for an age, or longer even than an age — abide each in its own place in the hearing of the victorious ones together with their heirs, without break; or may they arise and abide, each in its own place, before their eyes, without any lapse of time.

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Third, the offering by the power of aspiration-prayer. Beyond those offering-substances above — the ones actually possessed and the ones emanated by the mind — there is a host of offering-things: music sounded by blowing, striking, and plucking, endowed with the sweetest and most pleasing melody. May all those masses of clouds of sweet melody, whose very nature is to soothe — that is, to give happiness — even to the minds of beings tormented by fierce suffering such as the death of a parent or the burning of a home, abide each in its own place along the path of the hearing-faculty of the field of offering, the buddhas and bodhisattvas: so it is said. And relying on having offered such an offering of sweet melody, one also prays: may this become a cause for the sound of the sublime Dharma to be heard always, in future, in the minds of all beings, oneself and others.

##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **kunpal / khenpo-kunga:** they name this verse "third, the offering by the power of aspiration-prayer," placing it within the mind-emanated offering, at the point of the offering by the power of aspiration-prayer.
- **sabzang:** he names this verse "(twelfth,) the offering of music," identifying it directly as the occasion of the offering of music.

The meaning does not conflict, but each commentary differs in the number and the name it gives this verse in its outline.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. A Part of the Offering by the Power of Aspiration-Prayer — the Offering of Music

Kunzang Pelden and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk both identify this verse as "third, the offering by the power of aspiration-prayer," and explain the host of offerings of music and sweet melody, beyond what was offered before, as a part of the mind-emanated offering.

###### 2. The Offering-Substance — the Many Kinds of Instruments With Sweet Melody: Kettle-Drum, Conch, Lute and the Rest

Many commentaries explain that there are boundless kinds of instruments — kettle-drum, conch, lute, song and dance and so on — endowed with pleasing, sweet melody. Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains in detail that among the sixty-four arts there are some eighteen kinds of music.

###### 3. The Distinctive Power of the Music — by Mere Hearing It Dispels the Suffering of Beings and Soothes Them

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains clearly that these are masses of clouds of sweet melody which give a soothing happiness even to the minds of beings tormented by fierce suffering, such as the death of a parent or the burning of a home.

###### 4. The Content of the Prayer — May These Clouds of Offering-Music Abide Always, Without Interruption, Each in Its Own Place Before the Victorious Ones Together With Their Heirs

Many commentaries explain that the final sense of this verse is an aspiration-prayer that those offering-instruments abide without interruption before the victorious ones and their heirs throughout the ten directions.

###### 5. The Comparisons — Mahākāśyapa, and the Dance and Melody of Pañcaśikha, King of the Gandharvas

To convey the power of the sweetness of the offering-music, Kunzang Pelden gives two examples: Mahākāśyapa unable to keep still at the kinnara's sound and joining the dance, and Pañcaśikha, king of the gandharvas, striking up the strings of his tamburā.

###### 6. The Long-Term Aspiration — May It Become a Cause for the Sound of the Sublime Dharma to Be Heard Always

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that one also prays that, relying on the merit of offering this music, the sound of the sublime Dharma may become something heard always in the minds of oneself and others in future lives.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse, at the point of the offering by the power of aspiration-prayer within the mind-emanated offering, presents the offering of the many kinds of instruments with sweet melody — kettle-drum, conch, lute and the rest — and makes the aspiration-prayer that those clouds of offering, which by the mere hearing of their sound dispel the suffering of beings and soothe them, may abide always, without interruption, each in its own place before the victorious ones together with their heirs.

**Key points.**

- The offering of music is explained at the point of the offering by the power of aspiration-prayer, within the mind-emanated offering. ⚑ Sabzang identifies it directly as "the offering of music," while Ngulchu Thogme gathers it together with the following verse (2-21) and names the pair "the prayer that the offerings abide."
- The offering-substance is the boundless kinds of instruments — kettle-drum, conch, lute, song and dance and the rest — with pleasing, sweet melody.
- By the mere hearing of the music's sound, even beings tormented by fierce suffering such as the death of a parent or the burning of a home attain a soothing happiness.
- To convey the power of the music's sweetness, Kunzang Pelden gives two examples: Mahākāśyapa unable to keep still at the kinnara's sound and joining the dance, and Pañcaśikha, king of the gandharvas, striking up the strings of his tamburā.
- The prayer is that those offering-instruments abide always, without interruption, each in its own place before the victorious ones together with their heirs; and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that one also prays that it become a cause for the sound of the sublime Dharma to be heard always in future.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

As for "the Jewel of the sublime Dharma" — he remarks: may it not also be applied to the Dharma Jewel as the Dharma of scripture, the volumes and so on? And to the reliquary shrines, the supports of the buddhas' minds, and to those images which are like representatives of the Tathāgata: to these too, may a rain of jewels and flowers and the rest fall without interruption.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-21]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The Jewel of the sublime Dharma is the whole Jewel of the sublime Dharma of scripture and realization, together with its letters in the form of volumes. The reliquary shrines are the eight shrines corresponding to the eight deeds of the Teacher and the rest: the Heaped-Lotus shrine, built at the time of his birth; the Subduing-of-Māra or Enlightenment shrine, built at the time of his awakening; the Many-Doors-of-Auspiciousness shrine, of the time when he turned the wheel of Dharma; the Miracle shrine, of his taming the six teachers of the non-Buddhist schools; the Descent-from-Heaven shrine, of his descent from the god realm; the Reconciliation shrine, of his healing the schism in the Sangha; the Complete-Victory-of-Life shrine, of his blessing the formative factors of his lifespan; and the Parinirvāṇa shrine with its base, of his passing beyond sorrow — eight in all. Brahmā, the nāgas, the king of Kaliṅga and the gandharvas each built a shrine for their portion of the relics of his body, and with the eight great measures of Magadha too, eight shrines and more were built. The Dharma-king Aśoka built ten million shrines containing relics at ten million places in the world. Images are those painted, those cast, and those sculpted in relief: it is said that the master craftsman Karmavishva, by emanation, built three images of the Tathāgata to the measure of his eighty-second, twelfth, and twenty-fifth years. In any case, to all such shrines and images, however many there are, may a rain of various jewels and flowers and sweet scents and cloth and ornaments and grain and medicines fall always, without interruption: so one prays. If one's ethical discipline is thoroughly pure, whatever prayer is made is actually fulfilled; and even if it is not, one will still gain the distinctive benefit.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-21]]

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

To all the precious Jewels — the sublime Dharma and the rest; and to the supports of the Truth Body, the reliquary shrines containing relics; and to all the supports of the Form Body, the images painted, cast, and sculpted in relief and so on: may a rain of jewels and flowers and sweet scents and cloth and ornaments and grain and medicines fall always, without interruption, with no gap between.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

To the whole Jewel of the sublime Dharma — the Dharma of scripture, the three baskets of the collected teachings, or the twelve branches, and the Dharma of realization, comprised in the truths of cessation and of the path — and to its supports, the volumes and the rest; and to the supports of the Truth Body, the reliquary shrines containing relics; and to the supports of the Form Body, the images painted, sculpted in relief, and cast, and the rest: may many rains of offering-substances — a rain of jewels, that is, of precious things, and a rain of flowers, and cloth and grain and medicines and the rest — fall without interruption, ever, for as long as existence lasts. Most commentaries explain the preceding verse (2-20) as the offering of music and this one (2-21) as the offering of aspiration-prayer, or the offering that is consecrated by blessing.

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

The Jewel of the sublime Dharma: the three precious baskets of the Dharma of scripture which set forth as their subject matter those two — the truth of cessation and the truth of the path, that is, the Dharma of realization — together with all their supports, the scriptural volumes and their mass of letters; and the reliquary shrines, the supports of the minds of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, such as the shrine built from the relics of the Teacher's body after his passing beyond sorrow, or the eight shrines corresponding to the eight deeds performed during the Teacher's lifetime, together with those; and further, all those images of the buddhas and bodhisattvas that are of the kind painted, sculpted in relief, or cast. To all of these, may that very rain of offerings — of precious things such as gold, silver, pearl, cat's-eye, coral and camphor-crystal, and of flowers such as the utpala, saffron and kumuda — fall without interruption, always, continuously, again and again. This is the offering by the power of aspiration-prayer.

##### BCAC19_KKP — The History of the Eight Great Reliquary Shrines

The history of the building of the eight great reliquary shrines corresponding to the eight deeds of the Teacher is set out: the Heaped-Lotus shrine, built at the time of his birth by Śuddhodana of Kapilavastu; the Subduing-of-Māra shrine, built at the time of his awakening by Bimbisāra; the Many-Doors-of-Auspiciousness, built by the good group of five when he turned the wheel of Dharma; the Miracle shrine, built by the Licchavis at the time he tamed the six teachers of the non-Buddhist schools; the Descent-from-Heaven shrine, after he was invited down from the heaven of the Thirty-Three; the Reconciliation shrine, built by the people of Magadha when the schism in the Sangha was healed; the Complete-Victory-of-Life shrine; and the Parinirvāṇa shrine with its base, built by the Mallas — eight in all. Then Brahmā, the nāgas, the king of Kaliṅga and the gandharvas each built a shrine for their portion of the relics of his body; with the eight great measures of Magadha too, eight shrines and more were built; and the Dharma-king Aśoka, having bound yaksha servants to his command, is said to have built ten million shrines containing relics at ten million places in the world.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-21]]

<!-- story:BCAC19_KKP -->
##### BCAC19_KKP — How King Songtsen Gampo Invited the Relic

It is said that King Songtsen Gampo, emanating as the monk Akaramatiśīla, invited a relic of the Tathāgata from the Heaped-Lotus shrine in India, and that it now resides between the brows of the self-arisen five-fold Jowo. From that shrine, Padmasambhava, the second buddha, miraculously invited a full measure of relics, which reside in the white shrine of Samye; and from their increase, it is said, fortunate people can still find them today.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-21]]

<!-- story:BCAC19_KKP -->
##### BCAC19_KKP — How the Master Craftsman Karmavishva Built the Three Images

It is said that the master craftsman Karmavishva built, from various precious substances, three images: one to the measure of the Tathāgata's twenty-fifth year, which resides in the god realm of Tuṣita; one to the measure of his twelfth year, the precious Jowo Shakyamuni; and one to the measure of his eighth year, the Mikyö Dorje of Ramoche — the latter two now residing in Lhasa. It is also said that a Jowo made of sandalwood resides in China.

#### Metaphors and Examples

- **The rain of flowers falling by the miracle of the Dharma's appearing** (ཆོས་སྣང་བའི་ཆོ་འཕྲུལ་ལས་མེ་ཏོག་གི་ཆར་དབབ་པ།) → at the time when the buddhas and bodhisattvas teach the Dharma (just as it is taught that at the time of the Tathāgata's complete and manifest awakening the gods of the pure abodes let fall a rain of flowers). Kunzang Pelden, following that account, interprets the sense of the prayer as being that a rain of offerings should likewise fall at every time when the Dharma of scripture is taught and heard, and at every time when the Dharma of realization arises in the mind.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Essence of This Verse Is the Offering by the Power of Aspiration-Prayer

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk gives this — the prayer that a rain of jewels, flowers and the like fall without interruption — the definite name "the offering by the power of aspiration-prayer."

###### 2. The Threefold Presentation of the Field of Offering — the Jewel of the Sublime Dharma (the Volumes), the Reliquary Shrines, and the Images

As all the commentaries explain in final agreement, the field of offering is threefold: the Jewel of the sublime Dharma of scripture and realization abiding in the form of volumes; the reliquary shrines containing relics; and the images painted, sculpted in relief, and cast.

###### 3. The Difference Between the Offering of the Preceding Verse (2-20) and This One (2-21)

Minyak Kunzang Sonam says that most commentaries explain the preceding verse as the offering of music and this one as the offering of aspiration-prayer, or the offering that is consecrated by blessing.

###### 4. A Detailed Account of the Eight Great Reliquary Shrines and of the Images

Kunzang Pelden identifies actual places and objects: the eight great shrines corresponding to the eight deeds of the Teacher; the division of the relics; Aśoka's ten million shrines; the history of Songtsen Gampo's inviting the relic; and the three images built by the master craftsman Karmavishva.

###### 5. Whether the Prayer Is Fulfilled Depends on the State of One's Ethical Discipline

Kunzang Pelden says that if one's ethical discipline is thoroughly pure, whatever prayer is made is actually fulfilled, and that even if it is not, one still gains the distinctive benefit.

###### 6. Established by Scripture — Dedicating Vast Clouds of Offering by the Power of Merit and of Blessing

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the *Trisamayarāja* and the *Ratnamegha* to establish the way in which acts of offering are dedicated to the buddhas and bodhisattvas with an exceedingly powerful superior intention.

###### 7. A Question Raised About the Scope of the Jewel of the Sublime Dharma

His Holiness the Dalai Lama notes, by way of raising a question, whether "the Jewel of the sublime Dharma" may not also be applied to the Dharma of scripture, the volumes and so on.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse makes the aspiration-prayer that a rain of jewels and flowers and the like fall without interruption on the Jewel of the sublime Dharma (the volumes), on the reliquary shrines, and on all the images of the buddhas and bodhisattvas; and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk gives it the name "the offering by the power of aspiration-prayer."

**Key points.**

- All the commentaries agree in the end that the field of offering is threefold: the Jewel of the sublime Dharma (the volumes); the reliquary shrines (supports of the mind); and the images (painted, sculpted in relief, cast).
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam says that most commentaries explain the preceding verse (2-20) as the offering of music and this one (2-21) as the offering of aspiration-prayer, or the offering that is consecrated by blessing.
- Kunzang Pelden identifies "shrines and images" with actual places and objects: the eight great reliquary shrines, the division of the relics, Aśoka's ten million shrines, the history of Songtsen Gampo's inviting the relic, and the three images built by the master craftsman Karmavishva.
- Kunzang Pelden says that whether the prayer is fulfilled depends on the state of one's ethical discipline: if it is pure, the prayer is actually fulfilled, and if not, one still gains the distinctive benefit.
- ⚑ His Holiness the Dalai Lama raises the question whether the Jewel of the sublime Dharma may not also be applied to the Dharma of scripture, the volumes and so on — which differs from the other commentaries, who take it definitely to be the volumes.
