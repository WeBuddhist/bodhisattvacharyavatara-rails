## Today's Verse

> You cannot eat what is impure,
> nor can you drink blood.
> You cannot lick the intestines;
> what can the body do for you? ^5-65

> Therefore, it is right for foxes and vultures
> to guard this for food.
> This human body
> is merely something to be used. ^5-66

> Even if you guard it,
> the merciless Lord of Death
> will seize it and give it to birds and dogs.
> What can you do then? ^5-67

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the sixty-fifth, sixty-sixth, and sixty-seventh verses of the fifth chapter. However dearly we care for this body, one day we will lose it. At that time, will we think, "What have we done with it?"

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following passage, which is easy to understand and encouraging. The _Smrityupasthana_ sutra says that it is uncertain when death will come, and that the Lord of Death comes with no way to turn him back. The sutra says that for this reason we must use this body in a meaningful way.

> A great power that is hard to bear,
> a fierce mental force that moves everywhere,
> the many separate great fears
> are what is called the Lord of Death.

> The fire of time that cannot be turned back,
> which beings are sure to go through,
> which blocks people from living,
> is what is called here the Lord of Death.

So, since it is uncertain when death will come, we must quickly use this body for doing good.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will not cling to my own body. I will use it for the Dharma and for doing good.

**Explanation:** _(Doing good)_ This body has no essence, yet it is a fine tool for doing good. If we stay attached to the body, our time passes for nothing. So if we use this body for doing good, it benefits us in both this life and the next.
