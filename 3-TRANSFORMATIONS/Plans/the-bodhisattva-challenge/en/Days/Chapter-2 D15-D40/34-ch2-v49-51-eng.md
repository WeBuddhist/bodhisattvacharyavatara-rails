## Today's Verse

> I, terrified by fear,
> offer myself to Samantabhadra.
> I offer my body
> to Manjushri. ^2-49

> To Protector Avalokiteshvara,
> whose compassionate conduct is unfailing,
> I cry out with a wretched voice.
> Please protect me, a wrongdoer! ^2-50

> To Noble Akashagarbha,
> Kshitigarbha,
> and all the greatly compassionate Protectors,
> I cry out from my heart, seeking refuge. ^2-51

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on verses 49 through 51 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Yesterday Shantideva went for refuge to the Three Jewels, which are the Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha. Today he does something more personal. He hands himself over to Samantabhadra and Manjushri, calls out to Avalokiteshvara by name, and then widens the call to Akashagarbha, Kshitigarbha and every protector who has great compassion. Three verses, one long cry for help. Today asks what has to be true of us for a cry like that to be worth making.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The call gets wider as it goes. First he hands himself over. Then he cries out to one protector. Then to all of them.

The commentaries say who is being called and why each one. Samantabhadra carries the aspiration of all the buddhas, Manjushri their wisdom. Avalokiteshvara is their compassion gathered into a single form. Akashagarbha is described, on the Buddha's own authority, as a walking stick for beginners who have fallen, which is why he is the one people turn to after breaking something.

Khenpo Kunzang Palden adds the condition, and it is the uncomfortable part. The cry has to come out of fear you actually feel. Say the words without the fear behind them, he writes, and you are telling the buddhas and their heirs something untrue, and they are ashamed on your behalf. On the next verse he puts the same point the other way round: this is not a polite formula, it is a sound forced up from the marrow of the bones by real distress.

So the verses are not asking for a recitation. They are asking whether the trouble we are in has actually landed on us yet.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will offer my body and everything I own to the bodhisattvas in my mind, and then ask them for help in my own words instead of a set phrase.

**Explanation:** _(Doing good)_ The offering costs nothing and changes nothing outwardly, which is exactly why it is worth doing carefully rather than quickly. Sit for a minute. Bring to mind that this body will age, get sick and stop, and that none of what I call mine stays with me. Then hand it over, deliberately, as one act. After that, say what I actually want help with, in ordinary language. Not the beautiful phrase, the real one, even if the real one is small or embarrassing. And if nothing in me is frightened today and the words would come out hollow, I will say that honestly instead of performing a distress I do not feel. Shantideva's cry works because it is true, not because it is well put. The point is not to work myself up into something. It is to find out where I actually stand this morning and then say that much without dressing it up.
