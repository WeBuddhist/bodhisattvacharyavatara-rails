## Today's Verse

> If fishermen, hunters, farmers, and so forth,
> who only think of their own livelihood,
> endure the harm of cold, heat, and so forth,
> why should I not endure it for the happiness of beings? ^4-40

> When I vowed to liberate
> all beings, filling the ten directions of space,
> from defilements,
> I myself was not yet free from defilements. ^4-41

> How is it not madness
> to speak without knowing my own measure?
> Therefore, I must always
> be unwavering in conquering defilements. ^4-42

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 40 to 42 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Fishermen and farmers endure the hardship of cold and heat for the sake of mere food and clothing. Let us likewise join together and truly live up to our own promise, rather than talking big like a madman who does not know his own limits.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a saying that is easy to understand and encouraging.

"One who has not crossed cannot ferry others across. One who is not free cannot free others. A blind person cannot show the way. Only one who is free can set others free. Only one with eyes can show a blind person the way."

The meaning of this saying is this: unless we ourselves have crossed the water, we cannot carry someone else to the other side. A blind person who cannot see cannot show another blind person the way. In the same way, before we can free others from suffering, we must first free ourselves from our own afflictions.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, in order to help others, I will endure whatever hardship or discomfort of cold and heat comes, and I will help them.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ Fishermen and farmers endure the hardship of cold and heat for the sake of their own livelihood alone. We should endure even a small hardship for the happiness of all beings. If we act like this and help others, the strength of our patience will grow.
