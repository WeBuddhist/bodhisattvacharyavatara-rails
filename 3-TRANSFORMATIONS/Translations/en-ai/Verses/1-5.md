## Per-Commentary Summaries

### [[en-AI-generated-commentary-gyaltsab|Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen]] Summary
Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen explains verse 1-5 (treated under Section 1.2 in his commentary) as the contemplation of the "difficulty of generating the support of the mind (the virtuous mind)," which serves as a crucial prerequisite for generating bodhicitta.
- **The Analogy of the Lightning Flash:** Gyaltsab employs the analogy of a brief flash of lightning on a dark, cloudy night. Just as lightning reveals forms for a single instant, so too, by the power of the Buddha, a virtuous mind that wishes to perform merit arises only occasionally in worldly beings, but does not arise continuously.
- **Weakness of Virtue and Strength of Negativity:** Gyaltsab emphasizes that we live in an era where virtue is weak and negativity is powerful. We constantly abide in weak virtue, while the powerful negativity that produces birth in bad migrations—resembling the darkness of a cloudy night—is extremely difficult to overcome and produces great fear.
- **The Urgency of Effort:** Because virtue is weak and negativity is fierce and terrifying, we must make diligent efforts to accomplish virtue and abandon negativity. We must cultivate the virtuous mind as a remedy for negativity.
- **Nuance on "Weak Virtue":** Gyaltsab notes that the phrase "constantly abiding in weak virtue" is explained in terms of time in the classical commentaries, highlighting that virtue is of short duration and rare occurrence compared to the constant presence of negativity.

### [[en-AI-generated-commentary-sazang|Sazang Mati Panchen]] Summary
Sazang Mati Panchen Lodro Gyaltsen provides a highly structured, dual-part explanation of the lightning analogy, treating it both in the Preliminaries (as the rarity of the mind of Dharma) and in the main text of Chapter 1 (as the weakness of ordinary virtue compared to the power of negativity).
- **The Rarity of the Mind of Dharma (Preliminaries, Section 0.2):** Sazang compares cyclic existence to a dense darkness completely covered by a thick, cloudy night without stars or moon. A sudden flash of lightning momentarily reveals objects in brilliant light. Similarly, by the power of the light rays of compassion of the Buddha-Bhagavans, worldly beings occasionally experience the light of virtuous intelligence directing them to engage in the holy Dharma. This is extremely rare, and even when it arises, it does not remain long. Therefore, we must strive in the conduct of great beings when this rare opportunity is found.
- **The Weakness of Ordinary Virtue vs. Negativity (Chapter 1, Section 1.1):** Sazang explains that virtue that merely "accords with merit" (ordinary worldly virtue) is like a flash of lightning—arising only occasionally and extremely weak in strength. Negativity, on the other hand, is like the thick darkness of a cloudy night, possessing immense power to produce the fierce, unendurable sufferings of hell.
- **The Necessity of Bodhicitta:** Sazang argues that ordinary virtue is completely unable to overcome such powerful negativity. He poses a rhetorical question: "What other ordinary virtue, which is not the perfect *bodhicitta* endowed with the essence of emptiness and compassion, can overcome and outshine such negativity? None can." Thus, only *bodhicitta* possesses the capacity to overcome negativity, and we must hold it firmly.

### [[en-AI-generated-commentary-thokme|Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo]] Summary
Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo explains the difficulty of generating the support of the mind (the virtuous mind) as the second aspect of the support of the person who enters the path, immediately following the support of the body (leisure and fortune).
- **The Analogy of the Lightning Flash:** Thokme compares the dark, cloudy night to the pervasive ignorance and negativity of worldly beings. Just as a brief flash of lightning on such a night reveals forms for a single instant, likewise, by the power of the Buddha, a virtuous mind that wishes to perform merit arises only occasionally in worldly beings and does not arise continuously.
- **Urgency of Generating the Virtuous Mind:** Because we constantly abide in weak virtue, and the powerful negativity that produces birth in bad migrations is extremely difficult to overcome, ordinary beings are in a very precarious position. Therefore, it is absolutely essential to make efforts to generate and protect this weak virtuous mind.
- **Structural Transition:** Thokme uses this verse as a critical transition: since the support of the body (leisure and fortune) and the virtuous mind are so rare and difficult to find, we must seek a powerful method to protect and expand our virtue—which leads directly into the explanation of the benefits of *bodhicitta* in the subsequent verses.

## Combined Commentary Synthesis

### Consensus
All three commentators agree on the core meaning and symbolism of the lightning analogy in verse 1-5:
- **Symbolism of the Analogy:**
  - **The Dark, Cloudy Night:** Represents the thick, dense darkness of cyclic existence, ignorance, and powerful negativity that leads to birth in bad migrations (hells and other lower realms).
  - **The Lightning Flash:** Represents the fleeting, momentary, and weak nature of a virtuous mind (*dge ba'i blo* / *matiḥ*) or virtuous intelligence.
  - **The Power of the Buddhas:** Represents the essential external condition—the blessings, power, and compassion of the Buddhas—by which this rare virtuous mind is sparked in worldly beings.
- **The Precarious State of Worldly Beings:** All three agree that ordinary, worldly virtue is exceptionally weak and of short duration, while negativity is immense, powerful, and constant.
- **The Urgency of Effort:** Because of this imbalance (weak virtue vs. powerful negativity), ordinary beings are in constant danger of falling into lower realms. Therefore, there is an urgent necessity to make great efforts to generate and sustain a powerful virtuous mind to serve as a remedy.

### Divergences and Unique Insights
- **Sazang's Dual-Structural Treatment:** Sazang Mati Panchen is unique in splitting the explanation of the lightning analogy into two distinct parts of his commentary:
  - In the **Preliminaries (Section 0.2)**, he explains it as the **rarity of the mind of Dharma** (the aspiration to practice).
  - In **Chapter 1 (Section 1.1)**, he explains it as the **weakness of ordinary virtue** relative to negativity.
- **Sazang's Philosophical Definition of Bodhicitta:** Sazang is the only commentator who explicitly defines the remedy for negativity as "the perfect *bodhicitta* endowed with the essence of **emptiness and compassion**." He directly contrasts this with "ordinary virtue that accords with merit," which is helpless against powerful negativity.
- **Gyaltsab's Temporal Nuance:** Gyaltsab Je notes a specific scholastic detail: the phrase "constantly abiding in weak virtue" is explained in terms of **time** (*dus*) in the commentaries, emphasizing that virtue is of short duration, while negativity is constant.
- **Thokme's Structural Integration:** Thokme Zangpo integrates this verse tightly as the "support of the mind," pairing it with the "support of the body" (leisure and fortune, verse 1-4) under the overarching heading of "the support of the person who enters." This creates a logical bridge: once the rarity of both physical and mental supports is realized, the practitioner is urged to adopt *bodhicitta* as the ultimate protective force.
