- **Benefit to Others:** If other Mahayana practitioners who share the same spiritual fortune (disposition and capacity) see these formulations, the text will be as meaningful and beneficial for them as it is for the author.

- **Purpose and Relationship:** The statement of purpose and relationship serves as a branch or gateway to encourage other fortunate individuals to enter and engage with the treatise.

## Combined Commentary Synthesis

### Consensus
All three commentators agree that while Śāntideva's primary motivation for composing the treatise is self-familiarization (self-benefit), there is a clear secondary, altruistic purpose of benefiting others who possess equal spiritual fortune (disposition, capacity, and aspiration for the Mahayana path).
- **Consensus on Altruistic Benefit:** They agree that other Mahayana practitioners of equal fortune who encounter and practice the meaning of the text will receive immense benefit.
- **Consensus on "If" / "Perhaps" (*ci ste*):** Both Gyaltsab and Thokme note that the word "if" (*ci ste*) is glossed as "perhaps" or "by some chance," highlighting that the benefit to others is a secondary, hopeful outcome rather than a prideful assertion of teaching others.
- **Consensus on the Role of Humility:** Thokme and Gyaltsab emphasize that this section completes the expression of humility, which serves to show the author's freedom from pride and to request the reader's forgiveness for any scholastic or poetic shortcomings.

### Divergences and Unique Insights
- **Sazang** connects the benefit to others to the shared spiritual qualities and family, emphasizing that other trainees should engage with the text with joy.
- **Thokme** explicitly cites classical commentators Kṣemadeva and Vairocana to explain that the word "temporarily" (*re zhig*) in the preceding verse establishes the transition from self-benefit to the secondary purpose of benefiting others.
- **Gyaltsab** explains that showing the purpose and relationship here acts as a "branch of entry" (introduction) to inspire others to study the treatise.
