## Today's Verse

> How can I have happiness
> if these enemies, who are the guardians of the prison of cyclic existence,
> and have become executioners in hell and so forth,
> dwell in the net of attachment in my mind? ^4-35

> As long as I have not truly conquered this enemy,
> I will not abandon my striving here.
> If those puffed up with pride
> become angry even at a minor hindrance, they cannot sleep until it is conquered. ^4-36

> If, when defilements, which are naturally mortal and cause suffering,
> are arrayed on the battlefield, one wishes to conquer them forcefully,
> and disregards the suffering of being struck by arrows, spears, and weapons,
> and does not retreat without accomplishing the purpose, ^4-37

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 35 to 37 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Like a brave warrior who does not retreat even when struck by a weapon on the battlefield, we must build the courage to never retreat until we have conquered the enemy of our own afflictions.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

For example, if a wolf and a sheep are tied together with the same rope, the sheep will never find a moment of relaxed ease. As long as the wolf is not cut loose from it, the sheep will always feel fear and great worry.

In the same way, the afflictions living bound together with our mind are like a wolf and a sheep tied together. However good our outer circumstances may be, as long as the afflictions are present in our mind, we will not find real happiness. So freeing our own mind from the bondage of the afflictions becomes the most important thing of all.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when an affliction arises, I will apply its antidote again and again and work at it without giving up.

**Explanation:** _(Not giving up the effort)_ People of this world work without rest to conquer even a small enemy who does them little harm. In the same way, I will apply the antidote until I have conquered the enemy of the afflictions. Applying the antidote again and again is the best way to conquer the afflictions.
