# Day 29 — A dream worth waking from

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Do one thing today you'd be glad you did
> **Detail:** Today's verses see this life as a passing dream. Let that turn into one worthwhile act, now.

## Opening

The confession continues, and Shantideva asks why it matters. He looks back at everything he has enjoyed and sees it has already thinned into memory, the way a dream fades when you wake. The people are gone, but what he did for their sake is not. And death, he admits, could come at any moment. These are sobering verses, but they point somewhere useful.

>

> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

It is easy to read these verses as simply sad. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, reads them the other way, as a push to act. His point about the dream is not that life is worthless. It is that whatever we are gripping now will, at the end, be only a memory we cannot return to, so the right response is to decide, again and again starting today, to do what actually matters. He notes the hard middle line too: the friends and enemies we schemed and cut corners for are already passing away, but the wrongs we did for their sake stay with us. And death does not wait for our plans to be finished, or for us to get around to setting things right. That is not a reason to despair. It is a reason to begin now.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Pick one thing today that you would be glad to have done if the day were your last: a kind word you keep meaning to say, a small wrong you could put right, a few quiet minutes of practice. Then actually do it today, not someday. The dream does not wait, so neither does the doing.
