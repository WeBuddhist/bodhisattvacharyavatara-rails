## Today's Verse

> I go for refuge to Vajrapani,
> who, upon seeing them, causes the messengers of the Lord of Death
> and other enemies to flee in terror in all four directions. ^2-52

> In the past, I transgressed your words.
> Now, having seen great fear,
> I go for refuge to you.
> Please quickly dispel my fear. ^2-53

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on verses 52 and 53 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. The list of protectors closes here with Vajrapani, and then the tone changes completely. Shantideva stops asking for help and admits something instead: in the past, I transgressed your words. After days of building up fear and calling out for rescue, the verses arrive at the part that is actually in his hands. Today asks us to be that specific about our own record, and then to do the one thing that follows from it.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

Two verses, and the second one turns.

The first still belongs to the calling out. Vajrapani is the one at whose mere appearance the messengers of the Lord of Death and the other hostile beings scatter in every direction. Then verse 53 stops asking. In the past, I transgressed your words.

The commentaries do not leave that phrase vague. The word he transgressed is a particular instruction, and they quote it: commit no wrongdoing, practise virtue fully. So this is not a general feeling of being unworthy. It is the admission that he was told plainly what to do and did otherwise, and that this is what all the fear of the last several verses is actually made of.

His Holiness the Dalai Lama draws the consequence. Going for refuge is not finished when you have said it. Having taken refuge, you have to train in what refuge asks of you and stop confusing what should be taken up with what should be dropped. Without that, verse 53 becomes the same transgression again in a more devotional voice.

Which is why the verse ends asking for fear to be removed, not for the past to be undone.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will name one thing I already know I should not be doing, say it plainly to myself, and then not do it for this one day.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ Most of us do not need new information about this. There is usually something already on the list, known for a long time and quietly excused. Snapping at one particular person. A habit that is costing more than it gives back. Work I keep doing carelessly because nobody checks it. Today I will pick one of them, just one, and say it to myself in plain words rather than the softened version I normally use. The softened version is how it survives. Then I will leave it alone until tonight. One day is a short enough promise that I can actually keep it, and keeping it for one day is the whole difference between the confession in verse 53 and the transgression that verse is confessing. If I break it by lunchtime I will start the day again rather than write it off. Tomorrow can decide for itself.
