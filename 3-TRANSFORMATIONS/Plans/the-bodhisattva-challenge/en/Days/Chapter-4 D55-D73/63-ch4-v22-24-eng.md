## Today's Verse

> Even after experiencing that much,
> one is not set free.
> For while experiencing it,
> other misdeeds arise. ^4-22

> If, having found such an opportunity,
> I do not cultivate virtue,
> there is no greater deception than this,
> and no greater foolishness than this. ^4-23

> If, having understood this,
> I later become lazy due to delusion,
> at the time of death,
> great sorrow will arise. ^4-24

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 22 to 24 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. If we waste this good body of freedom and fortune we have found, great grief and regret will rise even more at the time of death.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a saying that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Whoever uses a bowl of gold set with jewels to wipe up filth and vomit is a great fool. But whoever is born human and does wrongdoing is far more foolish still.

The meaning of this saying is this: taking a gold bowl adorned with jewels and using it to wipe up dirty vomit is truly foolish. But more foolish still is the one who gains this precious human body, so hard to find, and then does wrongdoing. Our gaining this body of freedom and fortune is like gaining a precious bowl of gold. If we rely on this body and do wrongdoing instead of doing good, we become even more foolish than the one who wipes vomit with a gold bowl. So we should not spend this life of ours on pointless wrongdoing. We should always work at doing good.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, even if a discouraged feeling comes up while I practice Dharma, I will not give up. I will carry one small practice of doing good through to the end.

**Explanation:** _(Diligence Practice)_ If we do not become discouraged today while doing good, no regret will come later at the time of death. Losing heart on the path of learning Dharma is a mistake. If we do one small good action each day, the strength of our diligence will grow.
