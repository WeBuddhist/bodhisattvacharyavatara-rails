## 3. Chapter Three: Fully Taking Hold of Bodhicitta ^3-0


I happily celebrate all the goodness
that gives every being a rest from the sufferings of the lower realms,
and I celebrate every suffering being
who now lives happily instead. ^3-1


I celebrate every bit of goodness
that acts as a cause for full awakening.
I celebrate every being who truly escapes
the suffering of endless wandering through life after life.
And I celebrate the awakening of the protectors,
and the high levels reached by the children of the Buddhas. ^3-2


I happily celebrate the ocean-like goodness
of the wish that brings happiness to every being,
and I celebrate every action
that truly helps other beings. ^3-3


With my hands pressed together, I ask
the Buddhas of every direction:
please light the lamp of the true teachings
for beings lost in the darkness of suffering. ^3-4


With my hands pressed together, I ask
the Buddhas who wish to pass beyond suffering:
please do not leave this world blind and lost.
Please stay with us for countless ages. ^3-5


Through everything I have just done,
whatever goodness I have gathered,
by that goodness, may every single suffering
of every single being be cleared away. ^3-6


For as long as any being in this world is sick,
until they are completely well again,
may I become their medicine,
their doctor, and the one who cares for them. ^3-7


May I send down a rain of food and drink
to clear away the suffering of hunger and thirst,
and during the terrible ages of famine,
may I myself become food and drink for others. ^3-8


For beings who have nothing and are truly poor,
may I become an endless treasure for them,
and may every kind of thing they need
appear right in front of them. ^3-9


My body, all that I own,
and all the goodness I gather in past, present, and future —
I give all of it away without holding back,
so that I can help every single being. ^3-10


By giving up everything, I go beyond suffering,
and my own mind reaches that state of peace.
Since I must give up everything eventually anyway,
it is best to give it all to other beings now. ^3-11


I have already given this body of mine
to every living being, for them to use as they wish.
So they may kill it, criticize it,
beat it, or do whatever else they like with it, always. ^3-12


They may play with my body,
or make fun of it, or use it as a joke —
since I have already given this body away,
what is the point of holding onto it jealously? ^3-13


Let them do whatever they want with it,
as long as it does not truly harm them.
May no one who thinks of me
ever come away with nothing gained. ^3-14


Whether someone who thinks of me
feels anger or feels faith,
may that very feeling always become
the cause that fulfills everything they wish for. ^3-15


Whether people say unkind things about me,
or cause harm to others,
or even mock and insult me,
may every one of them come to share in awakening. ^3-16


May I become a protector for those without one,
a guide for travelers on the road,
and for those who wish to cross over,
may I become a boat, a raft, or a bridge. ^3-17


For those seeking an island, may I become an island;
for those wanting a bed, may I become a bed;
and for every being who wants a servant,
may I become their servant. ^3-18


May I become a wish-fulfilling jewel, a wonderful vase,
a powerful healing charm, a great medicine,
and a wish-granting tree
that provides for the needs of every living being. ^3-19


Just like the earth and the other great elements,
and just like the sky itself, forever,
may I become the very ground
that supports countless beings in every possible way. ^3-20


And so, until every single being
throughout the endless reaches of space
has gone completely beyond suffering,
may I remain, caring for and supporting them all. ^3-21


Just as the Sugatas who came before us
gave birth to the mind of awakening,
and just as they followed, one step at a time,
the training of a bodhisattva, ^3-22


in the same way, for the benefit of all beings,
I too will give birth to the mind of awakening,
and I too will follow, one step at a time,
that same training. ^3-23


Now that a wise person like this
has taken hold of bodhicitta with joy,
in order to help it keep growing,
they should praise this mind of awakening in this way: ^3-24


Today my life has finally borne fruit.
I have truly made the most of being born human.
Today I am born into the family of the Buddhas;
now I am a child of the Buddhas. ^3-25


From now on, I must do everything I can
to act in ways that fit this new family,
so that I never bring any stain
to this pure and noble family I now belong to. ^3-26


Just as a blind person might, by pure luck,
find a precious jewel in a heap of rubbish,
in that same surprising way,
this mind of bodhicitta has somehow arisen in me. ^3-27


This is the greatest medicine of all,
the one that destroys death itself for every being.
It is also the endless treasure
that clears away the poverty of the world. ^3-28


It is the finest medicine there is,
the one that heals the sicknesses of every being.
It is the tree that gives rest
to beings exhausted from wandering endlessly through life. ^3-29


It is the bridge that carries every being
across and away from the lower, difficult realms.
It is the rising moon of the mind,
that cools the burning pain of every being's disturbed emotions. ^3-30


It is the great sun
that clears away the fog of not knowing, that blinds beings.
It is the rich cream
that rises when the milk of the true teachings is churned. ^3-31


For every traveler journeying along the road of life,
wishing to enjoy true happiness,
this mind of bodhicitta waits close by, offering the greatest happiness,
satisfying every guest, every single being. ^3-32


Today, in front of every protector,
I invite all beings to become guests of the Sugatas themselves,
guests of true happiness, even along the way there.
Let gods, demigods, and everyone else rejoice. ^3-33


This is the end of the third chapter, called "Fully Taking Hold of Bodhicitta," from the Way of the Bodhisattva. ^3-a
