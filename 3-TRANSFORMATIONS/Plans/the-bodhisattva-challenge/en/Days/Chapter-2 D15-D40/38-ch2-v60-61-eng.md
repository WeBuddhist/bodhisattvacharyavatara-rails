## Today's Verse

> What remains of me
> from what I experienced and perished in the past?
> I clung to that,
> and transgressed the words of my guru. ^2-60

> If I must abandon this life,
> and likewise, my relatives and friends,
> and go somewhere alone,
> what good are all my friends and enemies? ^2-61

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 60 and 61 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Everything we enjoyed in the past has already been used up. Nothing solid is left of it. And when death comes, we leave behind our life and all our relatives and friends. We go on alone. So these verses call on us to drop our attachment to the people we like, and our anger at the people we do not. They call on us to put our effort into the Dharma.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give a comparison that is valuable, easy to understand, and encouraging.

As Khenpo Kunzang Palden says, a man of high rank in this life travels with many attendants. But when death arrives, "a great king and a beggar with his stick" are no different. They are equal. Both leave behind their life, their parents and children, and all their wealth and possessions. Both go on alone, empty-handed and bare. Neither has any choice about it.

At that moment the friend we love cannot help us. The enemy we dislike cannot harm us. Only the good and bad actions we have done follow after us. So the point is clear. From now on we should give up our attachment to friends and our anger at enemies, and put our effort into the Dharma.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when attachment or anger toward another person comes up, I will remember that at death I have to go on alone.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ In this life we spend a great deal of effort on liking our friends and disliking our enemies. But as the verse says, at the time of death none of that helps us. We go on alone. So from today we should thin out that attachment and that anger, and settle our own mind into peace. That is very important.
