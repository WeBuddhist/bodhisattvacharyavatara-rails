# Day 37 — The care you already give at a cliff edge

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Do one ordinary thing today with cliff-edge attention
> **Detail:** Today's verse asks for care, not dread: the steady attention you'd give near a long drop.

## Opening

Notice what you do at the edge of a high drop. You do not panic or freeze; you simply pay close attention and place each step with care. Shantideva points to that ordinary, alert care and asks us to bring it to how we live, where the stakes are quietly far higher. Today's verses are about impermanence again, but the response they ask for is not fear. It is attentiveness.

## From the Tradition

Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, keeps the focus on one word: conscientiousness. Near even a small cliff, he says, you naturally stay careful so as not to fall. The Tibetan word covers exactly the quality we use there, an awake, unhurried watchfulness, neither careless nor frantic. The verse simply scales it up. If that much care is sensible at a drop that might bruise a limb, how much more fitting for the longer fall the verse names. The thing worth noticing is the mood. This is not the white-knuckled fear of the last days; it is the calm attentiveness of someone walking a ledge well. Ngulchu adds the useful direction it points in: rather than sitting at ease or sinking into worry, keep gently asking the practical question, how do I avoid the fall, and let that steer the day. Care, not panic, is the whole instruction.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Pick one ordinary action today and do it with cliff-edge attention: making tea, crossing a street, sending one message, speaking to one person. Give it the same unhurried, awake care you would give your feet near a long drop. Notice that this kind of care feels steady, not anxious, and see whether a little more of the day can be lived that way.
