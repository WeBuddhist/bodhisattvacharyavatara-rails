# Day 43 — Asking the buddhas to stay, and dedicating all the good I have gathered

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the two verses 5 and 6 from the third chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. We join our palms and pray that beings are not left sightless and blind. And we dedicate all our own good to removing the suffering of beings.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses tell us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago, when our teacher Shakyamuni had reached the age of eighty, he formed the intention to pass into nirvana. At that time a lay practitioner named Chunda, the blacksmith's son, asked him again and again not to pass away. By this the Teacher's life was extended by three months.

In the same way, this verse joins the palms before the buddhas of the ten directions. They have won the battle against the four maras. It prays them not to pass into nirvana.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today I will dedicate all the good I do to removing the suffering of all beings.

**Explanation:** *(Generosity Practice)* This line shows that we should not dedicate only for ourselves, for a long life, freedom from illness, wealth and the like. All the roots of good we have gathered are to be dedicated completely. They are dedicated as a cause for removing the suffering of body and mind of all beings, who are as many as space is wide.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

To the victorious ones who wish to pass into nirvana I make this request, standing in for all beings: with my body making the gesture of joined palms, I ask that they not leave these beings blind — left in the condition of someone sightless, who does not know what to take up and what to give up — but that they teach the Dharma and so open the eye that sees what to take up and what to give up; and that, in the manner of one who leads a blind person by the hand, they remain for countless ages.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-5]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

To the buddhas who dwell in the fields of the ten directions — for in the definitive sense the victorious ones do not pass into nirvana at all, but, once their work for those to be trained is complete, choose to show the manner of passing into nirvana because circumstances require it — to them I join my palms with my body and make my request in words: do not leave these limitless beings blind, their eye for what to take up and what to give up covered by the cataract of confusion; rather, so as to make clear what is to be taken up and what given up, please remain for many countless ages without passing into nirvana. And one should also think that they accept the request and consent to remain without passing away.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-5]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

To the victorious ones who wish to pass into nirvana I join my palms and make this request: do not leave these beings with the eye of intelligence blinded by ignorance; rather, in order to dispel the darkness of ignorance, please remain for countless ages.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-5]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

To those victorious ones who wish to pass into nirvana I join my palms and make this request: do not leave these beings blind through not knowing; please remain, without passing into nirvana, for countless ages.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-5]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

The victorious ones' Truth Body does not pass into nirvana; but once the deeds of the Form Body are complete they choose to show the manner of passing into nirvana. To them I join my palms with my body and make my request in speech: do not leave these limitless beings with the eye of wisdom blinded by the cataract of confusion; rather, so as to make clear what is to be taken up and what given up, please remain a long while, for many countless ages, without passing into nirvana.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-5]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

The victorious ones, the perfect buddhas, complete their deeds for the sake of those who are the direct disciples of the Form Body; then, for the sake of other disciples — to give rise in them to a sense of urgency and so on — they choose to show, as their final deed, the manner of passing into nirvana. Bowing to them with a respectful body, joining my palms and making my request in words: do not leave these beings with the eye of intelligence that looks to what is to be taken up and what given up blinded by the darkness of ignorance, in a condition like that of someone whose eyes are sightless. Rather, dispelling the darkness of not knowing, and so as to make plain, just as it is, what is to be engaged in and what turned away from, please remain without passing into nirvana for countless ages. The request is made in these words because, if there is no one to show what to take up and what to give up, beings do not know what to abandon and what to adopt.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-5]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

However many victorious ones there are — perfect buddhas, blessed ones — who wish to pass into nirvana in the manner of having finished the work of teaching the Dharma to those to be trained, to all of them I join my palms with my body and make this request through my speech: if the buddhas pass into nirvana, these beings become like people with no eyes, sightless; so do not leave them in that state — blind, sightless, without eyes. Please remain and teach the Dharma for many countless ages and great ages. This is the request, made again and again.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-5]]

##### The Lay Practitioner Chunda's Request

Long ago the lay practitioner Chunda asked our Teacher not to pass into nirvana, and by this his life was extended by three months. Just so, it is explained, in the definitive sense the same request is made to the buddhas dwelling in the fields of the ten directions. The story is given as the illustration for the words of this verse, "with palms joined I make this request."

##### The Request of Chunda, the Blacksmith's Son

Long ago, when our Teacher Shakyamuni had reached the age of eighty and set his mind on passing into nirvana, the lay practitioner Chunda, the blacksmith's son, asked him again and again not to pass away but to remain. Just as he did, it is explained, this verse makes its request to the victorious ones who have won the battle against the four maras.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. A Beginner on the Bodhisattva Path Joins the Palms and Asks the Victorious Ones Who Wish to Pass into Nirvana to Remain

All the commentaries agree in the end that this is a request asking the buddhas not to pass into nirvana, but to remain for countless ages.

###### 2. The Reason for the Request — Without the Buddhas, the Darkness of Ignorance Blinds Beings' Eye for What to Take Up and What to Give Up

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen says the request is made "in order to dispel the darkness of ignorance"; Sabzang Mati Panchen says "do not leave them with the eye of wisdom blinded by the cataract of confusion"; and Minyak Kunzang Sonam says "do not leave them with the eye of intelligence blinded by the darkness of ignorance."

###### 3. The Buddhas' Truth Body Never Passes into Nirvana; It Is the Deeds of the Form Body That, Once Complete, Are Shown to End in Passing Away

Sabzang Mati Panchen explains that although the Truth Body does not pass into nirvana, the manner of passing into nirvana is shown once the deeds of the Form Body have been completed for those to be trained. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden and Minyak Kunzang Sonam agree, both explaining that once the work for those to be trained is complete, the manner of passing into nirvana is shown because circumstances require it.

###### 4. The Historical Illustration of This Request Is the Story of Chunda, the Blacksmith's Son, Who Asked the Teacher Not to Pass Away

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that because the lay practitioner Chunda made this request, the Teacher's life was extended by three months. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that when the Teacher Shakyamuni had reached the age of eighty, the lay practitioner Chunda, the blacksmith's son, asked him again and again to remain.

###### 5. One Person Makes the Request Standing in for All Beings

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that the one who makes the request does so on behalf of all beings, with the body making the gesture of joined palms.

###### 6. The Purpose of the Buddhas' Remaining Is to Teach the Dharma for Countless Ages and, as One Leads a Blind Person, to Open Beings' Eye for What to Take Up and What to Give Up

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that if the buddhas pass into nirvana beings become like blind people with no eyes, and so he asks them to remain and teach the Dharma for countless ages. His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that they open the eye for what to take up and what to give up in the manner of one who leads a blind person by the hand.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows a beginner on the path joining the palms before the victorious ones who wish to pass into nirvana and asking them to remain for countless ages. The reason for the request is that if the buddhas pass into nirvana, there is the danger that the darkness of ignorance will blind beings' eye for what to take up and what to give up, and that they will become like blind people with no eyes.

**Key points.**

- A person joins the palms before the victorious ones who wish to pass into nirvana and asks them to remain for countless ages.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

All these virtues of mine — represented by going for refuge, presenting offerings, paying homage, confessing wrongdoing, rejoicing, urging the buddhas to turn the wheel of Dharma, and asking them not to pass into nirvana — I do not dedicate to my own long life, my freedom from illness, or my great wealth; in short, I do not dedicate them even to my own liberation. May that whole store of virtue instead become the cause that clears away all the suffering of all beings.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-6]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Fourth. In general, the dedication of the Great Vehicle is a dedication made in order to attain great enlightenment. Yet here, for bodhicitta to arise in one's own mind, one must train the mind in the four immeasurables, or in compassion — as in the line "compassion is held to be its root." So I wonder whether what is being shown here is a dedication set out in accord with compassion.

In this way, taking as its representative the virtue of having done all of these — the seven branches explained above — whatever virtue I have done and gathered in the three times, by that may all the suffering, without exception, of samsara and of the lower realms, belonging to all beings equal in number to the reach of space, be cleared away and purified. So one thinks.

##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Having first accomplished well all these branches explained above — from presenting offerings up to asking the buddhas to remain without passing away — by the merit of whatever wholly pure virtue I have gathered, may all the suffering belonging to every being without exception — the suffering of samsara in general, and of the lower realms in particular — be cleared away without remainder, and purified.

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

As explained above, "all these" means: the branches of presenting offerings, paying homage, going for refuge and confessing wrongdoing, which come in the second chapter as the practices that go before; together with meditating on the joy of rejoicing, requesting that the wheel of Dharma be turned, and asking the buddhas to remain without passing into nirvana, which come here in the third chapter. Having accomplished all these preliminary branches, these roots of virtue that gather the accumulations, by whatever virtue I have gathered or accomplished — by that root of virtue — may whatever suffering there is in the body and mind of all beings throughout the whole reach of space be cleared away and left behind. So one prays.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. This Verse Dedicates All the Virtue Gathered by the Seven Preliminary Branches to Clearing Away the Suffering of All Beings

Presenting offerings, paying homage, going for refuge and confessing wrongdoing (from the second chapter); rejoicing, requesting that the wheel of Dharma be turned, and asking the buddhas to remain without passing into nirvana (here in the third chapter) — all the commentaries agree in the end that whatever virtue comes from first accomplishing these seven branches is dedicated to clearing away all the suffering of all beings.

###### 2. The Measure of "Beings" Is Limitless — Equal to Space, With Not One Left Out

The annotation commentary glosses "all beings" as limitless; Kunzang Pelden says "all beings equal in number to the reach of space"; and Kunga Wangchuk says "all beings throughout the whole reach of space."

###### 3. The Suffering to Be Cleared Away Is Both the Suffering of Samsara in General and That of the Lower Realms in Particular, With Nothing Left Over

Kunzang Pelden, Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam all three say that by the virtue of the preliminaries may all the suffering of samsara and of the lower realms belonging to all beings be cleared away and purified, without exception and without remainder.

###### 4. Kunzang Pelden Raises the Question of Whether the Dedication Here Is Set Out in Accord With Compassion

Since bodhicitta arises only if one trains the mind in the four immeasurables, or in compassion, and since compassion is held to be its root, Kunzang Pelden wonders whether the dedication here is presented in a way that accords with compassion.

###### 5. This Dedication Is Not for One's Own Long Life, Freedom From Illness or Wealth, Nor Even for One's Own Liberation, but Is Given as the Cause of Clearing Away Others' Suffering

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that the whole store of virtue from the preliminaries is not dedicated for one's own sake, but with the wish that it become the cause of clearing away all the suffering of all beings.

###### 6. This Verse Closes the General Dedication, and From the Following Verses the Particular Dedications Begin — the Dedication for the Sick and the Rest

Ngulchu Thogme explains that the particular dedication has three parts, and that the dedication for the sake of the sick is the first of them. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk likewise comments on what follows as "the particular aspiration for the sick."

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows how all the virtue accomplished through the seven preliminary branches explained from the second chapter through to the third — offering, paying homage, going for refuge, confessing wrongdoing, rejoicing, requesting that the wheel of Dharma be turned, and asking the buddhas to remain — is dedicated to clearing away, without exception, all the suffering of all beings, both the suffering of samsara and that of the lower realms. From this point on, the verses of particular dedication begin.

**Key points.**

- All the virtue gathered by the seven preliminary branches is dedicated to clearing away the suffering of all beings.
- The measure of "beings" is limitless — equal to the reach of space.
- The suffering to be cleared away is both samsara in general and the lower realms in particular, with nothing left out.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama makes it plain that this dedication is not for one's own sake — not for long life, freedom from illness, great wealth, or even one's own liberation — but is given as the cause of clearing away others' suffering.
- Kunzang Pelden raises the question of whether this dedication is presented in accord with compassion.
- Ngulchu Thogme and Kunga Wangchuk both explain that after this verse the section of particular dedications begins — the dedication for the sake of the sick and the rest.
