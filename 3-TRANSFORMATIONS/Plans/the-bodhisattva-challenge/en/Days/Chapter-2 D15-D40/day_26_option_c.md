# Day 26 — Nothing kept hidden

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Name one thing plainly, without softening it
> **Detail:** Today's verses confess past wrongs; the heart of it is keeping nothing hidden.

## Opening

Confession begins. Shantideva owns the harm of this life and every life before it: what he did, what he led others into, and what he was glad to see. He sees it as a mistake and lays it before the buddhas and bodhisattvas from the depth of his heart, with nothing tucked away.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## Today's Verses

> ཐོག་མ་མེད་ལྡན་འཁོར་བ་ནས། །
> ཚེ་རབས་འདི་འམ་གཞན་དག་ཏུ། །
> བདག་གིས་མ་འཚལ་སྡིག་བགྱིས་པའམ། །
> བགྱིད་དུ་སྩལ་བ་ཉིད་དང་ནི། །
>
> In this and in my other lives
> Throughout beginningless samsara,
> Deluded, I have done misdeeds.
> I’ve instigated them, and also—

> གཏི་མུག་འཁྲུལ་པས་བདག་ནོན་ཏེ། །
> རྗེས་སུ་ཡི་རང་གང་བགྱིས་པ། །
> ནོངས་པ་དེ་ནི་མཐོང་བགྱིས་ནས། །
> བསམ་པ་ཐག་པས་མགོན་ལ་བཤགས། །
>
> Compelled by ignorant confusion—
> I have rejoiced in them. I see
> They were mistakes, and I confess
> To the protectors from my depths.

## From the Tradition

When we admit something, we tend to round off the edges, mentioning the forgivable part and leaving out the rest. Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, points to the verse's last words: confess from the depths of the heart, concealing nothing. The weight a wrong leaves on you stays as long as part of it is kept in the dark. Naming the thing fully, without softening it and without an excuse attached, is what lets it be cleaned. Even the small, private gladness at someone else's wrong gets said too, because a confession that hides its worst part is not yet a confession.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Think of one thing you have been telling yourself in a softened version. Once today, before the buddhas and bodhisattvas, say it plainly instead, the whole of it, without the excuse you usually add.
