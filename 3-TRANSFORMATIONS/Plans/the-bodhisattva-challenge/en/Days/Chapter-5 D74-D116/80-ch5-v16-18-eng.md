## Today's Verse

> Even if one performs all recitations
> and ascetic practices for a long time,
> the wise teach that what is done with a distracted mind
> is meaningless. ^5-16

> Whoever does not know this secret of the mind,
> the supreme essence of the teachings,
> even if they wish to attain happiness and conquer suffering,
> they wander aimlessly without purpose. ^5-17

> Therefore, this mind of mine
> must be well-held and well-guarded.
> What use are many disciplines
> except for the discipline of guarding the mind? ^5-18

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the sixteenth, seventeenth, and eighteenth verses of the fifth chapter. Let us look together at this point: running away with the body alone does not give up desire. Guarding the mind is the root of every training.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us an explanation that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Even the perfection of wisdom, in the end, comes down to our own mind. The main point of all the teachings is what is called "the secret of the mind." This means that the mind's nature is empty, with no independent self. A sutra also says, "The mind has no mind; the nature of mind is clear light." This is called a secret because ordinary people, hearers, and solitary realizers have not fully understood the selflessness of phenomena. So it stays hidden from them. If we do not know this secret nature of the mind, then even if we wish to remove suffering and gain happiness, we will wander about with no purpose. All the conduct of bodhisattvas — the six perfections, skillful means, aspiration, strength, wisdom, and so on — arises based on our own mind.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will not let my attention scatter toward outer objects. Instead, I will guard my own mind well, with mindfulness and awareness.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ Guarding our own mind well with mindfulness and awareness is the root of every discipline. Outer discipline alone does not destroy the afflictions. For example, a novice monk may run away from desire with the body, but this does not give up the desire in the mind. We must give up desire in the mind itself. So today, we will not run away only from outer situations. Instead, we will guard our own mind continuously with mindfulness and awareness.
