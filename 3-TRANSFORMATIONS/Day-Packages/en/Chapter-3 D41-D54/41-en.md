# Day 41 — Rejoicing in the good that eases beings' suffering, and in liberation and awakening

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the two verses 1 and 2 from the third chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. We have finished confessing our own faults. Today is where something else begins: taking heartfelt joy, with no jealousy in it, in the good that others do. That runs from the smallest act which eases someone's suffering all the way up to the awakening of a buddha.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses tell us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago King Prasenajit, wanting to benefit others, made vast offerings to the Buddha and the Sangha for seven days. The Buddha said that each day he would dedicate in the name of whoever had done the most good.

At the palace gate was a beggar called Sada. He rejoiced at the king's giving from the depths of his heart. The good he gained by rejoicing turned out to be greater than the king's giving. So for six days the Buddha dedicated the beggar's good rather than the king's.

On the seventh day the ministers contrived to have the beggars beaten. Sada was upset, and his rejoicing left him. That day the Buddha dedicated the king's giving instead, and the king received a prophecy.

The story shows that rejoicing in what someone else does can bring us more good than our own giving.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when I see others doing good and finding happiness, I will rejoice without jealousy.

**Explanation:** *(Taming the mind)* This practice settles the jealousy and competitiveness in our own mind. If we cultivate heartfelt joy at other people's good and at their happiness, we gain great merit ourselves without any effort. This is the actual practice of rejoicing that the first verse teaches.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Rest from the suffering of the lower realms for all beings who remain in samsara: among the sufferings of existence, to be free for a time from the suffering of the lower realms and to gain a good support in the higher realms becomes, for beings who would otherwise have to undergo the suffering of the lower realms, a place of rest. So the word "rest" is applied to a high rebirth, and he explains that one rejoices in the virtue that is the cause of gaining it. And in those beings who, relying on that virtuous cause, are free from the suffering of the lower realms and dwell in the happiness of a high rebirth — in them too, he explains, one rejoices with joy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-1]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The third chapter, on fully taking hold of bodhicitta, is made up of three parts: the practices that go before, the actual pledge, and, at the end, meditating on gladness in oneself and others. Of the eight branches of gathering the accumulations that belong to the first of these, the earlier four were already explained in the second chapter; here the branch of rejoicing and the three that follow it are to be explained, and this verse is the first of them. Rejoicing in the cause — the virtue of a person of small scope, the virtue that accords with merit — is explained like this: any and all beings in samsara undergo the suffering of the lower realms and are worn out and tormented by it; and for a time they gain the fruit of the happiness of gods and humans, a resting place in the higher realms. He explains that one rejoices in their accomplishing the ten virtues and the like — virtue which, even when it is held by renunciation and by the intention of bodhicitta, is not held by the practice of realizing selflessness. Rejoicing in the fruit is explained like this: compared with the three lower realms, gods and humans are happy for the time being, yet they are still bound to whichever of the three sufferings applies — the all-pervasive suffering of conditioned things, the suffering of suffering, and the suffering of change. Even so, those who were suffering now dwell in the high states of gods and humans, in the excellent happiness of form and youth, power, renown, wealth and enjoyments, experiencing the fruit of their own earlier virtue; and he explains that one rejoices in them with heartfelt joy, without jealousy and without competitiveness.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-1]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

The virtue that is the cause of giving all beings who remain in samsara rest from the suffering of the lower realms and of their gaining an especially good high rebirth; and the fruit of that, those who were suffering now dwelling in the happiness of a high rebirth — in both, he explains, one rejoices with a glad heart, thinking, "How good that such a thing exists!"

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-1]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

That virtue which is the cause of rest from the suffering of the lower realms for all beings — that is, of their not having to undergo it for a time — and the fruit of that, those who were suffering being freed from it and dwelling in happiness: in both, he explains, one rejoices with joy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-1]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

All beings who remain in samsara, and especially those worn out and exhausted by undergoing the suffering of the three lower realms, gain for the time being a little rest in the happiness of the higher realms. He explains that one puts jealousy aside and rejoices, glad and pleased, both in their practising the worldly virtue that is the cause of gaining that happiness, and in those same suffering beings dwelling — by the power of contaminated virtue — in its fruit, the high state of the excellent happiness of gods and humans.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-1]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Among all the beings who wander in existence, those who have undergone suffering for a long time in the three lower realms and have grown worn out and exhausted in body and mind gain, for a while, an especially good support in the higher realms in which to rest a little. He explains that one puts jealousy aside and rejoices — thinking "how good!", giving rise to a clear and trusting faith accompanied by gladness of mind, glad and pleased — both in the worldly virtue that accords with merit, which is the cause of gaining that support, and in its fruit: those who suffered in the lower realms being freed from it and, for the time being, enjoying and dwelling in the happiness of gods and humans in the higher realms. He also says that, as the Great Commentary and other works explain, rejoicing is to be done with all three doors: the mind exceedingly clear and joyful, the body trembling with tears coming and the hairs standing on end, and the speech saying, "that was well done, that was excellent."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-1]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

He explains that one rejoices from the depths of the heart in both of these: in the contaminated virtue by which all beings — high, low and middling — are given rest from the sufferings of the lower realms, the heat and cold and hunger and thirst and the rest of the hell beings, hungry ghosts and animals — virtue of the three doors of body, speech and mind that is not held by the generating of bodhicitta and is not included in what accords with liberation; and in the fruit of that virtue, those suffering beings being born for the time being in the higher realms, in the abodes of gods and humans, and dwelling there with a little happiness of body and mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-1]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. This Verse Announces the Fifth of the Eight Branches of Gathering the Accumulations, the Branch of Rejoicing

After the four branches were explained in the second chapter, this verse introduces the first of the four branches that begin with rejoicing; it explains rejoicing in the cause and the fruit of the virtue of a person of small scope, the virtue that accords with merit.

###### 2. The Verse Rejoices Both in the Virtue That Gives Rest from the Suffering of the Lower Realms and in Its Fruit, Suffering Beings Dwelling in Happiness

All the commentaries agree in the end, dividing the verse into rejoicing in the cause and rejoicing in the fruit.

###### 3. The Virtue Rejoiced In Is Merely Contaminated, Worldly Virtue, Not Held by the Practice of Realizing Selflessness

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains it as the ten virtues and the like, which — though held by renunciation and by the intention of bodhicitta — are not held by the practice of realizing selflessness. Sabzang Mati Panchen too determines it as contaminated, calling it "worldly virtue." ⚑ Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk, however, explains that virtue as merely virtue of the three doors of body, speech and mind, not held by the generating of bodhicitta and not included in what accords with liberation; since he determines it to be contaminated virtue in which there is not even bodhicitta, there is a difference here. ⚑

###### 4. Even the Happiness of Gods and Humans Gained in This Way Is Happiness Only by Comparison with the Lower Realms, and Is Not Free of the Three Sufferings

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that even the happiness of gods and humans is still bound to whichever of the three applies: the all-pervasive suffering of conditioned things, the suffering of suffering, and the suffering of change.

###### 5. The Way of Rejoicing Is a Heartfelt Gladness, Free of Jealousy and Competitiveness

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden, Sabzang Mati Panchen, Minyak Kunzang Sonam and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk all agree in explaining that one puts jealousy aside and rejoices with joy from the depths of the heart.

###### 6. Rejoicing Is to Be Done with All Three Doors — Gladness in the Mind, Trembling and Tears in the Body, and "Well Done" in the Speech

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that this too is taught in the Great Commentary and other works.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse opens the third chapter. It is the first verse of the fifth of the eight branches of gathering the accumulations, the branch of rejoicing. It teaches rejoicing from the side of the cause — the contaminated virtue by which all beings are given rest from the suffering of the lower realms — and from the side of the fruit — those who were suffering now dwelling in the happiness of gods and humans — rejoicing in both with heartfelt joy and without jealousy.

**Key points.**

- This verse announces the fifth branch of gathering the accumulations, the branch of rejoicing, and explains rejoicing in the cause and the fruit of the virtue of a person of small scope, the virtue that accords with merit.
- The commentaries agree in the end that the virtue rejoiced in is merely contaminated, worldly virtue; but they differ over whether renunciation and the intention of bodhicitta are present in it. ⚑
- Even the happiness of gods and humans gained in this way is happiness only by comparison with the lower realms, and is not free of whichever of the three sufferings applies.
- The way of rejoicing is a heartfelt gladness, free of jealousy and competitiveness.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that rejoicing is to be done with all three doors: gladness in the mind, the body trembling with tears coming, and the speech saying "well done."

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

**Whatever virtue has been gathered** — keeping ethical discipline and the rest — **that has become the cause** of attaining the enlightenment of the shravakas or the enlightenment of the pratyekabuddhas: in that too **I rejoice.** And in the fruit of it — **embodied beings,** that is, beings of the three realms, who are **definitely freed from the suffering of samsara,** the noble arhats — **I rejoice.** And in the final fruit, the perfect **enlightenment of the protectors, and** in the temporary fruit, **the grounds of the buddhas' heirs — in these too I rejoice.**

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

In the virtue that accords with liberation which has been gathered, and is still being gathered, as the cause of the enlightenment of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas — in that too, as a cause of liberation, I rejoice.

The freedom of all embodied beings from the suffering of samsara is the liberation in which karma and afflictions are exhausted, the peaceful sphere of cessation; and in those who abide in it too I rejoice.

And in the final attainment of the bodhisattvas of the Great Vehicle, the state of omniscience, definite goodness; and in those qualities of the bodhisattva grounds and paths that are the means of attaining it — in these too I rejoice.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-2]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The virtue of a person of middling scope — the virtue of the Lesser Vehicle that accords with liberation — has two aspects, cause and fruit. As for the cause: whatever virtue has been gathered that has become the cause of the enlightenment of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas, virtue held by renunciation and by the realization of selflessness — in that I rejoice with joy.

As for the fruit: embodied beings, beings of the three realms, are definitely freed from the suffering of samsara — birth, ageing, sickness and death and the rest — and attain the states of the learners, stream-enterer, once-returner and non-returner, and of the one beyond learning, the arhat. In that I rejoice.

At that point the ocean of blood and tears has dried up and the mountain of bones has been broken, and for them gold and a clod of earth are equal. One remembers those qualities of abandonment and realization; and since our meeting with the holy Dharma and the Vinaya comes through the kindness of both the compassionate Teacher and his teaching, this too is their kindness. So, remembering that kindness, I rejoice in them with faith.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-2]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

In the gathering of the virtue that accords with liberation, and the rest, which becomes the cause of the enlightenment of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas — in that too I rejoice.

And in the fruit of it, embodied beings being definitely freed from the suffering of samsara and attaining nirvana — in that too I rejoice.

As for the last two lines, "I rejoice in the enlightenment of the protectors and in the grounds of the buddhas' heirs," he gives them the outline heading "third" and sets them out as the meaning of the third point.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-2]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

In the gathering of the virtue that has become the cause of the enlightenment of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas, I rejoice.

In embodied beings being definitely freed from the suffering of samsara — that is, attaining arhatship — I rejoice.

And in the final fruit, the enlightenment of the protectors, and in the temporary fruit, the grounds of the buddhas' heirs, I rejoice.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-2]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Whatever virtue of the two accumulations has been gathered that has become the cause of accomplishing the three enlightenments — definite goodness, taken in common — in that I am glad and rejoice.

And in embodied beings, through gathering the accumulations in that way, attaining the nirvana that is definite freedom from the suffering of samsara — in that too I rejoice.

In particular, in the protectors, the perfect buddhas, attaining the final, unsurpassable great enlightenment; and in the cause of that, the virtue of the grounds and paths of the buddhas' heirs, the bodhisattvas — in these too I rejoice most fully.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-2]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Of the three enlightenments of definite goodness, whatever virtue that accords with liberation — virtue included within the two accumulations — has been gathered that has become the cause of attaining the enlightenment of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas: in all of it I am glad and rejoice.

And in the fruit of that: whichever embodied beings are definitely freed from the whole of samsara's suffering, together with the subtle all-pervasive suffering of conditioned things, and attain the enlightenment of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas — in that I am glad and rejoice.

In some works this verse is also explained by applying it to the cause and fruit that the three enlightenments have in common.

And it is said that one rejoices in the cause and fruit of unsurpassable enlightenment as well — as in the words, "I rejoice in the enlightenment of the protectors and in the grounds of the buddhas' heirs."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-2]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

In all that virtue — hearing, reflecting and meditating — which has become the cause of attaining the fruit, the enlightenment of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas, I rejoice with joy from the depths of my heart.

And in the fruit of that: whichever embodied beings — that is, beings of the shravaka or pratyekabuddha family — are for the time being definitely freed from the suffering of samsara and have made actual the fruit of the arhat and of the pratyekabuddha, both the rhinoceros-like and those who live in groups — in them too I rejoice with joy from the depths of my heart.

He gives the passage the outline heading "third, rejoicing in the virtue of the Great Vehicle," and sets it out as the meaning of the third point.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-2]]

##### The Story of the Beggar Sada

Long ago King Prasenajit, with the intention of benefiting others, made vast offerings to the Buddha and the Sangha for seven days. The Buddha said that the dedication would be made in the name of whoever had gathered more virtue than the king. At the palace gate a beggar named Sada rejoiced from the depths of his heart in the king's giving; the virtue of that rejoicing turned out to be greater than the king's giving, and so it was the beggar's own virtue that was dedicated.

The same happened for six days. But on the seventh day the ministers contrived to have the beggars beaten, and the beggar Sada was upset and lost his mind of rejoicing; so on that day the king's own giving could be dedicated, and the king received a prophecy. This shows that the virtue of rejoicing in another's virtue can be greater even than one's own giving.

The example is also told of a woodcutter's wife who rejoiced when a bowl of cooked rice was offered to a sage, and a treasure appeared inside their house.

- **The ocean of blood and tears drying up and the mountain of bones being broken** (ཁྲག་དང་མཆི་མའི་རྒྱ་མཚོ་སྐམ་ཞིང་རུས་པའི་རི་བོ་བཅོམ་པ།) → an image of how much suffering beings had gathered in samsara before liberation was attained. #### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. This Verse Shows Three Objects of Rejoicing

The virtue that has become the cause of the enlightenment of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas; beings being definitely freed from the suffering of samsara; and the enlightenment of the protectors together with the grounds of the buddhas' heirs. All the commentaries agree in the end that one should rejoice in these three.

###### 2. The First, the Virtue, Is the Virtue That Accords with Liberation and Has Become the Cause of the Enlightenment of the Shravakas and Pratyekabuddhas

It is explained as virtue such as keeping ethical discipline, and the three of hearing, reflecting and meditating — virtue held by renunciation and by the realization of selflessness.

###### 3. The Second, the Fruit, Is Embodied Beings Being Definitely Freed from the Suffering of Samsara and Attaining the States of Arhat and Pratyekabuddha

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains it as the attainment of the states from the learner stream-enterer up to the arhat beyond learning. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains the fruit as the arhat and the pratyekabuddha, both the rhinoceros-like and those who live in groups.

###### 4. The Third Is the Final Fruit, the Perfect Enlightenment of the Protectors, and the Temporary Fruit, the Qualities of the Grounds and Paths of the Buddhas' Heirs

It is explained that the enlightenment of the protectors is to be understood as the final fruit, and the grounds of the buddhas' heirs as the temporary fruit.

###### 5. Minyak Kunzang Sonam Comments on This Verse on the Basis of the Framework of the Three Enlightenments of Definite Goodness

He says that in some works this verse is also explained by applying it to the cause and fruit that the three enlightenments have in common.

###### 6. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk Both Label the Last Two Lines "Third"

Each identifies the third object of rejoicing separately, as the object that is the virtue of the Great Vehicle.

###### 7. His Holiness the Dalai Lama Makes It Plain That the Liberation of Embodied Beings Is the Liberation in Which Karma and Afflictions Are Exhausted — the Peaceful Sphere of Cessation

He explains that one should rejoice in those who abide in it too.

###### 8. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden Explains That One Should Rejoice with Faith, Remembering the Qualities of Abandonment and Realization and the Kindness of the Teacher

He explains that one should reflect that meeting the holy Dharma and the Vinaya is itself the Teacher's kindness, and so rejoice with faith.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows three objects of rejoicing: one rejoices in the virtue that has become the cause of attaining the enlightenment of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas; one rejoices in the fruit, beings being definitely freed from the suffering of samsara; and one rejoices in the perfect enlightenment of the protectors and in the qualities of the grounds and paths of the buddhas' heirs. All the commentaries agree in the end that this is what the verse teaches.

**Key points.**

- The first, the virtue, is the virtue that accords with liberation and has become the cause of the enlightenment of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas.
- The second, the fruit, is embodied beings being definitely freed from the suffering of samsara and attaining the states of arhat and pratyekabuddha.
- The third is the final fruit, the perfect enlightenment of the protectors, and the temporary fruit, the qualities of the grounds and paths of the buddhas' heirs.
- It is also explained that this liberation of embodied beings is the peaceful sphere of cessation, in which karma and afflictions are exhausted.
- The story showing that the virtue of rejoicing from the heart in another's virtue can be greater even than one's own giving points to the power of rejoicing.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that this verse can be commented on either by applying it to the separate cause and fruit of each of the three enlightenments of definite goodness, or to the cause and fruit they have in common.
