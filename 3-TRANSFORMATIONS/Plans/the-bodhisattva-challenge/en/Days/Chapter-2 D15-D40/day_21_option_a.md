# Day 21 — Offer the thing you'd rather keep

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Before your next treat, offer it in your mind first
> **Detail:** Shantideva offers fine food and incense to the buddhas. Today, offer something you'd rather keep.

## Opening

Today's offering is food and fragrance, the good things. In his mind, Shantideva sends up billowing clouds of sweet incense and lays out a feast of fine foods and drinks for the buddhas and bodhisattvas. These are exactly the pleasures we tend to want all for ourselves, and he gives them away first.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

There is a small tightening we know well: the good thing arrives, and part of us wants it only for ourselves.

The verse offers fine foods and sweet incense to the buddhas. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, lists the delicacies plainly, sweet syrups, white sugar, the nicest things on the table. That detail matters. Shantideva does not offer the leftovers; he offers the very things he would most like to keep. Each time you hand over what you wanted for yourself, even in the mind, the tight grip of holding on loosens a little. Giving, it turns out, is a muscle, and this is how it grows stronger. The one who is freed of the clinging is you, and the people around you meet a more open hand.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

The next time something good is in front of you, a nice meal, a coffee, a small treat, pause before the first taste and offer it in your mind to the buddhas and bodhisattvas. If you feel a flicker of not wanting to share it, that is the very spot to give from. Then enjoy it freely.
