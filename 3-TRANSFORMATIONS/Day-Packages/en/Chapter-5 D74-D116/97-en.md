# Day 97 — The Borrowed Machine: Seeing the Body as Separate from the Mind and as a Contraption of Impure Parts

**Date:** Oct 10
**Chapter:** 5
**Verses covered:** 5-60 to 5-61

<!-- sec:challenge -->
## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the sixtieth and sixty-first verses of the fifth chapter. When we say "my body," are the "I" who guards and the body that is guarded really one?

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a valuable point. The *Sutra of Nanda's Entering the Womb* says that eighty thousand worms live in the rotting waste of the food we eat, in the intestines. The sutra also says that the foul, disgusting smell of feces and urine comes from these worms. So this body is not clean, and it is not right to feel pride or attachment toward it.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, if attachment to my own body arises, I will remember that it is a collection of impure things, and I will do something that helps others.

**Explanation:** *(Taming the mind)* This body is a collection of impure things and the home of eighty thousand tiny worms. There is no reason to guard it out of attachment. Even so, we keep it alive in order to take the essence of the body, and we do this by helping others. In this way we tame our own mind.

##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Just as an object borrowed from the territory of a very powerful lord must be handed back, so too this body is a loaned or borrowed body whose very nature is impure. It is not right to take a body of such a nature as "mine," to count it as something to be looked after, cherishing it with affection in every way, and to go to extremes on its behalf over food, clothing, ill talk, wrongdoing, and hardship. Of the mind that cherishes the body and the body that is cherished, one is by nature mere clarity and awareness (གསལ་རིག), and the other is by nature flesh, bones, and sinews (ཤ་རུས་རྡོས་བཅས). Those two are separate; so what can that body do for you — what benefit is there for the mind itself?

Although there is in fact no connection, having taken this body as linked to oneself and "mine" — "it is connected with me" — why, mind, do you cling to it and guard it? If you, mind, and this body are separate and distinct — cognizance (རིག་པ) and inert matter (བེམ་པོ) — then what can that inert body do for you, mind? There is no need for it.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

"Yet I guard it because it is connected to me as self and as mine" — if you think so: having taken this body as "mine," why, mind, do you guard it by way of clinging and attachment, cherishing and adorning it? To do so is not right, because it gives rise to many faults and sufferings.

If you think, "This is closely needed by myself": you, mind, are a continuum of mind that has wandered from beginningless time — the continuum of mind of the intermediate state that preceded this present existence — whereas this body is made from the impure matter of the father and mother of this life. So you, mind, and this body have no connection with each other whatever; they are separate, and their defining characteristics are different as well. Then what can that inert body do for you, the mind, the knower? For this body will abandon you, mind, and you, mind, will abandon this body, and each will go off separately to one place after another. Therefore it is not right to take this body as "mine" and guard it with a mind of attachment.

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

This present body, an impure heap of flesh and blood, you, unruly mind, take as yours; so, mind, for what reason do you guard this body from places of fear? It is not fitting to do so. You, mind, and this body are separate: the body is inert matter, whereas mind is one's own cognizance (ཤེས་པ). Since these two are by nature different — inert matter and cognizance — then what can that body do for you, mind? There is no need at all, he says.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Mind and Body Differ in Nature, So the Mind Has No Reason to Guard the Body as Its Own

This verse sets out that because mind and body differ in nature — cognizance (shes pa) and inert matter (bem po) — there is no reason for the mind to take the body as its own and guard it.

###### 2. Minyak Kunzang Sonam Contrasts the Mind's Beginningless Wandering with the Body Made of This Life's Impurity

Minyak Kunzang Sonam sets out the difference between the mind's continuum, which has wandered from beginningless time, and the body, which is made from the impure matter of this life's father and mother, and explains that the two have no connection with each other whatever.

###### 3. His Holiness Uses the Simile of a Borrowed Object, and Sets Clarity and Awareness Apart from Flesh and Bones

His Holiness the Dalai Lama shows the body's borrowed nature through the simile of borrowing an object from a very powerful lord, which must be handed back, and separately sets out the difference between the mind, whose nature is mere clarity and awareness, and the body, whose nature is flesh, bones, and sinews.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that it is not right for one's own mind to take this body as "mine," to cherish it, and to guard it: the mind, which is clarity and awareness, and the body, which is inert matter, are separate in nature, so the body can bring the mind no benefit at all.

**Key points.**

- The mind is by nature clear and knowing, whereas the body is inert matter made of flesh, blood, and the substances of one's parents; the two are different in nature and, ultimately, unconnected.
- The mind's continuum has wandered in samsara from beginningless time, while the body is made from the impure substance of this life's parents; so in the end mind and body abandon each other.
- This body is like a debt or a borrowed thing, impure in its nature; so even if it is cherished with food, clothing, and pleasant words, it brings the mind no benefit at all.
- Taking the body as "mine" and guarding and cherishing it out of attachment is the cause that produces many faults, sufferings, and unwanted results.

Deluded mind, why do you not take as your own a clean wooden form, free of rot, stench, and the like? ##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

"I need it just as a support in which to dwell" — if you say so: mind, deluded about what to take up and what to abandon, if you need a support in which to dwell, why do you not take a clean wooden human figure as "my body"? That would be fitting. But what good is it to guard this wretched, foul, rotting body — a machine (འཁྲུལ་འཁོར) that is by nature an assembly of the thirty-six impure substances (རྫས་སོ་དྲུག) such as flesh and blood, bound together by bones and sinews, stretching out here and drawing in there, with impurity leaking from its nine openings? There is no need for it.

Instead, what use is it to guard this stinking, rotting machine — an assembly of impurities? For it is not right to be attached to it.

But this machine, an assembly of impure things such as flesh and blood, is by nature rotten and foul-smelling; so what good is it to guard and maintain it? <!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

"Although there is no connection in that way, I earlier took it as the support in which I dwell, so it is not right to cast it aside now" — if you think so: alas, mind deluded about what to take up and abandon, for what reason do you not take as "my body," as the support in which you dwell, a clean wooden form that needs none of the hardship of nourishing with food, clothing, and so on? Instead, this very machine of a body, an assembly of many impurities gathered in one place, is by nature putrid, rotten, and foul-smelling, and must be maintained even at the cost of hardship; what good is it to guard it with cherishing and adornment and to keep it going? It is fit for no kind of necessary equipment, such as a support in which to dwell. Moreover, the phrases "why guard it?" and "what good is it to guard it?" teach that it is not right to guard it out of mere pointless clinging and attachment; apart from that, for the sake of extracting its essence, accomplishing the welfare of others, and so on, the body must be guarded — the means of guarding it, the purposes of guarding it, and so forth are explained at length in the *Compendium of Training* (བསླབ་བཏུས).

What good is it to guard it and hold it as self? It is not fitting — such is the meaning.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. If a Support Is All That Is Needed, a Clean Wooden Form Is Better Than This Machine of Impurities

This verse sets out that if the body is needed merely as a support in which to dwell, there is no reason to prize this machine of impure things above a clean wooden form.

###### 2. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden Cites the Sutra of Nanda's Entering the Womb: Eighty Thousand Worms in the Body's Waste

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden, quoting the *Sutra of Nanda's Entering the Womb*, sets out how eighty thousand worms live in the waste matter of the body.

###### 3. Minyak Kunzang Sonam Cites the Compendium of Training: Not Guarding Out of Attachment, but for Essence and Others' Welfare

Minyak Kunzang Sonam, citing the *Compendium of Training*, sets out that although it is not right to guard the body out of mere attachment, the body must be guarded for the sake of extracting its essence and accomplishing others' welfare.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that it is pointless and wrong to take this impure-natured body as a self out of attachment and to guard it. Guarding this body out of attachment is no different in meaning from taking a clean wooden form as "me"; therefore attachment must be abandoned.

**Key points.**

- The thought that this body must be guarded because we have held it for a long time, or because it is the mind's support, is the error of being deluded about what to take up and what to abandon.
- Guarding this rotten, foul-smelling mass of impure substances — flesh, blood, and so on — brings no benefit at all; it is as pointless as holding a clean wooden form to be "me."
- The *Sutra of Nanda's Entering the Womb* says that eighty thousand worms arise from the rotting flesh and fat in the intestines, which shows the body's impure nature clearly.
- Although it is not right to guard the body out of pointless attachment, the *Compendium of Training* explains clearly that it must be guarded for the sake of accomplishing others' welfare.
