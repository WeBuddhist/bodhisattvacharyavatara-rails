# Day 6 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: Benefits of Bodhicitta — Verses 1-16 to 1-18

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Knowing the Difference (Clear Understanding):** Today's teaching helps you see two levels of wishing to be enlightened. One is just a wish, and the other is active practice. Knowing this difference helps you move from just hoping to actually doing.

*   **Gaining Endless Merit (Continuous Virtue):** When you make a firm promise to help all living beings and begin to practice, good merit builds up every moment. This happens even when you are sleeping or not paying attention. Your mind continues to grow in goodness without stopping.

*   **Building a Stable Spiritual Life (Firm Commitment):** Making a sincere and clear commitment to help all living beings gives your practice a strong base. This kind of promise does not break easily. It protects your spiritual path and keeps your mind focused on what matters most.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-16**

> འགྲོ་བར་འདོད་དང་འགྲོ་བ་ཡི། །
> བྱེ་བྲག་ཇི་ལྟར་ཤེས་པ་ལྟར། །
> དེ་བཞིན་མཁས་པས་འདི་གཉིས་ཀྱི། །
> བྱེ་བྲག་རིམ་བཞིན་ཤེས་པར་བྱ། །
>
> *Think of the difference between wishing to travel and actually traveling. In the same way, the wise should understand the difference between these two minds.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-16**

The great teacher Śāntideva uses a simple journey to explain two kinds of the wish for enlightenment. The first is like a person lying in bed who wishes to travel but has not yet moved. The second is like a traveler who has stepped onto the road and is walking toward the goal. The first mind only wishes for enlightenment, while the second is actively practicing the six perfections. Wise practitioners should understand this difference clearly, because it shapes how much benefit their practice can bring to themselves and others.

---

#### **Verse 1-17**

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྨོན་པའི་སེམས་ལས་ནི། །
> འཁོར་ཚེ་འབྲས་བུ་ཆེ་འབྱུང་ཡང་། །
> ཇི་ལྟར་འཇུག་པའི་སེམས་བཞིན་དུ། །
> བསོད་ནམས་རྒྱུན་ཆགས་འབྱུང་བ་མིན། །
>
> *Great fruits arise in the cycle of life from the aspiring mind. However, it does not produce a continuous flow of merit in the way the engaging mind does.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-17**

The aspiring wish for enlightenment is extremely valuable — like a cracked diamond that still outshines all golden ornaments. Even this wish alone brings great happiness and good results in this life and future lives. However, it has one limitation: it cannot create a non-stop flow of good merit. Only the engaging mind, which is tied directly to active practice of generosity and the other perfections, produces merit that flows without interruption. The aspiring mind seeks the fruit, but the engaging mind actively builds the causes needed to reach it.

---

#### **Verse 1-18**

> གང་ནས་བཟུང་སྟེ་སེམས་ཅན་ཁམས། །
> མཐའ་ཡས་རབ་ཏུ་དགྲོལ་བའི་ཕྱིར། །
> མི་ལྡོག་པ་ཡི་སེམས་ཀྱིས་སུ། །
> སེམས་དེ་ཡང་དག་བླངས་གྱུར་པ། །
>
> *From the moment one formally adopts the engaging mind with an irreversible resolve to free infinite sentient beings,*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-18**

The moment a practitioner formally takes up the engaging wish for enlightenment — with a firm, unshakeable resolve to free all living beings from suffering — a great and unending stream of merit begins. This merit does not stop even when the practitioner is asleep, distracted, or unaware. It continues in all postures and all states of mind, as vast and endless as the sky. However, there is one important condition: this flow of merit continues only as long as the promise is not broken. Keeping the commitment and training in the path of a Hero of Enlightenment is therefore essential.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Noticing Wishing vs. Doing:** Today, I will check whether my spiritual practice stays only at the level of wishing. When I notice that I am only hoping to be kind or patient, I will take one small, real action to move from wishing into doing.

*   **2. Recalling My Commitment:** Each time I sit down, eat, or begin a new activity today, I will remind myself of my commitment to benefit others. I will let this reminder guide my behavior throughout the day, keeping my resolve firm and clear.

*   **3. Trusting the Flow of Goodness:** Even when I feel tired, distracted, or forgetful today, I will trust that my sincere commitment to help others continues to build goodness in my mind. I will not feel discouraged by my lack of focus, but instead renew my resolve gently and continue.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམར་བར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Aspiring Mind of Enlightenment (བྱང་ཆུབ་སྨོན་པའི་སེམས།):** This is the heartfelt wish to reach full enlightenment for the benefit of all living beings. It is like wanting to make a journey but not yet having taken the first step. It brings great goodness and happiness in this life and future lives.

*   **Engaging Mind of Enlightenment (བྱང་ཆུབ་འཇུག་པའི་སེམས།):** This is the active commitment to practice the six perfections and walk the full path toward enlightenment. It goes beyond wishing and enters actual practice. Once it is taken up sincerely, it creates an endless flow of goodness that never stops.

*   **Continuous Flow of Merit (བསོད་ནམས་རྒྱུན་ཆགས།):** This refers to the unbroken stream of goodness and positive energy that builds when the engaging mind is combined with real practice. Unlike the aspiring mind, this flow does not pause — it continues even during sleep or moments of carelessness.

*   **Irreversible Resolve (མི་ལྡོག་པའི་སེམས།):** This means a firm, unshakeable commitment that does not turn back. When a practitioner formally adopts the engaging mind with this kind of resolve, the promise to free all beings becomes the foundation of their whole spiritual life.

*   **Six Perfections (ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག):** These are the six main practices of a Hero of Enlightenment: generosity, moral discipline, patience, effort, meditation, and wisdom. The engaging mind is defined by actively training in these six perfections, which are the causes of full enlightenment.
