## Today's Verse

> The practices of bodhisattvas
> are taught as immeasurable.
> Therefore, one must definitely practice
> the conduct of purifying the mind for that long. ^5-97

> Three times a day and night,
> one should recite the three aggregates.
> By relying on the Buddhas and the Mind of Awakening,
> one pacifies the remaining downfalls. ^5-98

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the ninety-seventh and ninety-eighth verses of the fifth chapter. The training of bodhisattvas is endless. Even so, we must begin by wiping away our own mistakes three times in the day and three times in the night.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. The _Akashagarbha Sutra_ says as follows. Just as wind enters through a hole, so from whatever fault arises in the mind, from that very place Mara finds an opening. Therefore, a bodhisattva must keep the mind free of faults. By this, just as wind enters through a hole, Mara finds an opening where a fault arises in the mind. So a bodhisattva guards the mind so that no fault arises.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, three times in the day and night, I will recite the _Three Aggregates Sutra_ and confess my own mistakes.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ If we recite the _Three Aggregates Sutra_, relying on the buddhas and bodhisattvas, all the remaining downfalls are pacified. This fully purifies our own mind.
