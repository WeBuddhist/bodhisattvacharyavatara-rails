## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on verses 6 and 7 from the second chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. It teaches us that even if we are materially poor and have no physical wealth to offer, we should never feel discouraged. With a pure and devoted heart, we can mentally offer all the beautiful things in the universe to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas. Out of their great compassion, they accept our sincere mental offerings to help us complete our positive energy (merit).

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

These verses show that when it comes to gathering spiritual merit, a pure heart matters far more than physical wealth. There is a famous ancient story about Emperor Ashoka in a previous life when he was just a little boy. Seeing the Buddha walking by, the boy felt deep devotion and offered a handful of sand into the Buddha's bowl, imagining it was pure gold dust. Out of deep compassion, the Buddha accepted this humble gift. Because of the pure intention behind that simple offering, the boy was reborn as a great Dharma King.

Master Shantideva humbles himself by saying: "I am extremely poor and lack previous merit; I have no physical treasures to offer." However, through the power of his mind, he gathers all the unowned beauty of the universe and offers it to the Protectors of the world. The Buddhas and Bodhisattvas do not need our material gifts. Yet, out of their immense mercy and love for us, they accept these mental offerings to help us purify our negative karma and build our spiritual strength. This is the skillful art of a Bodhisattva — creating boundless positive energy through the power of a pure mind.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, even if I lack material wealth, I will mentally offer all beautiful things to the Three Jewels with pure devotion to gather merit.

**Explanation:** *(Generosity Practice)* Many people feel helpless or discouraged, thinking they cannot practice generosity or make offerings because they do not have money or expensive goods. But today, following the wisdom of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*, I will not let a lack of material wealth stop me. Just like the little boy who offered a handful of sand with a pure heart, whenever I see something beautiful — a lovely view, pleasant nature, or wonderful objects — I will mentally present them to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas with sincere devotion. This simple practice shows that we don't need money to build a rich spiritual life; a pure, generous mind is enough to gather immense positive energy.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

Having **taken** them up **with my own mind**, I offer well **to** the **Sages**, the **supreme** among **two-legged beings**, together **with their heirs**, the host of bodhisattvas. #### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

All those offering-substances reaching to the very ends of boundless space, whose number and measure cannot be grasped and which belong to no owner — recalling the qualities of the body, speech, and mind of the buddhas and their heirs described above — I take up with my mind and offer well, with my own great faith and respect, to the Sages, the supreme among two-legged beings, together with their heirs. To whatever field one offers, one gathers or gains measureless merit: so may you holy recipients of offering, endowed with great compassion and given over to the welfare of others, accept these offerings; look upon me with great compassion and love, and accept these offerings of mine. (If one had to offer only substances that one owned oneself, then a person of poor means and low fortune would be, as it were, empty-handed for want of offering-substances; that is exactly why here one must take up with the mind and offer all things, whether owned or unowned.)

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-6]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The special feature of the application: "having taken up with the mind" means holding them in mind, generating the notion that they are as if one's own, and offering without any grudging attitude. The special feature of the field: the Sage — who through his ability (thub pa) keeps the conduct of body, speech, and mind without any lapse — the Sage, supreme among two-legged beings, of greater courage than the beings of the six classes: the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, together with their heirs, the host of bodhisattvas. If one offers to them "well" — that is, for the purpose of giving rise to the precious awakening mind in one's own being — [it bears great fruit], for they are a great purifier of offerings, such that even a small offering, once made, bestows a great result. For example, by offering seven peas to the Buddha, and by a child's scattering a handful of sand, one became the Dharma-king Ashoka. So: may these holy recipients of offering, endowed with the great compassion that wishes to benefit beings without deceit or pretense, "look upon us with love" — draw near in mind with great tender compassion — and "accept these offerings of mine."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-6]]

May you, the holy recipients of offering, all endowed with great compassion, look upon me with love and accept these offerings of mine.

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Having taken them up with a mind of deep faith, I offer well — with the three spheres [giver, gift, recipient] completely pure — to the Lord of Sages, supreme among beings, the Buddha, together with his heirs, the host of bodhisattvas. May you, the holy recipients of offering, endowed with the great compassion that wishes to benefit all beings, draw near to us in tender compassion and be pleased to accept these offerings emanated by my mind, however you will.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

As for the manner of offering and the rest: such offering-substances I take up with my mind — that is, I make them my own in thought — and offer well, with faith and respect and with the three spheres pure, to the field of offering: the Lords of Sages, supreme bulls among all beings, the buddhas, together with their heirs, the host of bodhisattvas. For those to whom, if offering is made, a great result arises — the holy recipients of offering, the unsurpassed holy field, glorious with the very greatest compassion, who intend and act for the benefit of all beings — may they, looking on us destitute and afflicted beings with loving compassion, draw near and be pleased to accept, however they will, these offerings that I have taken up with my mind. That is: "take them as your own."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-6]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Likewise, even to the very ends of boundless space, whatever belongs to no owner — all of these too, I, the person making the offering, take up with my mind and receive; and then to the holy field: the Buddha-Jewel, the Sage who through his ability keeps the three doors free of misdeed and is thus supreme among two-legged beings, together with his heart-heirs, the bodhisattvas — to all of them I offer "well," for the purpose of giving rise to the awakening mind in my own and others' being, in a manner utterly free of grasping at things and attachment. So: you holy recipients of offering — you who, in return for whatever offering-substance is presented, whether much or little, great or small, fine or poor, bestow a merit-result vast or modest as befits it; you supreme and holy ones, endowed with compassion, the buddhas together with the host of bodhisattva heirs — look upon me, this person, with great love, and accept just as they are offered all these kinds of offering-substances of mine, both owned and unowned. Although the buddhas and bodhisattvas, having perfected the two accumulations and cleared away the two obscurations, have completed both their own and others' aims and so have no need to accept an offering as their own, still the person making the offering, out of great compassion for himself and for beings, prays: "in order to purify the obscurations in my own and others' being and to complete the accumulation of merit, be pleased to accept these offering-substances." That is the meaning.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Offering Well to the Sages Together With Their Heirs

All the commentaries explain in agreement that one takes up with the mind all the offering-substances described in the earlier verses and offers them well to the supreme among two-legged beings — the buddhas together with their heirs.

###### 2. The Holy Recipients, Great in Compassion, Are Asked to Look on Me With Love and Accept

On the definitive meaning of the text, all the commentaries share one basis: that the holy recipients, endowed with great compassion, are asked to look on beings with love and to accept the offering-substances.

###### 3. The Application in Which the Three Spheres Are Completely Pure

Sabzang Mati sets out that offering is made "well," with the three spheres completely pure, and Kunzang Pelden too states the special feature of the application: taking them up with the mind, generating the notion that they are as if one's own, and offering without any grudging attitude.

###### 4. Even a Very Small Offering Becomes a Great Result Through the Special Quality of the Recipient

Kunzang Pelden makes this clear with the examples of the seven peas offered to the Buddha and the child who became the Dharma-king Ashoka by scattering a handful of sand.

###### 5. The Purpose of the Buddhas' Accepting the Offering — a Means of Purifying Obscurations and Completing the Accumulation

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk teaches that, although the buddhas and bodhisattvas have perfected the two accumulations and cleared the two obscurations and so have no need to accept an offering as their own, they accept it for the sake of purifying the obscurations and completing the accumulation of merit of the person making the offering.

###### 6. The Sage (Thub Pa) — Able in the Face of the Misdeeds of the Three Doors

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains the meaning of the word "Sage" (thub pa) as being "able" (thub) in the face of the misdeeds of the three doors.

###### 7. Why an Offering Taken Up With the Mind Must Be Practiced — Because One Is Not Confined to What One Owns

Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso explains clearly the reason one must take up and offer all things, owned and unowned alike, with the mind: if one had to offer only substances of one's own, a person of poor means and low fortune would be as if empty-handed of offering-substances.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that one takes up with the mind all the offering-substances described in the earlier verses and offers them well to the supreme among two-legged beings — the buddhas together with their heirs — and prays that the holy recipients, great in compassion, look on beings with love and be pleased to accept the offering-substances. All the commentaries explain this in agreement.

**Key points.**

- The application matters greatly: taking up all the offering-substances in mind and offering them, without any grudging attitude, to the buddhas together with their heirs.
- For the manner of offering, the application in which the three spheres are completely pure is required, as Sabzang Mati sets out clearly.
- On the definitive meaning of the text: the holy recipients, great in compassion, are asked to look on beings with love and be pleased to accept the offering-substances.
- Kunzang Pelden gives the example that even a very small offering becomes a great result through the special quality of the recipient.
- Although the buddhas and bodhisattvas, having perfected the two accumulations, have no need of their own to accept an offering, Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk sets out in particular that they accept it for the sake of the offerer's purification of obscurations and completion of the accumulation.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

By the force of not having gathered merit in the past, I am destitute, and I have no other offering-wealth of my own that is fine or vast. Therefore, may the protectors — who, from the first generation of the awakening mind onward, are given over wholly to the welfare of others — together with their heirs, accept these offerings I have taken up with my mind and presented. Or again: whatever offering-substances I do have of my own, poor and few as they are, I shall present them too to the holy recipients, gladly and with joy. Though I have nothing to offer but poor and scanty substances, still, through the power of your own compassion, be pleased to accept these as a fine offering, taking them as your own.

##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Therefore, may the protectors, whose intent is wholly upon the welfare of others, through their own power accept these ownerless offerings of mine, for my sake.

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

I, not possessing the merit of former giving and the rest, have become in this life impoverished and greatly destitute in enjoyments; and of the offering-wealth worthy of the noble ones described before, I have nothing else in actual fact. Therefore, may the protectors — the victors together with their heirs, who ever intend the benefit and happiness of others alone — [accept] these offerings presented by my mind. Though you have no want, still, for the sake of completing the two accumulations in our being, through the might of your miraculous power, be pleased to accept well these [offerings] that abide in each of the buddha-fields.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

The reason these must be offered: I, not possessing the merit of former giving and the rest, am in this life destitute in the offering of enjoyments — become like a beggar among beggars grasping a staff — so that of offering-wealth worthy [of the noble ones] described before, I have nothing else in actual fact. Therefore — with no self-interest, from the first generation of the awakening mind onward, thinking "how could the accumulation of all beings be completed and their obscurations purified?" — may the protectors, the victors together with their heirs, who intend the welfare of others alone, [accept] these ownerless offerings of mine, free of all grasping at things as real. Though you have no want, still, for the sake of completing our two accumulations and purifying our two obscurations, through the might of your miraculous power be pleased to accept these well, from within each of the buddha-fields. Thus, if one has such power to offer, the victors together with their heirs, possessed of inconceivable knowledge, love, skillful means, and miraculous power, will accept them and will thereby complete our accumulation and purify our obscurations.

As for the way of making a mind-made offering in this manner: he says that this very offering, taken up in brief by the mind, is a skillful means of the bodhisattvas.

I myself, an ordinary being, by reason of not having gathered the merit of giving, of making offerings, and the like in former lives, am even now utterly destitute — poor and in want; and therefore, of any offering-substance to present other than the offering-wealth already offered above, I have nothing at all. #### Scriptural Quotations

> Son of good family, whatever forms of flowers or forms of fruit a bodhisattva has that are not made "mine" and are not held as a possession — these he offers, three times by day and three times by night, to the buddhas and bodhisattvas; and that root of virtue he fully dedicates to unsurpassed, perfect, complete enlightenment. Likewise, whatever incense-trees, scent-trees, jewel-trees, wish-fulfilling trees, flower-trees, or fruit-trees there are that are not made "mine" and not held as a possession — these too he offers, three times by day and three times by night, to the buddhas and bodhisattvas.
> — from the *Ratnamegha* (*Cloud of Jewels*). (Cited, as at the place in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya* on multiplying roots of virtue, to show the way of rightly offering an offering taken up by the mind.)

> Further, a great bodhisattva skilled in means, whatever flower-trees and scent-trees, flowers and flower-garlands, incense, scented powders, and ointments there are in the ten directions that are not made his own and not held as a possession — all of these he takes up in thought and gathers up, and offers to all the buddhas.
> — from the *Ratnamegha* (*Cloud of Jewels*). (Cited as witness that this very offering, taken up by the mind, is a skillful means of the bodhisattvas.)

> In this, even a bitter taste appears as the finest flavor.
> — from the *Abhisamayālaṃkāra* (*Ornament of Clear Realization*). (Cited as an example that even a poor and scanty offering-substance, when accepted through the compassion of the recipient, becomes a fine offering.)

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. My Own Condition — a Destitute One, Without the Wealth of Merit

All the commentaries establish that, by reason of not having gathered the merit of giving and offering from former lives, one is even now utterly destitute, without any offering-substance.

###### 2. A Skillful Means for the Twofold Aim — the Offering Taken Up by the Mind

Even though one has no offering-substances of one's own, taking up with the mind the things described in the earlier verses — which the field of offering has not made its own — and offering them, is itself a skillful means of the bodhisattvas, as Minyak Kunzang Sonam makes clear by citing the *Ratnamegha*.

###### 3. The Purpose of the Protectors' Accepting the Offering — the Completion of the Offerer's Accumulation

Although the victors together with their heirs have no want of provisions for themselves, nor any grasping at ownership, they accept the offering — through the might of their compassion — for the sake of completing the accumulation of merit of the offerer and purifying the obscuration of stinginess and the rest.

###### 4. The Way the Protectors, Through the Might of Compassion and Miraculous Power, Accept the Offering-Substances Present in Each of the Fields

Because the victors together with their heirs possess inconceivable knowledge, love, skillful means, and miraculous power, they are able to accept well the offering-substances sent by the mind that abide in each of the buddha-fields.

###### 5. Scriptural Source That Even a Poor Offering Becomes Fine Through the Recipient's Compassion

Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso illustrates with the citation from the *Abhisamayālaṃkāra*, "in this, even a bitter taste appears as the finest flavor": that although the substance one has to offer is poor and scanty, when it is accepted through the compassion of the holy recipient it becomes a fine offering.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse describes how, by reason of not having gathered merit in the past, one is utterly destitute, without any offering-substance; and it prays that the victors together with their heirs, whose intent is the welfare of others alone, accept — through the might of their compassion — the offering-substances presented in mind in the earlier verses, for the sake of completing the accumulation of the offerer.

**Key points.**

- Because one has not gathered the merit of giving and offering from former lives, one has become even now utterly destitute, without any offering-substance.
- Even having no substances, one takes up with the mind the offering-substances described in the earlier verses — which the field of offering has not made its own — and prays the protectors to accept them.
- Though the protectors have no want of provisions for themselves, they accept for the sake of completing the offerer's two accumulations and purifying the two obscurations.
- That this very offering, taken up by the mind, is a skillful means of the bodhisattvas is settled by Minyak Kunzang Sonam with two citations from the *Ratnamegha*.
- Because the victors together with their heirs possess inconceivable knowledge, love, skillful means, and miraculous power, they are able to accept well the offering-substances sent by the mind that abide in each of the fields.
