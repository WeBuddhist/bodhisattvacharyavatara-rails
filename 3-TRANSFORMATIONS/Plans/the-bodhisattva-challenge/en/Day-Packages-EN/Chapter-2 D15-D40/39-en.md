#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Suffering, too, comes about in this way: because the mind is untamed, bad actions are piled up, and those bad actions in turn produce suffering. Since we do not want suffering, we have to do away with non-virtue, the cause of suffering, and win our freedom from it whatever it takes. Thinking in that way, day and night, all the time, it is right for me to think of this one thing and nothing else.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-62]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

For the sake of those things, out of attachment and aversion and the rest, one commits the cause — wrongdoing, the ten non-virtues and so on — and from that the fruit follows: the suffering of the hells and the other lower realms comes upon one with no freedom at all, and one is tormented terribly. So one asks: what am I to do so that my own mind is freed for certain from that cause, from wrongdoing and non-virtue? Day and night, not sleeping, at all times, "I ... this alone." "This alone" is a word of exclusion: it means that it is right and fitting to think of this one thing only, and to put one's work into confessing wrongdoing. Moreover, just as with cloth to be dyed the washing matters more than the dyeing itself, so the reason the qualities of hearing, reflecting and meditating do not arise in one's own mind is the force of wrongdoing and non-virtue; and therefore, he says, this diligence in purifying wrongdoing is extremely precious.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-62]]

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^2-62]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Since the suffering of the hells and the rest arises from non-virtue such as killing, one asks: how am I to be freed for certain from that suffering? Day and night, at all times, it is right for me to think of this one thing alone — actions, virtuous and non-virtuous, and their effects. If one does not find a settled certainty about actions and their effects, right down to the root, then one will not find, with respect to any Dharma at all, the certainty that pleases the victorious ones; so everyone should make an effort in this. As for some who say they have found certainty about emptiness and on that basis pay no regard to actions and their effects: it is plain that their understanding has gone completely astray, and that they have not found certainty that emptiness abides in the sense of dependent arising.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^2-62]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Since suffering arises from non-virtue, one asks how one is to be freed for certain from that non-virtue; and day and night, at all times, it is right for me to think of this one thing alone.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^2-62]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Since committing wrongdoing — the cause, the ten non-virtues and the rest — brings as its fruit the fierce suffering of the hells and the other lower realms, one asks: what am I to do so as to be freed for certain from that suffering together with its cause? And the answer is that it is right to make an effort in virtue, the remedy for wrongdoing. So, day and night, at all times, what it is right for me to think of first, and to think of alone, is just this: the faith that trusts wholeheartedly in actions, their causes and their effects.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-62]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Since no one and nothing can help, it is like this: from doing wrongdoing and non-virtue — the cause, killing and the rest — the fruit certainly arises, the suffering of the hells and the other lower realms. And so one asks: by what means am I to be freed for certain from such a fruit, from suffering, and from that cause, non-virtuous action? Day and night, at all times, it is right for me to think in every way of this one thing alone — giving rise to the faith that trusts in the way an unwanted fruit comes from the cause, non-virtuous action; and it is not right to think of anything else, of this life, of relatives, of enjoyments and the rest. That is to say, the verse shows that at all times one should think only of the means of freedom from wrongdoing, the cause, and from suffering, its fruit, and so live carefully and with restraint.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-62]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

In short: from non-virtuous action prompted by the attachment and aversion of killing, of taking what is not given, and of sexual misconduct, it is simply the nature of things that the fruit is suffering and unease in body and mind, and nothing else — both for the time being and in the long run. So one asks: how am I to be freed for certain from such actions and afflictions? That is, from actions and afflictions, and, in short, from their fruit, suffering. Day and night, at all times, it is right for me to turn my mind to that means of becoming free, and — with nothing else to recall and nothing else to think about — to think of this one thing alone.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-62]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

1. **Since suffering arises from non-virtue, one must think day and night about the means of being freed from it for certain.** — All the commentaries agree in the end that non-virtuous action certainly brings the fruit of suffering, and that one should therefore think at all times about the means of being freed from it.
2. **"This alone" means that one should think only of putting one's work into confessing wrongdoing.** — Kunzang Pelden uses the example of cloth that has to be washed before it is dyed to explain that purifying wrongdoing is the preliminary without which the qualities of hearing, reflecting and meditating do not arise.
3. **"This alone" is explained as nothing other than giving rise to firm, trusting faith in actions, their causes and their effects.** — Sabzang Mati Panchen, Minyak Kunzang Sonam and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk make giving rise to trusting faith in actions and their effects the main point. ⚑
4. **To pay no regard to actions and their effects in the name of having found certainty about emptiness is a complete mistake.** — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen explains clearly that if one has not realized that emptiness abides in the sense of dependent arising, one has not found certainty about actions and their effects either.
5. **Non-virtuous action such as killing, taking what is not given and sexual misconduct produces suffering in both body and mind, for the time being and in the long run.** — Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk gives those three, killing and the rest, as actual illustrations of non-virtuous action.
6. **If one has firm trust in actions and their effects, one will not fall into the lower realms even in a thousand aeons.** — Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both explain this clearly, quoting the same passage — "the great correct view of a worldly person" — but under two different attributions.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

1. **The root of all wrongdoing is delusion and ignorance.** — All the commentaries agree in the end that every wrong I have done came about through the force of delusion and ignorance — of being bewildered about, and not knowing, what to take up and what to leave aside.
2. **The difference between what is by nature blameworthy and proscribed wrongdoing.** — What is by nature blameworthy is that which becomes wrongdoing whoever does it, like the ten non-virtues, killing and the rest; proscribed wrongdoing is explained as what becomes a fault when someone who has taken commitments goes against the rules pledged, like eating food in the afternoon or drinking alcohol.
3. **Proscribed wrongdoing applies only to a person who holds the commitments.** — Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that an ordinary householder incurs no fault over and above the act, but that when a person holding commitments — a lay vow-holder, a novice, a fully ordained monk and so on — digs the earth, cuts grass or trees, destroys seed or eats food in the afternoon, that becomes a fault: proscribed wrongdoing.
4. **The faults to be confessed must be identified as such, whether they were done by body, speech or mind.** — Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that one identifies the faults to be confessed with the words "whatever wrongdoing I have done through body, speech or mind, as the case may be," and then confesses them in the presence of the protectors.
5. **This verse is explained together with verse 64 as part of a single act of confession.** — Ngulchu Thogme, Sabzang Mati Panchen, Minyak Kunzang Sonam, Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and His Holiness the Dalai Lama all take this verse and verse 64 together and explain them as one single way of confessing.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows how one prepares to confess by first identifying all the wrongdoing one has done under the force of delusion and ignorance — bewildered about what to take up and what to leave aside — whether it was by nature blameworthy or proscribed wrongdoing that goes against a commitment; and many of the commentaries explain this verse together with the following one, verse 64, as part of a single act of confession.

**Key points.**

- All the commentaries agree that the root of every wrong is the delusion and ignorance of being bewildered about what to take up and what to leave aside.
- Wrongdoing is divided into two: what is by nature blameworthy (wrongdoing whoever does it) and proscribed wrongdoing (a fault incurred by going against a commitment).
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk makes it clear that when a householder does it there is no fault over and above the act, but that when a person holding commitments digs the earth or cuts grass and trees, that becomes proscribed wrongdoing.
- Every wrong done through body, speech or mind, as the case may be, is to be identified as something to be confessed, and then confessed in the presence of the protectors.
- Many commentators take this verse and verse 64 together as parts of a single stanza of confession.
