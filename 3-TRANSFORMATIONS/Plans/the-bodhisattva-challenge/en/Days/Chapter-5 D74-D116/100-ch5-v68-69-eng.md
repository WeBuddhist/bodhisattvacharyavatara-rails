## Today's Verse

> If one does not give clothes and so forth
> to a servant who cannot be employed,
> if this body goes to others even after being fed,
> why do you lavishly feed it? ^5-68

> Having given wages to this body,
> now let it do its own work.
> One should not give everything
> to this body without benefit. ^5-69

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the sixty-eighth and sixty-ninth verses of the fifth chapter. Does anyone pay wages to a hired worker who never does any work? Or is our own body also like a servant?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following explanation, which is easy to understand and encouraging. The meaning of "let it do its own work" is this. For a bodhisattva, accomplishing the benefit of beings is itself one's own benefit. Apart from that, there is no other benefit of one's own at all. The _Ornament of the Sutras_ says, "Others' benefit is one's own benefit." So this body must be put to work doing good for the benefit of others. For example, we pay a servant only the wages for the work done. In the same way, it is fine to give the body only what it needs, such as food and clothes. It is not right to give it everything it wants.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will give my body only enough food and clothes. Then I will work for the benefit of others.

**Explanation:** _(Doing good)_ This is the practice of giving the body enough food and clothes, and then working for the benefit of others. If we keep the body's wishes in check, the mind becomes peaceful. If we help others, both we and others find happiness.
