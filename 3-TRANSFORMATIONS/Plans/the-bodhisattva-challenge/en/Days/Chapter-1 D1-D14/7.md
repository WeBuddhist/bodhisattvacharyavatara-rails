# Day 7 — Merit that keeps growing even while you sleep

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Is your commitment still alive, or just a habit?
> **Detail:** Today's reading: once the resolve to free all beings is genuine, merit keeps growing even while you sleep. Practice: check in with the intention you set, and renew it if it has gone quiet.

## Introduction

Verses 18 and 19 are one sentence split across two verses. Once the engaging mind is properly adopted — through a genuine, irreversible commitment to freeing all beings — merit arises continuously, even while the practitioner is asleep or distracted. Verse 20 explains where this teaching comes from and who it was originally given for.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

*MIND TRAINING (recite 3 times)*

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in immeasurable equanimity, free from attachment and aversion.

*REFUGE (recite 3 times)*

> Until I reach the heart of awakening, I take refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.

*TAKING THE BODHISATTVA VOW (recite 3 times)*

> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And trained step by step in the bodhisattva conduct,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And train step by step in that same conduct.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Je explains who verse 1.20 was originally aimed at. Some practitioners, he says, were genuinely terrified of the Bodhisattva path — not from laziness but from fear. The collections of merit and wisdom required for Buddhahood seemed so vast that they turned away from the Great Vehicle and settled for the peace of individual liberation. The Buddha's response to these cautious practitioners was verse 1.19: you've misunderstood what the path requires. Once the resolve is genuinely made, the merit arises on its own — while you sleep, while you're distracted, even when you're not trying. The difficulty is not in the accumulation. It's in making the commitment.

## Aspiration

> May the precious and supreme bodhicitta —
> Where it has not yet arisen, may it arise;
> Where it has arisen, may it not decline,
> But grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of this practice,
> May all wandering beings, without exception,
> Enter the path of the bodhisattva.

## Today's Practice Challenge

Ngulchu Thokme adds a caveat: this continuous merit only flows if the mind of enlightenment doesn't degenerate. Commitment can erode quietly without you noticing. Today, check in with the resolve you made at the start of this practice. Is it still alive as an intention, or has it become more of a habit? Renewing a commitment is not a sign of failure — it's the practice itself.
