# Day 12 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: Benefits of Bodhicitta (བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་ཕན་ཡོན།) — Verses 1-34 to 1-36

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Protecting the Mind from Harmful Thoughts (Mindfulness of Actions):** Anger toward a person who holds the wish for enlightenment creates very heavy and lasting negative results. Paying attention to harmful thoughts helps reduce stress and prevents poor choices. This awareness brings greater calm and kindness into daily life.

*   **Growing Virtue through Faith and Respect (Building Positive Habits):** Looking at a person with the wish for enlightenment with a faithful and open mind creates strong positive results. Faith in good qualities grows much faster than harmful thoughts can cause damage. This shows how a single kind feeling can lead to real and lasting improvement.

*   **Finding Refuge in Compassionate Examples (Taking Inspiration from Goodness):** A person who holds the wish for enlightenment responds to harm with patience, not anger. Taking refuge in such a person brings a feeling of safety and peace. This teaches that true compassion can connect even difficult people with happiness.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-34**

> གང་ཞིག་དེ་འདྲའི་རྒྱལ་སྲས་སྦྱིན་བདག་ལ། །
> གལ་ཏེ་ངན་སེམས་སྐྱེད་པར་བྱེད་ན་དེ། །
> ངན་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་པའི་གྲངས་བཞིན་བསྐལ་པར་ནི། །
> དམྱལ་བར་གནས་པར་འགྱུར་ཞེས་ཐུབ་པས་གསུངས། །

> *If anyone harbors a negative mind toward such a child of the Buddha, they will dwell in hell for eons. The Sage said they will remain there for as many eons as there were moments of that negative mind.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-34**

This verse gives a serious warning about the results of anger or harmful thoughts. When someone gets angry at a person who holds the wish for enlightenment, the result is very heavy and lasts a very long time. The Buddha himself taught this, saying that each moment of an angry thought leads to great suffering. Even a single moment of hostility toward such a special person is enough to create very serious negative results. This is because a person with the wish for enlightenment is the very cause of all the Buddhas, and harming the cause of all goodness is the most serious action possible.

---

#### **Verse 1-35**

> འོན་ཏེ་གང་ཞིག་ཡིད་རབ་དང་བྱེད་ན། །
> དེ་ཡི་འབྲས་བུ་དེ་བས་ལྷག་པར་འཕེལ། །
> རྒྱལ་སྲས་རྣམས་ལ་དོ་གལ་ཆེན་པོས་ཀྱང་། །
> སྡིག་པ་མི་འབྱུང་དགེ་བ་ངང་གིས་འཕེལ། །

> *However, if one generates a faithful mind toward them, its fruit will increase even further. Even when great harm befalls these children of the Buddha, no negativity arises, and their virtue naturally increases.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-35**

Looking at a person with the wish for enlightenment with a faithful and open mind brings very great positive results. The fruit of this simple faith is far greater than the damage of any harmful thought. The verse also shows something very special about a person who holds the wish for enlightenment. Even when others harm them greatly, they do not become angry or feel hatred. Instead, their kindness and care for others naturally grows stronger under those very difficult conditions.

---

#### **Verse 1-36**

> གང་ལ་སེམས་ཀྱི་དམ་པ་རིན་ཆེན་དེ། །
> སྐྱེས་པ་དེ་ཡི་སྐུ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཞིང་། །
> གང་ལ་གནོད་པ་བྱས་ཀྱང་བདེ་འབྲེལ་བ། །
> བདེ་བའི་འབྱུང་གནས་དེ་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

> *I pay homage to the body of anyone in whom this precious jewel of the mind has arisen. I take refuge in those sources of happiness, who connect even their harmers with joy.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-36**

The great teacher Śāntideva closes the first chapter with deep respect for anyone who holds the wish for enlightenment. Such a person is like a precious jewel, rare and full of good qualities, and deserves great honor and respect. Even someone who has harmed this kind of person will, in the end, be guided to happiness through their compassion and aspirational prayers. This is not a contradiction with the earlier warning about anger. The suffering from anger comes from the harmer's own harmful mind, while the happiness that follows comes from the compassionate heart of the person with the wish for enlightenment.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Watching my thoughts with care:** Today, I will pay close attention to any feelings of anger or irritation that arise in my mind. When I notice a harmful thought, I will pause and remember that even small moments of anger can cause serious results. I will use this awareness to choose a kinder and more patient response.

*   **2. Practicing faith and respect toward good people:** When I meet someone who is living with kindness and sincerity, I will look at them with respect and appreciation. I know that a faithful and open mind creates positive results in my life. I will let this understanding motivate me to be more humble and respectful in all my relationships.

*   **3. Learning from those who respond to harm with compassion:** Today, I will think of someone who stays patient and kind even when treated badly. I will see this person as a model and a source of real inspiration for my own practice. I will try to bring the same kind of patience into my own reactions throughout my day.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **རྒྱལ་སྲས** (Child of the Victorious Ones): This term refers to a person who holds the precious wish for enlightenment. Just as a prince carries the qualities of a royal family, this person carries the qualities of the Buddhas. Harming or honoring such a person brings very strong positive or negative results.

*   **ངན་སེམས** (negative or harmful mind): This is any thought of anger, contempt, or ill-will toward another being. In today's verses, it points especially to such thoughts aimed at a person with the wish for enlightenment. Even a single moment of this harmful mind creates very serious negative karma.

*   **ཡིད་རབ** (faithful or clear mind): This is a positive state of mind that sees the good qualities of another person with clarity and openness. Generating this kind of mind toward a person with the wish for enlightenment brings very great positive results. It is the direct opposite of a harmful or doubtful mind.

*   **སེམས་ཀྱི་དམ་པ་རིན་ཆེན** (precious jewel of the mind): This refers to the wish for enlightenment itself, described as a jewel because it is rare, valuable, and can fulfill all needs. When this mind arises in a person, that person becomes worthy of the highest respect and honor. The great teacher Śāntideva compares this mind to a wish-granting jewel that clears all suffering.

*   **བདེ་བའི་འབྱུང་གནས** (source of happiness): This phrase describes a person with the wish for enlightenment, who through compassion and aspirational prayers brings happiness to all beings. Even those who have harmed such a person are eventually guided to real happiness. This shows the extraordinary power of true compassion.
