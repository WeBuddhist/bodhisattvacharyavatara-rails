# Day 1 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: The Benefits of Bodhicitta — Verses 1-1 to 1-3

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> 
> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Auspicious Commencement (Obstacle Pacification):** By paying homage and taking refuge in the Three Jewels and spiritual preceptors, practitioners accumulate immense merit and pacify obstacles to their spiritual study and practice. This devotion establishes a sacred connection that supports both temporary happiness and ultimate realization.
*   **Eradication of Pride (Humility and Receptivity):** Emulating Śāntideva's profound humility in acknowledging that his words are not original allows practitioners to dismantle intellectual pride and pretension. This psychological shift fosters deep receptivity and sincerity, preparing the mind to be seasoned with genuine virtue.
*   **Continuous Familiarization (Mental Development and Altruistic Resonance):** Writing and reflecting on these teachings habituates the mind with bodhicitta, preventing spiritual decline and steadily increasing faith, wisdom, and compassion. This personal transformation naturally extends outward, serving as an inspiring gateway that benefits other fortunate practitioners of equal disposition.

---

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-1**
> བདེ་གཤེགས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་མངའ་སྲས་བཅས་དང་། །
> ཕྱག་འོས་ཀུན་ལའང་གུས་པར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏེ། །
> བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་ནི། །
> ལུང་བཞིན་མདོར་བསྡུས་ནས་ནི་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ། །
> 
> *Paying homage with reverence to the Sugatas — the Dharmakāya and their heirs — and to all who are worthy of veneration, I shall now briefly describe, in accordance with scripture, the practice of entering the discipline of the Sugatas' heirs.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-1**
Paying homage and taking refuge in the Three Jewels—the Buddhas (Sugatas), the Dharma (Dharmakāya), and the Sangha (Sons of the Sugatas)—and spiritual teachers pacifies obstacles to composition and study, accumulating the merit necessary for temporary and ultimate happiness. Through a shared scholastic lineage relying on Maitreya's *Uttaratantra*, the commentators define the Buddha Jewel as the *Sugata*, characterized by perfect abandonment of afflictions (becoming graceful, irreversible, and complete) and perfect realization of selflessness, while identifying the Sangha as the noble Bodhisattvas who inherit the Sugata's lineage.

#### **Verse 1-2**
> སྔོན་ཆད་མ་བྱུང་བ་ཡང་འདིར་བརྗོད་མེད། །
> སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་མཁས་པའང་བདག་ལ་ཡོད་མིན་ཏེ། །
> དེ་ཕྱིར་གཞན་དོན་བསམ་པ་བདག་ལ་མེད། །
> རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་ངས་འདི་བརྩམས། །
> 
> *What is written here has not been said before, nor do I possess any skill in composition. Therefore I have no thought of benefiting others — I composed this solely to cultivate it in my own mind.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-2**
By rejecting claims to originality and literary skill, Śāntideva demonstrates profound humility, completely dismantling pride and pre-emptively seeking the readers' forgiveness for any structural or linguistic shortcomings. The primary purpose of composing the text is self-benefit: to habituate and familiarize (*goms pa*) his own mind with the virtues of bodhicitta and the six perfections, preventing spiritual decline while actively growing key qualities like diligence, mindfulness, concentration, and the three types of faith.

#### **Verse 1-3**
> དགེ་བ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་བདག་གི་དད་པའི་ཤུགས། །
> འདི་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་རེ་ཞིག་འཕེལ་འགྱུར་ལ། །
> བདག་དང་སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཀྱང་། །
> ཅི་སྟེ་འདི་དག་མཐོང་ན་དོན་ཡོད་འགྱུར། །
> 
> *Through these writings, my faith in virtue may grow stronger for a while, and should another of equal fortune encounter this, perhaps it may also be of benefit to them.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-3**
While self-familiarization is the immediate, temporary focus (*re zhig*), a secondary, altruistic purpose is established: to benefit other Mahayana practitioners who share the same spiritual fortune, capacity, and aspiration. The humble use of the word "perhaps" (*ci ste*) highlights the author's complete freedom from pride and expectation, demonstrating how a dedicated personal practice naturally becomes a "branch of entry" or gateway that inspires others to engage with the teachings.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Cultivating Daily Devotion and Homage:** Begin your day by taking refuge and paying mental or physical homage to the Three Jewels and your spiritual teachers, recognizing them as sources of perfect realization and abandonment. Before starting any major task, pause to generate a clear, respectful motivation, which helps clear mental obstacles and aligns your actions with a higher purpose.
*   **2. Practicing Humility in Communication:** When sharing knowledge, insights, or advice with others, consciously check your motivation to ensure it is free from pride and the desire to impress. Frame your contributions with humility, acknowledging that your wisdom is built upon the guidance of others, and focus strictly on the self-education and mutual benefit of the conversation.
*   **3. Sharing with Spiritual Peers:** Recognize that your spiritual study is not a solitary endeavor but a shared journey. When you learn something meaningful, share it without pretension with friends or peers of equal fortune, hoping that it might "by some chance" support their path, thereby strengthening your own faith and diligence.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**
> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །
> 
> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །
> 
> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **བདེ་གཤེགས། (*bde gshegs* / *Sugata*):** Literally "Gone to Bliss." In Verse 1-1, this refers to the Buddha Jewel, characterized by perfect abandonment of all mental afflictions and perfect realization of the two types of selflessness. The commentaries break this down into "leaving beautifully," "leaving so as not to return," and "leaving without exception."
*   **སྡོམ་པ། (*sdom pa* / *saṃvara*):** Translated as "vow" or "discipline." In the context of Verse 1-1, it refers to the Bodhisattva vows and ethical discipline (*sugatātmajasamvara*), which encompass the entire Mahayana path, lineage, and fruits. Entering this discipline is the central theme of the entire treatise.
*   **དགེ་བ། (*dge ba* / *kuśala*):** Translated as "virtue." In Verse 1-2 and 1-3, it refers to the wholesome mental states and actions associated with the practice of bodhicitta and the six perfections. The commentaries emphasize that familiarizing the mind with virtue prevents spiritual decline and acts as a cause for both higher rebirth and definite goodness.
*   **རེ་ཞིག (*re zhig* / *temporarily* / *for a while*):** In Verse 1-3, this term indicates that the immediate, primary focus of Śāntideva's writing is to strengthen his own faith and familiarize his own mind with virtue. Classical commentaries explain that this self-benefit is the essential prerequisite before one can subsequently and successfully bring lasting benefit to others.
*   **ཅི་སྟེ། (*ci ste* / *if* / *perhaps*):** In Verse 1-3, this term represents "by some chance" or "perhaps." It highlights Śāntideva's complete freedom from pride and expectation, showing that while he hopes his work may benefit others of equal fortune, he does not presume to be a teacher instructing them.
