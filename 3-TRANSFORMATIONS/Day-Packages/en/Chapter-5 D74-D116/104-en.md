# Day 104 — Joy That Cannot Be Bought: Rejoicing in Others' Good Qualities, Not Envying Them

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the seventy-seventh and seventy-eighth verses of the fifth chapter. The flower of joy that gold cannot buy blooms in our own mind.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. If we go to the big cities of India with money, we can buy whatever things we need. But if we go to buy joy, there is nothing to sell. Each of us must accomplish it by directing our own thoughts. In this way, we must meditate on joy in the good qualities that others have created, and enjoy the happiness of joy.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, I will not be jealous of the good qualities of others. I will rejoice in them.

**Explanation:** *(Taming the mind)* If we meditate on joy in the good qualities of others, we suffer no harm, and we find happiness in this life and the next. If we are jealous, our mind is unhappy, and we will also feel suffering in the next life.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

In short: if whatever one undertakes through the three doors is such that it can bring joy to others, one's own mind will be satisfied and one's thoughts will be at ease and happy. Such peaceful joy and well-being of the mind, for oneself and others both, cannot be bought with money in any shop one goes to. Here Khunu Lama Rinpoche says the following. When we take money to the big cities of India to buy goods, we can buy whatever we need; but if we go to buy joy, there is nothing to sell. This each of us must accomplish by directing our own thoughts, he said.

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

All the exertions of a bodhisattva's three doors are for the sake of others' joy; and others' joy is an undertaking that is rare even if one buys it at a price. Thus, through the qualities by which one has brought joy to others, one should in this life enjoy the happiness of joy without jealousy on the part of others; and because, through bringing joy to others, no loss of enjoyments and so on ever comes to me in this life — none at all — and in the next life too I will obtain great happiness.

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

In general, all undertakings, by their nature, are for obtaining a joy free from suffering; such joy is extremely rare even if one buys it at a very great price; therefore, by being without jealousy toward the qualities that others have achieved through effort, one should oneself enjoy the happiness of joy, and

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

In general, whatever actions of the three doors oneself and others undertake are all for the sake of abandoning each one's own suffering and generating joy and happiness. In particular, whatever a bodhisattva undertakes is solely for the joy of others — that is, solely as a means of generating joy. Such joy of oneself and others is extremely rare even if one buys it at the price of great wealth given away; and the word "even" means: what need is there to speak of when there is no price? Therefore, when the excellent qualities that others each have achieved through their own effort, whatever they may be in their own mindstreams, come to be seen or heard by oneself, one should not feel the jealousy that is displeased and cannot bear it; instead one should voice praise of this and that, generate joy in one's own mind through those qualities, and oneself enjoy such happiness of joy. This is fitting, because it need not be bought at a price but abides effortlessly. Moreover, by rejoicing in the qualities of others in this way, joy and contentment also arise in the minds of those others, and they too become satisfied.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-77]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

All the activities that oneself and others undertake are for the sake of each one's own mind being joyful. Therefore, meditating on joy in the qualities of others, without jealousy on one's own part, is something one could not find to buy even if one tried to buy it at a great price, and it is thus extremely rare. For that reason, through the qualities of other persons — hearing, reflecting, meditating, teaching and listening to the Dharma, and so on — one should enjoy whatever happiness of joy comes from rejoicing and meditating on joy in one's own mind without jealousy; so he taught.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-77]]

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** All undertakings of oneself and others, whatever they begin, are for the sake of attaining a joyful mind; and such joy is extremely rare to find even if one buys it at the price of great wealth. Therefore, by rejoicing without jealousy in the qualities that others have achieved through effort, one should enjoy that very happiness of joy that is found without effort.

**Key points.**

- **The aim of undertaking activity.** Whatever activities of the three doors oneself and others undertake are all for the sake of each one's own mind attaining joy.
- **Joy cannot be bought.** That joy is rare even if bought at a price. For example, if one takes money to a big city one can buy goods, but there is no joy to be bought.
- **Rejoicing without jealousy in the qualities of others.** Without jealousy toward the qualities that others have achieved — hearing, reflecting, meditating, teaching and listening to the Dharma, and so on — one should enjoy the happiness of joy through rejoicing.
- **Enjoying effortless joy.** Rejoicing in the qualities that others have created is a happiness of joy found without effort, with no need to buy it at a price.
- **Benefit.** By bringing joy to others, no loss of enjoyments and so on comes in this life, and great happiness is obtained in the next; joy and contentment also arise in the minds of those others, and they too become satisfied.

### Verse 5-78

#### Root Verse

*Plain English translation (source: `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-translate/BCA-Full-Plain-English.md#^5-78`):*

> In this I lose nothing, and happiness in the future is great; fault-finding brings displeasure now and great suffering later.

---

<!-- sub:interlinear -->
#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

In my joy, no loss at all comes in this life, / and in the next world too there is great happiness. / If, through one's own fault, one is displeased by the good qualities of others, one is unhappy and suffers in this life, / and in the next world too there is great suffering.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^5-78]]

---

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

One must enjoy the happiness of joy that satisfies one's own mind, through such good conduct and qualities of bringing joy to other beings; and as its result, even in this life there will be no misfortune and no loss or breakdown, and in the future life too something that gives happiness in every way will certainly come.

Conversely: instead of being personally pleased and joyful when others are joyful, one may wonder what happens if, when others are happy, one is unhappy and jealousy arises. As for the term "fault": although "fault" is a general term, here, contrary to what was explained above, if jealousy that is displeased by the excellence of others arises, then through the fault of that, in this life, for as long as jealousy arises one is personally unhappy; the mind becomes gloomy and oppressed, and because of unhappiness illness and fever arise; and in later lives too, through its power, great suffering will be drawn in.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-78]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

If one meditates on joy and enjoys it, then in this life not the slightest loss ever comes to that enjoyment; and as the result of the good intention free of jealousy, in the next life too one will obtain great happiness — great or very great — such as higher rebirth. When one sees the qualities, wealth, enjoyments, and so on of others, through the fault of hatred, jealousy, rivalry, and so on, in this life one's own qualities become exhausted, and since the weapon of ill will circles back upon oneself, one experiences displeasure and various sufferings; and in the next life too, as the result of ill will, one will experience great suffering — great or very great — in the lower realms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-78]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Thus, through the qualities by which one has brought joy to others, one should in this life enjoy the happiness of joy without jealousy on the part of others; and because, through bringing joy to others, no loss of enjoyments and so on ever comes to me in this life — none at all — and in the next life too I will obtain great happiness.

Otherwise, through the fault of anger at the good qualities of others, in this life one is displeased in mind and the body suffers, and in the next life too great suffering will arise.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-78]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

This is because, through that, no loss of enjoyments and so on ever comes to me in this life, and great happiness is produced in the next world as well. Through the fault of hating the good qualities of others, one is displeased and suffers here, and in the next world too great suffering will arise.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-120]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

By speaking of others' qualities and taking joy in them, not even the slightest loss at all comes to me in this life, and in the next world too I obtain, as its result, great happiness; and through the fault of hating, unable to bear, the good qualities of others, one is displeased and suffers even here, and in the next world too great suffering will arise.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-78]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

In that way, by praising without jealousy the good qualities of others and taking joy in them, no loss at all comes to me in this life — no loss, destruction, or decline of the qualities, enjoyments, and so on of my own mindstream; "never any" (yong ye) means none whatsoever, none in any respect. And not only that: in the next world too I will obtain, as the result of having rejoiced in the excellence of others, joy and happiness in abundance. The word "also" (ཡང) shows that not only in the later life but even in this one, merit increases and one experiences joy and happiness.

Conversely, through the fault of the ingrained intolerance of others' good qualities and of acting out of jealousy, in this life too an unhappy feeling arises in one's mind and a feeling of great suffering arises in the body; and not only that, in the next world too the result of it — the undesired and vast suffering — will arise. As it is said, "There is no harm to others like jealousy," and also, "The faults of jealousy are immeasurable," as was taught.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-78]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

For myself, as I meditate on joy in the good qualities of others, in enjoying that very happiness of joy in this life, nothing like a loss or exhaustion will come; and in the world of the later life beyond too, there is great happiness, the happiness of obtaining great happiness in body and mind. But if, through the fault of ill will and jealousy, one looks upon the excellent qualities of others with displeasure, then in this very life one will experience the suffering of mental unease, and in the later life beyond too, as the fruit of its ripening, extremely great suffering of body and mind will arise in the lower realms and so on; so he taught.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-78]]

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** By meditating on joy, without jealousy, in the good qualities and excellence of others, no loss or decline of one's own qualities and enjoyments comes at all in this life, and great happiness is obtained in the next; and conversely, if through the fault of jealousy one is displeased by the good qualities of others, one has great suffering in this life and the next. All the commentators teach this in agreement.

**Key points.**

- If one meditates on joy without jealousy toward the qualities and wealth of others, no loss, destruction, or decline of one's own enjoyments and qualities comes in this life — none at all.
- Through the result of the good intention free of jealousy, great happiness such as higher rebirth is obtained in the next life.
- Through the fault of jealousy, in this life the mind is displeased and the body suffers; one experiences the suffering of illness and fever.
- Through the ripening of jealousy and ill will, in the next life one will experience great suffering in the lower realms and so on.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that although "fault" is a general term, here it applies to the jealousy that is displeased by the excellence of others.
