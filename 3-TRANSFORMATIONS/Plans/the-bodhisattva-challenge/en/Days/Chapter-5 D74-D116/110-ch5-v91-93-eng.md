## Today's Verse

> When discarding tooth-sticks and saliva,
> one should cover them.
> It is improper to discard urine and other waste
> in water or on the ground. ^5-91

> One should not eat with a full mouth,
> with noise, or with an open mouth.
> One should not sit with outstretched legs,
> nor rub one's hands together. ^5-92

> In conveyances, beds, and dwelling places,
> one should not be alone with other women.
> All those who lack faith in the world
> should be avoided by seeing and inquiring. ^5-93

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the ninety-first, ninety-second, and ninety-third verses of the fifth chapter. We think together about how our small daily habits, such as how we eat and how we sit, show respect to others, or not.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable quotation, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Jowo Atisha taught the following about conduct that causes a lack of faith in others. "If it does not agree with the minds of people of the world, saying, 'This is not my Dharma,' first put a good intention forward, and ask about the customs of the world's places." That is, if some conduct of ours does not agree with the minds of people of the world and causes a lack of faith in them, we must first ask well about the customs of the place, with a good intention. Then we must give up that conduct.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when I eat, I will not stuff my mouth, and I will eat without making noise.

**Explanation:** _(Ethics Practice)_ If we are careful about small things in our way of eating and our conduct, others do not feel uncomfortable. This protects the faith and respect in the minds of others. This is a very important ethics practice.
