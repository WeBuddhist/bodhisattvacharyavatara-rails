# Day 11 — Repaying favors earns praise. Bodhisattvas never owed one.

## Opening

These verses close out the extended praise of bodhicitta that has run through Chapter One's middle section. The first two verses complete the argument — bodhicitta does three things for suffering beings that no other virtue can do — and the third turns to a new angle: a comparison with the ordinary human sense of gratitude. Today's reading brings this arc of praise to a sharp, vivid point.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

*MIND TRAINING (recite 3 times)*

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in immeasurable equanimity, free from attachment and aversion.

*REFUGE (recite 3 times)*

> Until I reach the heart of awakening, I take refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.

*TAKING THE BODHISATTVA VOW (recite 3 times)*

> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And trained step by step in the bodhisattva conduct,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And train step by step in that same conduct.

## From the Tradition

Verse 31 praises the bodhisattva by comparison with ordinary gratitude — but Sazang Mati Panchen draws out something the verse leaves implicit. In everyday life, we admire people who return a kindness they received. What makes this admirable is that they could have ignored the debt and didn't. But the bodhisattva has no debt to discharge. No one asked them. No one did them a prior favor. And yet, Sazang Mati Panchen specifies, they act "without expecting any return or maturation of action" — meaning not even the subtle expectation that their goodness will generate good karma for themselves. The purity of their motivation is total: no obligation owed, no request received, no personal benefit sought, not even a quietly hoped-for spiritual payoff. Most human generosity, even when genuine, carries some trace of wanting to feel good about itself. The commentators are pointing to a different category of action entirely.

## Aspiration

> May the precious and supreme bodhicitta —
> Where it has not yet arisen, may it arise;
> Where it has arisen, may it not decline,
> But grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of this practice,
> May all wandering beings, without exception,
> Enter the path of the bodhisattva.

## Today's Practice Challenge

The next time you do something kind for someone — hold a door, send a message checking on a friend, help a colleague with something they didn't ask about — pause for a moment afterward and look honestly at what you were hoping for. Not the big things, but the small ones: a thank-you, a feeling of being generous, a sense that you're the kind of person who helps. According to Sazang Mati Panchen, that trace of expectation is exactly what bodhicitta does not carry. You don't need to eliminate the expectation today — just see it clearly.
