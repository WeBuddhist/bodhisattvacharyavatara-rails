## Traditional Interpretation

### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Palden (Tibetan)

KKP reads verse 1-15 as beginning the section on the twofold classification of bodhicitta (*smon 'jug gi dbye ba*). Bodhicitta can be divided into 22 kinds and many other ways, but in brief, with regard to its essential nature (*ngo bo'i sgo nas*), it is twofold: (1) *smon pa'i sems bskyed* = aspiring bodhicitta (*byang chub mchog tu smon pa'i sems bskyed*) — the commitment to attaining the goal of perfect enlightenment (*'bras bu sangs rgyas kyi go 'phang tha dad du smon pa*); (2) *'jug pa'i sems bskyed* = engaging bodhicitta — the commitment to the cause/path (*rgyu lam pha rol tu phyin pa drug sgrub par dam bca' zhing bcas pa bzhin du zhugs nas bsgrub bzhin pa*). KKP then gives an extensive critical survey of seven distinct scholarly traditions (*gzhung lugs*) on where exactly the smon/'jug boundary falls — from Ācārya Jñānapāda (smon pa = ordinary being's bodhicitta; 'jug pa = noble being's) to Ābhāya/Jñānakīrti (smon pa = path of accumulation; 'jug pa = path of joining onward) to Śāntipa/Ratnākaraśānti, to the Longchenpa tradition, to Tsongkhapa (*kun mkhyen chen po*): smon pa = commitment (*dam bca' ba*) to the result; 'jug pa = commitment to the causal path. Tsongkhapa's position: "aspiring" and "engaging" each has (1) *sems tsam* (bare aspiration), (2) *sems bskyed* (formal arousal with ritual), (3) *mi nyams pa gong nas gong du spel ba* (non-degeneration and progressive enhancement).
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-15)

### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Tibetan)

GDR opens his analysis of verse 1-15 under the heading "Recognizing the essential nature of bodhicitta" (*byang chub kyi sems kyi ngo bo ngos 'dzin ba*). Bodhicitta directed at enlightenment as its object (*byang chub la dmigs pa*) is twofold: (1) *smon pa'i sems bskyed* — the aspiring bodhicitta directed at the fruit (enlightenment itself); (2) *'jug pa'i sems bskyed* — the engaging bodhicitta directed at the causes/path. GDR is careful on the ritual aspect: *smon sems bskyed* need not be taken by ritual (*cho ga la mi ltos par skye*) and can persist without being formally vowed; nevertheless, a stricter form is taken through the Atīśa tradition's ritual (*cho gas lena ste*), where one makes the irreversible vow (*dam bca'i lcags kyu gnyis pa zin pa*) never to relinquish it until buddhahood. GDR critiques those who hold that buddhas lack engaging bodhicitta (*sangs rgyas la theg chen sems bskyed med par 'dod pa*) as extremely grave. He also warns against those who claim engaging-vow (*'jug sdom*) and engaging-bodhicitta (*'jug pa sems bskyed*) are separate and must be taken separately — calling this "a great misunderstanding."
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-15)

### ngulchu-thogmed — Ngulchu Thokme Sangpo (Tibetan)

NTS places this verse under the sub-heading: "The twofold differentiation (*dbye ba gnyis*)" and frames the division simply: aspiring bodhicitta (*smon pa'i sems*) and engaging bodhicitta (*'jug pa'i sems nyid*). NTS provides the definition through the road analogy: intending to go to a desired destination = aspiring (*smon pa*); actually setting out and traveling the path to get there = engaging (*'jug pa*). NTS cites the Gaṇḍavyūha sūtra: "Son of good family, those beings who aspire to unsurpassed perfect enlightenment are rare (*de dag kyang dkon no*); those beings who engage with the mind in unsurpassed perfect enlightenment are extremely rare (*shin tu dkon no*)." The verse itself is read as establishing merely that the division is twofold; subsequent verses explain the distinction by example and the difference in their benefits.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-15)

### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Mi Nyag Kunzang Sönam (Tibetan)

MKS opens by noting that although bodhicitta can be classified in many ways — 1 to 6, 22, 72, 80 — the present verse gives the summary classification (*mdor bsdu na dbye ba'i grangs ngo bo'i sgo nas rnam pa gnyis su shes par bya ste*). The two types: (1) *smon pa'i sems bskyed pa* = aspiringbodhicitta directed at unsurpassed enlightenment (*bla med byang chub tu smon pa'i sems bskyed pa*); (2) *'jug pa'i sems bskyed pa* = engaging bodhicitta directed at unsurpassed enlightenment (*bla med byang chub tu 'jug pa'i sems bskyed pa*). MKS aligns with the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*: "bodhicitta is twofold: *smon pa'i sems* (aspiring mind) and *byang chub tu chas pa'i sems* (the mind set toward enlightenment / engaging mind)." He then cites the Gaṇḍavyūha sūtra: "beings who aspire to unsurpassed enlightenment are rare; those who engage are extremely rare." Both these are conventional bodhicitta (*kun rdzob sems bskyed mtshan nyid pa*). He then gives the formal definitions: smon pa = aspiring to the fruition, perfect enlightenment (*'bras bu byang chub la dmigs pa'i smon pa'i sems bskyed*); 'jug pa = aspiring to the causal path (*rgyu lam la dmigs pa'i 'jug pa'i sems bskyed*). MKS then distinguishes conventional (*kun rdzob*) from ultimate (*don dam*) bodhicitta and notes that both smon pa and 'jug pa are conventional.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-15)

### khenpo-kunga — Ngakwang Kunga Wangchuk (Tibetan)

NKW reads verse 1-15 as introducing the distinction of smon/'jug and gives the clearest pedagogical summary of the three-tier structure. He defines: smon pa = "wouldn't it be wonderful if I could attain the goal of perfect enlightenment?" (*'bras bu rdzogs pa'i byang chub kyi go 'phang de nyid bdag gis thob par gyur na ci ma rung snyam pa'i sems*); 'jug pa = "wouldn't it be wonderful if I could properly practice the causal path?" (*de'i rgyu lam la tshul bzhin du 'jug par nus na ci ma rung snyam pa lta bu'i sems*). Both of these also occur as (1) *sems tsam* (bare aspiration, not formally vowed), (2) *sems bskyed* (formally aroused through ritual, with the iron-hook vow), (3) *mi nyams pa gong nas gong du spel ba* (non-degeneration and progressive cultivation of the above). Total: 3 × 2 = 6 sub-types. NKW notes that what follows (verse 1-16) illustrates this smon/'jug distinction through the road-going analogy; verse 1-17 then establishes the difference in their merit.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-15)

### Divergences

⚑ **Boundary between smon pa and 'jug pa** (major divergence): KKP enumerates seven distinct scholarly positions:
- *Jñānapāda* (*ye shes zhabs*): ordinary-being bodhicitta = smon; noble bodhicitta = 'jug
- *Ābhāya / Jñānakīrti* (*a bha ya / ye shes grags pa*): path of accumulation = smon; path of joining onward = 'jug
- *Śāntipa / Ratnākaraśānti*: bodhicitta not held by the bodhisattva practices = smon; held by them = 'jug
- *Ratnākaraśānti* variant: held by bodhisattva practices but not by vow = smon; with vow = 'jug
- *Śes rab 'byung gnas* / others: object-referent distinction (directed at fruit = smon; directed at cause = 'jug), without mention of ritual
- *Atiśa* tradition: dam bca' without ritual = smon; with ritual = 'jug
- *Tsongkhapa* (*kun mkhyen chen po*): smon pa = commitment to the result (*'bras bu sangs rgyas la dmigs pa*); 'jug pa = commitment to the cause/path (*rgyu lam la dmigs pa*) — these are two distinct vows (*sdom pa*) taken in sequence, both subsumed under the single bodhicitta of the Mahāyāna.

GDR, NTS, MKS, NKW all follow the Tsongkhapa/Gelug reading (commitment to result vs. commitment to causal path), expressed in slightly varying ways. KS (mchan-'grel) follows a clean smon-then-'jug narrative without enumerating the schools.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-15)

⚑ **Does a buddha have engaging bodhicitta?** GDR addresses a position that buddhas lack engaging bodhicitta (*smon 'jug gi sems bskyed med par 'dod pa ni / kha na ma tho ba shin tu che ste*) — calling it extremely grave. This issue is specific to the Gelug tradition's analysis and is not raised by the other commentaries.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-15)

## Key Concepts (ཆོས་ཀྱི་གནད་ཚིག)

### ཚིགས་བཅད་ནང་གི་གནད་ཚིག

- **བྱང་ཆུབ་སྨོན་པའི་སེམས་** (aspiring bodhicitta / *praṇidhicitta*) — ངོ་བོ་ = འབྲས་བུ་རྫོགས་བྱང་ལ་དམིགས་ཤིང་དེར་སེམས་ཅན་ཀུན་འགོད་འདོད་པའི་ཤུགས་དང་ལྡན་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས། མཚམས་གཞི་= ཆོ་ག་བླངས་མིན་གྱི་དབྱེ་བ་ཡོད། ⚑ གཞུང་ལུགས་མི་འདྲ་བས་མཚམས་གཞི་སོ་སོར་འཇོག
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-15) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/བྱང་ཆུབ་སྨོན་པའི་སེམས་_(praṇidhicitta).md]]

- **བྱང་ཆུབ་འཇུག་པ་** (engaging bodhicitta / *prasthānacitta*) — ངོ་བོ་ = རྒྱུ་ལམ་ལ་དམིགས་ཤིང་ཕར་ཕྱིན་དྲུག་ལ་སྒྲུབ་པར་དམ་བཅས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས། མཚམས་གཞི་ = ⚑ གཞུང་ལུགས་མི་འདྲ་བས་འཇོག་ཚུལ་མི་མཐུན།
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-15) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/བྱང་ཆུབ་འཇུག་པ་_(prasthānacitta).md]]

### འགྲེལ་པ་ནས་འབྱུང་བའི་གནད་ཚིག

- **སེམས་བསྐྱེད་ཙམ་ / སེམས་བསྐྱེད་ / མི་ཉམས་སྤེལ་བ་** (three-tier sub-classification, NKW) — སྨོན་འཇུག་རེར་གསུམ་གསུམ་ཡོད། (༡) ཆོ་གས་མ་བཟུང་བའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ལ་སེམས་བཏབ་པ་ཙམ། (༢) ཆོ་གས་བཟུང་ཏེ་དམ་བཅའ་བ། (༣) དམ་བཅའ་མི་ཉམས་པར་གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་སྤེལ་བ།
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-15) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་_(bodhicitta).md]]

## Quotations (ལུང་།)

> རིགས་ཀྱི་བུ་སེམས་ཅན་གྱི་འཇིག་རྟེན་སེམས་ཅན་གང་དག་བླ་ན་མེད་པ་ཡང་དག་པར་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཏུ་སྨོན་པ་དེ་དག་ཀྱང་དཀོན་ནོ། །དེ་བས་ཀྱང་གང་བླ་ན་མེད་པ་ཡང་དག་པར་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཏུ་འཇུག་པའི་སེམས་ཅན་དེ་དག་ནི་ཤིན་ཏུ་དཀོན་ནོ།
> — སྡོང་པོ་བཀོད་པ་ལས། (Gaṇḍavyūha sūtra)
> (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-15)

> རིགས་ཀྱི་བུ་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ལ་དབྱེ་ན་རྣམ་པ་གཉིས་ཏེ། བྱང་ཆུབ་ཏུ་སྨོན་པའི་སེམས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཏུ་ཆས་པའི་སེམས་སོ།
> — བསླབ་པ་བཏུས་པ་ལས། (Śikṣāsamuccaya)
> (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-15)

## Disambiguated Restatement (Tibetan)

བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ལ་དབྱེ་སྒོ་མང་དུ་ཡོད་ཀྱང་། ངོ་བོའི་སྒོ་ནས་མདོར་བསྡུས་ཏེ་བརྗོད་ན། རྣམ་པ་གཉིས་སུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱ་སྟེ། གཅིག་ = འབྲས་བུ་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་མཆོག་ཏུ་སེམས་ཅན་ཀུན་འགོད་འདོད་པའི་ (= འབྲས་བུ་ལ་དམིགས་པའི་) བྱང་ཆུབ་སྨོན་པའི་སེམས་ཏེ། གཞུང་ལུགས་གཙོ་བོའི་དོན་ལྟར་ན་རྫོགས་བྱང་གི་གོ་འཕང་ལ་དམ་བཅའ་བ་ཡིན། གཉིས་ = སེམས་དེ་སྔོན་དུ་གྱུར་ཏེ་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཐོབ་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཚོགས་རྣམས་ལ་ (= ཕར་ཕྱིན་དྲུག་ལ་) འཇུག་པར་དམ་བཅས་ཤིང་དམ་བཅའི་ལྕགས་ཀྱུས་བཟུང་ཏེ་ (= རྒྱུ་ལམ་ལ་དམིགས་པའི་) བྱང་ཆུབ་འཇུག་པའི་སེམས་ཡིན། ⚑ སྨོན་འཇུག་གི་མཚམས་གཞིར་གཞུང་ལུགས་མི་འདྲ་བ་མང་དུ་ཡོད་པས་འཕྲིན་ལས་སྒྲུབ་པར་ཡུལ་གྱི་ལུགས་མ་ངེས་བར་ལུས་ཤིང་། གཞི་འདིར་གཞི་གྲུབ་བཀོད་མ་ཐུབ་པས་གསལ་བའི་འགྲེལ་བཤད་མི་དགོས།
