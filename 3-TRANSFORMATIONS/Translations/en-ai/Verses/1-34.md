## Combined Commentary Summaries

The three classical Tibetan commentators—Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme—demonstrate complete agreement in their analysis of Verse 1-34. They interpret this verse as a sobering explanation of the severe negative karma and terrifying consequences of harboring angry or harmful thoughts toward a Bodhisattva (a Son of the Victorious/heir of the Buddha). By acting as the supreme benefactor who holds the mind of enlightenment, the Bodhisattva represents an exceptionally powerful "field of negativity," meaning that even purely mental hostility directed at them carries catastrophic karmic weight.

### 1. The Direct Correlation Between Thoughts and Retribution
The core assertion of the verse is that the duration of suffering in the hell realms corresponds directly to the duration of the malicious mind:
- **Ngulchu Thokme** and **Sazang Mati Panchen** state that if anyone generates a negative or angry mind toward such a benefactor, they will dwell in the hell realms for "as many eons as there are moments of generating that negative mind."
- **Sazang Mati Panchen** adds a crucial distinction regarding the severity of mental actions (*manaskarma*), emphasizing that "let alone physical or verbal harm," merely harboring a single moment of a harmful thought like anger toward an heir of the Buddha is sufficient to trigger this massive karmic ripening.
- **Gyaltsab Je** refers to this hostility as "the ultimate of negativity" (*sdig pa'i mchog*), warning that because the consequences are so severe and direct, practitioners must maintain extreme conscientiousness (*bag yod*) and be "extremely careful regarding this point."

### 2. Scriptural Proof of the "Armor of Hell"
To validate this profound assertion, all three commentators cite the **Sūtra of the Miracle of Complete Pacification** (*Prāśantaviniścayaprātihārya Sūtra* / *最極寂靜決定神變經*). They quote the Buddha's address to Mañjuśrī:
- According to the sūtra, if a Bodhisattva (or any individual) generates an angry, contemptuous, or disparaging mind toward another Bodhisattva, they must "don the armor of remaining in hell" (*dmyal bar gnas pa'i go cha bgo bar bya*) for as many eons as there were moments of that negative state of mind.

### 3. The Gravity of Harming the Cause of Buddhas
**Gyaltsab Je** provides a unique and profound scholastic explanation for why generating anger toward a Bodhisattva is the most severe negativity possible, citing the **Sūtra of the Mudra of Generating the Power of Faith** (*Śraddhābalādhānāvatāramudrā Sūtra* / *信力發起手印經*):
- The sūtra declares that generating a harmful mind toward a Hero of Enlightenment produces far more countless negativity than destroying or burning as many stūpas (relic shrines) as there are grains of sand in the Ganges River.
- **Gyaltsab Je** explains the underlying philosophical reason: since all perfect Buddhas are born from Bodhisattvas, the Bodhisattvas are the direct, indispensable cause of the Buddhas. Therefore, harming or wishing harm to a Bodhisattva is harming the very cause of the Three Jewels and the world's happiness, making it the absolute pinnacle of negative karma.
