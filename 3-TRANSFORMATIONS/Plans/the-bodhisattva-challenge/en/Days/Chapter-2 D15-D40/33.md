# Day 33 — A refuge you take inside

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Carry one true line of teaching into your day
> **Detail:** Today's verse takes refuge in the dharma. The dharma helps only when you lean on it.

## Opening

After the fear of the last days, Shantideva turns and takes refuge: from this very day, in the buddhas, the dharma, and the sangha of bodhisattvas. The middle one is easy to picture as a shelf of holy books. Today's verse means something closer in than that. The dharma that protects is the dharma you actually take into yourself and live by.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, reads one phrase in the verse carefully: the dharma realized in the buddhas' own minds. The dharma that protects is not ink on a page or words in the air. It is something the buddhas took all the way in until it changed them, and the verse says we take refuge in it by relying on it in the same way, letting it work inside our own minds. Books and teachings point the way, but they shelter us only once we have actually taken some of it to heart. He reads the sangha the same down-to-earth way: not distant saints, but companions on the road, the ones walking the path alongside us. So refuge here is less about looking up at three holy objects and more about taking the teaching inward and keeping good company while we walk.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Pick one short line of teaching you actually believe: that everything changes, that kindness is never wasted, that you are more than your worst moment. Carry it with you today, and when something tests you, bring it to mind and lean on it for a moment. That is what taking refuge in the dharma looks like up close: not admiring the line, but using it.
