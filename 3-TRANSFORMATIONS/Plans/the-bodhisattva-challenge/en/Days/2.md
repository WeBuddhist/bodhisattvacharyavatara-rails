# Day 2 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: The Benefits of the Mind of Enlightenment — Verses 1-4 to 1-6

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Appreciating This Human Life (Preciousness):** A precious human life is extremely rare and very difficult to obtain. It gives a person the perfect chance to practice the path to enlightenment. Using this life well right now is the wisest choice one can make.

*   **Seeing Through Weak Virtue (Honest Reflection):** Most ordinary good thoughts last only a short time, like a quick flash of lightning in the dark. Seeing this clearly helps a person stop relying on weak or careless effort alone. This honest understanding makes the mind more focused and serious about practice.

*   **Relying on the Mind of Enlightenment (True Remedy):** Ordinary virtue is too weak to overcome powerful negative habits on its own. Only the wish to awaken for the benefit of all beings has the real strength to clear away deep negativity. Turning to this powerful intention is the most effective thing a practitioner can do.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-4**

> དལ་འབྱོར་འདི་ནི་རྙེད་པར་ཤིན་ཏུ་དཀའ། །
> སྐྱེས་བུའི་དོན་སྒྲུབ་ཐོབ་པར་གྱུར་པ་ལ། །
> གལ་ཏེ་འདི་ལ་ཕན་པ་མ་བསྒྲུབས་ན། །
> ཕྱིས་འདི་ཡང་དག་འབྱོར་པར་ག་ལ་འགྱུར། །
>
> *This precious human life of leisure and opportunity is extremely difficult to find. Now I have this chance to accomplish my goals. If I do not use it to do good, how will I ever find such an opportunity again?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-4**

This verse urges the practitioner to recognize how rare and valuable a precious human life truly is. The eighteen qualities of freedom and opportunity that make this life special are extremely hard to find, because they require both ethical discipline and pure intention to create. If this chance is not used to walk the path to enlightenment, it may be a very long time before such a perfect opportunity arises again.

---

#### **Verse 1-5**

> ཇི་ལྟར་མཚན་མོ་མུན་ནག་སྤྲིན་རུམ་ན། །
> གློག་འགྱུ་སྐད་ཅིག་རབ་སྣང་སྟོན་པ་ལྟར། །
> དེ་བཞིན་སངས་རྒྱས་མཐུ་ཡིས་བརྒྱ་ལམ་ན། །
> འཇིག་རྟེན་བསོད་ནམས་བློ་གྲོས་ཐང་འགའ་འབྱུང་། །
>
> *A flash of lightning on a dark, cloudy night reveals everything for a single instant. Similarly, the power of the Buddha occasionally inspires a brief moment of virtue in this world.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-5**

The great teacher Śāntideva uses the image of a lightning flash in a dark night to show how rarely a virtuous thought appears in the mind of an ordinary person. The darkness stands for the heavy suffering and negativity that fills ordinary life, while the flash of light stands for a brief, sincere wish to do good. This virtuous impulse does not come from the person's own strength — it arises only through the compassionate power of the Buddhas, so it must be valued and acted on without delay.

---

#### **Verse 1-6**

> དེ་ལྟས་དགེ་བ་ཉམ་ཆུང་ཉིད་ལ་རྟག །
> སྡིག་པ་སྟོབས་ཆེན་ཤིན་ཏུ་མི་བཟད་པ། །
> དེ་ནི་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མིན་པ། །
> དགེ་གཞན་གང་གིས་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་པར་འགྱུར། །
>
> *Therefore, ordinary virtue is always extremely weak. Negativity is immensely powerful and unbearable. Except for the perfect mind of enlightenment, what other virtue can overcome such great evil?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-6**

Ordinary virtue is always very weak — it appears rarely and does not last long. At the same time, negative habits are extremely powerful and build up easily and constantly. Only the perfect wish to attain enlightenment for all beings has the real strength to overcome this heavy negativity, because it is grounded in both wisdom and sincere compassion for every living being.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Reflect on the Preciousness of Today:** In the morning, I will take one minute to reflect on how rare and valuable my life is right now. Remembering that this chance to practice may not come again, I will set a clear intention to use today well.

*   **2. Notice the Weakness of Half-Hearted Effort:** When I notice myself doing something good out of mere habit rather than sincere care, I will pause and bring more attention to that action. Recognizing how weak and fleeting my ordinary virtue can be helps me understand why I need a deeper and more stable motivation.

*   **3. Strengthen My Wish to Help All Beings:** Once today, I will consciously remind myself why I am practicing — not just for my own peace, but to eventually be of real help to all living beings. I will let this wish guide at least one action I take during the day.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Leisure and Opportunity (དལ་འབྱོར་):** This term refers to the eighteen special qualities that make a human life truly useful for spiritual practice. It includes eight freedoms from difficult situations and ten favorable conditions that support the path to enlightenment. Such a life is called precious because it is extremely rare and requires great virtue to obtain.

*   **The Mind of Enlightenment (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་):** This is the wish to achieve full awakening in order to benefit all living beings. In verse 1-6, it is described as the only force powerful enough to overcome great negativity, because it combines deep wisdom with genuine compassion. This wish is the most precious quality a practitioner can develop.

*   **Virtue (དགེ་བ་):** In this context, virtue means good intentions and positive actions that lead toward happiness and eventual awakening. In verse 1-5, ordinary virtue is compared to a flash of lightning — it is brief and weak unless it is supported by the wish for enlightenment. Without this deeper support, virtue is easily overcome by negative habits.

*   **Negativity (སྡིག་པ་):** This refers to harmful thoughts and actions that lead toward suffering and lower rebirths. The great teacher Śāntideva says that negativity is immensely powerful and very difficult to overcome by ordinary means. Understanding this clearly helps the practitioner see why the wish for enlightenment is so necessary and urgent.
