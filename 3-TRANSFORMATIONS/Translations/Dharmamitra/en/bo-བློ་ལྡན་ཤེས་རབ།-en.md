Respectfully bowing to the Sugatas who possess the Dharmakāya, along with their heirs, and to all who are worthy of veneration,
I shall describe the engagement in the vows of the Sugatas' heirs,
Having condensed them in accordance with the scriptures. ^1-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-2]]

There is nothing here that has not been said before,
Nor do I possess any skill in the art of composition.
Therefore, I have no thought that this will benefit others;
I have composed this in order to habituate my own mind. ^1-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-3]]

Through this, the power of my faith to cultivate virtue may increase for a while;
Furthermore, if others of equal fortune to myself should see this,
It may prove meaningful for them as well. ^1-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-4]]

This leisure and endowment is extremely difficult to find.
Having attained that which can accomplish the purpose of a human being,
If one does not accomplish what is beneficial in this life,
How could such a perfect opportunity ever be found again? ^1-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-5]]

Just as a flash of lightning in the darkness of a cloud-shrouded night
Momentarily reveals everything in its brilliant light,
So too, through the power of the Buddha,
A virtuous mind occasionally and briefly arises in the world. ^1-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-6]]

Therefore, virtue is always extremely weak,
While sin is of great strength and utterly unbearable.
Except for the mind of perfect enlightenment,
What other virtue could ever overcome it? ^1-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-7]]

The Lords of Sages, who have deeply contemplated for many eons,
Have seen that this alone is beneficial.
By this, the boundless multitudes of beings
Will easily attain the supreme bliss. ^1-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-8]]

Those who wish to overcome the hundreds of sufferings of existence,
Those who wish to dispel the miseries of sentient beings,
And those who wish to enjoy hundreds of manifold happinesses,
Should never forsake the awakening mind. ^1-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-9]]

Once the awakening mind has been generated, in that very instant,
The wretched ones bound in the prison of cyclic existence
Are called children of the Sugatas,
And become objects of homage for the world, including its gods and humans. ^1-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-10]]

Like the supreme form of an alchemical elixir,
It takes this impure body and transforms it into the priceless
Jewel of a Victor’s body; therefore, firmly grasp
That which is called the awakening mind. ^1-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-11]]

Since the sole Guide of beings, through his immeasurable intellect,
Has thoroughly examined it and seen its great value,
Those who wish to escape the realms of cyclic existence
Should firmly grasp this precious awakening mind. ^1-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-12]]

All other virtues are like the plantain tree;
Once they have produced their fruit, they are exhausted.
But the tree of the awakening mind perpetually bears fruit,
And rather than being exhausted, it increases ever more. ^1-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-13]]

Even if one has committed extremely unbearable sins,
Just as one relies on a hero to escape great terror,
Why would those who are conscientious not rely upon
That which swiftly liberates them? ^1-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-14]]

Like the fire at the end of an eon, it will surely
Incinerate great sins in a single instant.
Its immeasurable benefits were explained
By the wise Protector Maitreya to Sudhana. ^1-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-15]]

To summarize that awakening mind,
It should be understood as being of two types:
The mind that aspires for awakening, and
The mind that engages in awakening. ^1-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-16]]

Just as one understands the distinction
Between the desire to go and the act of going,
In the same way, the wise should understand
The distinction between these two in proper sequence. ^1-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-17]]

Although great fruits arise from the aspiring mind
While wandering in cyclic existence,
It does not produce a continuous flow of merit
In the way that the engaging mind does. ^1-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-18]]

From the moment one has correctly adopted that mind
With an irreversible resolve
In order to thoroughly liberate
The infinite realms of sentient beings, ^1-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-19]]

From this moment onward, even while sleeping or
Becoming heedless, a force of merit
Continuous and manifold
Will arise, as vast as space. ^1-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-20]]

This is reasonable, and as it was
Requested by Subāhu,
For the sake of beings with lesser aspirations,
The Tathāgata himself has spoken. ^1-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-21]]

If even thinking, "I shall dispel
The mere headaches of sentient beings,"
Is a beneficial intention
Endowed with immeasurable merit, ^1-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-22]]

Then what need is there to mention the desire
To dispel the infinite suffering of every single sentient being,
And the desire to bring each one
To immeasurable excellence? ^1-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-23]]

Whether it be a father or even a mother,
Who else possesses such a benevolent intention?
Do even the gods or the sages possess it?
Does even Brahmā have such a mind? ^1-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-24]]

If those very beings have never before,
Even in their dreams, dreamed of such an intention
For their own sake,
How could it ever arise for the sake of others? ^1-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-25]]

That intention to benefit sentient beings,
Which does not arise in others even for their own sake—
This extraordinary jewel of the mind
Is born as an unprecedented wonder. ^1-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-26]]

The cause of joy for all wandering beings,
The medicine for the suffering of sentient beings—
How could the merit of such a precious mind
Ever be measured? ^1-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-27]]

If even the mere thought of being beneficial
Excels the worship of the Buddhas,
What need is there to mention striving
For the welfare and happiness of every single being without exception? ^1-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-28]]

Although they have the desire to cast off suffering,
They rush headlong into suffering itself.
Although they desire happiness, through delusion
They destroy their own happiness as if it were an enemy. ^1-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-29]]

For those who are destitute of happiness
And burdened by many sufferings,
It satisfies them with every joy,
Cuts through all their suffering, ^1-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-30]]

And even dispels their delusion.
Where is there any virtue equal to that?
Where could there be such a friend?
How could there ever be such merit? ^1-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-31]]

If even one who returns a favor is worthy of some praise,
What need is there to mention a Bodhisattva
Who performs good deeds without being asked? ^1-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-32]]

The world honors as a "doer of virtue" someone who provides
A mere meal of ordinary food to a few beings,
Giving it contemptuously just to satisfy them for half a day,
Even if it is only a momentary gift. ^1-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-33]]

What need is there to mention one who constantly bestows
Upon an infinite number of sentient beings for an endless duration
The unsurpassed bliss of the Sugatas,
Which fulfills every heartfelt aspiration? ^1-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-34]]

The Sage has declared that whoever gives rise to a malicious thought
Toward such a benefactor, a Son of the Conquerors,
Will remain in hell for as many aeons
As the number of malicious thoughts produced. ^1-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-35]]

However, if one develops a deeply devoted mind,
Its fruit will increase even more than that;
Even when great difficulties arise for the Bodhisattvas,
No sin occurs, and their virtue naturally increases. ^1-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-36]]

I prostrate to the bodies of those in whom
That most precious and excellent mind has arisen;
I take refuge in that source of happiness,
Who brings about bliss even for those who cause them harm. ^1-36

## Chapter Two: Confession of Negativities ^2-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-1]]

In order to fully embrace that precious mind,
I make excellent offerings to the Tathāgatas,
To the stainless Jewel of the sublime Dharma,
And to the Sons of the Buddha, those oceans of virtue. ^2-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-2]]

Whatever flowers and fruits there may be,
And whatever kinds of medicinal plants exist,
Whatever precious gems there are in the world,
And whatever pure and pleasing waters there are; ^2-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-3]]

Likewise, the mountains of precious gems,
The forest regions, secluded and delightful,
The trees adorned and beautified with flowers,
And all the trees whose branches hang low with excellent fruit; ^2-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-4]]

In the worlds of the gods and others, there are fragrances,
Incense, wish-fulfilling trees, and jewel trees,
Various kinds of harvests that grow without being sown,
And all other ornaments worthy of being offered; ^2-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-5]]

Lakes and ponds adorned with lotuses,
Graced by swans with voices most melodious and charming,
Reaching to the furthest limits of the vast expanse of space,
All these things, which are entirely unpossessed; ^2-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-6]]

I take up with my mind and offer them well to the Munis, the supreme beings,
Together with their spiritual heirs;
O sublime objects of offering, you who are filled with great compassion,
Regard me with affection and accept these offerings of mine. ^2-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-7]]

I am destitute and lack merit,
And have no other wealth for making offerings.
Therefore, O Protectors who care for the welfare of others,
Please accept this through your own power for my sake. ^2-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-8]]

To the Victors and their spiritual heirs,
I offer my entire body forever.
O Supreme Beings, please accept me completely;
With devotion, I shall become your servant. ^2-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-9]]

Since I have been fully accepted by you,
I shall be fearless in existence and act for the benefit of sentient beings.
I shall perfectly transcend my previous misdeeds,
And shall commit no further sins hereafter. ^2-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-10]]

In a bathing chamber of exquisite fragrance,
With a floor of clear and sparkling crystal,
Adorned with lovely pillars of blazing jewels,
And draped with canopies of shimmering pearls, ^2-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-11]]

To the Tathāgatas and their spiritual heirs,
I offer a bath from many precious vases,
Beautifully filled with delightful, scented water,
Accompanied by manifold songs and music. ^2-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-12]]

With incomparable, clean, and highly perfumed cloths,
I wipe their bodies dry.
Then, I offer them supreme, beautifully colored,
And exquisitely fragrant robes. ^2-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-13]]

With various fine garments, thin and soft,
And hundreds of those most sublime ornaments,
I shall adorn the noble Samantabhadra, Mañjuśrī,
Avalokiteśvara, and the rest. ^2-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-14]]

With the finest perfumes, whose fragrance pervades
The entire billion-fold world system,
I shall anoint the bodies of all the Lords of Sages,
Which blaze with a radiant luster like burnished, refined gold. ^2-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-15]]

To the Lords of Sages, the supreme objects of veneration,
I offer delightful Mandārava flowers, lotuses,
Utpalas, and all other fragrant blossoms,
As well as beautifully arranged and enchanting garlands. ^2-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-16]]

I also offer clouds of the finest incense,
Captivating and pervasive in their scent.
I shall also offer divine feasts
Consisting of various kinds of foods and drinks. ^2-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-17]]

I shall also offer precious lamps
Arranged in rows upon golden lotuses.
Upon the ground, smoothed and anointed with perfume,
I shall scatter beautiful loose flowers. ^2-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-18]]

Immeasurable palaces resonant with delightful songs of praise,
Beautifully ablaze with hanging ornaments of pearls and jewels—
These, which have become the ornaments of infinite space,
I offer to those whose very nature is compassion. ^2-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-19]]

Beautiful jeweled parasols with golden handles,
Adorned with lovely arrangements of ornaments around their perimeter,
Well-shaped and pleasing to behold, I raise these up
And offer them forever to the Lords of Sages. ^2-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-20]]

Furthermore, may a multitude of other offerings,
Possessed of music and sweet, captivating melodies,
And clouds that soothe the suffering of sentient beings,
Remain in their respective places. ^2-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-21]]

Upon all the jewels of the sublime Dharma,
And upon the reliquaries and the sacred images,
May a rain of precious flowers and the like
Fall in a continuous, uninterrupted stream. ^2-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-22]]

Just as Mañjughoṣa and the others
Have made offerings to the Victors,
So too do I make offerings to the Tathāgatas,
The Protectors, and their spiritual heirs. ^2-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-23]]

To those who are oceans of excellence,
I offer praise with oceans of melodic branches.
May those clouds of melodious songs of praise
Certainly arise in every way. ^2-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-24]]

To all the Buddhas of the three times,
And to the Dharma and the Supreme Assembly,
With as many bodies as there are atoms in the realms,
I bow down and pay homage. ^2-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-25]]

I prostrate to the foundations of the awakening mind and to the reliquaries;
I prostrate to the preceptors, the masters,
And to those who maintain the supreme ascetic discipline. ^2-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-26]]

Until I reach the heart of enlightenment,
I take refuge in the Buddhas;
Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma
And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas. ^2-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-27]]

To the fully enlightened Buddhas and Bodhisattvas
Who dwell in all directions,
And who possess great compassion,
I join my palms and pray: ^2-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-28]]

From beginningless cyclic existence,
In this life or in others,
Whatever sins I have committed,
Or caused others to commit, ^2-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-29]]

Overwhelmed by the confusion of ignorance,
And whatever I have rejoiced in—
Having seen those faults,
I confess them to the Protectors from the depths of my heart. ^2-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-30]]

Whatever harm I have done to the Three Jewels,
To my parents, or to my teachers and others,
Through the gateway of the afflictions
By means of my body, speech, and mind, ^2-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-31]]

Whatever sins I, a sinner stained by many faults,
Have committed through my many transgressions,
Those extremely unbearable deeds,
I confess to all the Guides. ^2-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-32]]

I am terrified that I might die
Before my sins have been purified.
Please protect me with all haste,
So that I may be certain to be liberated from this. ^2-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-33]]

This Lord of Death is not to be trusted;
He does not wait for what has or has not been done.
Whether sick or healthy,
No one can rely on this fleeting life. ^2-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-34]]

Since I must depart, leaving everything behind,
Not having understood this,
I have committed various kinds of sins
For the sake of friends and foes. ^2-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-35]]

Enemies will turn to nothing,
And friends will turn to nothing;
I too will turn to nothing,
And likewise, everything will turn to nothing. ^2-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-36]]

Just like the experiences of a dream,
Whatever things I have enjoyed
Will become objects of memory;
All that has passed will not be seen again. ^2-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-37]]

Even in this brief lifetime,
Many friends and enemies have passed away;
Yet the unbearable sins committed for their sake
Remain before me. ^2-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-38]]

Because I did not realize
That I am merely a transient guest,
Through ignorance, attachment, and hatred,
I have committed many kinds of sins. ^2-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-39]]

Without pausing for a moment, day or night,
This life is constantly diminishing;
Since no addition comes from elsewhere,
Why should someone like me not die? ^2-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-40]]

While I am lying upon my bed,
Though surrounded by all my kinsmen and friends,
The sensations of the severing of my life-force
Must be experienced by me alone. ^2-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-41]]

When seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death,
What help are kinsmen, and what help are friends?
At that time, merit alone provides protection,
Yet that is what I have failed to cultivate. ^2-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-42]]

O Protectors, because I was heedless
And did not recognize such terrors as these,
For the sake of this transient life,
I have committed many sins. ^2-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-43]]

Even a person being led today to a place where their limbs will be severed becomes terrified;
With parched mouth and sunken eyes,
Their appearance is transformed from what it was before. ^2-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-44]]

Seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death, who have such terrifying forms,
And stricken by the great illness of fear,
What need is there to mention my extreme distress? ^2-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-45]]

Thinking, "Who can truly protect me from this great terror?"
With eyes wide and bulging in panic,
I will search in the four directions for a refuge. ^2-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-46]]

Seeing that there is no refuge in the four directions,
I then fall into utter despair.
If there is no refuge to be found in that place,
What shall I do then? ^2-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-47]]

Therefore, to the Victors, the protectors of beings,
Who strive for the purpose of shielding all wanderers,
And who, possessing great power, dispel every fear,
From this very day, I go for refuge. ^2-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-48]]

To the Dharma realized within their hearts,
Which dispels the terrors of cyclic existence,
And likewise to the assembly of Bodhisattvas,
I also go for refuge perfectly. ^2-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-49]]

Terrified by fear,
I offer myself to Samantabhadra;
To Mañjuśrī, I also
Offer my own body. ^2-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-50]]

To the protector Avalokiteśvara,
Whose compassionate conduct is never mistaken,
I cry out with a piteous voice:
"Please protect this sinful one!" ^2-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-51]]

To the noble Ākāśagarbha,
And to Kṣitigarbha as well,
And to all the great compassionate protectors,
I cry out from my heart, seeking refuge. ^2-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-52]]

At the sight of whom, the messengers of Yama
And other hostile beings flee in terror
To the four directions,
I go for refuge to the Wielder of the Vajra. ^2-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-53]]

In the past, I have transgressed your commands;
Now, seeing this great fear,
I come to you for refuge;
Please, swiftly dispel my fears! ^2-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-54]]

If, when frightened by an ordinary illness,
One must act according to a physician's advice,
Then what need is there to mention those
Constantly afflicted by the diseases of a hundred faults, such as desire? ^2-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-55]]

If even one of these could destroy
All the people living in Jambudvīpa,
And no other medicine to cure them
Can be found anywhere in any direction, ^2-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-56]]

Then the intention not to act according to the instructions
Of the Omniscient Physician, who removes every pain,
Is a state of extreme delusion
And is truly contemptible. ^2-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-57]]

If one must remain cautious
Even at a small, ordinary precipice,
What need is there to mention the long-lasting abyss
That falls for a thousand leagues? ^2-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-58]]

It is not right to live at ease,
Thinking, "I will not die this very day."
The time when I shall cease to be
Will undoubtedly arrive. ^2-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-59]]

Who has granted me fearlessness?
How can I be certain of escaping this?
Since I will surely come to nothing,
How can my mind remain at ease? ^2-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-60]]

Of all that I have experienced in the past and which has vanished,
What remains for me?
Yet, through my obsessive attachment to such things,
I have acted in opposition to the Guru's commands. ^2-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-61]]

When I must leave this life and likewise
Abandon my kinsmen and friends to go
Somewhere all alone,
What is the use of all those who are dear or those who are not? ^2-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-62]]

From non-virtue, suffering arises.
Thinking, "How can I be certain of escaping this?"
It is right that I should constantly,
Day and night, think only of this. ^2-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-63]]

Because of my ignorance and delusion,
Whatever natural misdeeds
Or proscribed sins I have committed,
Whatever they may be, ^2-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-64]]

In the presence of the Protectors, openly,
With palms joined and a mind fearful of suffering,
I prostrate again and again,
And I confess all of these. ^2-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-65]]

I pray that the Guides will regard my sins
As the transgressions they are.
Since this was not good,
Henceforth, I shall never do it again. ^2-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-a]]

This concludes the second chapter, the Chapter on the Confession of Sin, from the Engaging in the Bodhisattva's Conduct. ^2-a

## Adopting the Bodhichitta ^3-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-1]]

I joyfully rejoice in the virtue that relieves
The suffering of all sentient beings in the lower realms,
And in the abiding in happiness of those who suffer,
With a heart full of delight. ^3-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-2]]

I rejoice in the accumulation of virtue
That has become the cause for enlightenment.
I rejoice in the definite liberation
Of embodied beings from the suffering of cyclic existence.
I rejoice in the enlightenment of the Protectors,
And also in the levels of the Bodhisattvas. ^3-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-3]]

I joyfully rejoice in the ocean of virtue
That is the generation of the mind of awakening, which brings happiness to all sentient beings,
And in the deeds that benefit all sentient beings,
With a heart full of delight. ^3-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-4]]

To the Buddhas of all directions,
I join my palms and pray:
For those wandering in the darkness of suffering,
Please kindle the lamp of the Dharma. ^3-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-5]]

To the Victors who intend to pass into nirvana,
I join my palms and pray:
Do not leave these beings in blindness,
But please remain for countless eons. ^3-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-6]]

Having performed all these acts in this way,
Whatever virtue I have accumulated,
By that, may all the suffering
Of every sentient being be dispelled. ^3-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-7]]

For as long as there are beings who are ill,
Until their diseases are cured,
May I become their medicine, their physician,
And their nurse. ^3-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-8]]

By raining down showers of food and drink,
May I dispel the suffering of hunger and thirst;
During the intermediate eon of famine,
May I myself become food and drink. ^3-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-9]]

For sentient beings who are destitute and poor,
May I become an inexhaustible treasure,
And remain present before them
As every variety of necessary provision. ^3-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-10]]

My body, and likewise my possessions,
And all my virtues of the three times,
I shall give away without a sense of loss,
To accomplish the welfare of all sentient beings. ^3-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-11]]

By renouncing everything, one transcends suffering,
And my mind seeks to achieve that transcendence;
Since I must give everything away at once,
It is best to give it to all sentient beings. ^3-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-12]]

I have already given this body
To all embodied beings to do with as they please;
Whether they constantly kill it, revile it,
Or beat it, it is up to them to do as they wish. ^3-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-13]]

Whether they make sport of my body,
Or make it an object of ridicule and jest,
Since I have already given this body away,
What is the use of my guarding it? ^3-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-14]]

Let them do whatever actions they wish,
Provided it does not cause them harm;
May no one who encounters me
Ever find it to be without meaning. ^3-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-15]]

For those who, focusing on me,
Develop a mind of anger or of faith,
May that very state always become
The cause for the fulfillment of all their goals. ^3-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-16]]

Whether they speak ill of me,
Or others do me harm,
Or even if they mock me,
May all be fortunate enough to attain enlightenment. ^3-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-17]]

May I be a protector for those without a protector,
A guide for those traveling on the path,
And for those who wish to cross over,
May I be a boat, a ship, and a bridge. ^3-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-18]]

May I be an island for those seeking an island,
And a place of rest for those desiring rest;
For all embodied beings who desire a servant,
May I become their servant. ^3-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-19]]

May I be a wish-fulfilling jewel, a vase of excellence,
A perfected mantra, and a great medicine,
And may I be a wish-granting tree,
And a cow of plenty for all embodied beings. ^3-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-20]]

Just as the great elements, such as earth,
And like space itself, may I always be
The basis for the various means of subsistence
For immeasurable sentient beings. ^3-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-21]]

Likewise, for the realms of sentient beings
Extending to the very ends of space,
May I be the cause of their sustenance in every way,
Until all have passed beyond sorrow. ^3-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-22]]

Just as the Sugatas of former times
Generated the mind of enlightenment,
And in the Bodhisattva trainings
Abided and trained in successive order, ^3-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-23]]

So too, for the benefit of all beings,
Shall I generate the mind of enlightenment,
And likewise, in those trainings,
I shall train in successive order. ^3-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-24]]

In this way, those endowed with wisdom,
Having firmly adopted the supreme mind of enlightenment,
In order to bring the conclusion to full completion,
Should uplift their minds as follows: ^3-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-25]]

Today my life has become fruitful;
I have well obtained this human existence.
Today I am born into the family of the Buddhas;
I have now become a child of the Buddhas. ^3-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-26]]

From now on, by any means necessary,
I shall undertake actions befitting this lineage,
And act in such a way that I do not defile
This faultless and noble lineage. ^3-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-27]]

Just as a blind person might find
A precious jewel within a heap of refuse,
So, by some stroke of fortune,
This spirit of awakening has been born within me. ^3-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-28]]

It is the supreme nectar that overcomes
The Lord of Death for all beings;
It is the inexhaustible treasure that dispels
The poverty of all beings. ^3-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-29]]

It is the supreme medicine that completely
Pacifies the illnesses of all beings;
It is the shade tree of rest for beings
Weary from wandering the paths of cyclic existence. ^3-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-30]]

It is the universal bridge that delivers
All beings from the lower realms;
It is the rising moon of the mind that dispels
The heat of the afflictions of all beings. ^3-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-31]]

It is the great sun that completely dispels
The dimness of ignorance of wandering beings;
It is the essence of butter extracted
From churning the milk of the sublime Dharma. ^3-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-32]]

For the guests among beings who travel the path of existence
And desire to enjoy the riches of happiness,
This stands as the supreme bliss close at hand,
Which brings total satisfaction to the great guests, the sentient beings. ^3-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-33]]

Today, in the presence of all the Protectors,
I invite the world to the state of the Sugatas and to temporal happiness;
Since I have invited you as my guests to this bliss,
May the gods, asuras, and all others rejoice! ^3-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-a]]

From "Engaging in the Bodhisattva Deeds," this is the third chapter, entitled "Fully Adopting the Awakening Mind." ^3-a

## Chapter Four: Demonstrating Conscientiousness ^4-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-1]]

Having thus firmly grasped the Awakening Mind,
The children of the Conquerors
Must always, without distraction,
Strive never to transgress the trainings. ^4-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-2]]

Whatever is undertaken impulsively,
Or whatever has not been well examined—
Even if a promise has been made regarding it,
It is right to reconsider whether to proceed or to let it go. ^4-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-3]]

But that which has been examined by the Buddhas and their heirs
With great wisdom,
And which I myself have also examined again and again—
What reason is there to delay in that? ^4-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-4]]

If, after having made such a vow,
I do not accomplish it through action,
Then by deceiving all those sentient beings,
What will my future destiny be? ^4-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-5]]

If it is said that even regarding a minor, ordinary object,
A person who intends in their mind to give it,
But then does not give it,
Will become a hungry ghost, ^4-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-6]]

Then, having sincerely invited all beings
To the unsurpassed bliss,
If I were to deceive all those beings,
How could I ever go to a happy realm? ^4-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-7]]

Even if one abandons the bodhicitta,
The way in which those are liberated
Is an inconceivable karmic process,
Known only to the Omniscient One. ^4-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-8]]

For a bodhisattva, this is
The gravest of all downfalls;
For should it occur,
The welfare of all sentient beings is diminished. ^4-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-9]]

Anyone who, even for a single moment,
Creates obstacles or hinders this merit,
Diminishes the welfare of sentient beings,
And for them, rebirth in lower realms is endless. ^4-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-10]]

If one’s own well-being is impaired by destroying the happiness of even a single sentient being,
What need is there to mention destroying the happiness of all embodied beings as vast as the limits of space? ^4-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-11]]

Thus, if powerful downfalls and the power of bodhicitta alternate and contend within cyclic existence,
It will take a very long time to attain the bodhisattva grounds. ^4-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-12]]

Therefore, just as I have vowed, I must respectfully fulfill it.
If I do not make an effort from this moment on, I shall sink lower and lower. ^4-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-13]]

Although countless Buddhas who act for the benefit of all sentient beings have passed by,
I, due to my own faults, have not become an object of their healing care. ^4-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-14]]

If I continue to act in this same way,
The same will happen again and again;
I will experience sickness, bondage,
Mutilation, and being cut asunder in the lower realms. ^4-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-15]]

The appearance of a Tathāgata, the attainment of faith and a human body,
And the fitness to cultivate virtue—
Since such things are so rare, when will they ever be found again? ^4-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-16]]

Although today I am free from illness, have food, and am not in harm's way,
Life is a momentary deception,
And the body is like a temporary loan. ^4-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-17]]

With conduct such as mine,
I shall not even attain a human body again.
If a human body is not attained,
There will be only sin and no virtue. ^4-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-18]]

If, while I have the fortune to practice virtue,
I do not perform virtuous deeds,
Then what shall I be able to do when I am completely bewildered
By the sufferings of the lower realms? ^4-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-19]]

If I have not performed virtuous deeds,
And have instead thoroughly accumulated sins,
Then for even a hundred million eons,
I shall not even hear the sound of a happy rebirth. ^4-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-20]]

For that very reason, the Blessed One said
That human status is extremely difficult to attain,
Like a turtle’s neck entering the hole
Of a yoke tossing upon a vast ocean. ^4-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-21]]

If even a sin committed in a single instant
Leads to remaining in the Avīci hell for an eon,
Then what need is there to mention that, due to sins
Accumulated in beginningless saṃsāra, I shall not go to a happy rebirth? ^4-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-22]]

Having experienced only that much,
One will not be liberated;
For while experiencing it in that way,
Other sins are produced in great abundance. ^4-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-23]]

Having found such leisure as this,
If I do not habituate myself to virtue,
There is no deception greater than this,
And there is no folly greater than this. ^4-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-24]]

If, having realized this,
I still succumb to laziness through delusion,
Then at the time when death arrives,
A great anguish will arise. ^4-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-25]]

If my body were to be burned for a long time
By the unbearable fires of hell,
There is no doubt that my mind would be tormented
By the blazing fire of unbearable remorse. ^4-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-26]]

Having somehow, by good fortune, found this state of benefit
That is so extremely difficult to obtain,
If I am led back to those same hells
Even while possessing conscious awareness, ^4-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-27]]

It is as if I have been rendered senseless by a magic spell;
I am here utterly without my mind.
I do not even know what has deluded me,
Or what it is that dwells within me. ^4-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-28]]

These enemies, such as hatred and craving,
Possess neither hands, feet, nor other limbs;
They are neither brave nor wise, yet somehow,
They have made me their slave. ^4-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-29]]

While dwelling within my own mind,
They harm me at their pleasure;
To not be angry at this and to remain patient
Is misplaced patience, a state worthy of reproach. ^4-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-30]]

Even if all the gods and demigods
Were to rise up against me as enemies,
They would be unable to lead me into
Or cast me into the fires of the Avīci hell. ^4-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-31]]

This powerful enemy, the mental afflictions,
Can in a single instant cast me
Into that fire which, upon meeting even Mount Meru,
Would not leave even its ashes behind. ^4-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-32]]

These enemies of mine, the mental afflictions,
Are as if without beginning or end;
No other enemies are capable
Of lasting for such a long time. ^4-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-33]]

If one serves and honors others in a friendly way,
They will all bring benefit and happiness;
But if one serves the mental afflictions,
They respond only with further suffering and harm. ^4-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-34]]

If such a long-standing, constant enemy,
The sole cause for the increase of a mass of harms,
Truly takes up residence within my heart,
How could I ever be fearless and joyful in cyclic existence? ^4-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-35]]

If these guards of the prison of cyclic existence,
Who become executioners in the hells and elsewhere,
Dwell within the net of attachment in my mind,
How could I ever find happiness? ^4-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-36]]

Therefore, as long as I have not decisively destroyed this enemy before my very eyes,
I shall not abandon my efforts in this regard;
For those full of pride, who become enraged even at a minor, temporary harm,
Will not sleep until they have defeated that enemy. ^4-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-37]]

If those who wish to forcefully defeat in battle their enemies,
Who are already miserable by nature and destined for death,
Can disregard the pain of being struck by arrows and spears,
And refuse to turn back until their goal is achieved, ^4-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-38]]

Then what need is there to mention that I,
Who am striving to defeat my natural and certain enemies—
The constant cause of all suffering—
Should not be discouraged or lazy, no matter what hundreds of sufferings arise? ^4-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-39]]

If even the scars inflicted by a meaningless enemy
Are cherished like ornaments upon the body,
Then why should suffering harm me,
Who am perfectly striving to accomplish the great purpose? ^4-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-40]]

If fishermen, outcasts, farmers, and the like,
Who think only of their own mere livelihood,
Can endure the hardships of cold, heat, and so forth,
Why should I not endure them for the sake of the world's happiness? ^4-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-41]]

When I vowed to liberate all beings,
As far as the limits of space in the ten directions,
From their afflictions,
I myself was not yet freed from those very afflictions. ^4-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-42]]

Was it not like the speech of a madman
To speak thus, without even knowing my own capacity?
Therefore, in order to destroy the afflictions,
I shall never turn back. ^4-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-43]]

I must be tenacious regarding this,
And harboring a grudge, I shall engage in battle;
Such forms of mental affliction are acceptable,
Except for those that destroy the afflictions themselves. ^4-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-44]]

Even if I were to be burned or killed,
Or even if my head were to be cut off,
I shall never, in any way,
Bow down to my enemies, the mental afflictions. ^4-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-45]]

When an ordinary enemy is driven from a country,
He settles in another land and takes possession of it;
Then, once his strength has increased, he returns.
But the nature of the enemy, the mental afflictions, is not like that. ^4-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-46]]

The afflictions are abandoned by the eye of wisdom;
Once dispelled from my mind, where will they go?
Where will they dwell to return and harm me again?
It is only that I, being weak-minded, lack persistence. ^4-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-47]]

The afflictions do not reside in sense objects, nor in the collection of faculties, nor in between them;
If they do not exist elsewhere either, then where do they dwell to harm the entire world?
They are like an illusion; therefore, to abandon fear in your heart, apply yourself to understanding them.
Why do you pointlessly inflict harm upon yourself in the hells and other such realms? ^4-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-48]]

Having reflected in this way, one should strive
To accomplish the trainings as they have been explained.
If one does not follow the physician's instructions,
How can a patient who needs treatment be cured by medicine? ^4-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-a]]

This concludes the fourth chapter, "Showing Heedfulness," from the *Entering the Bodhisattva's Conduct*. ^4-a

## Chapter Five: Guarding Introspection ^5-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-1]]

Those who wish to guard their training
Must guard the mind with great effort;
If one does not guard this mind,
One will be unable to guard the training. ^5-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-2]]

By letting the elephant of the mind run wild,
It inflicts the torments of the Incessant Hell;
An untamed, mad elephant in this world
Cannot cause such harm. ^5-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-3]]

If the elephant of the mind is firmly bound
With the rope of total mindfulness,
All fears will vanish,
And every virtue will fall into one's hands. ^5-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-4]]

Tigers, lions, elephants, bears,
Snakes, and all enemies,
The guardians of the hell realms,
And likewise sorcerers and demons— ^5-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-5]]

By binding this mind alone,
All of them are bound;
By subduing this mind alone,
All of them are subdued. ^5-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-6]]

In this way, all fears
And immeasurable sufferings as well
Arise from the mind;
Thus it was taught by the Speaker of Truth. ^5-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-7]]

Who intentionally created
The weapons of the hell beings?
Who created the floor of burning iron,
And from what did those masses of fire arise? ^5-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-8]]

The Sage has taught that all such things
Are the result of a sinful mind.
Thus, within the three worlds,
There is no other fear besides the mind. ^5-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-9]]

If the perfection of generosity
Consisted in the removal of the world's poverty,
Then since beings are still destitute today,
How could the Protectors of the past have reached that perfection? ^5-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-10]]

The perfection of generosity is said to be the mind
Of relinquishing all possessions, together with their results,
To all beings;
Therefore, it is the mind itself. ^5-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-11]]

To what place could fish and other creatures be moved
So that they would not be killed?
The perfection of ethical discipline is explained
As the attainment of the mind of renunciation. ^5-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-12]]

Unruly sentient beings are like space;
It is impossible to overcome them all.
But if this single mind of anger is overcome,
It is the same as overcoming all those enemies. ^5-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-13]]

Where could there be enough leather
To cover the entire surface of the earth?
But with just the leather of one's shoe soles,
It is as if the whole earth were covered. ^5-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-14]]

Likewise, I am unable to turn back
All external phenomena;
But I shall turn back this mind of mine;
What need is there to turn back anything else? ^5-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-15]]

Just as the result of generating a single clear mind
Is birth as a Brahmā or the like,
The results of body and speech are not such,
Because their conduct is weak. ^5-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-16]]

Even if one practices recitations and all kinds of austerities
For a very long period of time,
The Knower of Reality has said that if they are performed
With a mind distracted elsewhere, they are useless. ^5-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-17]]

Those who do not understand this secret of the mind,
The supreme essence of the Dharma,
Will wander aimlessly and in vain,
Even though they wish to attain happiness and destroy suffering. ^5-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-18]]

Therefore, I shall firmly grasp
And properly guard this mind of mine.
Apart from the ascetic discipline of guarding the mind,
What is the use of many other disciplines? ^5-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-19]]

If one dwells in the midst of a boisterous crowd,
One should be attentive, just as one protects a wound.
Even when dwelling among wicked people,
One should always guard this wound of the mind. ^5-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-20]]

If one protects a wound out of fear of even its slight pain,
Then why, fearing the crushing of the Samghāta and Raurava hells,
Does one not guard the wound of the mind? ^5-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-21]]

If one conducts oneself in this manner,
Whether dwelling among wicked people
Or even in the midst of women,
The steady practitioner of discipline will not degenerate. ^5-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-22]]

Better that my gains, honor, and body,
And even my very life should perish,
And better that my other virtues should decline,
Than that I should ever let my mind degenerate. ^5-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-23]]

To those who wish to guard their minds,
I join my palms in prayer and say:
"With all your effort, guard both mindfulness and introspective awareness!" ^5-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-24]]

Just as people who are disturbed by illness
Have no strength for any activity,
So too, a mind disturbed by confusion
Has no strength for any activity. ^5-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-25]]

For those whose minds lack introspective awareness,
Even what has been heard, reflected upon, and meditated on,
Will not remain in their memory,
Like water in a leaking jar. ^5-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-26]]

Even many who possess learning and faith,
And who apply themselves with great diligence,
Become defiled by the stain of downfalls,
Due to the fault of lacking introspective awareness. ^5-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-27]]

The thieves of non-introspective awareness,
Following upon the decline of mindfulness,
Steal even the merits one has carefully accumulated,
Leading one to the lower realms as if plundered by robbers. ^5-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-28]]

This band of thieves, the mental afflictions,
Is constantly searching for an opportunity;
Having found an opportunity, they steal one's virtue,
And destroy even the life of a happy rebirth. ^5-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-29]]

Therefore, mindfulness should never be allowed
To stray from the gateway of the mind.
If it should stray, one should bring it back
By recollecting the sufferings of the lower realms. ^5-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-30]]

Through associating with a spiritual master,
Following the instructions of a preceptor,
And through fear, the fortunate who act with respect
Will easily develop mindfulness. ^5-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-31]]

The Buddhas and Bodhisattvas
Possess vision that is completely unhindered;
Thinking, "I always stand
In the presence of them all," ^5-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-32]]

By reflecting in this way, one should maintain
A sense of shame, respect, and likewise fear.
Consequently, the recollection of the Buddha
Will also arise in one again and again. ^5-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-33]]

When mindfulness stands at the gateway of the mind
For the purpose of guarding it,
At that time alertness will arrive,
And even that which had slipped away will return. ^5-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-34]]

First, having recognized that a mind such as this
Is possessed of faults,
At that time I should remain
As steady as a log. ^5-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-35]]

I should never look around
With a distracted or aimless gaze;
With a resolute mind, I should always
Keep my eyes cast downward. ^5-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-36]]

To rest the gaze, one may
Occasionally look toward the horizon;
If someone appears in the field of vision,
One should look at them and say, "Welcome." ^5-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-37]]

To check for dangers on the path and so forth,
One should repeatedly look in the four directions.
After pausing to rest, one should turn around
And look toward the area behind. ^5-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-38]]

Having examined what is in front and behind,
One should then proceed to go or come;
Thus, in all circumstances,
One should act with an understanding of what is necessary. ^5-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-39]]

Having prepared for an action with the thought,
"My body shall remain in this way,"
One should then periodically observe
How this body is positioned. ^5-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-40]]

The intoxicated elephant of the mind
Must be bound to the great pillar of reflecting on the Dharma;
One should examine with all diligence
So that it does not escape from how it is tied. ^5-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-41]]

In such a way, one should strive
So that meditative concentration does not slip away for even a single moment;
One should individually examine the mind,
Thinking, "Where is this mind of mine wandering?" ^5-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-42]]

If one is unable to do so due to involvement in dangers, festivities, and the like, one should act as is appropriate;
For it is taught that at times such as when practicing generosity, the rules of discipline may be set aside in equanimity. ^5-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-43]]

Whatever task one has reflected upon and begun,
One should not think of anything other than that;
With the mind directed toward that goal,
One should first accomplish that very task. ^5-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-44]]

If one acts in this way, all is well performed;
Otherwise, neither task will be achieved.
If there is no introspection, the secondary afflictions
Will not fail to increase. ^5-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-45]]

When one encounters various kinds of idle chatter
And many sorts of wondrous spectacles,
One should abandon attachment
To all of them. ^5-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-46]]

If one begins to aimlessly dig the earth, cut the grass,
Or draw patterns in the dirt,
One should remember the training of the Sugatas
And, out of fear, cease immediately. ^5-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-47]]

Whenever one desires to move,
Or even when one desires to speak,
One should first examine one's own mind
And, remaining steadfast, act with propriety. ^5-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-48]]

Whenever one's mind is affected by attachment
Or the desire to be angry,
One should not act and should not speak;
One should remain like a log of wood. ^5-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-49]]

If there is agitation or mockery,
Or if there is pride and arrogance,
Or the intention to expose others' faults,
Or if there is divisiveness and deceit, ^5-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-50]]

Whenever one is intent on self-praise,
Or on disparaging others,
Or if one becomes abusive or filled with remorse,
At those times, one should remain like a log of wood. ^5-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-51]]

If one desires gain, honor, or fame,
Or if one seeks a circle of attendants,
Or if one's mind desires to be served,
At those times, one should remain like a log of wood. ^5-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-52]]

When the intention to neglect the welfare of others,
Or the desire to pursue one's own interests,
Or the urge to speak arises,
At those times, one should remain like a log of wood. ^5-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-53]]

When impatience, laziness, or fear arise,
And likewise when impudence or idle chatter,
Or attachment to one's own side arises,
At those times, one should remain like a log of wood. ^5-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-54]]

Having thus examined the mind for afflictions
And for strivings toward meaningless things,
At that time, the heroic one should firmly
Restrain it by means of the antidote. ^5-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-55]]

With deep certainty and supreme faith,
With steadfastness, respect, and courtesy,
With a sense of shame and dread of blame,
Be peaceful and strive to bring joy to others. ^5-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-56]]

Do not be weary of the conflicting
Desires of the childish,
But be compassionate, thinking,
"These arise from their afflictions." ^5-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-57]]

In matters that are naturally blameless,
For the sake of myself and other beings,
I should always maintain this mind
Without a sense of self, like an emanation. ^5-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-58]]

Reflecting again and again that after a long time I have obtained this supreme leisure,
I should hold my mind as utterly unshakable as Mount Meru. ^5-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-59]]

If you, O mind, are not displeased when vultures,
Craving flesh, drag this body all around,
Why do you cherish it so much now? ^5-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-60]]

Having grasped this body as "mine,"
O mind, why do you guard it?
Since you and this body are separate,
What can it ever do for you? ^5-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-61]]

O deluded mind, why do you not grasp instead a clean wooden image?
Why is it appropriate to guard this foul machine,
This collection of impurities? ^5-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-62]]

First, with your own intellect, peel away this layer of skin;
Then, with the blade of wisdom,
Separate the flesh from the lattice of bones. ^5-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-63]]

Having also dismantled the bones,
Look right into the marrow and examine for yourself:
"What essence is there in this?" ^5-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-64]]

If, despite searching with such effort, you find no essence there,
Then why do you still guard this body
With such an attitude of attachment? ^5-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-65]]

Since you cannot eat its impurities,
Nor drink its blood,
Nor suck its intestines, what use is this body to you? ^5-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-66]]

At best, it is only fit to be guarded as food for foxes and vultures;
This human body is meant only to be put to use. ^5-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-67]]

Even if you guard it in this way,
When the pitiless Lord of Death
Snatches it away and gives it to the birds and dogs,
What will you be able to do then? ^5-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-68]]

If you do not provide clothing and such
Even to a servant who cannot be put to work,
Then why do you exhaust yourself nourishing this body
Which, though cared for, will depart elsewhere? ^5-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-69]]

Having given this its wages,
Now make it perform your own purpose;
Do not give everything to this body
If it brings no benefit. ^5-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-70]]

Regarding the body as a boat, a mere support for coming and going,
Think of it as a vessel,
And in order to accomplish the welfare of sentient beings,
Transform it into a wish-fulfilling body. ^5-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-71]]

Having thus gained self-mastery,
Always maintain a smiling countenance;
Completely abandon frowning and scowling,
And be a friend to all beings and speak sincerely. ^5-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-72]]

Do not move chairs and such things
Carelessly or with loud noise;
Do not open doors forcefully,
But always delight in modesty and restraint. ^5-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-73]]

The heron, the cat, and the thief,
By moving stealthily and silently,
Accomplish their desired aims;
A sage should always behave in such a manner. ^5-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-74]]

One should respectfully accept upon one's head
The words of those skilled in advising others
And who provide unsolicited benefit;
Always remain a student to everyone. ^5-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-75]]

To all that is well-spoken,
One should say, "It is excellently said."
When seeing someone performing a meritorious deed,
Generate joy by praising them well. ^5-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-76]]

Speak of virtues in private,
And when virtues are spoken of, join in.
When one's own virtues are spoken of,
Recognize it as an appreciation of qualities. ^5-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-77]]

All such endeavors are for the sake of joy,
Which is rare even if one were to buy it with wealth.
Therefore, through the virtues performed by others,
One should enjoy the happiness of being joyful. ^5-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-78]]

In this, there is no loss for oneself,
And in the next life, there will be great happiness;
Through wrongdoing, one is unhappy and suffers,
And in the next life, the suffering will be even greater. ^5-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-79]]

When speaking, one should speak in a way that is agreeable, coherent,
And clear in meaning, and pleasant;
Free from attachment and aversion,
One should speak gently and in moderation. ^5-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-80]]

Even when looking at sentient beings with one's eyes,
One should think, "It is by relying on these very beings
That I shall attain Buddhahood,"
And look upon them with a straightforward and loving manner. ^5-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-81]]

Whether motivated by constant devotion
Or by the application of an antidote,
Great virtue arises toward those who possess qualities,
Those who have been helpful, and those who are suffering. ^5-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-82]]

Having become skillful and endowed with initiative,
I shall always perform my tasks.
In all activities, one should not
Depend upon anyone else. ^5-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-83]]

One should practice the perfections, such as generosity,
In an ever-ascending degree of excellence.
One should not sacrifice a great benefit for a small one;
The primary consideration should be the welfare of others. ^5-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-84]]

Having understood this, one should always
Remain diligent in working for the welfare of others.
Because the Compassionate One sees far ahead,
He permits even those things that were prohibited. ^5-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-85]]

One should share with those who have fallen into wrong paths, the protectorless,
And those established in ascetic vows;
One should eat only in moderation,
And give away everything except for the three religious robes. ^5-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-86]]

This body, which practices the sublime Dharma,
Should not be harmed for the sake of something minor.
By acting in this way, the aspirations
Of sentient beings will be swiftly fulfilled. ^5-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-87]]

As long as the compassionate intention is not pure,
One should not give away this body;
Rather, in this and other lives as well,
It should be given as a cause for accomplishing the great purpose. ^5-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-88]]

Do not teach the Dharma to those who are disrespectful.
Do not teach those who, unless they are ill, wear turbans,
Carry parasols, staffs, or weapons,
Or have their heads covered. ^5-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-89]]

Do not teach the profound and vast to those of lesser inclination,
Nor to a woman without a man present.
Toward both the lesser and the supreme teachings,
One should always act with equal respect. ^5-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-90]]

One who has become a vessel for the vast Dharma
Should not be applied to the lesser Dharma.
One must not completely forsake proper conduct,
Nor deceive others with Sutra or Mantra. ^5-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-91]]

When discarding tooth-sticks or spitting,
One should cover them.
It is also criticized to discharge urine and so forth
Into water or onto land intended for use. ^5-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-92]]

Do not eat with a full mouth, with noise,
Or with the mouth wide open.
Do not sit with legs stretched out,
And do not rub your hands together. ^5-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-93]]

In vehicles, on beds, or in dwellings,
Do not remain alone with another's wife.
Having observed and inquired,
Avoid everything that causes the world to lose faith. ^5-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-94]]

One should not make gestures with the fingers;
Rather, with a gesture of respect,
One should use the entire right hand
To indicate the way in that manner. ^5-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-95]]

Do not wave your arms excessively;
Move them only slightly and make a sound,
Such as snapping your fingers, to give a signal;
Otherwise, you will be acting without restraint. ^5-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-96]]

Just as the Protector lay when passing into nirvana,
One should lie down in the desired direction;
From the very beginning, firmly apply the intention
To wake up quickly with alertness. ^5-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-97]]

Although the conduct of Bodhisattvas
Is said to be immeasurable,
One should certainly practice those activities
That serve to train the mind. ^5-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-98]]

Three times during the day and three times at night,
One should recite the Three Heaps Sūtra;
By relying on the Victors and the spirit of awakening,
One should pacify the remaining downfalls. ^5-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-99]]

Whether by one's own power or the influence of others,
In whatever situation or whatever one is doing,
One should diligently train in whatever
Has been taught as the training for that occasion. ^5-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-100]]

There is nothing whatsoever
That the Sons of the Victors should not learn;
For the wise who abide in this way,
There is no merit that does not accrue. ^5-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-101]]

Whether directly or indirectly,
Do nothing other than act for the benefit of sentient beings;
Solely for the sake of sentient beings,
Dedicate everything toward enlightenment. ^5-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-102]]

One should never forsake a spiritual friend
Who is learned in the meaning of the Mahāyāna
And possesses the supreme conduct of a Bodhisattva,
Even at the cost of one's own life. ^5-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-103]]

Learn the manner of relying on a guru
As described in the life story of Śrīsambhava;
This and other teachings of the Buddha
Are to be understood by reading the sūtras. ^5-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-104]]

The trainings are revealed within the sūtras,
Therefore, one should read the sūtras;
The Ākāśagarbha Sūtra, in particular,
Should be looked at first of all. ^5-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-105]]

Because it extensively demonstrates
What must be practiced at all times,
The Śikṣāsamuccaya as well
Should certainly be looked at again and again. ^5-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-106]]

Alternatively, for the time being, one should look at the concise Compendium of Sūtras;
Both works composed by the noble Nāgārjuna should also be studied with diligence. ^5-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-107]]

Whatever is not prohibited therein
Is exactly what should be practiced;
In order to guard the minds of worldly beings,
Having seen the trainings, one should practice them correctly. ^5-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-108]]

Regarding the state of one's body and mind,
One should examine them again and again;
This alone, in brief,
Is the definition of guarding introspection. ^5-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-109]]

These must be practiced with the body;
What can be accomplished by merely speaking words?
Will a sick person be benefited
By merely reading a medical treatise? ^5-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-a]]

This concludes the fifth chapter, "Guarding Introspection," from the *Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva*. ^5-a

## The Perfection of Patience ^6-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-1]]

Whatever virtuous deeds have been accumulated,
Such as generosity and offerings to the Sugatas,
Over a thousand eons,
A single moment of anger destroys them all. ^6-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-2]]

There is no evil like hatred,
And no austerity like patience.
Therefore, one should diligently practice patience
In various ways. ^6-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-3]]

If one harbors a mind of stinging hatred,
The mind will not experience the taste of peace;
One will find neither joy nor happiness,
Will not sleep, and will become unstable. ^6-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-4]]

Even those who depend on a master's kindness,
Having been provided for with wealth and honors,
Will seek to kill that master
If he is possessed by anger. ^6-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-5]]

Because of that, his friends will become weary of him;
Even if gathered through generosity, they will not remain.
In short, there is no one at all
Who dwells in happiness while possessed by anger. ^6-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-6]]

The enemy of anger produces these
And many other such sufferings.
Those who strive to conquer anger
Will find happiness in this life and the next. ^6-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-7]]

Finding its fuel in the mental unease
Caused by the occurrence of the unwanted
And the obstruction of what is desired,
Hatred flourishes and then destroys me. ^6-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-8]]

Therefore, I shall utterly destroy
The sustenance of that enemy;
For this enemy has no function
Other than to cause me harm. ^6-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-9]]

No matter what befalls me,
I shall not allow my joyful mind to be disturbed;
For even if I become unhappy, my desires will not be fulfilled,
And my virtues will decline. ^6-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-10]]

If there is a remedy for it,
Why be unhappy about it?
If there is no remedy for it,
What is the use of being unhappy? ^6-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-11]]

For myself or for my friends,
I do not desire suffering, contempt, harsh words,
Or unpleasantness;
But for an enemy, it is the opposite. ^6-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-12]]

The causes of happiness occur only occasionally,
While the causes of suffering are extremely numerous.
Without suffering, there is no renunciation;
Therefore, O mind, remain steadfast. ^6-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-13]]

The followers of Durgā and the people of Karṇapa
Endure the sensations of being burned, cut, and so forth;
Why then am I so cowardly,
When my goal is liberation? ^6-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-14]]

There is nothing whatsoever
That does not become easy through habituation.
Therefore, by habituating yourself to minor harms,
Learn to endure great adversity. ^6-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-15]]

Why do you not see the meaningless suffering
Caused by snakes, gadflies, and the like,
By sensations such as hunger and thirst,
And by afflictions such as skin rashes? ^6-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-16]]

Against heat, cold, rain, wind, and the like,
And against sickness, bondage, beatings, and so forth,
I shall not be sensitive or fragile;
For if I am, the harm will only increase. ^6-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-17]]

Some, when they see their own blood,
Become even more exceptionally brave and steadfast;
Others, when they see the blood of another,
Become dazed and faint. ^6-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-18]]

This arises from whether the mind is steady
Or whether it has fallen into a state of cowardice;
Therefore, disregard all harm,
And remain unassailable by sufferings. ^6-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-19]]

Even when suffering arises,
The wise should not allow their mind to be disturbed.
For in the battle against the afflictions,
There is much harm during the fight. ^6-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-20]]

Disregarding all suffering,
To conquer the enemies of hatred and the like—
These are the victorious heroes;
The rest are merely slaying corpses. ^6-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-21]]

Furthermore, the qualities of suffering are:
Through weariness, it dispels arrogance,
Compassion arises for those in cyclic existence,
One avoids misdeeds and delights in virtue. ^6-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-22]]

Since one does not feel anger toward the great sources of suffering
Such as bile and the like,
Why be angry with sentient beings?
They too are all impelled by conditions. ^6-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-23]]

For example, just as this illness arises
Even though it is not desired,
So too do the afflictions forcibly arise
Even though they are not desired. ^6-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-24]]

Without thinking, "I shall be angry,"
People spontaneously become angry;
Without thinking, "I shall arise,"
Anger likewise arises. ^6-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-25]]

All faults, as many as there are,
And various kinds of misdeeds,
All arise through the power of conditions;
They do not possess any independence. ^6-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-26]]

Even those assembled conditions
Have no intention to produce anything;
And that which is produced by them
Has no intention, "I am being produced." ^6-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-27]]

That which is desired as the "Primal Substance"
And that which is imputed as the "Self,"
Do not arise by intentionally thinking,
"I shall come into being." ^6-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-28]]

If it does not exist because it has not been born,
Then at that time, what could have the desire to be born?
Since it is always distracted by sense objects,
It is also not something that could cease. ^6-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-29]]

If that self were permanent,
It would clearly be inactive, like space.
Even if it were to encounter other conditions,
What could they do to something that is unchanging? ^6-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-30]]

If it remains as it was before even at the time of acting,
Then what has the action done to it?
What kind of connection could there be
Such that one could say, "This is its action"? ^6-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-31]]

In this way, everything is dependent on other factors,
And because of those factors, it lacks independence.
Knowing this, one will not become angry
At any phenomena, which are like magical illusions. ^6-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-32]]

If you ask, "Who is there to prevent what?
Even the act of prevention is not reasonable,"
It is not unreasonable to desire the cessation
Of sufferings that arise in dependence upon that. ^6-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-33]]

Therefore, whether it be an enemy or a friend,
Should you see them acting improperly,
Reflect that "This has arisen from such conditions,"
And in that way, remain at ease. ^6-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-34]]

If things were accomplished by one's own independent will,
Then since no one desires to suffer,
Suffering would never arise
For any embodied being at all. ^6-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-35]]

Through heedlessness, people even harm themselves
With thorns and the like;
For the sake of obtaining women and other such objects,
They are consumed by craving and engage in fasting and the like. ^6-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-36]]

Some hang themselves or leap from cliffs,
Consume poison or unwholesome food,
And through non-meritorious conduct,
They inflict harm upon themselves. ^6-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-37]]

When they are under the power of afflictions,
They even kill their own beloved selves;
At that time, how could they possibly
Refrain from harming the bodies of others? ^6-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-38]]

Since they are driven by the arising of afflictions
To engage in such acts as killing themselves,
If compassion does not arise for them,
What is the point of becoming angry? ^6-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-39]]

If it is the nature of the childish
To cause harm to others,
It is inappropriate to be angry with them;
It is like resenting fire for its nature to burn. ^6-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-40]]

Alternatively, if this fault is adventitious
And sentient beings are gentle by nature,
It is still inappropriate to be angry,
Just as it is to resent the sky for being filled with smoke. ^6-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-41]]

If I am angry at the one who throws it
Because they directly employed a stick or other weapon,
Then since they too were incited by hatred,
It is more appropriate to be angry at that hatred. ^6-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-42]]

In the past, I myself inflicted
Such harm upon sentient beings;
Therefore, it is only right that this harm
Should befall me, who caused injury to others. ^6-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-43]]

Both his weapon and my body
Are the causes of this suffering.
Since he has drawn the weapon and I have provided the body,
At whom should I be angry? ^6-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-44]]

This body is like a human-shaped abscess,
Painful and unable to bear being touched.
Since I, blinded by craving, have grasped it,
At whom should I be angry when it is harmed? ^6-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-45]]

Childish beings do not desire suffering,
Yet they crave the causes of suffering.
Since they are harmed by their own faults,
Why should they be resentful toward others? ^6-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-46]]

For example, just as the guardians of hell
And the forest of sword-leaves are produced
By one's own karma,
At whom should I be angry? ^6-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-47]]

Impelled by my own past actions,
Those who harm me have arisen;
If by this they go to the hells,
Have I not been the one to ruin them? ^6-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-48]]

By relying on these people,
My many sins are purified through patience;
Yet by relying on me,
They go to the hells of long-lasting suffering. ^6-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-49]]

I am the one who causes them harm,
And they are the ones who benefit me;
Why, then, O wicked mind,
Do you perversely become angry? ^6-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-50]]

If I possess the virtue of a patient intention,
I shall not go to the hells;
If I protect myself,
What harm has been done to them in this? ^6-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-51]]

However, if I retaliate with harm,
They will not be protected,
And my own conduct will also be ruined;
Thus, my ascetic practice will be destroyed. ^6-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-52]]

Since the mind is not a physical entity,
No one can destroy it anywhere;
Because of intense attachment to the body,
The body is afflicted by sufferings. ^6-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-53]]

If contempt, harsh speech,
And unpleasant words
Do not cause any harm to the body,
Why, O mind, do you become so angry? ^6-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-54]]

If the dislike that others have for me
Does not consume me in this life or in other lives,
Why should I be averse to it? ^6-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-55]]

If I am averse to this because it creates obstacles to my gain,
My gains will be discarded in this world,
But my sins will remain firm. ^6-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-56]]

It is better that I die this very day
Than to live a long life through wrong livelihood;
Even if I were to remain for a long time,
The suffering of death will be exactly the same. ^6-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-57]]

Suppose one person experiences happiness for a hundred years in a dream and then wakes up,
And another experiences happiness for just a brief moment and then wakes up; ^6-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-58]]

For both of those who have awakened,
That happiness never returns.
Whether one’s life is long or short,
At the time of death, it is exactly the same. ^6-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-59]]

Even if one acquires great wealth
And enjoys happiness for a long time,
Just like someone plundered by thieves,
One will depart naked and empty-handed. ^6-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-60]]

If you think, "I shall live on my gains
And, while doing so, exhaust my sins and perform merit,"
Yet you become angry for the sake of that gain,
Will your merit not be exhausted and will you not incur sin? ^6-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-61]]

If the very purpose for which I live
Is itself undermined,
What is the use of a life
That consists only of committing sins? ^6-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-62]]

If you say you are angry at those who speak unpleasantly
Because they harm sentient beings,
Then why are you not likewise angry
When others are spoken of unpleasantly? ^6-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-63]]

Since a lack of faith depends on others,
If you can tolerate those who have no faith,
Then since the arising of afflictions depends on others,
Why can you not tolerate those who speak unpleasantly? ^6-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-64]]

Toward those who revile and destroy
Sacred images, stupas, and the sublime Dharma,
It is not appropriate for me to feel hatred,
For the Buddhas and the like cannot be harmed. ^6-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-65]]

Toward those who cause harm to my
Gurus, relatives, and friends,
I should see that these events arise from conditions,
As previously explained, and thus restrain my anger. ^6-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-66]]

Since harm is inflicted upon embodied beings
By both those with minds and those without,
Why single out and bear a grudge against those with minds?
Therefore, you should endure all harm. ^6-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-67]]

Some commit misdeeds out of delusion;
If others, being deluded, become angry,
Who among them is without fault,
And who is the one who is at fault? ^6-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-68]]

Why did I previously commit those actions
By which others now cause me harm?
Since everything is dependent upon karma,
Why should I be angry with this? ^6-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-69]]

Having seen this, I shall strive in every way
To gather merit, so that all beings
May come to have a mind of mutual love
For one another. ^6-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-70]]

For example, when a fire that has broken out in one house
Moves to another house,
One should pull out and discard the straw
Or whatever else causes it to spread. ^6-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-71]]

Likewise, when the mind becomes attached to something
And the fire of hatred begins to spread,
One should discard that immediately
Out of fear that one's merit will be consumed. ^6-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-72]]

If a man condemned to death is freed
By having a hand cut off, why is that not excellent?
If, through human suffering,
One is spared from hell, why is that not excellent? ^6-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-73]]

If I am unable to endure even this much present suffering,
Then why do I not turn away from anger,
Which is the cause of the sufferings of hell? ^6-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-74]]

For the sake of my desires, I have experienced
Being burned and so forth in the hells thousands of times,
Yet I have accomplished neither my own benefit
Nor the benefit of others. ^6-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-75]]

Since this is not harmful to that extent,
And great purposes will also be accomplished,
It is right to take only joy in this suffering
That dispels the harm of all migratory beings. ^6-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-76]]

If others find the happiness of joy
By praising those who possess good qualities,
Why, O mind, do you not also praise them
And likewise rejoice in that way? ^6-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-77]]

This happiness of your rejoicing
Is a source of bliss that is blameless;
It is permitted by those endowed with excellence,
And is the supreme way of attracting others. ^6-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-78]]

If you do not desire this happiness
Thinking, "In that way, the other person would be happy,"
Then by ceasing to give wages and the like,
You will be ruined in both this life and the next. ^6-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-79]]

When your own virtues are being praised,
You even wish for others to be happy;
But when the virtues of others are being praised,
You do not even wish for your own happiness. ^6-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-80]]

Since you have generated the mind of enlightenment
Out of a desire for all sentient beings to be happy,
Why do you become angry
When sentient beings find happiness for themselves? ^6-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-81]]

If you desire for sentient beings to attain Buddhahood,
Which is honored by the three worlds,
Why are you distressed when you see them
Receive even a meager amount of honor and respect? ^6-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-82]]

Those whom you should support are your own dependents,
To whom you yourself should be giving;
If they find a livelihood through a relative,
Would you not be glad instead of becoming angry? ^6-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-83]]

If you do not wish for them to have even this,
How can you wish for them to attain enlightenment?
Where is the bodhicitta in one
Who becomes angry at the prosperity of others? ^6-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-84]]

Whether that person receives it from another,
Or it remains in the benefactor's house,
In either case, it is not yours;
So what does it matter to you whether it is given or not? ^6-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-85]]

Why do you discard your own merit, faith, and good qualities?
Tell me, why are you not angry at yourself
For failing to grasp the gains that have come? ^6-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-86]]

Not only do you feel no remorse
For the sins you yourself have committed,
But do you actually wish to compete
With others who have performed meritorious deeds?
Second Fascicle. ^6-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-87]]

Even if an enemy is unhappy,
What reason is there for you to rejoice in that?
Your mere wishful thinking
Will not become a cause of harm to them. ^6-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-88]]

Even if that suffering were to be accomplished
Through your desire, what reason is there for you to rejoice?
If you think you will be satisfied by this,
What could be more ruinous than that? ^6-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-89]]

This hook cast by the fisherman of the afflictions
Is an unbearable barb;
Having been caught by it, you will surely be boiled
In the cauldrons of hell by the hell-guardians. ^6-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-90]]

The honors of praise and fame
Do not become merit, nor do they become long life;
They do not become strength, nor are they health,
And they do not even bring physical comfort. ^6-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-91]]

If I were one who understood my own best interests,
What benefit for myself would I find in those things?
If it is mere mental pleasure that I seek,
I might as well devote myself to gambling, alcohol, and the like. ^6-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-92]]

If, for the sake of fame, I squander my wealth
And even bring about my own death,
What use are these mere syllables?
Once dead, who is it that will experience pleasure from them? ^6-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-93]]

When their sandcastles crumble,
Children weep in despair;
Likewise, when praise and fame decline,
My own mind is just like a child's. ^6-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-94]]

Since sound is inanimate,
It cannot possibly have the intention to praise me.
If it is said that my joy comes from the fact that others are pleased,
Perhaps that is the cause of my joy? ^6-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-95]]

Whether that pleasure is directed toward someone else or toward myself,
What benefit is another's joy to me?
That joy and happiness belong only to that person;
I do not obtain even a fraction of it. ^6-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-96]]

If I am made happy by their happiness,
Then I should feel the same way toward everyone.
If I am happy because others are pleased,
Why am I not happy when they find joy in others? ^6-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-97]]

Therefore, the joy that arises
From thinking, "I am being praised,"
Is likewise unreasonable,
And is nothing but the behavior of a child. ^6-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-98]]

Praise and the like distract me;
They also destroy my sense of disillusionment.
They cause envy toward those with good qualities,
And they destroy my excellence as well. ^6-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-99]]

Therefore, are those who are present
To destroy my praise and the like
Not actually engaged in protecting me
From falling into the lower realms? ^6-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-100]]

For me, who seeks liberation,
I do not need the shackles of gain and honor.
How could I be angry with those
Who act to release me from my bonds? ^6-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-101]]

For me, who wishes to enter into suffering,
They are like a blessing from the Buddha;
They have become the very door that prevents my fall,
So how could I be angry with them? ^6-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-102]]

It is also not right to be angry with them,
Thinking, "This person is an obstacle to my merit."
Since there is no austerity equal to patience,
Shall I not abide in that? ^6-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-103]]

If, due to my own faults,
I do not practice patience toward this person,
I am merely creating an obstacle for myself
Regarding this cause of merit that has arrived. ^6-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-104]]

Since that which does not occur in the absence of something,
But does occur when that thing is present,
Is defined as its cause,
How can it be called an obstacle? ^6-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-105]]

A beggar who arrives at the proper time
Is not an obstacle to generosity;
And one who enables the taking of vows
Cannot be called an obstacle to ordination. ^6-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-106]]

In this world, beggars are many,
But those who cause harm are rare;
For if I do not cause harm to others,
No one will cause harm to me. ^6-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-107]]

Therefore, like a treasure appearing in one's home
Without being sought through wearisome toil,
Because he becomes a helper in my bodhisattva conduct,
I should rejoice in my enemy. ^6-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-108]]

Since it was accomplished by both him and me,
The fruit of this patience
Should be offered to him first,
For in this way, he is the cause of patience. ^6-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-109]]

If you say that this enemy should not be honored
Because they have no intention for me to practice patience,
Then why honor the sublime Dharma,
Which is also a fit cause for accomplishment but lacks intention? ^6-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-110]]

If you say that this enemy should not be honored
Because they have the intention to cause harm,
Then if they were to strive for my benefit like a physician,
How could my patience ever be achieved? ^6-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-111]]

Therefore, since patience arises
In dependence upon a mind of intense malice,
That very person is the cause of patience,
And is as worthy of honor as the sublime Dharma. ^6-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-112]]

For this reason, the Sage has spoken of the "field of sentient beings"
And the "field of the Victors."
Many who have pleased them have,
In this way, reached the perfection of excellence. ^6-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-113]]

Since the attainment of the Buddha's qualities
Depends equally upon sentient beings and the Victors,
Why is it that you do not show the same respect
To sentient beings as you do to the Victors? ^6-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-114]]

Their excellence is not based on their intention, but on the result;
In that same way,
Sentient beings also possess qualities,
And for that reason, they are equal. ^6-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-115]]

Whatever offerings are made to one who possesses a loving mind,
That is the very greatness of sentient beings.
Whatever merit comes from faith in the Buddhas,
That too is the very greatness of the Buddhas. ^6-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-116]]

Since they possess a share in accomplishing the qualities of a Buddha,
They are therefore asserted to be equal.
Yet no one is equal to the Buddhas,
Who are infinite oceans of excellence. ^6-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-117]]

If even a mere fraction of the qualities belonging to that unique collection of supreme excellence
Appears in someone,
Then offering the entire threefold world to honor them
Would still be too little. ^6-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-118]]

Since sentient beings possess a share in the arising of the supreme Buddha-Dharma,
It is reasonable, based on this mere correspondence,
To consider sentient beings as objects of worship. ^6-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-119]]

Furthermore, for those who have become my unwavering friends
And who perform immeasurable benefit,
What other way is there to repay them
Than by pleasing sentient beings? ^6-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-120]]

Since those who sacrifice their bodies and enter the Avīci hell
Are repaid by benefiting these beings,
Therefore, even if they cause me great harm,
I shall act toward them all with nothing but goodness. ^6-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-121]]

Since those who have become my masters do not even regard their own bodies,
How could a deluded person like me
Act with pride toward them,
Instead of acting as their servant? ^6-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-122]]

Since the Sages are delighted by the happiness of beings,
And are displeased when they are harmed,
By making them happy, all the Sages are delighted,
And by harming them, one harms the Sages. ^6-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-123]]

Just as one whose body is completely ablaze
Cannot find any mental pleasure through sensual gratifications,
Likewise, when sentient beings are harmed,
There is no way for the Great Compassionate Ones to be pleased. ^6-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-124]]

Therefore, whatever harm I have done to beings,
Which has caused displeasure to all the Great Compassionate Ones,
I confess each and every one of those sins today;
I pray the Sages will forgive whatever has displeased them. ^6-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-125]]

In order to please the Tathāgatas,
From this day forth I shall remain a servant to the world.
Even if many beings should kick me or trample upon my head,
Or even kill me, I shall not retaliate; may the Protectors of the World be pleased. ^6-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-126]]

There is no doubt that those whose very nature is compassion
Have taken all these beings as their own selves.
Since these very beings are seen in the nature of the Protectors,
Why do I not show respect to them? ^6-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-127]]

This alone is what pleases the Tathāgatas,
This is also the perfect accomplishment of one's own welfare,
This alone dispels the suffering of the world;
Therefore, I shall always make this my practice. ^6-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-128]]

For example, even if some of a king's men
Should cause harm to many people,
Those who are far-sighted
Will not retaliate, even if they have the power to do so. ^6-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-129]]

This is because such a person is not acting alone;
The king's might is his power.
Likewise, one should not despise
Even a weak person who causes harm. ^6-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-130]]

For the guardians of hell and the compassionate ones are their allies.
Therefore, just as subjects placate a fierce king,
One should please all sentient beings. ^6-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-131]]

Even if a king were to become enraged,
Could he inflict the torments of hell,
Which are the consequences one must experience
By displeasing sentient beings? ^6-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-132]]

Even if a king were to be pleased,
He could not possibly bestow the state of Buddhahood,
Which is what is attained
By pleasing sentient beings. ^6-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-133]]

Not to mention the future attainment of Buddhahood,
Which arises from pleasing sentient beings,
Why do you not see that even in this very life,
Great glory, fame, and happiness are achieved? ^6-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-134]]

In the cycle of existence, through patience one attains beauty and the like,
As well as health and renown,
A very long life,
And the extensive happiness of a universal monarch. ^6-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-a]]

This concludes the sixth chapter, entitled "The Teaching on Patience," from the *Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva*. ^6-a

## The Perfection of Diligence ^7-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-1]]

Thus, with patience, one should initiate diligence,
For enlightenment dwells in those who strive in this way.
Just as there is no movement without wind,
Merit does not arise without diligence. ^7-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-2]]

What is diligence? It is delighting in the virtuous.
Its opposing factors should be explained:
Laziness, attachment to what is base,
And the despondency of self-contempt. ^7-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-3]]

Through savoring the taste of idle pleasure,
And the craving that relies on sleep,
One does not feel weariness for the suffering of cyclic existence;
From these, laziness fully arises. ^7-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-4]]

Having been caught by the hunters of the afflictions,
And having entered the snare of rebirth,
You have walked into the jaws of the Lord of Death;
How can you still not realize this? ^7-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-5]]

Have you not seen your own kind
Being killed one by one?
Yet you still rely on sleep,
Like a buffalo in the hands of a butcher. ^7-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-6]]

With all paths of escape completely blocked,
And while the Lord of Death is watching you,
How can you take pleasure in eating,
And how can you find such joy in sleeping? ^7-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-7]]

Since death will come very quickly,
For as long as you can, gather the accumulations.
Even if you abandon laziness at that time,
What can you do when it is no longer the right time? ^7-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-8]]

While thinking, "This is not yet done,"
And "This is only half-finished,"
The Lord of Death suddenly arrives;
"Alas! I am destroyed!" you will think. ^7-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-9]]

With eyes red and swollen from the force of grief,
Tears will stream down your face;
As your relatives lose all hope,
You will gaze upon the faces of the messengers of the Lord of Death. ^7-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-10]]

Tormented by the memory of your own sins,
And hearing the sounds of the hells,
You will soil your body in terror;
When you have become thus delirious, what will you do? ^7-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-11]]

If you are possessed by such fear in this life,
Like a live fish writhing on the ground,
What need is there to mention the unbearable
Sufferings of hell resulting from your sinful deeds? ^7-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-12]]

When touched by even warm water,
Your tender flesh is intensely scalded;
Having committed the actions that lead to hell,
How can you remain so at ease? ^7-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-13]]

Desiring results without exertion,
And being thin-skinned, you are beset by many harms;
Though seized by death, you act like an immortal;
Alas, you are destroyed by your own sufferings! ^7-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-14]]

Relying upon this boat of a human form,
Cross over the great river of suffering;
Since this boat will be difficult to find again later,
O foolish one, do not sleep at such a time! ^7-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-15]]

Having abandoned the supreme joy of the sublime Dharma,
Which is a boundless cause of delight,
Why do you delight in distractions,
Frivolity, and the like, which are the causes of suffering? ^7-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-16]]

Without despondency, with an army of forces,
With earnest application and self-mastery,
Practice the equality of self and others,
And practice the exchange of self and others. ^7-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-17]]

One should not be discouraged, thinking,
"How could I ever attain enlightenment?"
For the Tathāgata, the Speaker of Truth,
Has spoken this truth. ^7-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-18]]

Even those who have become flies, gadflies, bees,
Or likewise those who have become insects,
If they develop the power of diligence,
Will attain the supreme enlightenment so difficult to find. ^7-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-19]]

Since I have been born as a human with the right potential,
And recognize what is beneficial and what is harmful,
If I do not abandon the conduct of a Bodhisattva,
Why should I not attain enlightenment? ^7-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-20]]

If you are afraid, thinking that you must give away
Your limbs and so forth,
It is merely the fear of a confused person
Who has failed to analyze what is heavy and what is light. ^7-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-21]]

For countless tens of millions of eons,
You have been cut, pierced, burned, and split apart many times over,
Yet you have not attained enlightenment;
And this will not lead to the attainment of enlightenment. ^7-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-22]]

The suffering I endure to achieve enlightenment is finite;
It is like the pain of an incision made in the body
To remove and destroy the harm of a deeply embedded splinter. ^7-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-23]]

All physicians use medical treatments
To eliminate the discomfort of disease;
Therefore, to destroy a multitude of sufferings,
One should endure a minor discomfort. ^7-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-24]]

The Supreme Physician does not employ
Common medical treatments such as these;
Through extremely gentle procedures,
He heals immeasurable and grievous illnesses. ^7-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-25]]

The Guide first engages one in the giving of things such as vegetables;
Once accustomed to that, one later gradually gives even one's own flesh. ^7-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-26]]

When one has developed an attitude toward one's own body
As being like vegetables and the like,
Then what difficulty could there be
In giving away one's flesh and so forth? ^7-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-27]]

Because non-virtue is abandoned, there is no suffering;
Because of wisdom, there is no unhappiness.
In this way, it is through distorted conceptions
And non-virtue that the mind and body are harmed. ^7-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-28]]

If the body is at ease through merit,
And the mind is at ease through wisdom,
Then even while remaining in samsara for the sake of others,
Why would those with compassion ever be weary? ^7-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-29]]

Because this, through the power of the awakening mind,
Exhausts previous misdeeds
And gathers an ocean of merit,
It is explained as superior to the Hearers. ^7-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-30]]

Therefore, having mounted the horse of the awakening mind,
Which dispels all weariness and fatigue,
As one proceeds from joy to joy,
What person of intelligence would ever succumb to despondency? ^7-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-31]]

To accomplish the welfare of sentient beings, one's forces are aspiration, steadfastness, joy, and relinquishment.
Aspiration is generated by fearing suffering and contemplating its benefits. ^7-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-32]]

Thus, having abandoned the opposing factors,
One should strive to increase diligence through the powers of aspiration, pride, joy, relinquishment,
Earnest application, and mastery. ^7-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-33]]

I must eliminate the immeasurable faults of both myself and others;
Since for each and every one of these faults,
It would take an ocean of eons to exhaust them. ^7-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-34]]

If I do not perceive in myself even a fraction of the effort
Needed to begin exhausting these faults,
Then as a vessel of immeasurable suffering,
Why does my heart not burst? ^7-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-35]]

I must accomplish many virtues for both myself and others;
Yet if even for a single virtue,
One must train for an ocean of eons, ^7-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-36]]

I have never developed the habit of practicing even a fraction of such virtues.
It is astonishing that I have rendered this birth,
Which was somehow obtained, so meaningless. ^7-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-37]]

I have not made offerings to the Blessed One,
Nor have I provided the joy of great festivals;
I have not performed service for the Teachings,
Nor have I fulfilled the hopes of the poor. ^7-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-38]]

I have not granted fearlessness to the frightened,
Nor have I given comfort to the distressed;
I have merely produced the pain and suffering
Of the womb of my mother. ^7-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-39]]

Because I lacked devotion to the Dharma
In the past and even now,
I have come to such a state of destitution;
Who would ever abandon devotion to the Dharma? ^7-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-40]]

The Sage has taught that aspiration is the root of all virtuous qualities;
Its own root, in turn, is constant meditation upon the results of karmic ripening. ^7-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-41]]

Physical suffering, mental unhappiness,
Various kinds of fear,
And separation from what is desired—
All these arise from the practice of misdeeds. ^7-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-42]]

By performing the virtues intended by the mind,
Wherever one may go,
In that very place one will be honored
By the rewards that are the fruits of that merit. ^7-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-43]]

Although those who commit sins desire happiness,
Wherever they may go,
Those very sins will, in that place,
Utterly crush them with the weapons of suffering. ^7-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-44]]

Abiding within the heart of a vast, fragrant, and cool lotus,
Their splendor nourished by the food of the Victorious One's melodious speech,
Possessing a supreme body born from a lotus blossomed by the Sage's light,
Through their virtue they become a Son of the Sugata dwelling in the presence of the Victorious One. ^7-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-45]]

With their skin entirely flayed by the henchmen of Yama, they are utterly tormented,
And molten copper, melted by a searing fire, is poured over their bodies;
Pierced by flaming swords and spears until their flesh is torn into hundreds of pieces,
They fall upon the intensely blazing iron ground as a result of their many non-virtues. ^7-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-46]]

Therefore, one should aspire to virtue,
And cultivate it with genuine respect.
Having undertaken the rite of the Vajradhvaja,
One should cultivate the pride of self-confidence. ^7-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-47]]

First, examine your resources,
And then decide whether to begin or not.
It is best not to begin at all,
But once begun, one should not turn back. ^7-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-48]]

By becoming habituated to this in other lives,
Sin and suffering will increase.
Furthermore, the time for the result will be inferior,
And even that goal will not be achieved. ^7-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-49]]

One should apply pride to these three:
Actions, afflictions, and ability.
Thinking, "I alone shall do it,"
This is the pride of action. ^7-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-50]]

Since this world, under the power of afflictions,
Is unable to accomplish its own welfare,
Migratory beings are not capable like me;
Therefore, I shall do this. ^7-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-51]]

If others perform inferior tasks,
How can I remain idle?
I do not act out of pride;
For me, to be without pride is supreme. ^7-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-52]]

When encountering a dying snake,
Even a crow acts like a Garuda.
If I am weak and feeble,
Even a minor downfall will cause me harm. ^7-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-53]]

For one who is despondent and has abandoned effort,
How could there be liberation from misfortune?
By generating the pride of exertion,
Even the great will find it difficult to overcome you. ^7-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-54]]

Therefore, with a steadfast mind,
I shall overcome all downfalls;
For if I am defeated by a downfall,
My wish to conquer the three realms will become a mockery. ^7-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-55]]

I shall triumph over everything;
None shall triumph over me.
As a son of the Lion-like Conqueror,
I shall abide in this pride. ^7-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-56]]

Those beings who are overcome by pride are afflicted;
They do not possess the pride of which I speak.
The truly proud do not fall under the power of the enemy,
Whereas those others are under the power of the enemy of pride. ^7-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-57]]

Inflated by the pride of the afflictions,
They are led by that pride to the lower realms;
Their human joy is destroyed, and
They become slaves eating the food of others, ^7-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-58]]

The foolish, the ugly, and the weak,
Are held in contempt everywhere;
If even those ascetics who are puffed up with pride
Are counted among the conceited,
Then tell me, how much more pathetic are the lowly? ^7-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-59]]

Whoever maintains pride in order to conquer the enemy of pride
Is the truly proud one, the victorious hero;
For they have certainly vanquished the burgeoning enemy of pride,
And as they wish, they bring the fruit of the Conquerors to perfection for all beings. ^7-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-60]]

If I must dwell in the midst of the faction of afflictions,
I shall endure in a thousand ways;
Like a lion among foxes and the like,
I shall not be overcome by the host of afflictions. ^7-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-61]]

Even if great danger should arise,
Just as a person protects their eyes,
Likewise, even when peril occurs,
I shall not fall under the power of the afflictions. ^7-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-62]]

Just as one desires the joyful results of play,
One should be attached to whatever task is at hand;
One should find delight in that work,
And never be satiated by it. ^7-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-63]]

Although actions are performed for the sake of happiness,
It is uncertain whether happiness will actually result;
But for one whose very work is happiness,
How could they be happy without doing that work? ^7-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-64]]

If one is never sated by sensual desires,
Which are like honey on the edge of a razor,
Then how could one ever be sated by merit,
Which ripens into peace and happiness? ^7-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-65]]

Therefore, in order to bring the task to completion,
One should engage in that work
Just as an elephant, tormented by the midday sun,
Plunges into a lake upon encountering it. ^7-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-66]]

If one's strength declines,
One should set it aside in order to resume it later;
When a task is well-accomplished, one should leave it behind,
Desiring the next and the next. ^7-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-67]]

Just as a veteran warrior in the heat of battle
Faces the clash of an enemy's sword,
One should dodge the weapons of the afflictions
And rise up to defeat the enemy afflictions. ^7-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-68]]

Just as one would swiftly pick up a sword
Dropped in battle out of fear,
So too, if the weapon of mindfulness is lost,
One should quickly retrieve it by remembering the terrors of hell. ^7-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-69]]

Just as poison spreads through the body
By depending on the blood,
So too will a fault pervade the mind
If it finds an opportunity. ^7-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-70]]

Just as one carrying a vessel filled with mustard oil,
Followed by a man with a drawn sword threatening to kill him,
Is terrified of spilling it, so should the practitioner be focused. ^7-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-71]]

Therefore, just as one would swiftly spring up
If a snake crawled into one's lap,
So too, when sleep and lethargy approach,
One should quickly repel them. ^7-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-72]]

For every fault that occurs,
I shall reproach myself and reflect for a long time,
"By whatever means necessary,
I must ensure that this does not happen to me again." ^7-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-73]]

Thinking, "How can I become habituated to mindfulness
In these various circumstances?"
Through this cause, I shall desire to meet with teachers
Or to perform the appropriate tasks. ^7-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-74]]

In order to have the capacity for all activities
Before starting any work,
I should recall the discourse on heedfulness
And make myself rise with alacrity. ^7-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-75]]

Just as a tuft of cotton is moved to and fro
By the coming and going of the wind,
So should I be governed by enthusiasm;
In that way, success will be achieved. ^7-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-a]]

From the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*, this concludes the seventh chapter, titled "The Explanation of Diligence." ^7-a

## Chapter Eight: The Exposition of Meditative Concentration ^8-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-1]]

Having thus generated diligence,
One should place the mind in meditative concentration.
A person whose mind is distracted
Dwells between the fangs of the mental afflictions. ^8-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-2]]

Through solitude of body and mind,
Distractions will not arise.
Therefore, one should renounce the world
And completely cast aside discursive thoughts. ^8-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-3]]

Because of attachment and craving for gain and the like,
One does not renounce the world.
Therefore, completely abandon these things,
And the wise should analyze in this way. ^8-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-4]]

Through special insight thoroughly conjoined with calm abiding,
One should understand that the mental afflictions are completely destroyed.
Therefore, one should first seek calm abiding;
This is achieved by those who find genuine delight in detachment from the world. ^8-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-5]]

Since all is impermanent, who that is impermanent
Would feel a true attachment to another?
Because of that, for thousands of lifetimes,
One will never again see the beloved. ^8-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-6]]

If they are not seen, there is no joy,
And the mind will not remain in meditative equipoise;
Even if they are seen, there is no satisfaction,
And one is tormented by craving just as before. ^8-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-7]]

If one is attached to sentient beings,
The perfect reality is completely obscured.
It destroys even a mind of disillusionment,
And in the end, one will be tormented by grief. ^8-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-8]]

By thinking only of them,
This life will pass away in vain.
Through impermanent friendships,
Even the eternal Dharma is destroyed. ^8-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-9]]

If one shares the same conduct as the foolish,
One will certainly go to the lower realms.
If they lead one to what is unwholesome,
What is the use of relying on the childish? ^8-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-10]]

In a single instant they become friends,
And in a mere moment they turn into enemies.
Because they react with anger toward things that should bring joy,
Ordinary beings are difficult to please. ^8-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-11]]

If one speaks for their benefit, they become angry,
And they even turn one away from what is helpful.
If their own words are not heeded,
They become enraged and will fall into the lower realms. ^8-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-12]]

They are jealous of superiors and competitive with equals;
They are arrogant toward inferiors and conceited when praised.
If something unpleasant is said, they become angry;
When could one ever receive benefit from a fool? ^8-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-13]]

If one associates with the childish, then from such childishness,
Self-praise, the disparagement of others,
And talk that delights in cyclic existence and the like,
All manner of non-virtue will certainly arise. ^8-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-14]]

Thus, relying on one another in such a way
Will result in nothing but ruin;
Since they do not accomplish my purpose,
And I will not be of benefit to them either. ^8-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-15]]

I shall flee far away from the childish.
If I encounter them, I shall please them with a joyful manner,
Yet without becoming overly intimate,
I shall maintain a polite and ordinary relationship. ^8-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-16]]

Like a bee taking nectar from a flower,
Take only that which is meaningful for the Dharma;
And remain without intimacy toward anyone,
As if you had never seen them before. ^8-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-17]]

If I harbor such pride, thinking,
"I have many gains and honors,
And many people are fond of me,"
Then fear will arise after death. ^8-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-18]]

Therefore, whatever it is that your deluded mind
Becomes attached to,
Those very things will combine to arise
As suffering, multiplied a thousandfold. ^8-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-19]]

Therefore, the wise should not form attachments,
For from attachment, fear is born.
Since these things are by nature subject to loss,
Remain steadfast and understand this well. ^8-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-20]]

Though one may acquire many gains,
And though fame and renown may arise,
Even with a collection of gain and fame,
There is no knowing where one will go. ^8-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-21]]

If there are others who disparage me,
What reason is there for me to be pleased by praise?
If there are others who praise me,
What reason is there for me to be displeased by disparagement? ^8-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-22]]

Since sentient beings have such diverse inclinations,
Even the Victors cannot satisfy them all;
What need is there to mention a wretched person like me?
Therefore, I shall abandon concern for the world's opinions. ^8-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-23]]

Sentient beings disparage those who have no possessions,
And speak ill of those who have them.
Since they are by nature so difficult to get along with,
How can any joy arise from associating with them? ^8-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-24]]

Because the childish find no pleasure
Unless their own interests are served,
The Tathāgatas have said that no childish person is a friend;
Thus have the Tathāgatas spoken. ^8-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-25]]

In the forest, the wild animals, the birds, and the trees
Do not speak unpleasant words.
When shall I come to dwell together
With those whose company is a delight? ^8-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-26]]

Having made my home in a cave, an empty temple, or at the foot of a tree,
When shall I come to be without attachment,
Never looking back,
And become free from all clinging? ^8-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-27]]

When shall I dwell in vast, naturally spacious regions
That are not claimed as "mine,"
Acting with total freedom
And without attachment? ^8-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-28]]

Possessing only a few items such as an alms bowl,
And wearing clothes that no one else desires,
When shall I come to dwell without fear,
Even without attempting to conceal this body? ^8-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-29]]

Having gone to a charnel ground,
When shall I come to treat as equal
My own body and the skeletal remains of others,
Seeing them both as having the nature of decay? ^8-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-30]]

This very body of mine
Will eventually become such that
Even jackals will not approach it
Because of its stench. ^8-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-31]]

Although this body arose as a single entity,
The flesh and bones that were born with it
Will disintegrate and separate;
What need is there to mention other loved ones? ^8-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-32]]

One is born alone,
And likewise, one dies alone;
Since others do not take a share of one's suffering,
What use are friends who only create obstacles? ^8-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-33]]

Just as travelers on a road
Take up a temporary lodging,
So too do those on the path of existence
Take up a place of birth. ^8-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-34]]

While the world is grieving all around,
As long as this body has not yet
Been carried away by four men,
In the meantime, I shall go to the forest. ^8-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-35]]

Having made myself free of friends and free of enemies,
This body shall dwell alone in a secluded place;
Regarding myself as already dead,
There will be no mourning even when I actually die. ^8-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-36]]

Since there is no one nearby
To cause the harm of mourning,
No one at all will distract me
From such practices as the recollection of the Buddha. ^8-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-37]]

Therefore, in a forest that is exceedingly delightful,
Free of hardship, blissful, and the pacifier
Of all distractions,
I shall dwell entirely alone. ^8-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-38]]

Abandoning all other thoughts,
With a mind focused only on myself,
I shall strive to settle the mind in meditative equipoise
And to bring it under discipline. ^8-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-39]]

In this world and in the next,
Desires produce ruin;
Here, they lead to being killed, bound, and mutilated,
And in the next, they bring about the hells and so forth. ^8-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-40]]

For whose sake you previously employed male and female messengers,
And for whose sake you did not shrink from even sin or infamy,
And for whose sake you disregarded everything, ^8-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-41]]

Entering into dangers and exhausting your wealth—
Those very things that you embrace with such supreme delight
Are nothing other than skeletons; ^8-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-42]]

If they are nothing but skeletons and nothing else,
And are neither independent nor a self,
Why, after desiring them intensely and becoming thoroughly attached,
Do you not go to the state beyond sorrow? ^8-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-43]]

That which you first exerted yourself to lift,
And which looked down bashfully even when drawn up—
Whether it was seen before or not,
That face was covered by a cloth. ^8-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-44]]

That face of your affliction,
Is now revealed just as it is;
Now that you see it being uncovered by the vultures,
Why do you flee from it? ^8-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-45]]

Even when others merely looked with their eyes,
You kept it thoroughly guarded;
While it is being devoured by them,
Why, O miser, do you not protect it? ^8-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-46]]

Seeing this heap of flesh being eaten by vultures and others,
Why do you honor the food of others with flower garlands,
Sandalwood, and ornaments? ^8-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-47]]

Seeing it as nothing but a heap of bones,
If you are terrified even when it is motionless,
Why are you not afraid when it is moved by someone,
Like a reanimated corpse? ^8-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-48]]

Since both excrement and saliva arise from the very same food,
Why do you dislike the excrement,
And yet, why do you delight in the saliva? ^8-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-49]]

Instead of taking delight in a pillow,
Which is soft to the touch like cotton,
The lustful are deluded regarding the impure,
Claiming, "It does not leak a foul odor." ^8-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-50]]

Since you are attached to this when it is covered,
Why do you not desire it when it is uncovered?
If you have no need for it then,
Why do you embrace it when it is covered? ^8-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-51]]

If you have no attachment to what is impure,
Why do you embrace in your lap another,
A cage of bones bound together by sinews,
And plastered over with the mud of flesh? ^8-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-52]]

You yourself contain plenty of filth,
And you constantly wallow in that;
Yet, out of a craving for the impure,
You also desire other bags of filth. ^8-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-53]]

If you say, "I am fond of this flesh,"
And desire to touch and look at it,
Then why do you not desire flesh
In its natural, insentient state? ^8-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-54]]

That mind which you desire
Cannot be touched or seen,
And that which can be touched is not consciousness;
What is the use of such a meaningless embrace? ^8-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-55]]

It is not so wonderful that you do not realize
The foul nature of another's body;
But that you do not realize your own very self
To be foul is truly a great wonder. ^8-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-56]]

Having abandoned the young lotus
Opened by the rays of a cloudless sun,
Why does your mind, with its attachment to the impure,
Take delight in a cage of filth? ^8-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-57]]

If you do not wish to touch a patch of ground
Because it is soiled with filth,
How can you wish to touch the body
From which that very filth arose? ^8-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-58]]

If you have no attachment to what is impure,
Then why do you embrace in your lap another
Who was produced by that seed
And arose from an impure field? ^8-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-59]]

You do not desire even a tiny worm
That arises from filth,
Yet you desire a body that is a mass of many impurities
And was likewise born from filth. ^8-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-60]]

Not only do you not feel revulsion
For your own impurity,
But out of a craving for filth,
You also desire other bags of impurity. ^8-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-61]]

Even pleasant things like camphor,
Or boiled rice and vegetables,
Once taken into the mouth and spat out,
Make even the ground foul and unclean. ^8-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-62]]

If you still have doubts about such impurity,
Even though it is plainly evident,
Then observe other impure bodies
Abandoned in the charnel grounds. ^8-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-63]]

If you know that great terror would arise
If the skin were peeled back,
Then how could you ever again
Find pleasure in that very same thing? ^8-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-64]]

Even the fragrance applied to the body
Is that of sandalwood and the like, not of anything else.
Why then do you become attached to one thing
Because of the fragrance of another? ^8-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-65]]

If it is naturally foul-smelling,
Is it not better to be unattached to it?
Why do those with meaningless worldly cravings
Anoint it with sweet scents? ^8-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-66]]

If the fragrance is that of sandalwood,
What part of it comes from the body?
Why then do you become attached to one thing
Because of the fragrance of another? ^8-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-67]]

If the body’s natural state—with its long hair and nails,
Yellowish teeth, and the stench of mud—
Is a terrifying sight when left naked,
Why do you take such pains to polish it? ^8-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-68]]

Why do you groom it so zealously,
As if it were a weapon that will only harm you?
Through the efforts of those deluded regarding the self,
The entire earth is thrown into turmoil by the mad. ^8-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-69]]

If you feel revulsion in a charnel ground
Upon seeing nothing but a heap of bones,
Why do you delight in the "city charnel ground,"
Which is filled with moving skeletons? ^8-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-70]]

That which has become so impure
Cannot be obtained for free;
By striving for its sake, one suffers exhaustion
And is harmed by the torments of hell and the like. ^8-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-71]]

Since a child is unable to increase wealth,
What happiness is there when one reaches one's prime?
If one's life is exhausted by the accumulation of wealth,
Once old, what is the use of sensual desires? ^8-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-72]]

Some wretched, lustful people,
Utterly exhausted by a long day's work,
Return home and lay their weary bodies
Down to sleep like corpses. ^8-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-73]]

Some are afflicted by the hardships of travel
And the suffering of going far away;
Though they long for their wives and children,
They do not see them for years on end. ^8-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-74]]

Deluded by the desire for their own benefit,
They sell themselves for the sake of profit;
Yet without attaining it, they are meaningless,
Driven by the winds of others' labor. ^8-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-75]]

Some sell their own bodies
And, powerless, are put to work by others;
Even when their wives give birth,
It happens in the wilderness or at the foot of a tree. ^8-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-76]]

Fools deceived by desire,
Thinking, "I must make a living,"
Enter into battle fearing the loss of life,
And for the sake of profit, they go into servitude. ^8-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-77]]

Some who are full of desire even have their bodies mutilated;
Some are impaled on the points of stakes;
Some are pierced by short spears;
And some are even seen being burned. ^8-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-78]]

Through the torments of accumulating, guarding, and losing,
One should understand that wealth is an endless source of ruin.
Those who are distracted by their attachment to wealth
Have no opportunity to be liberated from the suffering of cyclic existence. ^8-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-79]]

For those possessed by desire, such faults are many and the benefits are few;
It is like a beast of burden pulling a cart,
That eats only a few mouthfuls of grass. ^8-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-80]]

For the sake of such a small benefit, which is not even rare for a beast to achieve,
Those tormented by their karma destroy
This precious human life of leisure and endowment, so difficult to find. ^8-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-81]]

Desires will certainly perish and cause one to fall into the hells and other lower realms;
Because it is not for a great purpose, one undergoes constant wearisome toil. ^8-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-82]]

If Buddhahood could be accomplished with even a millionth part of such hardship,
Then for those possessed by desire, compared to the conduct of enlightenment,
There is great suffering but no enlightenment. ^8-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-83]]

If one considers the sufferings of the hells and so forth,
Even weapons, poison, fire, precipices,
And enemies cannot be compared to the objects of desire. ^8-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-84]]

Thus, having become weary of desires,
One should generate a delight in solitude,
Within peaceful forests
That are empty of strife and afflictions. ^8-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-85]]

On delightful, expansive stone slabs like palaces,
Cooled by the sandalwood-scented moonlight,
Fanned by the silent, peaceful forest breeze,
One walks about, contemplating the welfare of others. ^8-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-86]]

In empty houses, at the foot of trees, or in caves,
Staying wherever and for as long as one desires,
Having abandoned the suffering of guarding and possessing,
One lives in spacious relaxation, free from attachment. ^8-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-87]]

Acting with independence and without attachment,
Having no connection with anyone at all,
That joy of contentment, whatever it may be,
Is difficult even for Indra to find. ^8-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-88]]

By means of these and other such aspects,
Having reflected upon the virtues of solitude,
One should completely pacify discursive thoughts,
And meditate upon the awakening mind. ^8-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-89]]

One should first strive to meditate
On the equality of oneself and others;
Since all are equal in happiness and suffering,
One should protect everyone just as one does oneself. ^8-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-90]]

Just as the body, despite its many different parts like the hands and so forth,
Is protected as a single entity,
So too are all diverse beings, with their various joys and sorrows,
One in their equal desire for happiness, just as I am. ^8-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-91]]

If my own suffering
Does not cause harm to the bodies of others,
Nevertheless, because I cling to it as "mine,"
That suffering is unbearable to me. ^8-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-92]]

Likewise, although the suffering of others
Does not fall upon me,
Nevertheless, because of attachment to a self,
That suffering is difficult to bear. ^8-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-93]]

I should dispel the suffering of others
Because it is suffering, just like my own;
I should benefit others
Because they are sentient beings, just like my own body. ^8-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-94]]

Since both myself and others
Are equal in desiring happiness,
What is so special about me
That I strive for my own happiness alone? ^8-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-95]]

Since both myself and others
Are equal in not desiring suffering,
What is so special about me
That I protect myself and not others? ^8-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-96]]

If I do not protect them
Because their suffering does not harm me,
Then why protect against future suffering,
Since it does not harm me now? ^8-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-97]]

The conceptual thought that "I" will experience that
Is a mistaken conception,
For the one who dies is one,
And the one who is born is another. ^8-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-98]]

If the suffering of any particular person
Must be protected against by that person alone,
Then since the suffering of the foot is not that of the hand,
Why should the hand protect it? ^8-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-99]]

If you argue that even if it is illogical,
One acts in this way because of grasping at a self,
Then whatever is illogical regarding self and others
Is precisely what should be abandoned as much as possible. ^8-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-100]]

What are called "continua" and "assemblies"
Are false, like garlands, armies, and the like.
Since there is no one who possesses suffering,
Who could be the owner of this? ^8-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-101]]

Because suffering has no owner,
All instances of it are without distinction.
Since it is suffering, it must be dispelled;
What is the use of making restrictions here? ^8-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-102]]

"Why should the suffering of all be averted?"
Is not a point of contention.
If it is to be averted, then all of it must be averted;
If not, then I am just like other sentient beings. ^8-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-103]]

If you ask, "Since compassion brings about so much suffering,
Why should one strive to generate it?"
If one contemplates the suffering of the world,
How can the suffering of compassion be considered great? ^8-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-104]]

If by means of a single suffering,
A multitude of sufferings can be eliminated,
Then those endowed with compassion should certainly
Bring about that suffering for themselves and others. ^8-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-105]]

Therefore, although Supuṣpacandra
Knew of the harm the king would inflict,
He did not avert his own suffering,
So that the suffering of many might be exhausted. ^8-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-106]]

Thus, those whose mind-streams are habituated,
Finding joy in the pacification of others' suffering,
Will plunge even into the Avīci hell
Like swans into a lotus pond. ^8-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-107]]

When sentient beings are liberated,
What ocean of joy could be greater?
Is that not sufficient in itself?
What is the use of desiring liberation for oneself? ^8-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-108]]

Therefore, even when acting for the benefit of others,
There is no conceit or sense of wonder;
Because one is single-mindedly devoted to the welfare of others,
There is no hope for a ripening result. ^8-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-109]]

Therefore, just as I protect myself
From even the slightest unpleasantness,
I should likewise cultivate a protective mind
And a heart of compassion toward others. ^8-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-110]]

Through the power of habituation,
One comes to perceive the drops
Of another's semen and blood as "I,"
Even though they are not inherently one's own. ^8-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-111]]

In the same way, why should the bodies of others
Not also be held as "I"?
It is not difficult to establish
My own body as being other. ^8-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-112]]

Having recognized oneself as being full of faults
And others as an ocean of virtues,
One should practice completely discarding self-grasping
And adopting others. ^8-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-113]]

Just as the hands and other parts
Are accepted as limbs of the body,
Why are all embodied beings
Not accepted as limbs of the world? ^8-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-114]]

Just as the thought of "I" has arisen
Toward this selfless body through habituation,
Why should the thought of "I" not likewise
Arise toward other sentient beings through habituation? ^8-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-115]]

Therefore, when acting for the benefit of others,
No sense of conceit or wonderment will arise,
Just as when one has eaten food oneself,
No expectation of a reward arises. ^8-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-116]]

Therefore, just as I protect myself
From even the slightest unpleasantness,
I should likewise cultivate a protective mind
And a mind of compassion toward the world. ^8-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-117]]

Therefore, the Protector Avalokiteśvara,
Out of great compassion,
In order to dispel the fears of beings in cyclic existence,
Blessed even his own name. ^8-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-118]]

One should not turn back from hardships;
For through the power of such habituation,
Even that whose very name once caused fear,
Becomes something one is unhappy to be without. ^8-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-119]]

Whoever wishes to quickly provide protection
For both themselves and others,
Should practice the supreme secret:
The exchanging of oneself and others. ^8-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-120]]

Because of attachment to this body of mine,
Fear arises even toward small things that cause alarm;
Who would not detest as an enemy
This body that gives rise to such fear? ^8-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-121]]

Desiring to perform the rituals to heal
The body’s ailments, such as hunger and thirst,
People kill birds, fish, and deer,
Or lie in wait along the paths. ^8-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-122]]

If those who, for the sake of profit and honor,
Kill even their own fathers and mothers,
And steal the property of the Three Jewels,
Will be burned in the Incessant Hell, ^8-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-123]]

What wise person would desire,
Protect, and make offerings to such a body?
Who would not view it as an enemy,
And treat it with contempt? ^8-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-124]]

Thinking, "If I give this away, what will I have to enjoy?"
Is the way of a demon, thinking only of one's own benefit.
Thinking, "If I use this myself, what will I have to give?"
Is the Dharma of the gods, thinking of the welfare of others. ^8-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-125]]

If one harms others for the sake of oneself,
One will be tormented in the hells and other lower realms.
If one harms oneself for the sake of others,
One will attain all forms of excellence. ^8-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-126]]

One who desires high status for oneself
Will be reborn in the lower realms, in wretchedness, and as a fool.
If that very desire is transferred to others,
One will attain good migrations and be honored. ^8-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-127]]

If one employs others for one's own benefit,
One will experience servitude and the like.
If one employs oneself for the benefit of others,
One will experience lordship and the like. ^8-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-128]]

Whatever happiness there is in the world,
It all arises from desiring the happiness of others.
Whatever suffering there is in the world,
It all arises from desiring one's own happiness. ^8-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-129]]

What need is there to say more?
Observe the difference between these two:
The childish who work for their own sake,
And the Sages who work for the sake of others. ^8-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-130]]

If one does not truly exchange
One's own happiness for the suffering of others,
Buddhahood will not be attained,
And there will be no happiness even in cyclic existence. ^8-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-131]]

Setting aside the next life,
Even the goals of this life will not be achieved,
Just as a servant will not do his work
And a master will not pay his wages. ^8-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-132]]

Having abandoned the perfect happiness
That achieves well-being in both the seen and the unseen,
Through the cause of inflicting suffering on others,
The deluded take up unbearable misery. ^8-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-133]]

Whatever harm exists in the world,
And however many fears and sufferings there may be,
If they all arise from grasping at a self,
Then what use is that great demon to me? ^8-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-134]]

If I do not completely let go of the self,
I will be unable to abandon suffering,
Just as one who does not let go of fire
Is unable to avoid being burned. ^8-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-135]]

Therefore, in order to pacify harm to myself
And to pacify the suffering of others,
I shall give myself away to others
And cherish others as I do myself. ^8-135

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-136]]

O mind, you must understand with certainty:
"I am now under the power of others."
Except for the welfare of all sentient beings,
You must now think of nothing else. ^8-136

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-137]]

It is not right to achieve your own ends
With eyes and so forth that belong to others;
It is not right to act for your own sake
With those eyes and so forth that are for their sake. ^8-137

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-138]]

Therefore, make sentient beings your primary concern;
Whatever you see upon my body,
Snatch it away and use it
To act for the benefit of others. ^8-138

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-139]]

Having identified oneself with those who are inferior and so forth,
And having identified others as oneself,
With a mind free of conceptual doubt,
Meditate on envy, rivalry, and pride. ^8-139

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-140]]

"This one is honored, but I am not;
I have not obtained such things as this.
This one is praised, while I am reviled;
This one is happy, while I am suffering." ^8-140

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-141]]

"I am the one who does the work,
While this one remains in a state of ease.
This one is renowned as great in the world,
While I am known as lowly and without merit." ^8-141

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-142]]

What is the use of one who lacks good qualities?
I myself am fully endowed with good qualities.
There are those compared to whom this person is inferior,
And there are those compared to whom I am superior. ^8-142

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-143]]

The decline of my ethical discipline, views, and so forth,
Is due to the power of the afflictions and is not under my control.
I must be healed as much as possible,
And I shall even accept the hardship involved. ^8-143

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-144]]

If, however, I am not the one to be healed by him,
Why does he treat me with contempt?
What use are his good qualities to me?
I am the one who possesses good qualities. ^8-144

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-145]]

Lacking compassion for beings who dwell in the jaws of the miserable realms,
He is outwardly conceited about his qualities,
And wishes to challenge the wise. ^8-145

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-146]]

Looking at one who is equal to himself,
In order to make himself superior,
He will certainly secure his own gain and honor,
Even through contention. ^8-146

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-147]]

I shall ensure that my own good qualities
Are made known to the entire world,
While ensuring that no one ever hears
Of any good qualities he may possess. ^8-147

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-148]]

I shall conceal my own faults,
And I shall be the one who is honored, not he.
I shall secure great gain today,
And I shall be the one who is respected, not he. ^8-148

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-149]]

I shall watch with long-lasting delight
As this person is brought to ruin.
I shall make him the laughingstock of all beings,
And the object of their mutual derision. ^8-149

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-150]]

It is said that this miserable one is even competing with me.
But is he my equal in learning, wisdom,
Physical appearance, lineage, or wealth? ^8-150

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-151]]

Having heard of my own virtues
Being proclaimed to all in this way,
I shall experience the thrill of joy that makes my hair stand on end,
And I shall fully enjoy that happiness. ^8-151

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-152]]

Even if he should acquire some gain,
If he is working for me,
I shall give him only enough for his livelihood,
And I shall take the rest by my own power. ^8-152

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-153]]

I shall cause his happiness to decline,
And I shall constantly subject him to harm;
For throughout hundreds of lifetimes,
He has caused me harm within saṃsāra. ^8-153

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-154]]

O mind, though countless eons have passed while you desired to pursue your own interests,
Through such great hardship,
You have achieved nothing but suffering. ^8-154

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-155]]

Therefore, you must certainly and thoroughly apply yourself to the welfare of others;
Since the Buddha's word is unerring,
You will later see the benefits of this. ^8-155

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-156]]

If you had performed this work in the past,
It would be impossible for you to be in this present state,
Lacking the perfect bliss of a Buddha. ^8-156

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-157]]

Therefore, just as you have grasped as "I" these drops of others' semen and blood,
You should likewise habituate yourself to regarding others in the same way. ^8-157

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-158]]

Having formed the great conception of "other,"
Seize whatever appears as your own body,
And take that very thing away,
To use it for the benefit of others. ^8-158

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-159]]

"I am happy, but others are not;
I am high, but others are low;
I am the benefactor, but others are not"—
Why do you not feel envy toward yourself in this way? ^8-159

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-160]]

Separate yourself from your own happiness,
And take the sufferings of others upon yourself.
At all times, examine your own faults,
Asking, "What is this [self] doing?" ^8-160

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-161]]

Even when others commit a wrong,
Transform it into your own fault;
And though your own mistake be small,
Confess it openly to many people. ^8-161

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-162]]

By speaking highly of the renown of others,
Let your own reputation be eclipsed.
Like the lowliest of servants,
Employ yourself in all matters of benefit. ^8-162

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-163]]

This [self] is faulty by nature,
So do not praise it for any fleeting qualities.
Act in such a way that no one at all
Will ever know of its virtues. ^8-163

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-164]]

In short, whatever harm you have done
To others for your own sake,
Let that harm fall upon yourself
For the sake of sentient beings. ^8-164

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-165]]

Do not allow it to grow strong
In an overbearing and arrogant manner;
Like a new bride,
Remain bashful, fearful, and restrained. ^8-165

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-166]]

That should be done, and one should remain so;
You must not act in that way.
In this manner, you must be brought under control;
If you transgress this, you shall be suppressed. ^8-166

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-167]]

However, if you, my mind, do not act accordingly
Even when you have been so instructed,
Then, since all faults reside in you,
You shall simply be suppressed. ^8-167

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-168]]

The former time when you could ruin me
Was another matter;
Now that I see you, where can you go?
I shall crush all of your pride. ^8-168

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-169]]

Furthermore, abandon the thought that you still have your own interests;
I have sold you to others.
Do not be distressed, but offer up your service. ^8-169

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-170]]

If, through heedlessness, you do not give yourself to sentient beings,
You will certainly hand me over to the guardians of hell. ^8-170

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-171]]

By handing me over in that way, you have caused me to suffer for a long time;
Now, remembering those grudges, I shall destroy your self-interested thoughts. ^8-171

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-172]]

Therefore, if I desire to be happy,
I should not seek my own pleasure.
Therefore, if I desire to protect myself,
I should constantly protect others. ^8-172

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-173]]

To whatever extent this body is pampered and cared for,
To that same extent it falls into a state of extreme fragility. ^8-173

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-174]]

For one who has fallen into such a state,
Even this entire earth is unable to satisfy their desires;
Who then can fulfill such cravings? ^8-174

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-175]]

When one desires the impossible, afflictions and a shattered peace of mind will arise;
However, for one who is independent of all such things, their excellence is inexhaustible. ^8-175

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-176]]

Therefore, one should not provide any opportunity for the desires of the body to increase;
Whatever one does not grasp as attractive is the finest of things. ^8-176

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-177]]

Ultimately ending in ashes, it is inert and moved by others;
Why do I grasp this unbearable, impure form as a self? ^8-177

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-178]]

Whether it is alive or even if it is dead,
What use is this machine to me?
If it is no different from a clod of earth or the like,
Alas, why do I not dispel my pride? ^8-178

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-179]]

By following the dictates of the body,
I have accumulated meaningless suffering;
What is the use of attachment or anger
Toward this thing that is just like a piece of wood? ^8-179

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-180]]

Whether I nurture it in this way,
Or it is eaten by vultures and the like,
Since it has neither attachment nor hatred,
Why then do I feel attachment for it? ^8-180

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-181]]

If that which is angered by insults
And that which is pleased by praise
Is itself without consciousness,
For whose sake do I exert myself? ^8-181

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-182]]

If you say that those who desire this body
Are my friends,
Since everyone desires their own body,
Why do I not take pleasure in them as well? ^8-182

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-183]]

Therefore, without attachment,
I shall give up this body for the benefit of all beings;
Thus, although it has many faults,
I shall maintain it as a tool for my actions. ^8-183

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-184]]

Therefore, enough of childish behavior!
Following in the footsteps of the wise,
And recalling the instructions on conscientiousness,
I shall dispel sleep and mental dullness. ^8-184

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-185]]

Therefore, in order to clear away obscurations,
I shall withdraw my mind from mistaken paths,
And always rest it in meditative equipoise
Upon the perfect object of focus. ^8-185

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-a]]

This concludes the eighth chapter, titled "The Explanation of Meditative Concentration," from the *Entering the Conduct of a Bodhisattva*. ^8-a

## Chapter Nine: The Perfection of Wisdom ^9-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-1]]

All of these various branches
Were taught by the Sage for the sake of wisdom.
Therefore, those who wish to pacify suffering
Should generate wisdom. ^9-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-2]]

The conventional and the ultimate:
These are held to be the two truths.
The ultimate is not an object of the intellect;
The intellect is said to be the conventional. ^9-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-3]]

In this regard, two kinds of worlds are seen:
The yogic and the ordinary.
In that regard, the ordinary world
Is invalidated by the yogic world; ^9-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-4]]

Even yogis are superseded by those at higher and higher levels
Due to differences in their intellect.
Both are accepted through examples they agree upon,
Because they do not analyze for the sake of the result. ^9-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-5]]

The worldly perceive things and conceptualize them
As being truly real, rather than like an illusion;
It is on this point that
The yogis and the worldly dispute. ^9-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-6]]

Even the direct perception of forms and so forth
Exists by worldly renown, not by valid cognition.
This is as false as the common renown
Of purity in things that are actually impure. ^9-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-7]]

For the sake of engaging the world,
The Protector taught these things as if they were real;
If you object that they are not even momentary,
And that this is contradictory even in conventional truth, ^9-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-8]]

There is no fault in the conventional truth of the yogis,
For they see reality in relation to the world;
Otherwise, the world's perception would invalidate
The realization that a woman's body is impure. ^9-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-9]]

How can merit from an illusory Conqueror
Be the same as if he were truly existent?
If sentient beings are like illusions,
Then how are they reborn after death? ^9-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-10]]

As long as the conditions are assembled,
For that long even an illusion will arise.
How can a sentient being be truly existent
Merely because their continuity lasts a long time? ^9-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-11]]

In killing an illusory person and the like,
There is no sin because they lack a mind;
But in the case of one who possesses an illusory mind,
Merit and sin do arise. ^9-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-12]]

Because mantras and such lack the capacity,
An illusory mind does not arise from them.
Arising from various conditions,
That illusion is itself of various kinds. ^9-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-13]]

Nowhere is there any single condition
That has the power to produce everything.
If ultimate reality is nirvana
And cyclic existence is the conventional, then ^9-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-14]]

Since even a Buddha would be in cyclic existence,
What would be the use of the conduct of enlightenment?
As long as the continuity of conditions is not severed,
Even an illusion will not cease; ^9-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-15]]

But since the continuity of conditions is severed,
It does not arise even conventionally.
When even the confusion itself does not exist,
By what means can an illusion be perceived? ^9-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-16]]

When, for you, the illusion itself
Does not exist, then what is perceived?
If it were something else in reality,
That aspect would be the mind itself. ^9-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-17]]

When the mind itself is an illusion,
Then what is seen, and by what is it seen?
The Protector of the World has also said
That the mind does not see the mind. ^9-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-18]]

Just as the edge of a sword cannot
Cut itself, so it is with the mind.
Just as a lamp’s own nature
Perfectly illuminates itself, ^9-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-19]]

A lamp is not something to be illuminated,
Because it is not obscured by darkness.
Just as the blueness of a blue crystal
Does not depend upon anything else, ^9-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-20]]

So too, some things are seen to depend
On others, while some are seen not to depend.
That which is not blue cannot make itself blue;
It is not made so by itself. ^9-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-21]]

If you say, "The lamp illuminates,"
Having known this through cognition,
Then by what is it known that the mind is luminous,
Such that you can say so? ^9-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-22]]

Since it is never seen by anyone at all,
Whether it is clear or not clear,
Is like the graceful posturing of a barren woman's daughter;
It is meaningless even to speak of it. ^9-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-23]]

If self-awareness does not exist,
How can consciousness be remembered?
Memory arises through a connection to the experience of something else,
Just like the venom of a rat. ^9-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-24]]

If it were self-luminous because it is seen
When endowed with other conditions,
Then, just as a vase is seen through the application of a perfected eye-salve,
The eye-salve itself would not be the vase that is seen. ^9-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-25]]

Whatever is seen, heard, or known,
Is not what is to be negated here;
What is to be overturned here is the conceptualization of them as true,
Which is the cause of suffering. ^9-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-26]]

If an illusion is not other than the mind,
And yet you do not consider it to be the same,
If it were a real entity, how could it not be other?
If you say it is not other, then it cannot exist as a real entity. ^9-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-27]]

Just as an illusion is not true,
So too is the mind that sees it;
If cyclic existence were based on a real entity,
It would otherwise be like empty space. ^9-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-28]]

Since a non-entity depends upon an entity,
How could it possess the capacity for action?
Your mind would then be without any companion,
It would become entirely solitary. ^9-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-29]]

When the mind is free from a perceived object,
At that time, all are Tathāgatas.
That being the case, what excellence is there
In the conceptual construction of "Mind Only"? ^9-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-30]]

Even if one knows things are like an illusion,
How will the afflictions be averted?
For even the creator of an illusory woman
May feel desire for her. ^9-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-31]]

The creator of such things is an object of knowledge,
For the latencies of the afflictions have not been abandoned.
Therefore, when one sees that object,
The latency for emptiness is very weak. ^9-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-32]]

By habituating the latency for emptiness,
The latency for substantial things will be abandoned;
And by habituating the thought that "nothing exists,"
Even that will later be abandoned. ^9-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-33]]

When one thinks that "nothing exists,"
And the substantial thing to be analyzed is not perceived,
At that time, how can a non-entity, lacking any basis,
Remain before the mind? ^9-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-34]]

When neither entities nor non-entities
Remain before the mind,
Then, since there is no other aspect,
It is thoroughly pacified in non-apprehension. ^9-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-35]]

Just as a wish-fulfilling jewel or a wish-granting tree
Completely fulfills all hopes,
Likewise, through the power of the aspirations of those to be trained,
The form of the Victorious One appears. ^9-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-36]]

For example, just as a Garuḍa shrine,
Once established, remains even after the practitioner has passed away,
And continues to pacify poisons and the like
Even though a long time has elapsed since his passing. ^9-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-37]]

Constructing a shrine to the Victorious One
In accordance with the conduct of enlightenment
Accomplishes all purposes, even though
The Bodhisattva has passed into nirvana. ^9-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-38]]

If one makes offerings to that which lacks a mind,
How can it be possessed of a result?
Because it is explained that whether they remain or have passed into nirvana,
It is exactly the same. ^9-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-39]]

Whether from the perspective of conventional or ultimate truth,
The scriptures state that there is a result,
Just as there is a result
From offerings made to the actual Buddha. ^9-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-40]]

Liberation comes from seeing the truths;
What is the use of seeing emptiness?
Because the scriptures state that without this path,
There is no enlightenment. ^9-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-41]]

If the Mahāyāna were not established,
How would your own scriptures be established?
Because this is established for both of us,
Initially, this was not established for you. ^9-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-42]]

Whatever conditions make you believe in that,
They are the same for the Mahāyāna as well.
If it were true because two others accept it,
Then the Vedas and so forth would also be true. ^9-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-43]]

If you say it is because the Mahāyāna is contested,
Since your own scriptures are contested by non-Buddhists,
And other scriptures are contested by your own and other schools,
They would also have to be rejected because they are contested. ^9-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-44]]

If the root of the teaching is the monkhood itself,
Then even being a monk is a difficult state to maintain.
For those whose minds are still directed toward objects,
Even nirvana is a difficult state to maintain. ^9-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-45]]

If one is liberated through the abandonment of afflictions,
Then one should become so immediately thereafter.
Even if they are free from afflictions,
The potency of karma is still seen within them. ^9-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-46]]

If you argue that for the time being it is certain that there is no craving that constitutes grasping,
Then why should this craving not exist—just like their confusion—even if it is not of an afflicted nature? ^9-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-47]]

Craving is conditioned by feeling, and they also possess those feelings.
A mind that possesses an object of observation will remain fixed upon something. ^9-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-48]]

A mind that is devoid of emptiness, even if it has ceased, will arise once again,
Just like the meditative absorption of non-perception;
Therefore, one should meditate on emptiness. ^9-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-49]]

If you accept that any speech which accords with the sutras is the word of the Buddha,
Then why do you not accept the majority of the Mahayana,
Which is similar to your own sutras? ^9-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-50]]

If, because of a single exception, everything were to be considered faulty,
Then why would the whole not be the word of the Victorious One,
Because a single point is similar to the sutras? ^9-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-51]]

Regarding those words whose depth could not be fathomed
By the likes of the great Mahākāśyapa,
Because you have not understood them,
Who are you to say they should not be accepted? ^9-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-52]]

To remain in cyclic existence for the sake of those suffering through delusion,
Liberated from the extremes of attachment and fear,
And having accomplished the state of abiding in cyclic existence—
This is the fruit of emptiness. ^9-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-53]]

Thus, it is not reasonable
To direct refutations toward the position of emptiness.
Therefore, without harboring any doubt,
One should meditate upon emptiness. ^9-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-54]]

Emptiness is the antidote
To the darkness of the afflictive and cognitive obscurations.
Why, then, would those who desire to swiftly attain omniscience
Not meditate upon it? ^9-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-55]]

If fear arises toward something that produces suffering,
Then fear should arise toward that;
But emptiness is what pacifies suffering,
So how could fear arise toward it? ^9-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-56]]

If there were some "self,"
Then one might be afraid of anything;
But since there is no self at all,
Who is there to be afraid? ^9-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-57]]

Teeth, hair, and nails are not the self;
The self is not the bones or blood;
It is not the mucus or the phlegm,
Nor is it the lymph or the pus. ^9-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-58]]

The self is not fat or sweat;
The lungs and liver, too, are not the self.
Other internal organs are not the self;
The self is not the excrement or the urine. ^9-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-59]]

Flesh and skin are not the self;
Heat and wind are also not the self.
The orifices are not the self in any way;
The six consciousnesses are also not the self. ^9-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-60]]

If the consciousness of sound were permanent,
Then sound would be apprehended at all times.
If there is no object of knowledge, what does it perceive?
On what basis could it be called "consciousness"? ^9-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-61]]

If that which lacks cognition were consciousness,
Then it would follow that even a piece of wood is consciousness.
Therefore, it is certain that without the presence of an object of knowledge,
There is no consciousness. ^9-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-62]]

If it is that very same consciousness that knows a form,
Why at that time does it not also hear?
If you say it is because the sound is not present,
Then the consciousness of that sound is also absent. ^9-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-63]]

How could that which has the nature of apprehending sound
Become that which apprehends form?
One person being designated as both father and son
Is not the ultimate reality. ^9-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-64]]

In this way, sattva, rajas, and tamas
Are neither son nor father.
They are not observed to have a nature
That possesses the apprehension of sound. ^9-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-65]]

If it is seen in another mode, like an actor,
Then it is not permanent.
If that other mode is the reality itself,
Then that oneness is a oneness never seen before. ^9-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-66]]

If that other mode is not real,
Then describe its own inherent nature.
If you say it is consciousness itself, then in that case,
It would follow that all beings are one. ^9-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-67]]

Those with mind and those without mind would also
Become one, because they are the same in their mere existence.
When their specific differences are mistaken,
What could then be the basis for their similarity? ^9-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-68]]

That which is without mind is also not the self,
Because it lacks consciousness, just like a vase and so forth.
However, if it were the self because it is endowed with consciousness,
It would follow that when it is not conscious, the self would perish. ^9-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-69]]

If the self is immutable,
What could consciousness possibly do to it?
Thus, being unconscious and inactive,
It would be as if space were made into the self. ^9-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-70]]

If the self does not exist,
The connection between action and result is illogical;
Since the action perishes after being performed,
Whose action would it be? ^9-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-71]]

Since it is established for both of us
That the action and the basis of the result are distinct,
And that there is no self acting therein,
Is this dispute not pointless? ^9-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-72]]

To see a cause existing simultaneously with its result
Is not possible;
It is by relying on a single continuum
That the "agent" and "experiencer" are taught. ^9-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-73]]

The minds of the past and future
Are not the self, because they do not exist;
If, however, the presently arising mind were the self,
Then when that mind perishes, the self would also cease to exist. ^9-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-74]]

For example, when the trunk of a plantain tree
Is dissected into its parts, nothing is found;
Likewise, when sought through analytical investigation,
The self is not found to be real. ^9-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-75]]

If sentient beings do not exist,
Then for whom should one feel compassion?
It is directed toward those imputed by delusion,
Which are accepted for the sake of the result. ^9-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-76]]

If there are no sentient beings, whose is the fruit?
That is true, yet such an aspiration is made from the perspective of delusion.
For the sake of pacifying suffering,
One should not dispel the delusion regarding the result. ^9-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-77]]

The pride that is the cause of suffering
Increases through the delusion of a self.
If you say that this cannot be averted,
Then meditating on selflessness is the supreme remedy. ^9-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-78]]

The body is not the feet or the calves;
The thighs and the waist are not the body either.
The belly and the back are not the body;
The chest and the shoulders are not the body either. ^9-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-79]]

The ribs and the hands are not the body,
Nor are the armpits and the shoulders the body.
The internal organs are not it either,
And if the head and the neck are also not the body,
Then what in this is the body? ^9-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-80]]

If this body were to abide in all these parts
By way of its individual portions,
Then while the parts would indeed abide in the parts,
Where would the body itself abide? ^9-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-81]]

If the body in its entirety
Were to abide in the hands and so forth,
Then as many as there are such parts as hands,
There would be that many bodies. ^9-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-82]]

If the body is not found outside or within its parts,
How could the body exist in the hands and so forth?
If it is not something other than the hands and so forth,
How could it possibly exist? ^9-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-83]]

Therefore, the body does not exist; it is only due to delusion
That the notion of a body arises with respect to the hands and so forth,
Just as the mind perceives a person in a scarecrow
Due to a particular arrangement of shape. ^9-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-84]]

As long as the collection of conditions remains,
The body appears like a person;
Likewise, as long as the hands and so forth are present,
The body will appear to exist therein. ^9-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-85]]

Similarly, since it is a collection of fingers,
What indeed is the "hand"?
Since that too is a collection of joints,
And the joints themselves can be divided into their own parts, ^9-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-86]]

Even the parts are reduced to atoms,
And those atoms are divided into directional facets.
Since the directional facets are also devoid of parts,
They are like space; therefore, even atoms do not exist. ^9-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-87]]

Such being the case, what discerning person
Would be attached to forms that are like a dream?
When the body is thus non-existent,
Then who is a man and who is a woman? ^9-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-88]]

If suffering exists in its own right,
Why does it not afflict those who are joyful?
If happiness is real, why do those tormented by grief and the like
Not find joy in delicious food and such? ^9-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-89]]

If it is argued that it is not experienced because it is overwhelmed by something more powerful,
How can that which is not of the nature of experience
Be a sensation? ^9-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-90]]

If you say that suffering exists in a subtle state
And its gross form has merely been removed,
Then that is simply a state of joy other than that;
If you say it is subtle, then that too is of the same nature. ^9-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-91]]

If, because an opposing condition has arisen,
Suffering is not produced,
Then is it not established that what is considered "feeling"
Is merely a stubborn conceptual attachment? ^9-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-92]]

For that very reason, one should meditate
On this analytical investigation as its antidote;
The meditative concentration arising from the field of such analysis
Is the food of the yogi. ^9-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-93]]

If there is a gap between the sense faculty and the object,
Where would those two meet?
If there is no gap, they are but one;
What, then, could meet with what? ^9-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-94]]

An atom cannot enter another atom,
For it has no space and they are equal in size.
Without entering, there is no mingling,
And without mingling, there is no contact. ^9-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-95]]

How could it be logical to speak of "contact"
Between things that have no parts?
If you have ever seen contact occurring without parts,
Then please demonstrate it. ^9-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-96]]

For a consciousness that is formless,
Contact is not at all logical.
A collection also lacks substantial reality,
Just as was previously analyzed. ^9-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-97]]

If, in this way, there is no contact,
From what could feeling arise?
For what purpose is all this toil?
What could cause harm to what? ^9-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-98]]

When there is no experiencer at all,
And feeling itself does not exist,
Then, having seen this situation,
Why does craving not turn away? ^9-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-99]]

Since seeing and even touching
Are of the nature of dreams and illusions,
And arise simultaneously with the mind,
That feeling is not seen by it. ^9-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-100]]

Even if born previously or later,
They would be remembered but not experienced.
The self does not experience itself,
Nor is it experienced by others. ^9-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-101]]

There is no experiencer at all,
And therefore, feeling itself does not exist.
Thus, how can this collection,
Which is devoid of a self, be harmed by this? ^9-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-102]]

The mind does not abide in the sense faculties,
Nor in forms and so forth, nor in between them.
It is not the mind within, nor is it without,
Nor is it to be found anywhere else. ^9-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-103]]

That which is not the body, nor something other than it,
Neither mixed with it nor separate in any way,
Is not even the slightest thing; therefore,
Sentient beings are by nature in the state of nirvana. ^9-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-104]]

If consciousness existed prior to the object of knowledge,
Then directed toward what would it arise?
If consciousness and the object of knowledge are simultaneous,
Then directed toward what would it arise? ^9-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-105]]

If, however, it exists after the object of knowledge,
Then from what would consciousness arise?
In this way, the arising of all phenomena
Cannot be understood. ^9-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-106]]

If in that way the conventional does not exist,
How could there be the two truths?
Furthermore, if that conventional exists through another,
How could sentient beings pass into nirvana? ^9-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-107]]

This is the conceptualization of another's mind;
It is not one's own conventional reality.
If that is later ascertained, then it exists;
Otherwise, the conventional simply does not exist. ^9-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-108]]

Conceptual thought and the object to be analyzed
Are both mutually dependent.
Based upon what is renowned in the world,
All analytical investigations are expressed. ^9-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-109]]

When one analyzes with an analysis
That is itself being analyzed,
Then because that analysis is also to be analyzed,
There is no end to it. ^9-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-110]]

When the object of analysis is thoroughly analyzed,
There is no basis remaining for the analysis.
Because there is no basis, it does not arise,
And that is described as nirvana. ^9-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-111]]

For those who hold that these two are real,
Their position is extremely difficult to maintain.
If the object is established through the power of consciousness,
What basis is there for the existence of consciousness itself? ^9-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-112]]

If, however, consciousness is established through the object of knowledge,
What basis is there for the existence of the object of knowledge?
If they exist through mutual dependence,
Then both of them will be non-existent. ^9-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-113]]

If there is no father without a son,
From what could the son himself have arisen?
Without a son, there is no father;
Likewise, both of them are non-existent. ^9-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-114]]

Just as a sprout is produced from a seed,
And the seed itself is known by that very sprout,
Why is the existence of the object of knowledge not realized
By the consciousness that arises from it? ^9-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-115]]

If a consciousness other than the sprout
Realizes that the seed exists,
Then by what means is the existence of that consciousness,
Which realizes that object of knowledge, itself realized? ^9-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-116]]

For the time being, through worldly direct perception,
All causes are seen.
The distinctions in the lotus stalk and so forth
Are produced by the distinctions among their causes. ^9-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-117]]

If you ask what created the variety of causes,
It is from the very variety of preceding causes.
Why is a cause able to produce an effect?
It is from the very power of the preceding cause. ^9-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-118]]

If Īśvara is the cause of the world,
Then for a start, explain who Īśvara is.
If you say he is the elements, that may be so;
But why weary yourself over a mere name? ^9-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-119]]

However, because earth and the others are multiple,
Impermanent, inert, and not divine,
And because they are things to be trodden upon and are impure,
They are not Īśvara himself. ^9-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-120]]

Īśvara is not space, because space is inert;
He is not the self, as that has already been refuted.
And if the creator is also inconceivable,
What is the point of speaking of him as "inconceivable"? ^9-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-121]]

Furthermore, what is it that he is asserted to produce?
Are not the self, the earth, and so forth,
And the nature of Īśvara himself, also eternal?
Since consciousness arises from objects of knowledge, ^9-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-122]]

Pleasure and pain have arisen from karma since beginningless time.
Tell me, what is it that is created by him?
If there is no beginning to the cause,
How could there be a beginning to the effect? ^9-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-123]]

Why does he not create constantly?
For he is not dependent on anything else.
If there is nothing that was not created by him,
Upon what would he depend? ^9-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-124]]

If he depends on something, then the collection of conditions
Is the cause, and not Īśvara.
When the collection is present, he has no power not to produce,
And without it, he has no power to produce. ^9-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-125]]

If he acts without desiring to do so,
It follows that he is under the power of another.
If he does desire to act, he is dependent upon that desire;
If he is thus dependent, how can he be Īśvara? ^9-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-126]]

Those who claim that atoms are eternal
Have already been refuted previously.
The Sāṃkhyas assert that a permanent Primal Substance
Is the cause of the world. ^9-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-127]]

The equilibrium of the qualities known as
Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas
Is proclaimed to be the Primal Substance;
Their disequilibrium is said to be the world. ^9-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-128]]

It is illogical for a single entity to have three natures;
Therefore, it does not exist.
Likewise, the qualities themselves do not exist,
Because they too each have a threefold nature. ^9-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-129]]

If the qualities do not exist, then even the existence
Of sound and so forth becomes extremely remote.
It is also impossible for pleasure and the like
To exist in insentient things such as clothing. ^9-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-130]]

If things possess the nature of their cause,
Has the analysis of things not already been completed?
For you, the cause is pleasure and so forth,
But from that, clothing and the like do not arise. ^9-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-131]]

Pleasure and the like arise from clothing and such;
Since those do not exist, pleasure and the like do not exist.
Furthermore, a state in which pleasure and the rest are permanent
Is never observed at any time. ^9-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-132]]

If pleasure and the rest were manifest as existing entities,
Why is their experience not perceived?
If that itself becomes subtle,
How can it be both coarse and subtle? ^9-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-133]]

By abandoning the coarse and becoming subtle,
Those coarse and subtle states are themselves impermanent.
Why, then, do you not accept that
All things are likewise impermanent? ^9-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-134]]

If the coarse state is not other than the pleasure,
Then the manifest pleasure is itself impermanent.
If you claim that nothing non-existent can ever arise
Because it lacks being, ^9-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-135]]

Then even if you do not wish it,
The arising of a manifest state from what was non-existent remains a fact.
If the effect exists within the cause,
Then one who eats food would be eating filth. ^9-135

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-136]]

With the price of a garment, buy cotton seeds
And wear them instead.
If the world does not see this due to ignorance,
Then those who know the truth have established this very fact. ^9-136

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-137]]

If that knowledge exists even in the world,
Why is it not seen?
If the world is not a valid authority,
Then even seeing what is manifest is not true. ^9-137

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-138]]

If a valid authority is not valid,
Would that which is measured by it not be false?
In that case, the meditation
On emptiness would be untenable. ^9-138

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-139]]

Without contacting the imagined entity,
Its non-existence cannot be apprehended.
Therefore, whatever entity is false,
Its non-existence is clearly false as well. ^9-139

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-140]]

Thus, when a son dies in a dream,
The conceptual thought that he does not exist
Is an obstacle to the conceptual thought that he exists,
And yet that [thought] is also false. ^9-140

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-141]]

Therefore, when analyzed in such a manner,
Nothing at all exists without a cause,
Nor does it abide within conditions,
Whether they be individual or assembled. ^9-141

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-142]]

It does not come from elsewhere,
It does not remain, and it does not depart.
What difference is there between a magical illusion
And this which the deluded take to be real? ^9-142

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-143]]

Whatever is conjured by illusion,
And whatever is produced by causes—
Examine where it has come from,
And examine also where it goes. ^9-143

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-144]]

Whatever is seen through the proximity of something else,
And does not exist without it,
Is like a fabricated reflection;
How could there be any true reality in that? ^9-144

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-145]]

If a thing already exists,
What need is there for a cause?
If, on the other hand, it does not exist,
What need is there for a cause? ^9-145

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-146]]

Even by means of hundreds of millions of causes,
A non-existent thing cannot be transformed.
How could that state ever be an entity?
And what else could possibly become an entity? ^9-146

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-147]]

If an entity cannot exist while it is non-existent,
When will it ever become an entity?
As long as an entity has not been produced,
It will not be separated from non-existence. ^9-147

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-148]]

If it is not separated from non-existence,
The opportunity for existence is impossible.
Nor can an entity become non-existent,
For that would entail having a dual nature. ^9-148

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-149]]

Thus, there is no cessation,
And likewise, there is no existence.
Therefore, all these wandering beings
Are eternally unborn and unceasing. ^9-149

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-150]]

Wandering beings are like a dream;
When analyzed, they are like a plantain tree.
Between nirvāṇa and the state not passed beyond sorrow,
In reality, there is no distinction. ^9-150

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-151]]

Thus, when things are empty,
What is there to gain and what is there to lose?
Who is there to show respect,
And what is there to be held in utter contempt? ^9-151

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-152]]

From what do happiness and suffering arise?
What is there to be displeased with, and what is there to be pleased with?
When searched for in that very reality,
Who is it that craves, and for what do they crave? ^9-152

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-153]]

When analyzed, in this world of the living,
Who is it that will die here?
What is it that will come to be, and what has already occurred?
And who is a relative, and who is a friend? ^9-153

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-154]]

Everything should be embraced by those like me
As being just like space.
Those who desire their own happiness
Are, through the causes of conflict and excitement, ^9-154

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-155]]

Greatly agitated or delighted.
Through grief, exertion, and dispute,
And by mutually cutting and piercing one another,
They live with great hardship due to their sins. ^9-155

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-156]]

Having come again and again to the higher realms
And having enjoyed many pleasures,
After death they fall into the long-lasting
And unbearable sufferings of the lower realms. ^9-156

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-157]]

Within existence there are many precipices,
And there, things are not as they truly are.
Because there is mutual contradiction there as well,
In existence, such a true reality does not exist. ^9-157

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-158]]

Furthermore, that ocean of unbearable suffering
Is beyond compare and without end.
In that state, one's strength is likewise feeble,
And there, one's lifespan is also very short. ^9-158

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-159]]

Even during that time, while living and free from illness,
Through the hardships of activities, hunger, and fatigue,
Through sleep and injury, and likewise
Through meaningless association with the childish, ^9-159

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-160]]

Life passes by quickly and meaninglessly,
And analytical discernment is extremely difficult to find.
Moreover, when one is habituated to such distractions,
Where is there any means to turn them back? ^9-160

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-161]]

Furthermore, in that state, Māra exerts himself
To make one fall into the great miserable realms.
There, the wrong paths are numerous,
And it is difficult to transcend even doubt. ^9-161

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-162]]

Again, leisure is difficult to find,
And the appearance of a Buddha is extremely rare.
The river of afflictions is hard to abandon;
Alas, suffering will continue in succession! ^9-162

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-163]]

Even though they are in such extreme misery,
Those who do not see their own suffering
While remaining in this river of pain—
Alas, they are worthy of compassion! ^9-163

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-164]]

For example, just as some might bathe again and again
Only to cast themselves repeatedly into fire,
So too do these beings imagine themselves happy
While remaining in a state of intense suffering. ^9-164

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-165]]

Thus, by behaving as if they will never grow old or die,
They are first of all killed in these very places,
And then the unbearable fall into the lower realms occurs. ^9-165

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-166]]

When shall I be able to pacify those tormented by the fires of suffering
With a cooling rain of my own bliss,
Which has arisen from the clouds of merit,
And thus become a source of peace? ^9-166

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-167]]

When, by respectfully accumulating the collection of merit
In a manner free from conceptual reference,
Shall I teach emptiness to those
Who are ruined by their conceptual grasping? ^9-167

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-a]]

The ninth chapter of the Engaging in the Bodhisattva Deeds, the Perfection of Wisdom. ^9-a

## Chapter Ten: Dedication ^10-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-1]]

Through whatever virtue I have gained
By composing this "Engaging in the Bodhisattva Deeds,"
May all wandering beings
Enter into the conduct of enlightenment. ^10-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-2]]

May all those in every direction
Who are suffering from diseases of body and mind
Obtain an ocean of happiness and joy
Through the power of my merit. ^10-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-3]]

For as long as they remain in cyclic existence,
May their happiness never decline;
May wandering beings attain
The uninterrupted bliss of the Bodhisattvas. ^10-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-4]]

In all the world systems,
However many hells there may be,
May the embodied beings within them
Rejoice in the happiness of Sukhavati. ^10-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-5]]

May those tormented by cold find warmth;
May those tormented by heat
Be cooled by the infinite rivers
Flowing from the great clouds of the Bodhisattvas. ^10-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-6]]

May the forest of sword-leaves
Become for them a dense sandalwood grove;
May the Shalmali trees likewise
Grow into wish-fulfilling trees. ^10-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-7]]

May they be beautified by the melodious calls of mallards, ruddy geese, swans, and cranes;
May the regions of hell become delightful
With lakes of great fragrant lotuses,
And may they be made beautiful by such sweet sounds. ^10-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-8]]

May those heaps of glowing coals become heaps of jewels;
May the scorched earth be spread as a crystal floor;
May the mountains of the Crushing Hell also become
Celestial palaces of offerings, filled with Sugatas. ^10-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-9]]

May the rain of embers, burning stones, and weapons
From this moment on become a rain of flowers;
And may those who strike one another with weapons
Henceforth toss flowers in play. ^10-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-10]]

May those submerged in the fordless river, which is like a pit of fire,
Their flesh all decayed and their skeletons the color of white kunda flowers,
Attain celestial bodies through the power of my merit,
And dwell with goddesses by the gently flowing waters. ^10-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-11]]

Why are the terrifying minions of Yama, the crows, and the vultures afraid here?
Thinking, "Whose excellent power is this that dispels darkness everywhere and generates blissful joy?"
They look upward and behold the blazing Vajrapāṇi dwelling in the midst of the sky;
Through the force of their intense joy, may they be freed from sin and dwell in his company. ^10-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-12]]

When a rain of flowers falls, mingled with scented water,
And they see the glowing embers of hell extinguished with a hiss,
They wonder, "What is this that has suddenly satiated us with bliss?"
And thus may the denizens of hell behold Padmapāṇi. ^10-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-13]]

Friends, cast away your fear and come here quickly to our side!
Who is this radiant youth with five knots of hair who has arrived to grant us fearlessness—
The compassionate Bodhisattva who thoroughly protects all beings,
And through whose power we are freed from all suffering and reach the heights of joy? ^10-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-14]]

The crowns of a hundred gods offer worship to his lotus feet,
His eyes are moist with compassion, and a rain of various flowers falls upon his head;
Look at him, accompanied by the songs of praise from thousands of goddesses in lovely celestial palaces!
Having seen such a Mañjughoṣa, may the denizens of hell now break into cries of joy. ^10-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-15]]

Thus, through my roots of virtue, may they see clouds of unobstructed Bodhisattvas such as Samantabhadra,
Like clouds of bliss, raining down cool and fragrant showers;
Upon seeing this,
May those denizens of hell be filled with manifest joy. ^10-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-16]]

May animals be free from the fear
Of preying upon one another;
And just like the people of the Northern Continent,
May the hungry ghosts become happy. ^10-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-17]]

By the stream of milk flowing
From the hand of the noble Avalokiteśvara,
May the hungry ghosts be satisfied,
And by bathing in it, may they be forever cooled. ^10-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-18]]

May the blind see forms,
And may the deaf always hear sounds;
Just like the goddess Māyādevī,
May pregnant women also give birth without harm. ^10-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-19]]

May those who are naked obtain clothing,
And those who are hungry find food;
May those who are thirsty obtain water,
And delicious drinks as well. ^10-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-20]]

May the destitute obtain wealth,
May those afflicted by grief find joy;
May the despondent be refreshed,
And attain perfect steadfastness. ^10-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-21]]

May all sentient beings who are sick
Be swiftly freed from their illnesses;
May every disease of living beings,
Without exception, never occur again. ^10-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-22]]

May those who are terrified be fearless;
May those who are bound be liberated;
May the powerless be endowed with power,
And may their hearts be kind toward one another. ^10-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-23]]

For all travelers,
May every direction be auspicious;
May whatever purpose they travel for
Be accomplished without effort. ^10-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-24]]

May those who have boarded boats and great ships
Have the desires of their hearts fulfilled,
And having safely reached the water's shore,
May they rejoice together with their kinsmen. ^10-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-25]]

May those who have strayed onto desolate paths
Encounter fellow travelers,
And without fear of robbers, tigers, and the like,
May they proceed easily without fatigue. ^10-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-26]]

In fearful, pathless wildernesses and the like,
May the gods protect children, the elderly, the helpless,
And those who are asleep, intoxicated, or utterly deranged. ^10-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-27]]

May they be freed from all states of non-leisure,
Endowed with faith, wisdom, and compassion,
And possessing excellent food and conduct,
May they always remember their past lives. ^10-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-28]]

May all have inexhaustible enjoyments,
Just like the treasury of space.
Without dispute and without harm,
May they act with complete freedom. ^10-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-29]]

May those sentient beings who have little splendor
Come to possess great splendor.
May those ascetics who are physically unattractive
Become endowed with perfect, beautiful forms. ^10-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-30]]

May all the women in the world
Become men.
May those of lowly status attain high positions,
And may their pride be utterly subdued. ^10-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-31]]

By this merit of mine,
May all sentient beings without exception,
Having abandoned all misdeeds,
Always engage in virtue. ^10-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-32]]

May they never be separated from the mind of enlightenment,
May they be devoted to the conduct of awakening,
May they be cared for by the Buddhas,
And may they abandon the activities of Māra. ^10-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-33]]

May all those sentient beings
Have lives of immeasurable length;
May they always live in happiness,
And may even the sound of death never be heard. ^10-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-34]]

May all directions be filled with groves of wish-fulfilling trees,
Resounding with the sweet Dharma proclaimed
By the Buddhas and the sons of the Buddhas,
And may they be filled with these. ^10-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-35]]

May the ground everywhere
Be free from pebbles and the like,
And remain naturally smooth,
As level as the palm of a hand and of the nature of lapis lazuli. ^10-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-36]]

In all the various circles of retinues,
May many Bodhisattvas
Dwell, having adorned the surface of the earth
With their own excellence. ^10-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-37]]

May all embodied beings continuously hear
The sound of the Dharma from birds,
From trees, from all rays of light,
And even from the sky itself. ^10-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-38]]

May they always meet with the Buddhas
And the sons of the Buddhas.
With infinite clouds of offerings,
May they worship the teachers of the world. ^10-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-39]]

May the gods also send rain at the proper time,
And may the harvests be bountiful.
May kings act in accordance with the Dharma,
And may the world also flourish. ^10-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-40]]

May medicines be potent,
And may the recitation of secret mantras be successful.
May ḍākinīs, rākṣasas, and the like
Be endowed with a mind of compassion. ^10-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-41]]

May no sentient being ever suffer,
May they not be afraid or held in contempt,
And may no one ever be unhappy. ^10-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-42]]

May temples be well-established,
Adorned with study and recitation.
May the Saṅgha always be in harmony,
And may the purposes of the Saṅgha be accomplished. ^10-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-43]]

May those monks who desire to train
Also obtain the state of solitude.
Having abandoned all distractions,
May they meditate with a workable mind. ^10-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-44]]

May the nuns be endowed with requisites,
And may they abandon quarreling and harm.
Likewise, may all those who have gone forth
Never let their ethical discipline decline. ^10-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-45]]

Having become disillusioned by lapses in discipline,
May they always exhaust their misdeeds.
Having attained a higher rebirth,
May their spiritual conduct not decline even there. ^10-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-46]]

May the wise be honored,
And may they also receive alms.
May their mental continuums be completely purified,
And may they be renowned in all directions. ^10-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-47]]

May they not experience the suffering of the lower realms,
And even without undergoing hardships,
With bodies superior even to the gods,
May they swiftly achieve Buddhahood. ^10-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-48]]

May all sentient beings repeatedly
Make offerings to all the Buddhas,
And through the inconceivable bliss of the Buddhas,
May they always be endowed with happiness. ^10-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-49]]

May the Bodhisattvas
Fulfill their intentions to benefit wandering beings.
Whatever the Protectors have intended,
May that be attained by all sentient beings. ^10-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-50]]

Likewise, may the Pratyekabuddhas
And the Śrāvakas also be happy. ^10-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-51]]

Through the kindness of Mañjuśrī,
Until I attain the Ground of Supreme Joy,
May I always remember my past lives,
And may I obtain the state of a renunciate. ^10-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-52]]

May I be sustained and live,
Even on a diet of the simplest food.
In all my lives, may I obtain
Perfect circumstances for living in solitude. ^10-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-53]]

Whenever I wish to see him,
Or even wish to ask the slightest question,
May I behold the Protector Mañjuśrī himself
Without any hindrance. ^10-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-54]]

For the sake of accomplishing the welfare of all sentient beings,
Reaching to the very limits of space in the ten directions,
Just as Mañjuśrī acts,
May my conduct also be like that. ^10-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-55]]

For as long as space remains,
And for as long as sentient beings remain,
Until then, may I too remain,
To dispel the sufferings of the world. ^10-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-56]]

Whatever sufferings sentient beings have,
May they all ripen upon me.
Through the assembly of Bodhisattvas,
May all beings enjoy happiness. ^10-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-57]]

May the teaching—the sole medicine for the suffering of beings
And the source of all happiness—
Endure for a long time,
Together with support and honor. ^10-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-58]]

I prostrate to Mañjughoṣa,
Through whose kindness a virtuous mind arises.
I also prostrate to my spiritual friend,
Through whose kindness I have flourished. ^10-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-a]]

This concludes the tenth chapter, entitled "Dedication," from the *Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva*. ^10-a
