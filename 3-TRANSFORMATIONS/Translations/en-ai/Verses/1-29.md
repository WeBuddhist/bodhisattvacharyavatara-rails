## Combined Commentary Summaries
The three classical commentators—Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme—express absolute consensus on the profound altruistic and soteriological import of Verse 1-29. They analyze this verse as the direct logical consequence of and remedy to the universal human tragedy diagnosed in Verse 1-28: because sentient beings are trapped in a cycle of self-induced suffering, the Bodhisattva's mind of enlightenment (*bodhicitta*) represents the only force capable of rescuing them through the perfect combination of love, compassion, and wisdom.

### 1. The Target of Altruism: The Plight of Sentient Beings
All three commentators define the target of the Bodhisattva's resolve by contrasting the desires of sentient beings with their actual, miserable circumstances.
*   **Destitute of Happiness:** The commentators emphasize the Sanskrit description of beings as "starving for happiness" (*sukharankānām*). **Ngulchu Thokme** describes them as "destitute of happiness," while **Sazang Mati Panchen** characterizes them as "wretched ones who are destitute and impoverished of happiness along with its causes." **Gyaltsab Je** similarly defines them as "destitute of happiness" and "ignorant of the methods of happiness and suffering."
*   **Tormented by Suffering:** The commentators highlight that beings are "afflicted in many ways" (*pīḍitānām anekaśaḥ*). **Ngulchu Thokme** notes they are "endowed with many sufferings," **Sazang Mati Panchen** states they "possess abundant sufferings along with their causes and are tormented," and **Gyaltsab Je** describes them as "endowed with abundant sufferings" and "running directly toward endless cycle of existence and the sufferings of bad migrations."

### 2. The Threefold Action of Bodhicitta: Love, Compassion, and Wisdom
The commentators explain that the mind of enlightenment does not merely wish for the welfare of others, but actively resolves to accomplish it through three distinct, powerful actions:
*   **Satisfying with Happiness (Great Love):**
    *   **Ngulchu Thokme** explains that the Bodhisattva satisfies these destitute beings with all happiness through "great love" (*བྱམས་པ་ཆེན་པོ* / *mahā-maitrī*).
    *   **Sazang Mati Panchen** specifies that this love satisfies them with "all the happiness of higher rebirth and liberation."
    *   **Gyaltsab Je** similarly explains that the Bodhisattva's activity satisfies them with "all the happiness of happy migrations (higher rebirth) and definitive goodness (liberation)."
*   **Cutting Off All Suffering (Great Compassion):**
    *   **Ngulchu Thokme** states that the Bodhisattva cuts off all suffering through "great compassion" (*སྙིང་རྗེ་ཆེན་པོ* / *mahā-karuṇā*).
    *   **Sazang Mati Panchen** notes that this compassion cuts off "all the sufferings of the cycle of existence and the hell realms."
    *   **Gyaltsab Je** asserts that this mind "cuts off all suffering" arising from cyclic existence.
*   **Dispelling Ignorance (Great Wisdom):**
    *   **Ngulchu Thokme** explains that the Bodhisattva dispels the ignorance of not knowing the causes of happiness and suffering through "great wisdom" (*ཤེས་རབ་ཆེན་པོ* / *mahā-prajñā*).
    *   **Sazang Mati Panchen** states that this wisdom clears their confusion by "showing them the way of adoption (of virtue) and avoidance (of non-virtue)."
    *   **Gyaltsab Je** emphasizes that it "dispels the ignorance of not knowing the causes of happiness and suffering through great wisdom."

### 3. The Absolute Incomparability of Bodhicitta
To conclude their analysis of Verse 1-29, the commentators raise a series of rhetorical questions to demonstrate that the Bodhisattva is the supreme being in the universe.
*   **No Equal Virtue:** All three commentators agree that there is no other virtue of equal strength or capacity to accomplish such vast welfare. **Gyaltsab Je** asks, "what other virtue is equal to this mind?" and **Sazang Mati Panchen** echoes, "what other virtue of equal strength is there to this motivation...?"
*   **The Ultimate Spiritual Friend:** **Gyaltsab Je** asks, "Where else is there such a helpful spiritual friend, who accomplishes all benefit and protects from all harm?" **Sazang Mati Panchen** and **Ngulchu Thokme** agree that there is no other trustworthy spiritual friend or companion (*kalyāṇamitra*) equal to the Bodhisattva, who acts as a savior to those who are otherwise helpless.
*   **The Ultimate Merit:** The commentators conclude that the merit of this mind is unmatched because it unerringly teaches all points of adoption and abandonment, ensuring that the self-destructive ignorance of worldly beings is finally illuminated and cured.
