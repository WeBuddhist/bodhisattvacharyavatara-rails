## Today's Verse

> If the mind arises wishing to neglect others' benefit,
> or wishing to pursue one's own benefit,
> or wishing to speak,
> then one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-52

> If impatience, laziness, fear,
> recklessness, idle talk,
> or a mind clinging to one's own side arises,
> at that time, one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-53

> Thus, having examined the mind
> that strives for all troubling emotions and meaningless things,
> the hero should firmly grasp
> the antidote at that time. ^5-54

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the fifty-second, fifty-third, and fifty-fourth verses of the fifth chapter. As soon as an affliction arises in the mind, we practice together how, like a hero, to hold the mind steady with the antidote.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us an explanation that is easy to understand and encouraging. First, we must always rely on mindfulness. By relying on mindfulness continually, we can give up pointless actions. By giving up pointless actions, we can give up harmful actions. Giving up harmful actions is the true meaning of guarding the conduct of the body. In the same way, today's verses teach us to stay still like wood. This is also the best method when a pointless or harmful thought arises. With mindfulness, we hold the thought before it slips into action.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when I want to pursue only my own benefit and neglect the benefit of others, I will stay still like a piece of wood.

**Explanation:** _(Doing good)_ When the wish to pursue only our own benefit arises, we can do nothing and keep body and mind steady. If we do, no harm reaches others. This makes our bad thought of cherishing ourselves smaller. If we stay still like wood in this way, great benefit comes to both ourselves and others.
