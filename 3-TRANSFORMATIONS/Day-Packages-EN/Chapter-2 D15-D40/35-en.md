# Day 35 — Vajrapani, and finally seeing the danger

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on verses 52 and 53 from the second chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. The list of protectors closes here with Vajrapani, and then the tone changes completely. Shantideva stops asking for help and admits something instead: in the past, I transgressed your words. After days of building up fear and calling out for rescue, the verses arrive at the part that is actually in his hands. Today asks us to be that specific about our own record, and then to do the one thing that follows from it.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

Two verses, and the second one turns.

The first still belongs to the calling out. Vajrapani is the one at whose mere appearance the messengers of the Lord of Death and the other hostile beings scatter in every direction. Then verse 53 stops asking. In the past, I transgressed your words.

The commentaries do not leave that phrase vague. The word he transgressed is a particular instruction, and they quote it: commit no wrongdoing, practise virtue fully. So this is not a general feeling of being unworthy. It is the admission that he was told plainly what to do and did otherwise, and that this is what all the fear of the last several verses is actually made of.

His Holiness the Dalai Lama draws the consequence. Going for refuge is not finished when you have said it. Having taken refuge, you have to train in what refuge asks of you and stop confusing what should be taken up with what should be dropped. Without that, verse 53 becomes the same transgression again in a more devotional voice.

Which is why the verse ends asking for fear to be removed, not for the past to be undone.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today I will name one thing I already know I should not be doing, say it plainly to myself, and then not do it for this one day.

**Explanation:** *(Avoiding wrongdoing)* Most of us do not need new information about this. There is usually something already on the list, known for a long time and quietly excused. Snapping at one particular person. A habit that is costing more than it gives back. Work I keep doing carelessly because nobody checks it. Today I will pick one of them, just one, and say it to myself in plain words rather than the softened version I normally use. The softened version is how it survives. Then I will leave it alone until tonight. One day is a short enough promise that I can actually keep it, and keeping it for one day is the whole difference between the confession in verse 53 and the transgression that verse is confessing. If I break it by lunchtime I will start the day again rather than write it off. Tomorrow can decide for itself.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

On the root lines — "At the sight of whom the messengers of the Lord of Death and the rest, who bear malice, take fright and scatter in the four directions: in the Vajra-Holder I take refuge" — where the text reads མཐོང་ནས ("having seen"), with the particle of source attached, the reading མཐོང་ན ("when one sees") is easier to understand. Whatever the object may be that, at the sight of it, the messengers of the Lord of Death and the others who bear minds of malice take fright and scatter in the four directions — in that Vajrapani too I take refuge, crying out from the very depths of my heart. And since simply having gone for refuge is not enough, one must train in the precepts of going for refuge and see to it that one does not confuse what is to be given up with what is to be taken up.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-52]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The word "whoever" is a general term applied here to one particular figure: the glorious Lord of Secrets. At the mere sight of him, the messengers of the Lord of Death — the executioners, and the birds and dogs and the rest, who bear malice like a patricide caught red-handed and who do harm — are utterly terrified and scatter in the four directions, or flee. In that Vajrapani, with faith and reverence, I take refuge. Moreover, according to the common explanation: from the time when all the buddhas of the three times first give rise to the awakening mind, through their full awakening and their turning of the wheel of Dharma, until they pass into nirvana, glorious Vajrapani bears the blazing vajra weapon, crushes the heads of all the malevolent ones who would harm the body, speech and mind of those victorious ones, and keeps watch over them — acting as worldly people describe a "deity born together with one," and following after them. Again, "the secret one" means a yaksha, and its lord is also called Vajrapani, lord of the yakshas. According to the uncommon way of secret mantra, he is the very embodiment of the vajra mind of all the victorious ones, empowered as the lord of the inconceivable secret of body, speech and mind, and he holds in his hand the vajra that is the token of that empowerment — hence the name. As for ourselves, there is simply no doing without at least one yidam deity of one's own devotion — one of the buddhas' heirs, such as the protectors of the three families. So one should request its empowerment, meditate on the deity and recite the mantra, and never let it slip from memory, being able to call it to mind whenever fear arises and even in the time of dreams at night. If that comes about, he says, then when death comes tomorrow there will arise the appearance of the deity coming forward to welcome one.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-52]]

On the words "At the sight of whom the messengers of the Lord of Death and the rest, who bear malice, take fright and scatter in the four directions: in the Vajra-Holder I take refuge" — the bodhisattva Vajrapani is one at the sight of whom the men or messengers of the Lord of Death, who carry out judgement upon the beings in hell, utterly terrifying and dreadful, in the forms of rakshasas, who bear malice towards the hell-beings and kill them, cut them down, beat and batter them, are all of them suddenly seized with fear and terror and scatter from one another and flee in the four directions, east and the rest. In that very bodhisattva, who bears in his right hand the vajra that is the token of the empowerment of all the buddhas of the three times, I too seek refuge, and again and again I take refuge.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Point of Crying Out for Refuge to Vajrapani, the Third Object of Refuge

All the commentaries agree in the end that one takes refuge in Vajrapani, at whose mere sight the messengers of the Lord of Death, the yakshas and rakshasas and all the other beings who bear malice towards living beings take fright and scatter in the four directions.

###### 2. What Vajrapani Is — the Common Explanation

Kunzang Pelden explains that glorious Vajrapani is the guardian who follows after the victorious ones, crushing the heads of all the malevolent ones who would harm the body, speech and mind of the buddhas of the three times, and keeping watch over them.

###### 3. What Vajrapani Is — the Uncommon Explanation of Secret Mantra

Kunzang Pelden explains that, according to the uncommon way of secret mantra, Vajrapani is the very embodiment of the vajra mind of all the victorious ones, empowered as the lord of the inconceivable secret of body, speech and mind.

###### 4. What Vajrapani Is — Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk's Explanation

He explains that the bodhisattva Vajrapani is the one who bears in his right hand the vajra that is the token of the empowerment of all the buddhas of the three times.

###### 5. What the Messengers of the Lord of Death Are Like, and the Power of the Sight of Vajrapani

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that the messengers of the Lord of Death are the men who carry out judgement upon the beings in hell, taking the forms of rakshasas, who bear malice towards the hell-beings and kill them, cut them down, beat and batter them; and he explains that the moment they see Vajrapani they are suddenly terrified and flee in the four directions.

###### 6. This Going for Refuge Belongs to the Power of Confessing Wrongdoing

Minyak Kunzang Sonam states plainly that this taking of refuge in Vajrapani is not the same as the general going for refuge shown earlier, but is part of the power of confessing wrongdoing.

###### 7. The Practice of Receiving a Yidam Deity's Empowerment and Keeping It Constantly in Mind

Kunzang Pelden explains that if one requests the empowerment of whichever yidam deity one is drawn to, meditates on the deity and recites the mantra, and can call it to mind whenever fear arises and even in the time of dreams, then at the moment of death the appearance will arise of the yidam coming forward to welcome one.

###### 8. The Transition: One Must Train in the Precepts of Going for Refuge

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that merely having gone for refuge is not enough; one must train in the precepts of going for refuge and see to it that one does not confuse what is to be given up with what is to be taken up — and he links this to the meaning of the following verse.

At the mere sight of him, the messengers of the Lord of Death, the yakshas and rakshasas and all the other beings who bear malice towards living beings take fright and scatter in the four directions; and in him one takes refuge. <!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Since merely having gone for refuge is not enough — one must train in the precepts of going for refuge and see to it that one does not confuse what is to be given up with what is to be taken up — the sense is this: in the past I went against your word and was unable to keep from mistaking what is to be given up and what is to be taken up; but now, having seen these many kinds of fear, I take refuge in you, and from now on I shall act as I should, without confusing what is to be given up with what is to be taken up. So please protect me quickly from fear. And the way one is freed from fear is this: with regard to the causes of one's own suffering, one confesses the wrongdoing done before and restrains oneself from it; one keeps up the intention to restrain oneself from now on, and so gives up wrongdoing; and one accomplishes virtue, rejoicing in the virtue gathered before and from now on accomplishing virtue on a vast scale. Such is the giving up and taking up that has to be done.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-53]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

I, whose actions have been bad, went in the past against the word of you victorious ones and your heirs — your word about what is to be taken up and what given up, virtue and wrongdoing. But now, seeing the great fear of the moment of death, of the intermediate state, and of the lower realms in the life to come, I come to you protectors who possess great compassion and take refuge in you — just as one who has stumbled gathers himself at the very place where he fell and rises again by leaning on a support. And so one prays: "Quickly clear away those fears of suffering, the result of wrongdoing and non-virtue."

##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

*(Outline heading:)* Praying out of regret. I, in my confusion, went in the past beyond the word and the command that you buddhas and bodhisattvas gave out of your great tender love — "Give up wrongdoing and accomplish virtue" — transgressing it; I did and gathered actions of wrongdoing and did not do what was virtuous, and so for a long time, from beginningless time, I have undergone measureless suffering in samsara in general and in the lower realms in particular. But now, seeing more and more clearly the unbearable fear that is the result of such wrongdoing, I have become frightened and terrified. And so, with a mind that from this day on enters upon giving up wrongdoing and accomplishing virtue properly, in accordance with your word, I take refuge in you who possess great compassion. Please, by the power of your compassion, clear away swiftly all the fear and suffering that is the result of the wrongdoing I gathered before.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-53]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

As for "in the past I went against your word": from earlier births, from lifetimes without beginning up until now, I went against the word and the pledge of you — the Three Jewels together with the eight close bodhisattva sons — and gathered many actions of wrongdoing and non-virtue, and so have wandered here in samsara. But now, seeing with great fear and dread the dreadfulness of the suffering of samsara, both general and particular — "in you," that is, in short, in the Buddha, the Dharma and the noble bodhisattvas, the Three Jewels — in you I take refuge from the depths of my heart. Please protect me, a person who has done wrong, swiftly from these fears of samsara, general and particular, and make all that fear and suffering cease — clear it away. With this request, he shows the very manner of confessing wrongdoing by way of the power of the support.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-53]]

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **sabzang / minyak-kunzang-sonam:** they take the sense of "you" to be "the victorious ones together with their heirs" / "the buddhas and bodhisattvas" — that is, the buddhas and bodhisattvas in general. ⚑
- **khenpo-kunga:** he explains the sense of "you" as the Three Jewels, together with the eight close bodhisattva sons. ⚑
- **kunpal / gyaltsab / ngulchu-thogmed / tenzin-gyatso:** they do not state the sense of "you" explicitly, explaining it in the same way as the objects of refuge in the preceding verses (Avalokiteshvara, Vajrapani and the rest); tenzin-gyatso alone, having said at the close of his commentary on verse 2-52 that one takes refuge in Vajrapani himself, joins the present passage to it as continuing the same context. ⚑

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Terrified by the Fear of the Result of Wrongdoing, One Cries Out for Refuge to the Objects of Refuge

All the commentaries agree in the end that, although in the past one went against the word of those who are worthy to be a refuge, now, seeing the great danger of the result of non-virtue, one takes refuge from the depths of the heart and prays that every fear be cleared away quickly.

###### 2. The Commentaries Differ Over Who "You," the Object of Refuge, Is

⚑ — Sabzang and Minyak Kunzang Sonam explain it as the buddhas and bodhisattvas in general, while Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains it as the Three Jewels together with the eight close bodhisattva sons; so there is a difference in how the object of refuge is identified.

###### 3. What the Fear Is in Particular — the Suffering of the Moment of Death, of the Intermediate State, and of the Lower Realms

Kunzang Pelden explains that one sees the great fear of the moment of death, of the intermediate state, and of the lower realms in the life to come; Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk speaks of the dreadful suffering of samsara, both general and particular.

###### 4. The Way of Seeking Refuge Is Shown by the Image of Rising Again by Leaning on a Support After a Fall

Kunzang Pelden explains the way of seeking refuge as being like gathering oneself at the place where one has fallen and rising again by leaning on a support.

###### 5. Merely Having Gone for Refuge Is Not Enough: One Must Train in the Precepts of What to Give Up and What to Take Up

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that merely having gone for refuge is not enough; one must train in the precepts of going for refuge and act as one should, without confusing what is to be given up with what is to be taken up — confessing and restraining the wrongdoing done before, keeping up the intention to restrain oneself and so giving up wrongdoing, rejoicing in virtue, and accomplishing virtue on a vast scale. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen likewise explains that one prays for fear to be cleared away by accomplishing what is to be taken up and what given up in accordance with your word.

###### 6. The "Word" That Was Transgressed Is the Instruction to Give Up Wrongdoing and Accomplish Virtue

Sabzang Mati Panchen explains that one regrets having gone against the word, "Do not commit any wrong at all; practise virtue in its fullness."

###### 7. How One Regrets Having Gone Against the Word in the Past and Gathered Wrongdoing

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that "I, in my confusion," having transgressed the command given by the buddhas and bodhisattvas, did and gathered actions of wrongdoing and did not do what was virtuous, and so from beginningless time have undergone the sufferings of samsara.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows how, having in the past gone against the word of the objects of refuge, one now sees the great danger of the result of wrongdoing, takes refuge from the depths of the heart, and prays that every fear be cleared away quickly. All the commentaries explain it in the same way.

**Key points.**

- The reason for crying out for refuge is that one sees the great danger that is the result of the wrongdoing done in the past, when one went against the word of the objects of refuge.
- What that fear contains includes the suffering of the moment of death, of the intermediate state, and of the lower realms.
- The commentaries differ over who "you," the object of refuge, is: Sabzang and Minyak Kunzang Sonam explain it as the buddhas and bodhisattvas in general, while Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains it as the Three Jewels together with the eight close bodhisattva sons. ⚑
- Kunzang Pelden gives, as an image of the way one seeks refuge, gathering oneself at the place where one has fallen and rising again by leaning on a support.
- Sabzang cites the verse "Do not commit any wrong at all; practise virtue in its fullness" as the source of the "word" that was transgressed.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that merely having gone for refuge is not enough: the precepts about what to give up and what to take up must actually be put into practice.
