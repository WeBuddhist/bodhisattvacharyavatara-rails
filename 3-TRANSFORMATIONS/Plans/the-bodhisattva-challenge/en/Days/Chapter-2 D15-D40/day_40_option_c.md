# Day 40 — Clearing the ground

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Look back over the ground you've cleared, and rest in it
> **Detail:** Chapter Two ends today. The confession is complete; next comes not more clearing, but planting.

## Opening

Today closes the whole Confession chapter, and forty days of walking through it. The verse seals the confession, and then the text simply notes that the second chapter is finished. It is worth pausing here. All this looking at fear, death, and our own mistakes was never the point in itself. It was clearing the ground. And the very next chapter does not ask for more digging; it begins by rejoicing.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

> The second chapter, “Confessing Misdeeds,” from Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, treats these last verses as the sealing of the whole work. Confession, in his reading and in the others, is not one act but several fitted together: turning to the protectors, honest regret, the resolve never to repeat, and the reliance on refuge. When they are all present, the verse says the deeds are cleared from the root, and the colophon can simply announce that the chapter is done. That completeness is the point of today. It is easy to keep circling the same old faults, confessing endlessly and trusting nothing. The tradition says: when you have honestly named it, meant the turn, and handed it over, the matter is finished, so let it be finished. The ground is cleared. And clearing was only ever the preparation. The next chapter turns to what grows there, and it opens, tellingly, not with more remorse but with joy.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Take a few quiet minutes to look back over these confession days. Notice what you have honestly faced and set down, and let it be genuinely finished rather than something to keep reopening. Then, in the spirit of the chapter that comes next, find one small thing to rejoice in, a bit of good you did, or someone else's. You have cleared the ground; let yourself feel it, and turn toward what you would like to grow there.
