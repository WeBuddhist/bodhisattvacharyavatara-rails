## Today's Verse

> Whenever one wishes to act
> or wishes to speak,
> one should first examine one's own mind
> and remain with steady awareness. ^5-47

> Whenever one's mind desires attachment
> or wishes to be angry,
> one should not act or speak,
> but remain like a piece of wood. ^5-48

> Whether prone to laughter or mockery,
> or if arrogant and puffed up,
> or with the intention of exposing faults,
> or if flattering and deceitful, ^5-49

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the forty-seventh, forty-eighth, and forty-ninth verses of the fifth chapter. This practice is to look at our own mind for a moment, before we move our body or speak. It is very easy. Yet very few people actually do it.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following points, which are easy to understand and encouraging. The small faults in our mind are not very obvious, so they are hard to recognize. Restlessness makes the mind scattered and unsteady. Mockery means making fun of others and putting them down. Pride fills the mind with seven kinds of pride. Arrogance means becoming haughty by relying on five things: family, looks, youth, learning, and strength. Exposing faults means searching for the faults of others and announcing them. Going back on our word means turning away from what we promised and not supporting it. So we must always check whether these small faults are in our own mind.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, before I do anything with my body or my speech, I will examine my own mind.

**Explanation:** _(Wisdom Practice)_ Before we speak and before we act with our body, we must look at the intention in our mind. If we begin an action while hopes, fears and anger are in the mind, we make mistakes and feel distress. If we examine the mind first and act calmly, both we and others will benefit.
