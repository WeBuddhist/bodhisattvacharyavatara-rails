### Notification

**Title:** Renew your resolve so it doesn't fade  
**Detail:** Once the resolve is real, good keeps growing on its own, as long as it doesn't fade. Today, renew it.

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's verses compare the two minds by what each one leads to. The wish to awaken brings great results, but they come and go. The mind that has actually set out is different. Once you take it up and truly mean to free all beings, good keeps growing on its own, even while you sleep or forget about it.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

Today's three verses show very clearly the difference in benefit between "aspiring bodhicitta" and "engaging bodhicitta."

**Verse 17 shows the benefit of the aspiring mind.** As the sutra says, even a cracked piece of precious diamond still outshines every golden ornament. In the same way, simply giving rise to the aspiring mind — even without actually doing the practice — outshines the qualities of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas and brings the great result of a good rebirth as a god or human. Even so, this alone does not create a non-stop stream of merit the way the engaging mind does.

**Verses 18 and 19 show the wonderful power of the engaging mind.** From the moment you truly take on, and keep unbroken, the firm vow to actually practise in order to free all beings from suffering, something amazing happens. From then on — even while you are asleep or not paying attention — your merit keeps growing more and more, without stopping, as vast as the sky. This shows how valuable it is to put our good intentions into real practice and firm commitment.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice**: Tonight, before I go to sleep, I will dedicate all the good things I did today to the benefit of all living beings.

**Explanation:** Today's verse explains that once you develop a truly kind heart, your good merit keeps growing continuously—even when you are sleeping or just relaxing. It becomes as limitless as the sky. By dedicating your good actions right before you sleep, you unlock this endless flow of positive energy.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Of the two just explained — the mind that aspires to awakening and the mind that engages in awakening — if one asks what benefit there is in cultivating the mind that aspires to awakening: while one abides in samsaric existence, there is inconceivably great benefit and power, illustrated by the excellent results of high status, that carry one from happiness to happiness. Even so, aspiration bodhicitta does not possess the kind of merit that increases of itself, uninterruptedly, even during ethically neutral (*lung ma bstan*) states of mind — such as heedlessness or being asleep — when a virtuous attitude is not manifest. So he says.

<!-- cm:⚑-Divergences -->

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^1-17]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->
##### Zhenphen Jampé Gocha (annotation commentary)

Merely by giving rise to the aspiring mind, great results arise while one circles in samsara; but merit does not arise continuously and unbroken as it does from the engaging mind. In saying this, the verse chiefly sets out the difference — between the aspiring and the engaging minds — in their results and in their merit.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-17]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Second: merely giving rise to the mind that aspires to awakening, while circling here in samsara, brings the vast and excellent result of the sovereignty of a king of gods such as Brahmā or Indra, or of a human wheel-turning monarch and the like; yet it does not — as the engaging mind does — bring merit, or virtue such as generosity and ethical discipline, in a continuous, unbroken stream.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-17]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

From the *Liberation of Ārya Maitreya*:

> Son of noble family, just as a precious vajra-gem, even when cracked, outshines every distinguished golden ornament, does not lose the name "precious vajra-gem," and dispels all poverty — son of noble family, in the same way, the precious vajra-gem of generating the mind set on omniscience, even bereft of diligent practice, outshines all the ornaments of the qualities of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas, does not lose the name "bodhisattva," and dispels all the poverty of samsara.

As this says: from the mind aspiring to awakening, great results arise while one abides in samsara; yet merit does not arise continuously and unbroken as it does — as an unbroken stream of merit — from the engaging mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-17]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Even bereft of practice, merely from giving rise to the mind that aspires to awakening, at the temporary stage while one wanders in samsara one gains great results — such as attaining the excellent sovereignty of gods and humans, and so forth; yet merit, or virtue, does not arise continuously and unbroken as it does from the mind that engages in generosity and the rest, carried out with diligent effort.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^1-17]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

One might object: does the benefit [of the aspiring mind] surpass even that of the engaging mind? If the aspiring mind is not seized by the activity of practising a bodhisattva's conduct, then it amounts only to a claim about the result, so the benefits described earlier could not arise; but if it is seized by practice, it would be no different from the engaging mind.

The aspiring mind, generated by way of aspiring to awakening, though bereft of diligent effort in practice and application, is still a cause of buddhahood itself. So even at the temporary stage, while abiding in samsara, one attains the sovereignty of gods and humans, turns back the poverty of samsara, and so on — great results and benefits arise. Therefore it should not be disparaged.

One might then say: in that case it would be no different from the engaging mind. It would not. Although the aspiring mind yields such results, merit — the cause, the accumulation of virtue — does not arise in a continuous, unbroken stream from the aspiring mind as it does from the engaging mind, which is joined with the diligent effort of actually practising generosity and the rest.

From the *Liberation of Maitreya* in the *Gaṇḍavyūha*:

> Son of noble family, for example: a precious vajra-gem, even when cracked, outshines every distinguished golden ornament, does not lose the name "precious vajra-gem," and turns back all poverty. Son of noble family, in the same way, the precious vajra-gem of generating the mind set on omniscience, even bereft of diligent practice, outshines all the golden ornaments of the qualities of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas, does not lose the name "bodhisattva," and turns back all the poverty of samsara.

It is as this and similar passages say. The words "even when the precious vajra-gem is cracked" show the aspiring mind bereft of practice and application.

In the *Śikṣāsamuccaya* too, this same scripture and the *Advice to the King* are cited:

> Great king, since you have much to do and much to attend to, you cannot train — always, everywhere, in every way — in the perfection of generosity through to the perfection of wisdom. Therefore, great king, whether you walk or stand, sleep or wake, eat or drink, constantly and continuously keep in mind, hold in your heart, and cultivate your longing, faith, earnest striving, and aspiration toward perfect complete awakening.

Having [taught] this directly, it is said: even bereft of practice, this generation of the aspiring mind produces an exceedingly great ripening of merit, so it should not be disparaged. Therefore, even one who cannot train in the conduct of the six perfections should by all means train in the ripening mind of awakening.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-17]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Again, with respect to that very aspiring mind alone — the mere aspiration, such as "may I attain the state of perfect awakening, the result," or "how good it would be if I attained it" — while one circles through the three realms of samsara, virtuous and meritorious results arise, vast and in great number.

From the *Gaṇḍavyūha sutra*:

> For example, just as a precious vajra-gem, even when cracked, outshines every distinguished golden ornament and does not lose the name "precious vajra-gem" — exactly as in that example, so too those beginner bodhisattvas who have only just given rise to the supreme mind of awakening, even if they lack actual exertion or diligence on the path, outshine all those [qualities], which are like the golden ornaments of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas, and do not lose the name "bodhisattva."

As it says thus, although such vast results arise, [merit] does not arise continuously — with the very nature of being unbroken — as it does from the root of virtue of the engaging mind, which actually engages in the practice of the six perfections, the path that brings one to attain the state of perfect awakening.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-17]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Great Results Arise in Samsara From the Aspiring Mind

Even without the practice of conduct, merely giving rise to the aspiring mind can win, within samsara, the excellent sovereignty of a king of gods such as Brahmā or Indra, or of a human wheel-turning monarch; so it should not be disparaged.

###### 2. The Distinctive Feature of a Continuous Stream of Merit

Though the aspiring mind brings great results, it does not — as the engaging mind does, engaged with diligent effort in the perfections such as generosity — produce merit in a continuous, unbroken stream; therefore the engaging mind is supreme.

###### 3. The Vajra Example

Just as a cracked vajra still outshines golden ornaments, so the aspiring mind, though it has not entered the path, outshines the qualities of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas and does not lose the name "bodhisattva."

###### 4. The Instruction Not to Disparage the Aspiring Mind

Even one who cannot train in the six perfections should by all means train in ripening the mind of awakening.

###### 5. During Ethically Neutral States, the Aspiring Mind's Merit Does Not Increase

Tenzin Gyatso specifies the technical reason for verse 17's claim: during ethically neutral (*lung ma bstan*) mental states such as heedlessness or sleep, when virtuous intention is not manifest, aspiration bodhicitta (*smon sems*) does not produce the kind of naturally continuous, ever-increasing merit that engagement bodhicitta (*'jug sems*) does. This sets up the contrast developed in the next verse.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** From the aspiring mind, great results arise within samsara; yet, unlike the engaging mind — which actually engages in the path of the perfections — merit does not arise continuously and unbroken. In this way the verse clearly sets out the difference in benefit between the two.

**Key points.**

- From the aspiring mind, great results such as the sovereignty of gods and humans arise within samsara; yet merit does not arise continuously as it does from the engaging mind.
- Just as a cracked vajra still outshines golden ornaments, so the aspiring mind, though it has not entered the path, outshines the qualities of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas.
- Even one unable [to train] in the perfections is counselled to hold the aspiring mind and keep it firm and constant; it is certain to be a cause that ripens the mind of awakening.
- ⚑ There is a divergence in how the source of the vajra example is cited: for gyaltsab it is known as the Liberation of Maitreya, while for minyak-kunzang-sonam and khenpo-kunga it is known as the Gaṇḍavyūha sutra. ⚑
- **His Holiness the Dalai Lama** — sets out the further key reason why the [aspiring mind's] merit does not increase: during ethically neutral states of mind (times of heedlessness or sleep), the aspiring mind has no continuous increase.

##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

*Note: minyak-kunzang-sonam groups verses 1-18 and 1-19 under consecutive transclusions; the following prose covers both.*

From whatever moment, in order to completely free the boundless realms of sentient beings from the bondage of the origin — karma and afflictions — and from the prison of samsara, whose nature is suffering, and to establish them in perfect awakening, one genuinely takes up into one's own mind-stream the engaging mind-generation, with a mind that will never turn from others' welfare for as long as existence lasts: from that moment on, the mind-generation remains unimpaired. Thereafter, whether one becomes heedless through sleep or mental distraction — or, as included by the word "even," faints, is intoxicated, walks, stands, or sits — the momentum of the meritorious virtue comprised in the three kinds of ethical discipline, vast, unbroken, and inexhaustible, arises without interruption in each and every instant; and that too arises immeasurably, to a measure equal to space.

One may also explain "with a mind that does not turn back" as the thought "even at the cost of my life I will not give it up"; and by this, [the verse] is said to teach the excellence of intention. Moreover, since the aspiring mind amounts only to yearning for the result, its result too arises accordingly; but the engaging mind has entered upon actually accomplishing the direct cause of attaining buddhahood, so during sleep and the like the roots of virtue comprised within the six perfections arise ever-increasing and uninterrupted.

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

*Note: khenpo-kunga groups verses 1-18 and 1-19 under consecutive transclusions; the following prose covers both.*

Focusing on the state of perfect awakening and properly entering its cause, the path — that is, taking up and holding in one's own mind-stream the vow of the mind-generation, once pledged and committed — in order to free those boundless realms of sentient beings from all the sufferings of samsara, both general and specific, one gives rise, through diligent effort, to a mind that will not turn back from that path of proper endeavour, and takes it into practice. Thus that very mind-generation is genuinely taken up into one's own mind-stream. It may be taken either before a support [an image or representation], by way of the ritual complete in its three parts — preparation, main practice, and conclusion — or before an abbot, master, or spiritual friend. So he says.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. "From Whatever Moment" — Marking a Point in Time

With the words "from whatever moment," a definite point in time is set out: the moment when the mind of awakening is genuinely taken up. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk says it may be taken up either by the ritual or before an abbot, master, or spiritual friend.

###### 2. The Pledge to Completely Free the Boundless Realms of Sentient Beings

The pledge to practise the six perfections in order to completely free the boundless, limitless realms of sentient beings from the suffering of samsara and establish them on the ground of buddhahood. Minyak Kunzang Sonam expresses it vividly as "completely freeing [beings] from the bondage of the origin — karma and afflictions — and from the prison of samsara, whose nature is suffering."

###### 3. The Manner of Engaging With a Mind That Does Not Turn Back

With "with a mind that does not turn from others' welfare, one engages," the distinctive irreversibility of the engaging mind is shown. Khenpo Zhenga's annotation says it engages in generosity and the rest. Minyak Kunzang Sonam allows that it may also be glossed as the thought "even at the cost of my life I will not give it up," and says this teaches the excellence of intention.

###### 4. Genuinely Taken Up and Guarded Unimpaired

Not merely taking up the mind of awakening, but guarding it unimpaired: this key point is shown. Sabzang, with "genuinely taken up by the ritual," sees great significance in receiving it through the ritual. Gyaltsab says that, distinguished from the aspiring mind, the engaging mind is seized by accomplishing the complete cause of buddhahood.

###### 5. The Difference Between the Aspiring and Engaging Minds

Both Gyaltsab and Minyak Kunzang Sonam set out the difference clearly in this verse: the aspiring mind amounts only to yearning for the result, whereas the engaging mind has entered upon actually accomplishing the direct cause of attaining buddhahood; therefore its roots of virtue arise continuously and ever-increasing.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse marks the point in time when, with a mind that does not turn back, the mind of awakening is genuinely taken up. From whatever moment one genuinely takes up into one's own mind-stream — and guards unimpaired — the mind pledged to practise the six perfections for the boundless realms of sentient beings, from that moment on the cause of all well-being and goodness arises uninterruptedly. So it is said.

**Key points.**
- With "from whatever moment," the point in time when the mind of awakening is taken up is definitely stated.
- The basis of purpose is the boundless, limitless realms of sentient beings — to free them from the suffering of samsara and quiescent peace and [lead them] to attain buddhahood.
- The mind that does not turn back is the engaging mind that engages in the six perfections; Minyak Kunzang Sonam says it may also be explained as the thought "even at the cost of my life I will not give it up." ⚑
- Sabzang, with "genuinely taken up by the ritual," sees the point of taking it up by way of the ritual path, while Minyak Kunzang Sonam, with "taken up into one's own mind-stream," sees the point of its entering the mind-stream.
- Both Gyaltsab and Minyak Kunzang Sonam set out the key doctrinal point at length: the aspiring mind amounts only to yearning for the result, whereas the engaging mind has entered upon actually accomplishing the cause that brings the attainment of buddhahood.

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Thus, at whatever time, having taken the mind that aspires to awakening as the condition, one sees that in order to attain awakening one must train in the conduct; and, gaining the certainty that without training in the conduct one cannot attain awakening, one pledges and commits to training in the conduct — "from this time on, I will properly train in the entire conduct of a bodhisattva." Thus one resolves, in a manner free of doubt. In order to completely free all the measureless and boundless realms of sentient beings, with an irreversible mind — that is, having resolved from the depths of the heart, with a firm pledge, to train in the conduct — from the moment one genuinely takes up that very engaging mind, once pledged and committed, then by its momentum, even during states of heedlessness (such as when the person endowed with [that mind-stream] is asleep or intoxicated), the momentum of merit arises uninterruptedly, measureless, equal to space. So he says.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse says that for the person who has genuinely taken up the engaging mind, even while asleep or heedless, merit arises uninterruptedly, equal to space. In this verse Kunpal sees both the point of benefit and the grave fault that accompanies its importance: once the engaging vow is taken up, if one does not continually apply oneself to generosity and the rest, the grave fault of a root downfall arises. So he says.

**Key points.**
- Once the mind of awakening is genuinely taken up, in each and every instant the merit born of the three ethical disciplines arises uninterruptedly, as marked by Khenpo Zhenga's annotation.
- Kunpal says that even if one becomes heedless through sleep or play and the like, merit arises higher and higher.
- Kunpal says that once the engaging vow has been taken up, failing to apply it in practice brings the grave fault of a root downfall; and he acknowledges that although this is not set out explicitly in this verse, it is the essential intended meaning of the text.
- The *King of Samādhi* says that, when joined with the means of accomplishment, accomplishing awakening is not hard; and in this connection Khenpo Zhenga's annotation adduces such a scripture.
- Gyaltsab, Sabzang, Minyak Kunzang Sonam, and Khenpo Kunga all take the taking up of the engaging mind in the preceding verse together with this verse — grouping the two under one and explaining their key point as one.
