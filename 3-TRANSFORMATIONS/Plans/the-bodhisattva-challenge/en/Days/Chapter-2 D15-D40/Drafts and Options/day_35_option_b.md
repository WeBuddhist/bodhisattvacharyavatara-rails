# Day 35 — Coming back after walking away

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Come back today to one thing you knew and let slide
> **Detail:** Today's verse says plainly: I walked away before, and now I return. The door is open.

## Opening

The verse is honest in a way that is easy to miss. Shantideva does not pretend he has been faithful all along. He says it straight: I broke your word before. He had received the guidance and let it slide. And then the turn: but now, having seen where that leads, I come back and take refuge in you. There is no suggestion that it is too late.

## From the Tradition

Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, keeps the sequence of the verse intact, because the order is the lesson. First the admission: I transgressed your command and did wrong. Then, without a gap, the return: now, seeing where it led, I take refuge in you, swiftly dispel this fear. He does not have Shantideva grovel or earn his way back in. The honest naming of the lapse and the turning back happen in the same breath. This matters for anyone who has ever taken up a practice, drifted from it, and then felt too embarrassed to return. The verse shows there is no waiting period and no closed door. You say plainly that you walked away, and in the same moment you walk back. Ngulchu even pictures the reunion warmly elsewhere: looking up, seeing the protector still there, blazing in the sky, and gladly keeping his company again.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> By the merit of practicing

## Today's Practice

Bring to mind one thing you knew was right and quietly let slide: a practice you dropped, a resolve you broke, a standard you stopped keeping. Without scolding yourself for the gap, simply come back to it today. Name it plainly, "I let this go, and I am returning to it now," and take one small step back onto it. Returning is always available.
