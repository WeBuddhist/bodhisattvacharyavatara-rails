# Day 20 — Your ordinary day, made an offering

> **Notification**
> **Title:** When you smell or see something lovely today, offer it
> **Detail:** Shantideva offers scent and flowers in his mind. Today, lift the lovely things you already meet into offerings.

## Opening

For days the offerings have been grand, jewelled bathhouses, robes, oceans of flowers, all built in the mind. Today the same scent and flowers appear, the finest perfumes and mandarava blossoms offered to the buddhas. But the gentle invitation underneath is small and close to home: the beautiful things are not only in the imagination. Some of them are already in your day.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Most of us walk past a dozen small, lovely things a day without really seeing them.

The verse offers perfume and flowers, and Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, describes a scent so fine it spreads through countless worlds and flowers gathered by the armful. The point the tradition draws out is not that you must imagine something grander. It is that the lovely things in front of you can be lifted up the same way. The smell of coffee, a flower by the path, warm light on a wall: instead of letting them pass unnoticed, you can offer them in your mind to the buddhas. Nothing about the day changes except your attention, and that quietly turns an ordinary morning into something that gathers merit.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Once or twice today, when a pleasant smell or a beautiful sight reaches you, stop for a breath and quietly offer it to the buddhas and bodhisattvas before moving on. You do not have to change your day at all, only notice what is already in it and pass it upward.
