# Day 30 — The day is spending itself

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Give one stretch of today to something that matters
> **Detail:** Today's verses watch life leak away, day and night. Spend a little of it well, on purpose.

## Opening

Yesterday's reflection on death sharpens into something plainer. Shantideva notices that his life is being used up every hour, day and night, with nothing pausing and nothing ever added back. He sees that when death comes he will go through it alone, and that the only thing that will help him then is the good he has actually done. These are honest verses, and they are meant to wake us up, not weigh us down.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, starts from something we never actually notice. Whether we are walking, sitting, or asleep, this life is being spent the whole time, day and night, without a single moment's pause, and nothing we do adds even a little back. The clock only runs one way. He does not stop at that fact. He uses it. If the time is leaving anyway, the only sane response is to put some of it toward what lasts. He then follows the thought to its end: when death comes, the relatives around the bed cannot feel the pain for us, and Yama's messengers are not impressed by our connections. Only the good we have actually done comes with us, and his honest regret is that he built so little of it. The point is not to dread the leak of time, but to let a little of it drain somewhere worthwhile.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Pick one ordinary stretch of today, a commute, a queue, a coffee, and notice it quietly passing. The day is spending itself whether you use it or not. Give just that one stretch to something that matters: a few breaths of practice, a kind message, one honest minute. The time was leaving anyway; let a little of it leave well.
