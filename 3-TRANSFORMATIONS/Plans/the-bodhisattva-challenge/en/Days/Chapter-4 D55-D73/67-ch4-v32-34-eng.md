## Today's Verse

> My enemy, defilement,
> has been an enemy continuously for a beginningless time.
> No other enemy
> can last for such a long time. ^4-32

> If I serve my friends respectfully,
> they all bring benefit and happiness.
> But if I rely on defilements,
> they again inflict suffering and harm. ^4-33

> How can I be fearless and joyful in cyclic existence
> if this enemy, which has been a continuous foe for so long,
> the sole cause for the increase of all harm,
> is truly established in my heart? ^4-34

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 32 to 34 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Serving an outer enemy well can bring benefit, but the more we serve the inner enemy, the afflictions, the more harm it brings us again and again. This is the difference this lesson shows us clearly.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

For example, if a person wants to be kind to a snake and gives it milk, the snake will not become calm and gentle. The more milk it is given, the more its poison grows and gains strength. In the same way, if we follow our own anger and craving and satisfy their wishes, the afflictions do not calm down. The more we satisfy the wishes of the afflictions, the greater the harm they bring us.

Being kind to an ordinary enemy can turn them into a friend. But the more we serve the enemy of the afflictions, the more harm it does to us. This enemy, always dwelling in our own heart, takes away our happiness. So we must cut off the food of the afflictions. We must not feed them.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, the moment a thought of anger or craving arises in me, I will not feed it. I will stop it right away.

**Explanation:** _(Not feeding the afflictions)_ Being kind to an outer enemy can turn them into a friend. But serving and following the afflictions makes them grow much larger and brings us suffering. This is like giving milk to a snake: its poison only grows. So we must not let a thought of affliction grow. We must cut it off right away.
