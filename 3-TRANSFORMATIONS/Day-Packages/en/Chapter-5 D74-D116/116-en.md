## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and seventh, one hundred and eighth, and one hundred and ninth verses of the fifth chapter. Does a sick person get well just by saying the name of a medicine?

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. A very sick person gets no benefit if the doctor does not give medicine and only reads a medical text into the person's ear. But if the body takes the medicine and follows the proper way of living, it benefits. In the same way, merely saying or hearing words of Dharma accomplishes no purpose. Therefore, we must make an effort to put it into practice.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, I will not only read a lesson. I will actually put one Dharma teaching that I already know into practice with my body.

**Explanation:** *(Diligence Practice)* Merely reading a medical text cannot cure a sick person's illness. In the same way, merely saying words of Dharma brings no great benefit. Therefore, we must actually practice the meaning of the Dharma with our body.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

From whichever, whatever is not forbidden, / that very thing should be practiced; / in order to guard the worldly mind, / having seen the training, practice properly — this means: from whichever sutras and treatises, whatever actions are not forbidden, those very things must be practiced. In order to guard the minds of worldly people, one must see the trainings and then practice those trainings that are to be trained in, for the benefit and the happiness of the world.

In this way, what is to be adopted and what is to be abandoned, how it is and how it is not — one must come to know this by relying on a learned lama, and do well at listening, reflecting, and study. For those who practice the Dharma of the Buddhist insiders, one must know the Dharma; so the fact that study of the trainings has been begun at places like Tharpa Chöling nowadays is extremely good. It will certainly benefit the Buddha's teaching itself. If study of the trainings goes well, one can bring forth pure certainty that cuts off superimposition on the framework of the Buddha's teaching, and so faith, too, will have a firm root. Even when one teaches others: if one has done the three of burning, cutting, and rubbing and then comes to teach, the quality will be pure.

New people who are seeking such study of the trainings, and especially people from foreign countries who earlier had no special connection with the Dharma of the Buddhist insiders — that they take it up of their own wish and apply themselves to the study of the Buddhist Dharma is extremely good. I rejoice. In the same way, we Tibetans, though we are Tibetans, do not need to learn a new language: since one can understand the inner Dharma in one's own language, we meet no difficulty with language. It is easy to study in one's own language. There is a favorable circumstance unlike others, so if one pays attention, it will bring good benefit. As I mentioned earlier, this time copies of the *Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life* have been distributed, so look at the *Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life* again and again. From time to time, if there is someone giving teachings, listen.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-107]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

In whichever of the sutras and treatises just explained, those actions that bodhisattvas — in the manner of "this is not to be done with body and speech" and "this is not to be meditated on with the mind" — have not been forbidden but permitted: those very things should be practiced and accomplished, while those that are forbidden and not permitted should be abandoned with all one's strength. In brief, the purpose of training in all these things to be taken up and turned away from is said to be to protect the minds of worldly people, by way of abandoning their lack of faith and generating joy and delight in them. Therefore, one should look into the texts mentioned above that show how to abandon lack of faith, and so on; see and realize properly the points to be trained in; and then practice and train only in the methods that do not contradict them and that take beings under one's care. There is also the reading "Having seen the training, one does not practice," which means: having seen that one must train in order to guard the mind, one does not practice the causes of lack of faith. Thus, from the *Jewel Cloud Sutra*:

> In order to look after beings and to perfect his own accumulations for awakening, his conduct is excellent; he speaks gently and slowly, has little bustle and company, inclines toward complete solitude, and his face is clear and radiant — and so on, it says.

And in the *Precious Garland*, too:

> Dharma is the supreme way of conduct; / through Dharma the world is delighted, / and because the world is delighted, / one is not deceived in this life or the next.
> Whatever way of conduct is wanted that is not Dharma — / through that the world is displeased, / and because the world is displeased, / one will not be happy in this life or the next.

So it says. This shows how to engage in what scripture has not forbidden but permitted, and how to turn away from what it has forbidden and not permitted; and since it also condenses the need to rely on mindfulness of how to adopt and abandon properly in that way, this is also the condensed meaning of the earlier extensive explanation of how to rely on mindfulness.

In the states of one's own body, speech, and mind — whether one has come under the power of afflictions or not, whether it contradicts the training or not, whether it is virtuous or nonvirtuous, and whatever conduct it is, such as going and sitting — one examines again and again with the discriminating wisdom that knows things as they are, and so comes to know properly what to adopt and abandon. That very thing, in brief, is the definition of guarding awareness without letting it decline — so it is taught. #### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This very examination again and again of the states of one's own body and mind is, in brief, the definition of guarding awareness. This is an exhortation to put it into practice, not merely words.

**Key points.**

- With a mind that knows what to adopt and abandon, one must examine again and again all the states of one's own body, speech, and mind.
- A bodhisattva must examine, in the body's walking and moving and in the conceptual thoughts of speech and mind, whether they become virtuous, nonvirtuous, or neutral, and whether affliction is present or not.
- Relying on mindfulness and awareness and being strict, one must examine oneself again and again by oneself.

#### Root Verse

*Plain English translation (source: `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-translate/BCA-Full-Plain-English.md#^5-109`):*

> All of this must be put into practice by the body; what is accomplished by words alone? Does merely reading a medical treatise ever benefit the sick?

---

<!-- sub:interlinear -->
#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

"These things should be practiced earnestly with the body." What purpose is accomplished by merely saying in words, "One must practice in this way"? For example, if one merely reads a medical treatise but does not take the medicine, will it benefit the sick? Therefore, all that is to be done should be accomplished with earnestness. From the *Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life*: the chapter on guarding awareness, the fifth chapter.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^5-109]]

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

So it says. If one practices Dharma for the sake of benefit and happiness, not wanting suffering, then one must apply Dharma in practice and free oneself from suffering; merely showing dry words, saying "Dharma" with the mouth, cannot benefit. For example, a medical text does show the means of overcoming a severe illness, yet merely reading it brings no benefit. In the same way, this was said with very firm insistence on applying it in practice.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-109]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The trainings explained earlier through body, speech, and mind — "these things" — must actually be practiced and trained in earnestly; what need, or what purpose, is accomplished by merely reciting the words of the text or merely explaining them? It is not accomplished; therefore one must make effort to put the meaning into practice. For example, without taking medicine, will merely reading or explaining the *Four Tantras* of medicine and the like benefit the illness of the sick? It will not; rather, benefit comes from the body's taking the medicine and properly following conduct and so on.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-109]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

So it says: what purpose is accomplished by merely speaking words, without properly practicing these things with body and mind — just as merely reading a medical text does not benefit the sick. If one lacks the two eyes of mindfulness and awareness, then even if one has the strength of body such as learning, one cannot cross the vast ground of samsara; therefore, you who have understanding, hold mindfulness and awareness dear. The fifth chapter has been explained.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-185]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If, with body and speech led by the mind, one trains earnestly in these trainings and practices them, one will benefit; but what purpose is accomplished by merely uttering the words of the text? Nothing is accomplished at all, so make effort to put the meaning into practice. For example, without taking medicine, would merely reading a medical treatise benefit the sick? It would not benefit at all; rather, benefit comes from relying on the body's medicine and conduct and so on. As is also said in the *King of Samadhi Sutra*:

> Therefore, young man, one should train in making practice the essence. For one who makes practice the essence, unsurpassable, perfect, complete awakening is not difficult to find — as it says.

From the *Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life*, mainly (the chapter) called "Guarding Awareness" — this is the explanation of the fifth chapter.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-109]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Having thus become skilled in all the points to be trained in, then, by putting them into practice directly with body and speech, led by the mind, and by taking these trainings to heart with earnestness and practicing them, it will benefit one's own mind-stream; apart from that, if there is nothing at all of the meaning to put into practice and one merely recites and reads many varieties of the words of the texts, what purpose of benefiting oneself and others is accomplished? Nothing is accomplished at all. As an example, it is taught that it is like this: without taking medicine as the doctor has instructed, merely reading a medical treatise or amassing much medicine will not benefit the sick — not even a little — just as, when one is parched with thirst, merely seeing or hearing of water without drinking it does not quench thirst, and so on. In the *Avataṃsaka*:

> This teaching of the perfect buddhas / is not accomplished by merely hearing.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-109]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

With body, speech, and mind, these trainings of a bodhisattva, as explained earlier in the chapter's text, must be taken up properly and practiced; but what is accomplished by merely saying words such as "one must train in this and this," or by merely listening to words? No needed purpose at all is accomplished. For example: when a doctor comes to a place where a patient is gravely ill and, without giving medicine, merely reads a medical treatise into the patient's ear, will it benefit the patient? It will not. The Dharma is similar to that example — such is the meaning.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-109]]

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** The trainings must be put into practice with body, speech, and mind; nothing is accomplished by merely speaking words. One must apply Dharma in practice and free oneself from suffering.

**Key points.**

- Having become skilled in all the points to be trained in, one must put them into practice with the mind leading, and hold mindfulness and awareness dear.
- For example, just as merely reading a medical treatise without taking medicine does not benefit the sick, one must rely on medicine and on proper conduct.
- Just as a gravely ill patient does not recover if one merely reads a medical text and gives no medicine, one must apply the teaching in practice with great firmness.
