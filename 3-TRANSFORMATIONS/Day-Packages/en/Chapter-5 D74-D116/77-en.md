# Day 77 — The Perfection of Giving Is the Mind Itself

**Date:** Sep 20

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the eighth, ninth, and tenth verses of the fifth chapter. Let us think together about this remarkable question: since there are still poor people in the world, does that mean even the Buddha did not perfect generosity?

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a point that is easy to understand and encouraging.

If the perfection of generosity meant ending the poverty of every being in the world, then even the buddhas of the past would not have perfected it. The reason is simple: today, too, we still see many poor people in the world.

But the perfection of generosity does not depend on ending the poverty of others. It depends on our own mind. We must perfect the habit of giving without any stinginess. We give away our body, our possessions, and the roots and fruits of our good deeds to all beings. Perfecting this stingy-free mind of giving is the true perfection of generosity.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, I will help someone or give something to another person, with a mind free of attachment.

**Explanation:** *(Generosity Practice)* The perfection of generosity does not come from ending all poverty in the world. It comes from perfecting our own wish to give away our body and possessions, free of attachment. So, starting today, we should train our mind to give even small things to others, without any attachment.

### Verse 5-8

#### Root Verse

*Plain English translation (source: `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-translate/BCA-Full-Plain-English.md#^5-8`):*

> The Buddha taught that all of it comes from a mind full of wrongdoing; in the three worlds, therefore, there is no fear apart from the mind.

---

<!-- sub:interlinear -->
#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

All such things are taught by the Sage to have, as their cause, precisely the mind that actively accumulates sinful karma. And because this is so, therefore, in the three worlds, there is no other cause of fear apart from a sinful mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^5-8]]

---

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains: the Sage teaches that all such things arise through the power of an untamed, sinful mind; and since, in the three worlds below, upon, and above the earth, the root of every fear is this display of the mind, or its arising through the power of a sinful mind, there is, ultimately, nothing whatsoever beyond the mind that could be the source of fear.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-8]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

So the Blessed Sage, the Buddha, teaches in the sūtras that every such fearsome harm-doer arises through the power of one's own habituation to a sinful, non-virtuous mind — a point also explained in the *Sūtra Distinguishing Migrations*. Accordingly, in the three worlds — the three realms of desire, form, and formlessness, or the three worlds below, upon, and above the earth — there is no fear whatsoever apart from what arises through the power of one's own deluded mind. Therefore, he explains, if one guards one's own mind well and does not let it fall under the sway of distraction and delusion, every harm of this life and the next will vanish.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-8]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

If the sufferings experienced by the beings of hell, and the like, did not arise from their own past sinful mind, then someone else would have to be the deliberate agent who fashions the weapons of the hell-beings and so forth — yet there is no such other agent, and it is equally untenable that these things arise causelessly. Therefore, he explains, the Sublime Dharma — the *Foundations of Mindfulness* and other sūtras — teaches that all such things arise from the mind that has committed wrongdoing. He then refutes at length, as thoroughly unreasonable, the mistaken view of those who, lacking careful training in logic, argue — by analogy with how water appears as pus and blood to a preta — that the fire of hell, too, is merely an appearance produced by the ripening of past bad karma, and that no actual fire exists there; for this amounts to denigrating the working of karma and its result. Since every fault depends upon the mind, he explains that in all three worlds there is no fear apart from what one's own mind has done.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-8]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

The Sage teaches that all such things arise from a sinful mind, and that accordingly, in the three worlds, there is no fear apart from the mind — therefore the mind must be tamed. He clarifies this above by citing scriptural passages from the *Ratnamegha Sūtra*, the *Foundations of Mindfulness*, and others.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-8]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

The All-Knowing Sage, the Lord Endowed with All Sovereign Faculties, teaches that every such experience of suffering arises from a mind that has actively accumulated sinful karma — a point he clarifies above with scriptural citations from the *Sūtra Distinguishing Migrations*, the *Vajragarbha Sūtra*, and the *Daśabhūmika Sūtra*. Since all fear thus arises from the mind, he explains that in the three worlds of desire, form, and formlessness, there is no fear apart from a mind that has turned sinful, and so one should bring the mind under control and strive to guard it.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-8]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Since every fear and suffering arises solely from the mind, in all three worlds — the worlds below, upon, and above the earth, or the three realms of desire and the rest — there is no cause of fear apart from the mere appearance produced when one's own mind falls under the power of wrongdoing. Therefore, he says, one should bring the mind under control and strive to tame it — a point he clarifies above by citing the *Foundations of Mindfulness* and the great commentary on the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-8]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

The Teacher, the Lord Endowed with Sovereign Faculties, teaches in the *Vinaya-vibhaṅga Sūtra* that every such fearsome, harmful, deluded appearance is produced by one's own habituation to sinful, non-virtuous minds thoroughly incited by attachment and aversion, and by having committed wrongdoing. As explained above, in the three worlds — the world of nāgas below the earth, the world of humans upon the earth, and the world of gods above the earth, or the three realms of desire, form, and formlessness — he explains that all such fears or sufferings arise in dependence on this very self-grasping mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-8]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. All the Fears of the Three Worlds Arise from a Sinful Mind, and There Is No Other Cause of Fear Apart from the Mind

All the commentaries agree in explaining this, and it is the chapter's central summary point.

###### 2. A Wrong View That Denies Hell's Torments Amounts to Denying Karma and Its Result, and Is Refuted

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen refutes at length, as unreasonable, an argument that denies hell-fire based on the analogy of a preta's water appearing as pus and blood, since this ultimately amounts to denying karma and its result altogether.

###### 3. Where the Sinful Mind Is Absent There Is No Enmity or Fear, and a Mastered Mind Never Loses Its Happiness

Clarified by a citation from the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*.

###### 4. A Textual Variant in the Root Verse: "Mass of Weapons" Rather Than "Mass of Fire"

Both Gyaltsab Je and Ngulchu Thogme reaffirm this in their summary of the chapter.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** All the fearsome harms and sufferings of the three worlds arise from one's own sinful mind; since there is no other cause of fear apart from the mind, one must bring one's own mind under control, guard it, and tame it — a point on which all the commentators speak with a single voice.

**Key points.**

- **Root cause of fear.** All the fears and sufferings of the three worlds arise from habituation to, and lack of taming of, the mind that actively accumulates sinful, non-virtuous karma.
- **Benefit of guarding the mind.** If one guards one's own mind well and does not let it fall under the sway of delusion, every harm of this life and the next will vanish, and happiness will never be lost.
- **Meaning of the three realms.** "The three worlds" may be understood either as the three realms of desire, form, and formlessness, or as the three worlds below, upon, and above the earth.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

If this is so, how then should one regard generosity and the rest? One might think: giving is defined precisely as removing the poverty of sentient beings. But if the perfection of generosity consisted in giving to beings and thereby ridding them of poverty, then, since the poverty of beings has still not been pacified — since many destitute beings still exist — how could the past protectors ever have perfected the perfection of generosity?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^5-9]]

---

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains: with regard to the bodhisattva's practice of generosity, if the perfection of generosity had to be defined as freeing all beings from the suffering of poverty, then, since destitute and wretched beings of just this kind are visibly present even now, it would be reasonable to ask how the past protectors could ever have perfected the perfection of generosity — or would it? This, he explains, is the initial doubt raised at the start of the reply.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-9]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Second: generosity, too, resides within the inner mind. If the perfection of generosity consisted precisely in giving vast material wealth so as to remove the poverty of every being without exception, until not a single beggar remained, then, since destitute beings and beggars still exist in just this way, it would follow that the past protectors, the Buddhas, Blessed Ones, had not perfected the perfection of generosity — but that is not the case.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-9]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

If perfecting the perfection of generosity required ridding all beings of poverty, then, since destitute beings still exist — and they do exist — it follows that it would be untenable for the past protector Buddhas to have perfected the perfection of generosity at all.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-9]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

By saying, "If the perfection of generosity consisted in ridding beings of poverty, then since destitute beings still exist, how could the past protectors have perfected it?" he sets forth the prior position — as a step toward showing that generosity, too, depends on the mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-9]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Setting forth the prior position that, if the Śrāvaka schools hold that the perfection of generosity is fully perfected by ridding all beings without exception of poverty, then — since it is established by direct perception that many destitute and impoverished beings still exist — it would follow that the past, fully enlightened protector Buddhas had not perfected the perfection of generosity. Yet this cannot be accepted, for it is precisely by each of them having perfected this perfection in the past that they became buddhas at all.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-9]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

By saying, "If the perfection of generosity consisted in ridding beings of poverty, then since destitute beings still exist, how could the past protectors have perfected it?" he sets forth the flaw in the prior position.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-9]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

All virtue, too, arises from one's own mind. If, contrary to this, one had to maintain that the completion of a bodhisattva's practice of the six perfections meant that, from the standpoint of the outer object, not a single destitute sentient being remained — all having been rid of poverty — and that only on this basis could the perfection of generosity be said to be perfected or completed, then, since countless destitute and impoverished sentient beings still exist in the realms of the world, it would follow, absurdly, that none of the past, fully enlightened protector Buddhas — Śākyamuni, Dīpaṃkara, Amitābha, and the rest — could ever be said to have perfected the perfection of generosity. So he explains the flaw in this position.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-9]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. If Perfecting Generosity Meant Removing Poverty, Even Past Buddhas Would Not Have Perfected It

All the commentaries agree in setting forth this flaw in the prior position, since destitute beings still exist in great numbers.

###### 2. If Generosity Were Perfected Solely by Actually Removing Beings' Poverty, All Buddhas Would Absurdly Have Failed to Perfect It

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** If the perfection of generosity meant actually removing the poverty of all beings so that no beggar remained, then, since many destitute beings still exist in the world, it would follow that even the past protector Buddhas had not perfected the perfection of generosity — this is the flaw the verse sets forth in the prior position.

**Key points.**

- **The standard of the prior position.** The prior position takes the perfection of generosity to be perfected or completed by actually freeing all beings of the external world from the suffering of poverty.
- ⚑ **Whose position this is.** Sabzang Mati Panchen states explicitly that this prior position belongs to the Śrāvaka schools, while the other commentators present it more generally, as a prior position or an initial point of doubt.
- **Established as evident fact.** It is established as evident fact that many destitute and impoverished beings still exist in the world.
- **The flaw that even the Buddhas would fail.** If perfecting the perfection of generosity required actually removing the poverty of the external world, then it would follow, absurdly, that the past protectors — Śākyamuni, Dīpaṃkara, Amitābha, and the rest — had not perfected the perfection of generosity.
- **Purpose of the verse.** This verse sets forth the initial doubt, or prior position, as a step toward establishing that the perfection of generosity is not located in an external object but depends on the mind.

*Plain English translation (source: `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-translate/BCA-Full-Plain-English.md#^5-10`):*

> The perfection of generosity is taught to lie in the intention to give all one's possessions to all beings, together with their fruit — and so it is the mind itself.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

One might ask: then how is it? All one's possessions — every outer and inner thing, together with the fruit of giving them — are perfected as the perfection of generosity precisely through having brought to its utmost completion the habituated mind that gives them, free of attachment, to all beings; so the sūtra teaches in the *Akṣayamatinirdeśa*. Therefore, it is the mind itself.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains: when one repeatedly cultivates, toward all beings, the mind of giving away all material possessions together with the virtuous fruit of giving them, and brings the habituation of this mind of giving to its completion, that alone is called the perfection of generosity. Since what is required, on the level of the mind, is only that the mind of giving grow and expand with certainty — and there is no need for the poverty of others to be actually removed by the material thing itself — it follows that generosity is the mind itself. Thus he shows that even the completion of the practice of generosity depends on the mind alone.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-10]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The *Akṣayamatinirdeśa* teaches that the perfection of generosity consists precisely in the mind of giving, or the habituated mind of giving — that is, giving all one's possessions, whether one's body or one's material wealth, together with their fruit, without hoping for any reward in this life or any ripened result in future lives, to all recipients of giving without exception, high or low, rich or poor, such that there is nothing whatsoever one is unable to give away out of stinginess or attachment. "Perfection" means being sealed by the absence of conceptualizing the three spheres [giver, gift, and recipient]. Therefore, generosity does not depend on the size of the object given, but consists precisely in this mind of giving, free from the attachment that could prevent one from giving away any outer or inner thing whatsoever — and so it arises from the mind itself.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-10]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

He teaches that the perfection of generosity is perfected precisely through habituating oneself to the mind that wishes to give all one's possessions — one's body, one's enjoyments, and one's roots of virtue — together with the merit that is their fruit, to all beings. For this reason, he sums up the point: generosity depends on the mind itself.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-10]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

In reply to the doubt raised by the prior position, the *Akṣayamatinirdeśa* teaches that the perfection of generosity consists in a mind free of stinginess, brought about by having carried to its utmost completion the habit of giving all outer and inner possessions, together with the virtuous fruit of giving them, to all beings. What, then, is the perfection of generosity? It is the mind of giving away all one's possessions together with their fruit. Therefore, it is the mind itself.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-10]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Therefore, he teaches that the perfection of generosity is fully perfected precisely at the time when, through having brought to completion the habit of giving away entirely, to all beings, all one's own outer and inner possessions together with the fruit of giving them, one becomes free from the obscuration of stinginess and the like — as is likewise taught in the *Acintyajñāna Sūtra* and the *Ratnamegha Sūtra*. Therefore, the perfection of generosity is accomplished precisely by bringing one's own mind under control.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-10]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

The Sabzang commentary explains that it is the Śrāvaka schools who hold that giving material things to be given away, so that all beings become free from destitute poverty and grow wealthy, is itself the standard by which the perfection of generosity is perfected. This is untenable, since many destitute and impoverished beings are still directly observed to exist; it would absurdly follow that even the past protector Buddhas had not perfected the perfection of generosity — and no one could possibly accept that. Therefore, the perfection of the perfection of generosity does not depend on all beings actually being freed from poverty; whether or not sentient beings are outwardly free from poverty, it depends on this alone: that within one's own mindstream, one has brought to its utmost completion the habituated mind that, having shattered the grasping of stinginess toward everything one possesses — comprised of body, enjoyments, and roots of virtue — wishes to give it, together with its fruit, to all beings. So it is taught in the *Akṣayamatinirdeśa* and the *Ratnamegha Sūtra*. And the *Bodhisattvabhūmi* likewise states that the essential nature of generosity is the mind of non-attachment that does not regard one's own possessions or body, together with the physical and verbal action motivated by it. Since mind, rather than body and speech, is what is essential, he shows that generosity too must be established on the basis of the mind — and that this teaching should likewise be applied below to ethical discipline and the rest.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-10]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

The giver gives away all the possessions or existing things under their own control — body, enjoyments, and roots of virtue together — and gives away also all the fruits of such giving, whether happiness or enjoyment. The recipients of this giving, too, are all beings without exception, without distinction of superior or inferior, old or young: it is the mind that gives, without holding back, to whatever is requested by whomever asks. In short, the *Akṣayamatinirdeśa Sūtra* teaches that if one has habituated this mind of giving — able to give even a single eye or ear without any limit or restraint — to the point where the perfection of generosity is thereby perfected. For this reason, the perfection of generosity means precisely this: habituating, to its utmost completion, this very mind of giving from within the inner mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-10]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Perfection of Generosity Depends on Completing the Habituated Mind of Giving, Not on Actually Removing the World's Poverty

This is the answer to the doubt raised in Verse 5-9, agreed on by all the commentaries.

###### 2. Since No One Can Accept That the Past Buddhas Failed to Perfect Generosity, the Śrāvaka Schools' Standard for Generosity Is Untenable

Minyak Kunzang Sonam clarifies this.

###### 3. The Essential Nature of Generosity Is a Mind of Non-Attachment Toward One's Possessions and Body, Together with the Action It Motivates

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites this teaching from the *Bodhisattvabhūmi*.

###### 4. Since Generosity Depends on the Mind, So Do the Perfections That Follow, Such as Ethical Discipline

Gyaltsab Je and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both explain this as a template applying to the other perfections as well.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** The perfection of generosity is fully perfected not by actually removing the poverty of all beings in the external world, but by bringing to its utmost completion, within one's own mindstream, the habituated mind that gives away all one's outer and inner possessions, together with their fruit, free from attachment and stinginess.

**Key points.**

- **The standard of generosity is the habituated mind of giving.** The perfection of generosity is completed by bringing to its utmost completion, without expectation of reward and without stinginess, the mind that gives all one's outer and inner possessions and roots of virtue, together with their ripening, to all beings.
- ⚑ **Why the prior position is untenable.** The Śrāvaka schools hold that actually removing all beings' poverty and making them wealthy is the standard for the perfection of generosity being perfected; this is untenable, since destitute beings visibly still exist, which would mean even the past Buddhas failed to perfect it.
- **The essential nature of generosity.** As taught in the *Bodhisattvabhūmi*, the essential nature of generosity is the intention of non-attachment that does not regard one's possessions or body, together with the bodily and verbal action motivated by it.
- **Extending to the other perfections.** This teaching that generosity depends on the mind applies as a template to the other perfections — ethical discipline and the rest — that follow.
- **Scriptural sources.** This point is taught in the *Akṣayamatinirdeśa Sūtra*, the *Ratnamegha Sūtra*, and the *Acintyajñāna Sūtra*.
