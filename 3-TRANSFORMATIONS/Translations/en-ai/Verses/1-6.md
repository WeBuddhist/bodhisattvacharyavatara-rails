## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three classical commentators agree that verse 1-6 underscores the absolute necessity of generating the mind of awakening (bodhicitta) as the only spiritual force powerful enough to overcome and purify immense, deep-seated negativity (sins or misdeeds). Ordinary virtues are presented as feeble and fleeting, leaving the practitioner highly vulnerable to falling into lower realms unless protected by this supreme mind.

### 1. The Fleetingness of Ordinary Virtue vs. the Strength of Negativity
- **Consensus:** The commentators agree that ordinary worldly virtues are weak and short-lived, whereas the forces of non-virtue are immense, persistent, and easily accumulated.
- **Sazang Mati Panchen's Explanation:** Sazang explains that ordinary virtue that merely "accords with merit" is like a fleeting flash of lightning—arising only occasionally and possessing extremely weak strength. Conversely, negativity is like the thick, dense darkness of a cloudy night, possessing massive power to produce the fierce, unendurable sufferings of the hells.
- **Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo's Transition:** Thokme Zangpo connects this verse directly to the preceding contemplation on the rarity of physical and mental supports (leisure, fortune, and a virtuous mind). He notes that while a virtuous mind is extremely difficult to find and of short duration, the causes of non-virtue are easily gathered, leading to the continuous arising of fierce negativity. 

### 2. Bodhicitta as the Sole and Sovereign Remedy
- **Consensus:** Only bodhicitta possesses the capacity to conquer and purify these immense non-virtues; ordinary worldly goodness is entirely helpless against them.
- **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen's Scholastic Distinction:** Gyaltsab Je introduces a crucial doctrinal nuance, distinguishing between conventional and ultimate bodhicitta. He notes that while *ultimate bodhicitta* (the direct realization of emptiness) has the power to completely eradicate the seeds of cognitive and afflictive obscurations, this verse is specifically praising *conventional bodhicitta*. It is conventional bodhicitta that has the power to purify previously accumulated causes of bad migrations and cut off future ones.
- **Sazang's Definition of Bodhicitta:** Sazang explicitly defines the conquering remedy as "the perfect *bodhicitta* endowed with the essence of **emptiness and compassion**," posing a rhetorical question: what other ordinary virtue, lacking this dual essence, could ever hope to overcome such powerful negativity? None can.

### 3. Scriptural Proofs and Analogies
- **The Power of the Support (Sazang):** Sazang cites the *Teaching of the Four Dharmas* (*Caturdharma-nirdeśa Sūtra*) to explain that "the power of the support" (*āśraya-bala*) is established by taking refuge in the Three Jewels and never discarding bodhicitta. Relying on these powerful supports allows one to overcome all negativities.
- **The Cat and Mouse Analogy (Thokme):** Thokme Zangpo cites a vivid scriptural analogy from the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*: "Just as the eyes of a cat instantly overcome mice so they cannot endure, likewise, the eyes of the precious mind of awakening also instantly overcome the mice of karma and afflictions so they cannot endure."
