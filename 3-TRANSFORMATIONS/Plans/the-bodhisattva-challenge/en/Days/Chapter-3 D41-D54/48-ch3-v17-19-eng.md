## Today's Verse

> May I be a protector for the unprotected,
> a guide for those on the path,
> a boat,
> a raft, and a bridge for those who wish to cross. ^3-17

> May I be an island for those desiring an island,
> a resting place for those desiring a resting place.
> May I become a servant for all
> beings who wish for a servant. ^3-18

> May I become a wish-fulfilling jewel, an excellent vase,
> an accomplished spell, a great medicine,
> a wish-granting tree,
> and a wish-granting cow for embodied beings. ^3-19

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 17 to 19 from the third chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. For the sake of all beings we wish to become a bridge, a lamp, and even a servant to everyone. What sort of stirring does that prayer set off in our minds?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us an explanation and a passage of scripture, easy to understand and encouraging. When His Holiness the Dalai Lama explained verse nineteen, he quoted this from the _Ratnavali_ of the Protector Nagarjuna:

> Like earth and water and fire and wind,
> like medicine and the trees of the wilderness.

Those six things are earth, water, fire, wind, medicine and the trees of the wilderness. By their very nature they benefit every being, holding none near and none far. In the same way a bodhisattva prays to become like that for the sake of all beings.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will listen to whatever an elderly or sick person asks of me, without pride, and help them.

**Explanation:** _(Generosity Practice)_ This verse says: may I become a servant to others. So today we should give the strength of our body and speech for the benefit of others. If we serve those who need help, the sick and the elderly especially, without any pride, then our own pride settles. And a great store of merit is completed.
