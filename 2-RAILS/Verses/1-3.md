## Traditional Interpretation

### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Palden (Tibetan)

The fourth of the four opening functions: *inspiring enthusiasm* (སྤྲོ་བ་བསྐྱེད་པ). The verse gives two reasons for the text's value, one self-directed and one other-directed, which together deflect both the extreme of pride and the extreme of despair. Self-benefit: the *stream of Śāntideva's own faith* (དད་པའི་ཤུགས་) — his confident and aspirational faith in the Three Jewels and the bodhisattva path — will itself *increase progressively* (རེ་ཞིག་འཕེལ་འགྱུར་) through the act of composing and studying the text. The phrase "these writings themselves, gradually" (འདི་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་རེ་ཞིག་) is important: the growth is progressive, happening through repetition, and is not yet complete — it is a work in progress. Other-benefit: those of *equal fortune* (སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་ = those with the same mahāyāna aspiration and capacity), who are "of my type," will, *if they see these texts*, find them beneficial — i.e., will also experience growth in their bodhicitta and faith. The verse thus inspires both self-confidence (the text is worth writing) and social hope (others like me will benefit). KKP notes this also teaches the fourfold dependent connection of the treatise: subject matter, purpose, immediate purpose, and the connection between chapters.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-3)

### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Tibetan)

Gyaltsab reads the verse as a genuine statement of the two benefits. For self-benefit: the bodhicitta cultivation that occurs through composing and studying this text causes the author's stream of faith — faith in the Three Jewels, in karma and result, and in the attainability of liberation and omniscience — to grow. Gyaltsab specifies that the proximate demonstrative (འདི་) refers to the act of composing specifically: this very text, not just any reflection, grows faith. The qualification "gradually" (རེ་ཞིག་) acknowledges the progressive nature of spiritual growth and the text's still-incomplete state. For others' benefit: those whose fortune equals the author's — those who aspire to Mahāyāna and abide stably in its practice — will find these writings beneficial if they see them, because the path they lay out meets the capacity and inclination of such readers.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-3)

### ngulchu-thogmed — Ngulchu Thokme Sangpo (Tibetan)

NTS (covering 1-2 and 1-3 together) reads verse 1-3 as declaring: for the purpose of cultivating the great virtue of bodhicitta, which Śāntideva has already entered, this text was composed so that the stream of his faith-confidence will grow uninterrupted. The phrase "those of equal fortune" (སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་) refers to others with the same ripe mahāyāna disposition — those who are ready for this path and seeking to abide in it — who, seeing the text, may find it useful. The implication is that Ngulchu Thokme's reading emphasises the inseparability of self-cultivation and others' benefit: the text grows the author's own virtue-stream, and, because minds of equal capacity naturally resonate, the text thereby also benefits those others.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-3)

### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Mi Nyag Kunzang Sönam (Tibetan)

MKS addresses the objection: if you have already realized the content and writing is not needed for that, and if it doesn't bring novel content for others, what is the use of a written text? The answer is verse 1-3. For self-benefit: those who have already entered the great mahāyāna path in terms of the union of method and wisdom still need the stream of practice to remain unbroken and growing — and composition (along with study) is what sustains that stream for the long term, since an unbroken stream of virtue is the ground for further realization. For others' benefit: those of the same ripe mahāyāna disposition ("those with the same fortune") who encounter these writings will find them useful — not everyone, but those of equal capacity. The phrase "if they see these" (ཅི་སྟེ་འདི་དག་མཐོང་ན་) uses a conditional, acknowledging that the text's benefit to others depends on their having the right disposition, not just encountering it.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-3)

### khenpo-kunga — Ngakwang Kunga Wangchuk (Tibetan)

NKW reads the verse as establishing both a self-purpose and an other-purpose that together constitute the "purposes" (དགོས་པ) and "immediate purpose" (ཉིང་དགོས་) of a classical treatise. The self-purpose (self-benefit): faith — here specified as the three types of faith: trusting faith in the Three Jewels, confident faith in karma and result, and aspiring faith toward liberation and omniscience — will grow through the act of cultivating bodhicitta via composing and studying this text. The other-purpose: those of equal spiritual fortune (the same ripe mahāyāna aspiration) who encounter the text will find it beneficial. NKW adds: the condition "if they see these" is important — it means the benefit is conditional on the reader's receptivity, not guaranteed merely by proximity. The word "gradually" (རེ་ཞིག་) marks that the growth is progressive and begins immediately but is not completed at once.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-3)

### Divergences

The referent of **དད་པའི་ཤུགས་** is interpreted narrowly by some and broadly by others. GDR and NKW specify three types of faith (trusting / confident / aspiring), while KKP takes "faith" more holistically as bodhicitta-aspiration. ⚑ The referent of **སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་** (those of equal fortune): all commentaries agree this means those of equal mahāyāna disposition, but some stress "equal realization level" (NKW) while others stress "equal capacity and inclination" (KKP, MKS). ⚑

## Key Concepts (ཆོས་ཀྱི་གནད་ཚིག)

### ཚིགས་བཅད་ནང་གི་གནད་ཚིག

- **དད་པའི་ཤུགས་** (stream of faith) — མཆོག་གསུམ་ལ་དང་བའི་དད་པ། ལས་འབྲས་ལ་ཡིད་ཆེས་ཀྱི་དད་པ། ཐར་པ་དང་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པར་འདོད་པའི་དད་པ་གསུམ་གྱི་རྒྱུན་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ལ་འཇུག་པ་ཡིན།
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-3) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/དད་པ་_(faith-three-types).md]]

- **སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་** (equal fortune / same capacity) — རྫོགས་ས་མ་ལུས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ལ་གཉེར་བ། རང་དང་མཐུན་རྐྱེན་ཞིང་རྒྱུད་མ་སྨིན་པའི་གཞན་ལ་མི་སྤྲེལ།
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-3) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་_(equal-fortune).md]]

### འགྲེལ་པ་ནས་འབྱུང་བའི་གནད་ཚིག

- **སྤྲོ་བ་བསྐྱེད་པ་** (inspiring enthusiasm) — བདུན་མ་སྔ་མའི་ཁར་བསྟན་བཅོས་ཀྱི་མགོ་བྱང་རྩོམ་པའི་ཕུགས་ཀྱི་ཚེ། རང་དང་གཞན་གྱི་ཐད་ཐུམ་མོ་དང་ཡིད་བདེ་བར་གྱུར་ཏེ་ལེ་ལོར་མི་ལྟུང་བར་གཞུང་ལ་འཇུག་བའི་ཆེད་ཡིན།

## Disambiguated Restatement (Tibetan)

ཞི་བ་ལྷ་ཞལ་ནས། བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་དང་སྤྱོད་པ་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་དྲུག་གི་དགེ་བ་རྒྱ་ཆེ་བ་དེ་བསྒོམ་ཞིང་རྒྱུན་མི་ཆད་པར་གོམས་པར་བྱ་བའི་ཕྱིར། རང་གི་མཆོག་གསུམ་ལ་དང་བའི་དད་པ་དང་ལས་འབྲས་ལ་ཡིད་ཆེས་ཀྱི་དད་པ་དང་ཐར་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་ལ་འདོད་པའི་དད་པ་གསུམ་གྱི་རྒྱུན། སྤྱོད་འཇུག་འདི་ཉིད་རྩོམ་ཞིང་བསྒོམ་ཞིང་བགྲིམ་པ་ (འདི་དག་) གིས་ཀྱང་ཐོག་མར་གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་འགྱུར་ལ། རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་དོན་དུ་གཉེར་ཞིང་གཟུ་བོར་གནས་པའི་རང་དང་ རིགས་འདྲ་ཞིང་སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཀྱང་གལ་ཏེ་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་འདིའི་གཞུང་ངམ་དོན་མཐོང་ཞིང་རྟོགས་ན་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་ལམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་སོགས་ཀྱི་དོན་ཡོད་པར་འགྱུར་བས་ལེ་ལོར་མི་ལྟུང་ཞིང་གཞུང་ལ་དད་པས་འཇུག་དགོས་སོ།
