## Today's Verse

> If one must be careful
> even on a small, ordinary precipice,
> what need is there to speak of
> a long precipice, falling for thousands of miles? ^2-57

> It is not right to sit happily,
> thinking, "I will not die today."
> The time when I will cease to exist
> will certainly come. ^2-58

> Who will grant me fearlessness?
> How will I definitely be freed from this?
> If I will certainly cease to exist,
> how can my mind be at ease? ^2-59

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 57 through 59 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. In everyday life we do not think about death. We pass the time hoping we still have many years ahead of us. But no one knows when death will come, and the drop into the lower realms is very deep. So these verses call on us to start today. They ask us to give up the laziness of not paying attention, to confess our wrongdoing, and to take up doing good without delay.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses quote scripture that is valuable, easy to understand, and encouraging.

The _Letter to a Friend_ says that our life is more fleeting and more fragile than a bubble on water stirred by a fierce wind. We fall asleep at night. That we wake again the next morning is shown to be a great wonder.

Likewise the _Letter to Kanika_ says that the Lord of Death has no affection for anyone, and that there is no certainty at all about when he will come. So we should not put things off by saying "I can do it tomorrow" or "I can confess later." From this very day we should make an effort in the Dharma, confess our wrongdoing, and quickly take up doing good. The letter teaches this as a point of the greatest importance.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Tonight, as I am falling asleep, I will remember death and confess with regret whatever wrong I did during the day.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ The time of death is uncertain, and life is as fragile as a bubble on water. So we should not grow lazy and say "I can confess tomorrow." As the verses say, we should make a quick effort today, before we die, to clear away what we have done wrong. That is the best way there is to take away the fear that comes at the moment of death.
