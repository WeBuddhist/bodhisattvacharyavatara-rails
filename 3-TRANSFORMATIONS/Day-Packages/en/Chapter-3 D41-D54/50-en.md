## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the three verses 22 to 24 from the third chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Following the buddhas of the past, today the time has come to actually generate bodhicitta and to take the bodhisattva vow.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses tell us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

As Khenpo Kunzang Palden says, when we take up bodhicitta we should take the buddhas of the past as our example.

Long ago, when our teacher Shakyamuni was the potter's son called Nangje, he generated bodhicitta before the Tathagata Great Shakyamuni. In the same way the monk Akshobhya generated bodhicitta before Buddha Great Eye. The Blessed One Amitabha, the Medicine Buddha and others also did the same, before the buddhas of the past.

At first they too were beginners, just as we are. But they trained in the bodhisattva trainings step by step, from beginner all the way up to the level of a buddha. That is how they became buddhas. That is why we too can take them as our example, generate bodhicitta, and practise.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today I will decide on one act that helps someone else, and do it.

**Explanation:** *(Doing good)* This verse says that we should follow the buddhas and generate the mind that benefits others. Then we train in stages in what is to be learned. If today we do one small act that helps someone, our own mind grows better. We can move one step forward on the bodhisattva path.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

This verse, he explains, is the actual pledge by which the commitment of bodhicitta — the awakening mind — can be taken.

Whichever of the three ways of generating bodhicitta it may be — generating it like a king, like a boatman, or like a shepherd — any of the three, he says, can be used here.

As for how the commitment is taken, there are two systems. Following the system of the noble Asaṅga, the aspiration and the engagement are taken separately; following the system of Nāgārjuna, the commitments of aspiration and engagement are taken both together at once.

If one takes it from a qualified teacher, that is the supreme support, since it gives a firm basis for a sense of shame and self-respect. But even if no qualified teacher is available, he explains that the commitment still arises if one takes it before the three supports of the Great Vehicle — representations of the buddhas' body, speech and mind — and before the field of accumulated merit.

If one takes them separately, then, having roused the buddhas and bodhisattvas with the threefold request for their attention and taken the three refuges as one's ground, the two lines beginning "Just as the Blissful Ones of the past…" together with the two lines of the verse that follows, "Likewise, for the benefit of beings…," take the commitment of aspiring bodhicitta; and the two lines "The trainings of a bodhisattva…" together with the two lines "So too shall I…" take the commitment of engaged bodhicitta.

If one takes them both at once, then, just as with taking the three sets of commitments — those of individual liberation, of the bodhisattva and of the mantra way — one takes them following the example of those who went before. So here too, he explains, the example carried by the word "just as" means this: the Blissful Ones of the past generated the intention set on unsurpassed great awakening, and trained in the vast trainings of a bodhisattva and abided in them step by step, from a beginner all the way to the level of buddhahood.

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->
##### Zhenphen Jampé Gocha (annotation commentary)

He explains the words "just as the Blissful Ones of the past" as spoken on the strength of the threefold request for the buddhas' attention going first, and of the three refuges being taken as one's ground.

The words "generated the intention toward awakening," he explains, mean generating the intention that wishes to establish all sentient beings in perfect awakening.

And the words "just as they abided in them in due order," he explains, mean that the Blissful Ones of the past abided in the trainings a bodhisattva undertakes step by step, without confusing their sequence.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^3-22]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

> [!note] Grouped span
> In Ngulchu Thogme's commentary this passage is shared in common by verses 3-21, 3-22 and 3-23; what is given below is only the portion that bears directly on 3-22 and on its continuation in 3-23.

If one takes the commitment from a master, or from a support in place of a master, one first says, "May all the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the ten directions give me their attention." Then, if aspiration and engagement are taken separately, one takes aspiring bodhicitta by reciting three times, "Just as the Blissful Ones of the past / generated the intention toward awakening, / so too, for the benefit of beings, / I shall generate bodhicitta"; and one takes the commitment of engaged bodhicitta by reciting three times, "Just as the Blissful Ones of the past / abided step by step / in the trainings of a bodhisattva, / so too shall I train, step by step, in those trainings." If they are taken both together, he explains, it is enough to recite the text just as it stands.

The meaning of the words "just as" is this. Just as the Blissful Ones of the past generated the intention set on awakening, wishing to attain buddhahood for the sake of others, and trained step by step in the trainings a bodhisattva undertakes and abided in them — so too, he explains, I for my part shall generate bodhicitta for the benefit of beings, and shall train in those trainings step by step.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse is the actual pledge by which the commitment of bodhicitta can be taken. With the word "just as" it sets up an example: the Blissful Ones of the past generated the intention set on great awakening, and trained in the trainings of a bodhisattva and abided in them step by step, from a beginner all the way to the level of buddhahood. Taking that as the model, one pledges to accomplish the very same thing oneself.

**Key points.**

- This verse is the actual pledge by which the commitment of bodhicitta can be taken.
- The four lines are divided into the commitment of aspiring bodhicitta and the commitment of engaged bodhicitta and taken as two; and there are two systems for taking them — separately, following the system of the noble Asaṅga, and both at once, following the system of Nāgārjuna.
- "The intention toward awakening" is the intention that wishes to establish all sentient beings in perfect awakening.
- With the word "just as," the way the Blissful Ones of the past generated the intention set on awakening and trained in the trainings step by step is taken as the example, and one pledges to do the same oneself.
- As for the support from which the commitment is taken, a qualified teacher is best; but if there is none, the commitment still arises when it is taken before the three supports of the Great Vehicle and the field of merit.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Taking as my example the way the Blissful Ones of the past trained in the training, I too, for the sake of the immediate happiness and the lasting benefit of wandering beings, shall generate the mind set on supreme awakening. And likewise, in the whole range of the bodhisattva trainings — the six perfections, the four means of attracting disciples and the rest — I shall train step by step, in keeping with the level of my own character and disposition. This, it is explained, is the main body of the ceremony, the actual taking hold of the awakening mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-23]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Likewise I too, for the sake of benefiting all sentient beings, as many as fill the whole of space, shall generate bodhicitta — the awakening mind, the aspiration to attain the unsurpassable state of a buddha. And from this time onward, matching what I take on to my own capacity, I shall train step by step in the bodhisattva trainings as well: the six perfections and the rest. In this way one generates the engaged mind. Since the moment when the vow is actually obtained is the end of the third repetition of these words, at that point one needs to give rise to the sense of having received it, thinking: "Now the vows of aspiration and of engagement have arisen in me." As the master Sāgaramegha said — the first recitation makes the aspiration, the second the engagement, and the third makes it firm — so one must give rise to that sense of having received it.

As to the meaning of "step by step," the Sakyapas apply it to the ethical discipline of restraint from wrongdoing as it concerns the three kinds of bodhisattva: those of highest, middling and lowest capacity. Those of highest capacity must guard the eighteen root downfalls taught in the *Ākāśagarbha Sūtra*. Those of middling capacity must guard the four root downfalls taught in the *Sūtra Requested by the Householder Ugra*, such as not giving Dharma or wealth out of miserliness. And for those of lowest capacity it is held to be enough to guard simply the not abandoning of the aspiring mind, as taught in the *Sūtra of Great Secret Skill in Means*. Since there are extensive, middling and abridged levels of the root downfalls, it says "step by step." There is also the view that one trains in the ethics of restraint from wrongdoing on the path of accumulation, in the ethics of gathering what is virtuous on the path of joining, and in the ethics of acting for the welfare of beings on the path of the noble ones. And there is the view that one trains in keeping with one's mental capacity, from giving away a plateful of food and the like up to being able to give away one's own body. As for our own tradition: the *Sūtra Requested by Subāhu* says "a little at a time, from time to time," and so on, which clearly teaches that one cannot train in the ocean-like conduct of a bodhisattva from the very outset but trains step by step. And the *Compendium of Trainings* says, "The training should be taken up in accordance with one's own strength." So it is taught that by training step by step in the bodhisattva trainings, in keeping with one's own capacity, things go from strength to strength.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-23]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Just as the Blissful Ones of the past, when they generated the awakening mind, generated the aspiring mind set on supreme awakening in the presence of the victors together with their heirs, and then, in order to take up the bodhisattva vow, generated the engaged mind and trained step by step in the trainings and abided in them — so too I, for the sake of the immediate and the ultimate benefit of all wandering beings, shall generate the aspiring mind of awakening. If a master who genuinely holds the pure vow of engagement is actually present, I take it from him; and if there is none, then I invite the victors together with their heirs to come before me and ask them to be my witnesses. And likewise, in the trainings taken on with the engaged mind, I shall train step by step. This should be said up to three times. Even where there is no actual repeating after a master, one should hold the devoted attitude of repeating after the victors and their heirs.

As for the meaning of "training step by step": it is what the scriptures teach — that one does not give away one's body and the like while one's intention is impure, since to do so would become a fault, and that one does it at a time when there is no fault and a great accumulation is gained. That is what training step by step means. Some explain it as training in stages in the three kinds of ethical discipline — the ethics of restraint and the rest — assigning them to different boundaries of the grounds; that is not the meaning. Furthermore, performing separate rituals for taking the engaged mind and for taking the vow of engagement is not the position of any of the great charioteers, and it does not hold up. Taking the aspiring mind first, cultivating the wish to train in the conduct, and taking the engaged mind later is an excellent method suited to particular individuals; but taking them step by step in one single sequence is also perfectly acceptable.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-23]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

(This passage is a single explanation covering verses 3-21 to 3-23; here the concluding portion, the part aimed at the actual words of this verse, is given.) As to how the generation of the aspiring and engaged mind is taken: if one takes it from a master, one takes it from the master; and if one takes it before a support, one begins by saying, "All buddhas and bodhisattvas of the ten directions, please attend to me." If aspiration and engagement are taken separately, then one makes the aspiration by saying three times, "Just as the Blissful Ones of the past generated the awakening mind, so too, for the sake of benefiting beings, I shall generate the mind of awakening"; and one takes the engagement by saying three times, "Just as the Blissful Ones of the past abided step by step in the bodhisattva trainings, so likewise in the trainings shall I train step by step." If they are taken together, it is done simply by reciting the text as it stands. In this, "just as" means: for example, just as the Blissful Ones of the past generated the awakening mind that wishes to attain buddhahood for the sake of others, and trained step by step in the trainings to be undertaken by a bodhisattva and abided in them — so too I, for the sake of benefiting beings, shall generate the mind of awakening, and likewise in the trainings shall I train step by step.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-23]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Before a spiritual friend who has the proper qualities — or, if there is none, imagining before oneself the victors together with their heirs, who possess the eye of unobstructed wisdom — moved throughout by faith and great compassion, one begins with, "All buddhas and bodhisattvas dwelling in the ten directions, please attend to us." Then: just as the Blissful Ones of the past, the buddhas, the bhagavans, generated the awakening mind set on unsurpassable great awakening, and trained step by step in the vast trainings to be undertaken by a bodhisattva — from the very first steps of a beginner up to the ground of buddhahood — and abided in them, so I too, in order to bring about the immediate and the ultimate benefit of all wandering beings, shall generate the mind set on supreme awakening. And just as that mind is generated, so also, as its application, I shall train step by step in the vast trainings to be undertaken by a bodhisattva, from this time onward until awakening. This should be taken by saying it three times. A person who has taken it in this way need not receive the bodhisattva vow separately, for no vow has been taught that is anything more than the pledge, having generated the mind, to train in what is to be trained in; and because the commentaries explain that this itself is the taking of the bodhisattva vow.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-23]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

As to how the generation of the aspiring and engaged mind is taken: if one takes it from a master, he should be a Mahayana spiritual friend who has the proper qualities, one who holds bodhicitta and the vow in his own mind, and who is actually present. If one takes it before a support, one has confidence that all the victors together with their heirs have come and are seated in the space before one, and having first said, "May those objects attend to me," and asked them to be witnesses, one makes present in oneself the thought of faith and of great compassion. Then, citing the way of those who went before as one's example: just as the Blissful Ones of the past, with an altruistic intention, generated the awakening mind by aspiring, "May I attain unsurpassable great awakening in order to bring about the benefit and happiness of all sentient beings," and, as those who had generated the mind in order to take the bodhisattva vow, trained step by step in the trainings that are always fit to be put into practice and abided in them — so I too, likewise, in order to bring about the immediate benefit and the ultimate happiness of all wandering beings, shall generate the aspiring mind of awakening; and likewise, having taken the engaged generation of the mind, I shall train step by step in the bodhisattva trainings as well. Saying from the depths of one's heart that one will follow in the footsteps of the Blissful Ones of the past, up to three times — either actually repeating after a master or by way of devoted imagination — one should take the Mahayana generation of the mind and the bodhisattva vow together. If aspiration and engagement are taken separately, then one takes the aspiring mind by saying three times, "Just as the Blissful Ones of the past / generated the awakening mind, / so too, for the sake of benefiting beings, / I shall generate the mind of awakening"; and one takes the engaged mind together with the vow by saying three times, "Just as the Blissful Ones of the past / abided step by step / in the bodhisattva trainings, / so likewise in the trainings / shall I train step by step."

The meaning of "abiding and training step by step" is this. A beginner trains first in those things he is able to train in, and for the time being does not train in the difficult points he is not yet able to manage; then, from the stage of devoted conduct and from the attainment of the grounds onward, he gradually trains in whatever he is able to do at each of those stages. For example, one does not set about giving away one's body while the compassionate intention is impure, since to do so would become a fault; whereas at a time when giving it with a pure intention becomes a great accumulation, then not only is there no fault, one definitely must give it. To explain the phrase otherwise, as meaning that one trains in stages in the three kinds of ethical discipline — the ethics of restraint and the rest — is not the meaning. And since in the *Compendium of Trainings* bodhicitta is first established as arising in each individual, and only after that is the bodhisattva vow again established as arising in each individual, it is not right to hold that bodhicitta itself is the bodhisattva vow; and since taking the engaged mind is itself taking the bodhisattva vow, neither is it right to make separate rituals for taking the engaged mind and for taking the vow of engagement — so he says. Taking the aspiring mind first, then cultivating the wish to train in the conduct of a bodhisattva, and only then taking the engaged mind, is an excellent method for the kind of person who cannot train in the trainings from the outset; whereas if one is able to train in the trainings of the engaged mind from the start, it is fine to take aspiration and engagement both at once, in the manner of taking them that is shown in the text.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-23]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

For example, just as the Blissful Ones of the past, who have already fully and manifestly awakened, first generated the awakening mind of aspiration and of engagement, and then abided step by step in the trainings to be undertaken by a bodhisattva of aspiration and engagement — the six perfections and the four means of attracting disciples, or, put briefly, the practice of the three trainings, that is, the three kinds of ethical discipline — so likewise, for the sake of benefiting the beings who fill the whole of space, I too shall properly generate that very bodhicitta of aspiration and of engagement. And just as the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the past trained, so I too shall train step by step in the three kinds of ethical discipline, or in the six perfections and the four means of attracting disciples and the rest, in keeping with the measure of my own capacity. In effect, a beginner is chiefly to train in the ethics of restraint from wrongdoing; then those on the path of accumulation and the path of joining, who abide in devoted conduct, are to train in addition in the ethics of gathering what is virtuous; and bodhisattvas who have attained the first ground and above are chiefly to train, on top of that, in the ethics of acting for the welfare of beings. Hence it says, "I shall train step by step."

This is the way of taking the two at once, by means of the Madhyamaka system's ritual for generating the aspiring and engaged mind of awakening; and it is said that a somewhat different way of taking them, that of the Chittamatrins, appears in other texts. If one arranges the recitation so that the two illustrative lines, "Just as the Blissful Ones of the past generated the awakening mind," are followed by the fifth and sixth lines, "I shall generate the mind of awakening," and after that come the third and fourth lines, "just as they abided step by step in the bodhisattva trainings" — then this is the way of first taking the aspiring generation of the mind and after that taking the engaged generation of the mind. Alternatively, one first generates the mind set on supreme awakening and takes on the vow of that generation of the mind, and after that takes on training properly in the trainings to be undertaken; the earlier masters explain the sequence in that way too. In any case, whether the two verses are repeated after a master up to three times, or taken by oneself in the presence of the buddhas and bodhisattvas as represented by supports of body, speech and mind, one is properly to generate the mind set on supreme awakening; and that is the main body of the ceremony, the actual pledge.

##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

The commentaries do not have a single tradition of explanation for the phrase "training step by step."

- **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam:** they directly refute the way some people explain it — as training in stages in the three kinds of ethical discipline, the ethics of restraint and the rest, assigned to different boundaries of the grounds — saying outright that this "is not the meaning." ⚑
- **Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (reporting the Sakyapa tradition of explanation) and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk:** they explain it as training in proper sequence, matching the three kinds of ethical discipline to the successive stages of the path — the path of accumulation, the path of joining, and the path of the noble ones (or, alternatively, the first ground and above). ⚑

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. These Are the Words of a Ceremony of Pledging: Taking the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas of the Past as the Example, I Too Shall Generate the Aspiring and Engaged Mind of Awakening and Shall Train

All the commentaries agree in the end that the verse takes as its example the way the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the past generated the awakening mind and trained, and that on that model one pledges to generate the aspiring and engaged bodhicitta oneself and to train accordingly.

###### 2. The Ceremony Must Be Said Three Times: If Taken From a Master One Repeats After Him, and if There Is No Master One Invites the Victors and Their Heirs to Come Before One and Asks Them to Be Witnesses

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sabzang Mati Panchen, Minyak Kunzang Sonam, Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and Ngulchu Thogme all state this plainly.

###### 3. This Very Pledge — Having Generated the Mind, to Train in What Is to Be Trained In — Is Itself the Obtaining of the Bodhisattva Vow; the Vow Need Not Be Taken Separately

Sabzang Mati Panchen says this plainly. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen refutes the practice of performing separate rituals for taking the aspiring mind and the vow of engagement, saying that no one among the great charioteers holds such a position. Minyak Kunzang Sonam, citing the *Compendium of Trainings*, says that taking the engaged mind is itself taking the bodhisattva vow, and that it is not right to make the rituals separate.

###### 4. Both Are Acceptable — Taking the Aspiring Mind First and the Engaged Mind Later, or Taking Aspiration and Engagement Together in One Sequence — and the First Is an Excellent Method for Someone Who Cannot Train in the Trainings at the Outset

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both explain this in the same way.

###### 5. There Are Differing Traditions of Explanation for the Meaning of "Training Step by Step" ⚑

The Sakyapas apply it to the stages of the ethical discipline of restraint from wrongdoing for bodhisattvas of highest, middling and lowest capacity. Another explanation matches the three kinds of ethical discipline to the successive stages of the path (the path of accumulation, the path of joining, and the path of the noble ones — or, alternatively, the first ground and above). Yet another matches it to differing degrees of mental capacity, from small acts of giving up to giving away one's own body. And some — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam — refute the very explanation that assigns the three kinds of ethical discipline to different boundaries of the grounds, saying that it "is not the meaning."

###### 6. "Training Step by Step" Means Training in Keeping With One's Own Capacity and the Level of One's Own Disposition

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden, Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and His Holiness the Dalai Lama all say the same thing here — "matching it to one's own capacity," "in keeping with the measure of one's own capacity," "in keeping with the level of one's own character and disposition."

###### 7. This Is the Madhyamaka System's Ritual for Generating the Aspiring and Engaged Mind of Awakening, Taken Both at Once; a Somewhat Different Way of Taking Them, That of the Chittamatrins, Appears in Other Texts

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains this.

The bodhisattva who has the intelligence that is skilled in the way of taking bodhicitta and the commitments into himself by means of the two rituals, the preparatory and the main one, does not do this by being swept along in another's wake, nor on a mere whim. #### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows how, after a wise person has taken both kinds of bodhicitta — aspiring and engaged — well into himself by means of the preparatory and the main ritual, he then uplifts his own mind, so that in the end too that mind may not decline but stay firm and grow further and further. The commentaries agree in the end that this is the opening of the section, at the conclusion of the ritual, on meditating on one's own joy and the joy of others.

**Key points.**

- Having taken hold of bodhicitta in the proper way, the wise one uplifts his own mind so that in the end too it may not decline but increase.
- This is the "first" part of the section, at the conclusion of the ritual, on meditating on one's own joy and the joy of others — the beginning of meditating on one's own joy.
- Even if the preparatory and main rituals are performed at length, if the concluding ritual is missing the ritual is unfinished, and so the result of generating bodhicitta becomes unstable and declines; uplifting the mind and rejoicing guard that mind, purify it and extend it.
