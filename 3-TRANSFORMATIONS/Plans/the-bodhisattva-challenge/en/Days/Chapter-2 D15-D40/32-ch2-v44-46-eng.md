## Today's Verse

> So imagine, then, how much worse it is to be seized by the terrifying messengers of Death, and struck by fear and sickness beyond anything we know. ^2-44

> "Who can truly save me from this terrible fear?" — with eyes wide open in panic, that person searches every direction, looking for anyone who can help. ^2-45

> Seeing no help anywhere, in any direction, that person falls into complete despair. And if there really is no refuge anywhere — what will I do, when my own time comes? ^2-46

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on verses 44 through 46 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Yesterday we looked at a man being led away to lose a limb, and at the fear that takes hold of him before anything has been done to him. Today Shantideva turns that comparison on us. He walks through what happens at death in three steps: being seized, searching for help, and finding none. He is not writing this to frighten us. He is writing it so that the searching happens now, while it can still change what we do with a day.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

These three verses are one movement, and the order carries the meaning. In verse 44 the person is seized. In verse 45 he is still looking, eyes wide, turning to every direction for anyone who might help. In verse 46 the looking stops, because nobody is there.

The commentaries quote *Advice to a King* at this point, and the passage is worth hearing. A man lies on the last bed he will use. His breath is failing and his mouth is open. Parents, brothers, sisters and children are gathered around him, and they are already saying, let us divide up the property. They call out, alas father, alas mother, alas son. And the sutra says that at that time there is no other refuge, no other protector, no other place, no other ally. Only Dharma.

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk, a later commentator on the text, gives that helplessness a job. He reads verse 46 as the first of the four powers of confession taking shape, which are regret, reliance, applying the antidotes, and restraint. The despair is the power of regret arriving. It is not the end of the passage, it is the start of the confession.

And notice where Shantideva has placed it: here, while he is still free to act.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, at the first small discomfort that comes my way, I will stop for a moment and go for refuge to the Three Jewels, which are the Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha, instead of just pushing the discomfort aside.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ A headache, a delay, bad news on the phone, a body that aches getting out of bed. Our habit with these is to get past them as fast as we can. Today I will let one of them stop me instead, and use it the way these verses use the man being led away, as a small version of something larger that is certainly coming. Then, standing there, I will go for refuge. Not as a formula, but because in verse 46 the searching starts too late, and today it does not have to. Small troubles are useful for this precisely because they are small enough to think clearly in.
