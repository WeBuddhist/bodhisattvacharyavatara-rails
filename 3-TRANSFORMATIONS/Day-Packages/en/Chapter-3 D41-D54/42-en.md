## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the two verses 3 and 4 from the third chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. Two things can be felt in them. One is the joy that rises in the heart at the good others have done. The other is the warmth and the light of lighting the lamp of the Dharma for beings left in the dark.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses tell us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago King Prasenajit invited the Buddha and his retinue and served them with great honour. At that time a poor woman rejoiced from deep in her heart at the king's good deed. The Buddha dedicated the good not to the king but to that poor woman. Why? Because the good she gained by rejoicing with a pure mind turned out to be greater than the king's offering.

What does this teach us? When we see others doing good, we may have nothing of our own. But if we rejoice with a pure mind, we can gain a great deal of good. Rejoicing needs no wealth at all, which makes it a special kind of good that anyone can do.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when I see someone doing good out of kindness, I will rejoice from my heart.

**Explanation:** *(Doing good)* When others do good that helps beings, and we cultivate heartfelt joy without jealousy, we gain a great deal of good ourselves. It makes our own mind peaceful and clear. As the verse says, rejoicing in what others do is a practice that is easy and brings great benefit.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that one rejoices with joy in the wonderful aspiration that is bent single-mindedly on bringing all beings to happiness — a virtue like an ocean — and in the application as well: those deeds of the bodhisattvas that work single-mindedly for the benefit of beings. With this verse, he says, a special act of rejoicing is made in the bodhisattvas' aspiration and in their deeds.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-3]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

He divides the virtue of the great beings — the Great Vehicle virtue that is concordant with liberation — into fruit and cause. As for the fruit: the ultimate fruit is great and perfect enlightenment, which has the very nature of an ocean of good qualities, the qualities of abandonment and realization, complete and without exception, of the buddhas who are the protectors of all beings; and the provisional fruit is the qualities of abandonment and realization along the path of the bodhisattvas, the buddhas' heirs, from the first ground, Perfect Joy, up to the tenth, Cloud of Dharma. Knowing these, he explains, one rejoices in them. As for the cause: the intention — the virtue, like an ocean, of giving rise to the mind set on supreme enlightenment, which works to establish all beings, as many as space is wide, in the supreme happiness, the state of a buddha; and the application — the virtue of bringing measureless benefit to all beings by training in the six perfections. In these, he explains, one rejoices from the heart, with joy and without envy. And he says that since this rejoicing does not turn into pride or self-display, and is a pith instruction for quickly accomplishing vast merit with little effort, one should cultivate it towards whatever roots of virtue of others one sees or hears of.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-3]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->
##### Zhenphen Jampé Gocha (annotation commentary)

Zhenga's annotation commentary explains that the cause of that aspiration is the intention to bring all beings to happiness; that the application is settled as benefiting beings by the four means of gathering; and that one rejoices in these from the heart, with joy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^3-3]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen explains that one rejoices also in the perfect enlightenment of those who protect all other beings, and in the ten grounds of the buddhas' heirs. And he explains that in their cause too — the virtue, like an ocean, of giving rise to the mind set on supreme enlightenment for the sake of bringing all beings to happiness, and the conduct that benefits beings by way of the application — one cultivates a clear faith that goes together with gladness of mind, and rejoices with joy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-3]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Ngulchu Thogme explains it in brief: the intention is the virtue, like an ocean, of the aspiration that brings all beings to happiness; the application is the benefiting of beings; and in these one rejoices with joy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-3]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Sabzang Mati Panchen explains that one rejoices with great joy and clear faith in the intention — the virtues of giving rise to the mind of enlightenment, deep and vast as an ocean, for the sake of bringing all beings both provisional and ultimate happiness — and in the application as well, benefiting all beings by the four means of gathering. And he says that if one rejoices in this way, one gains measureless benefit.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-3]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that one rejoices in the ultimate fruit — the perfect enlightenment that is to be attained by the great beings who protect all other beings in the stream of existence — and in the provisional fruit as well: the virtues of the two accumulations belonging to the ten grounds and the five paths of the buddhas' heirs. As for the word "protector" here, he links it, following the line from the *Pramāṇavārttika* that "a protector is one who teaches the path he has seen," with the enlightenment of the buddhas — those protectors who show to others the path they have realized in their own minds. Yet he also notes that the Sanskrit text reads "in bodhisattvas themselves, in buddhahood itself, and in the protectors too I rejoice," with no equivalent for the word "grounds." He explains that one rejoices too — with a mind utterly clear, glad and delighted — in the causes that bring those about: the special intention, that is, the root of virtue that arises in each single moment when, by way of aspiration, one gives rise to the mind set on supreme enlightenment so as to bring all beings to happiness both provisionally and ultimately, a root of virtue like an ocean whose limit and depth cannot be measured; and the special application, that is, the virtue of directly bringing benefit and happiness to all beings by way of the engaged mind of awakening, which is actually taken up in the special activity of practising the conduct of the six perfections, the four means of gathering and the rest. And he says that cultivating rejoicing in the virtue of others is a means of completing a vast accumulation with little effort, while rejoicing in one's own virtue is a means of increasing one's own virtue further and further; so this very thing is taught in brief as the bodhisattvas' skill in means.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-3]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that one rejoices with joy in the ultimate fruit of the perfect buddhas, the blessed ones, who protect the beings of samsara from suffering — that enlightenment, together with the three bodies, the wisdom and the qualities; and that one rejoices with joy in the provisional fruit as well: the roots of virtue and the qualities of the buddhas' heirs, from the first, second and third grounds up to the tenth. As for rejoicing in the other virtue gathered by the Great Vehicle intention and application, he explains that one rejoices from the depths of the heart, with joy, in the intention — the virtue, like an ocean, of giving rise to the mind set on supreme enlightenment, which works to benefit all beings and bring them happiness both provisionally and ultimately — and in the application, benefiting all beings, that is, accomplishing the welfare of others by way of the six perfections and the four means of gathering.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-3]]

##### King Prasenajit and the Poor Woman

Long ago King Prasenajit invited the Buddha together with his retinue and served them. A poor woman rejoiced at what he did, and it is said that she herself gained a virtue greater than his, so that the Buddha made the dedication to the poor woman. This story shows how, by rejoicing alone, one can gain something greater than the virtue of the person who actually does the good deed.

#### Metaphors and Examples

- **Ocean** (རྒྱ་མཚོ) → the deep and measureless nature of the virtue of giving rise to the mind set on supreme enlightenment. Many commentaries develop this image, which comes from the root verse itself: Zhenga's annotation commentary says it is "hard to fathom in its depth"; Sabzang Mati Panchen says it is "deep and vast"; and Minyak Kunzang Sonam says that "its limit and depth cannot be measured."
- **Space, the sky** (ནམ་མཁའ) → the measureless nature both of the objects of rejoicing and of the extent of the virtue. Kunzang Pelden explains that the objects of the intention — all beings — are equal to space, and that the virtue gained through the application of the six perfections is likewise vast and long-lasting, like space; and he says that one should rejoice from the heart, without envy.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. This Verse Shows Rejoicing in Both the Aspiration (the Cause) and the Application (the Conduct That Works for Others)

All the commentaries agree in the end that one rejoices from the heart, with joy, in both: the virtue, like an ocean, of giving rise to the mind set on supreme enlightenment for the sake of bringing all beings to happiness, and the conduct that directly benefits beings.

###### 2. The Objects of Rejoicing Are Two — the Ultimate Fruit (the Buddhas' Enlightenment) and the Provisional Fruit (the Qualities of the Ten Grounds of the Buddhas' Heirs)

Kunzang Pelden, Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk all three explain that one rejoices both in the buddhas' ultimate enlightenment and in the qualities of the ten grounds of the buddhas' heirs.

###### 3. The Commentaries Differ Over How to Identify "Application" ⚑

Kunzang Pelden explains it as the six perfections; Zhenga's annotation commentary and Sabzang Mati Panchen both explain it as the four means of gathering; and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains it as both — so they do not agree.

###### 4. By Rejoicing Alone One Can Gain More Than the Person Who Actually Does the Good Deed

Kunzang Pelden shows this by telling the story of King Prasenajit and the poor woman.

###### 5. The Virtue of Rejoicing Cannot Be Measured by Any Measure

Kunzang Pelden, Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam all three explain this by citing the scripture which says that although Mount Meru and the three-thousandfold world could be weighed on scales and their measure taken, the virtue of rejoicing is not like that.

###### 6. There Are Two Ways of Explaining the Word "Protector"

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the line from the *Pramāṇavārttika*, "a protector is one who teaches the path he has seen," and links "protector" with the enlightenment of the buddhas alone; yet he also records that the Sanskrit reads "in bodhisattvas themselves, in buddhahood itself, and in the protectors too I rejoice," with no equivalent for the word "grounds."

###### 7. Rejoicing Is a Skilful Means for Completing a Vast Accumulation With Little Effort

Kunzang Pelden explains that it is a pith instruction which does not turn into pride or self-display and which quickly accomplishes vast merit with little effort; and Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that rejoicing in the virtue of others is a means of completing the accumulation with little effort, while rejoicing in one's own virtue is a means of increasing one's own virtue — so, he says, this is taught in brief as the bodhisattvas' skill in means.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Looking to the bodhisattvas' practice of rejoicing, this verse shows how one rejoices from the heart, with joy, in both: the virtue, like an ocean, of giving rise to the mind set on supreme enlightenment for the sake of bringing all beings to happiness, and the conduct that directly benefits beings. Many of the commentaries explain that the objects of this rejoicing include both the buddhas' ultimate enlightenment and the provisional qualities of the ten grounds of the buddhas' heirs.

**Key points.**

- What one rejoices in is twofold: the aspiration (the cause) and the conduct that benefits beings (the application).
- The commentaries do not agree on how to identify "application": some explain it as the six perfections, others as the four means of gathering. ⚑
- The story of King Prasenajit and the poor woman shows that by rejoicing alone one can gain more than the person who actually does the good deed.
- The virtue of rejoicing cannot be measured, and it is a skilful means for quickly completing a vast accumulation with little effort.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam notes that the wording of the Tibetan translation and that of the Sanskrit text differ over the meaning of "protector."

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Beings are worn down by suffering; and in time to come as well, because of the cause of suffering — the darkness of ignorance — their minds are wholly confused about what to take up and what to give up, so that they are bewildered by that darkness and obscured by it. For them too, one asks: "Please light the lamp of the Dharma that clears away the darkness." This is the request that the wheel of Dharma be turned.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-4]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The buddhas, the blessed ones, who abide in all ten directions and throughout the three times, see with their knowing and hold us in mind with their love, so that we are not hidden from them. To them, imagining that I offer a golden wheel and the rest, I join my palms with my body and make my request in words: for beings who are tormented by every kind of suffering, and who — not knowing the difference between what is a proper ground and what is not, nor the distinction between the path and what is not the path, nor what is to be taken up and what given up — are bewildered in the thick darkness of ignorance, please light for each of them, in accord with their own fortune, the lamp of the holy Dharma, blazing brightly, and so clear away the darkness of not knowing and show them the true light of wisdom. Having made this request, one should think that they consent to turn the wheel of Dharma. And he says that there is a purpose in urging them to turn the wheel of Dharma: in all one's lives to come, no wrong view will arise in one's mind, and one will never be parted from the light of the holy Dharma.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-4]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

To the buddhas of all directions — those who have not long since awakened and who remain without teaching the Dharma — one joins the palms in the manner of respectful conduct and makes the request with reverence. Its purpose: for beings who suffer and are bewildered in the darkness of ignorance, please light the lamp of the Dharma of scripture and realization, which makes clear the path to liberation.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-4]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

To the buddhas, the blessed ones, who abide in all ten directions and throughout the three times, imagining that I offer a golden wheel as a gift, I join the palms of my body and make my request in words: for beings who are tormented by suffering, and who, bewildered by the darkness of ignorance, do not know the path from what is not the path, please light for each of them, in accord with their own fortune, the light of the holy Dharma, like a bright lamp, and so clear away the darkness of not knowing and show them the true path.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-4]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

In the buddha-fields of all the world-realms of the ten directions there are those who have not long since awakened and who, unasked, do not teach the Dharma but remain with little inclination to do so. To them, imagining that I offer a golden wheel and a dharma conch as the support of my petition, with a respectful body, palms joined, and with a mind utterly clear, I make my request in words: for those beings who are tortured by every kind of suffering as the fruit, and whose stream of knowing is obscured and clouded over by the darkness of ignorance as the cause, so that they know nothing of the path and what is not the path, of what is to be taken up and what given up — for them please light the great lamp of the holy Dharma, each in accord with their own fortune, and so clear away the darkness of not knowing; make everything that is to be taken up and given up appear and become clear just as it is, and show them the true path of liberation.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-4]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

I too join my palms to those perfect buddhas, the blessed ones, who abide in all the boundless world-realms of the ten directions — the east and the rest — and make my request through my speech: the beings of the three realms are worn down by the suffering of samsara, and about giving up non-virtue, the cause of suffering, and about accomplishing virtue, they are utterly confused, so that they are bewildered as though their minds were in darkness. For them, please light the unsurpassed lamp of the holy Dharma. That is the request that is made.

#### Stories and Illustrations

<!-- story:kunpal -->
##### Brahmā and Indra Requesting That the Wheel of Dharma Be Turned

Long ago, when the Teacher awakened to perfect buddhahood beneath the tree of enlightenment, he said: "I have found a Dharma like nectar — profound, at peace, free of elaboration, luminous, uncompounded. Whoever I taught it to would not understand, so I shall stay in the forest without speaking." And for seven weeks he showed the manner of not teaching the Dharma. Then Indra, lord of the gods, offered a white conch that spirals to the right, and Brahmā offered a golden wheel with a thousand spokes and other gifts, and they asked again and again that the wheel of Dharma be turned. The Buddha then said: "For those who have ears, who have faith and intelligence, who are without any thought of harm and who listen to the Dharma constantly — for them I open the door of nectar." One should think that in this way he consented to turn the wheel of Dharma. In this verse it is shown that I too, in just that way, imagine offering a golden wheel and the rest to the buddhas and ask them to light the lamp of the Dharma.

##### Brahmā of the Topknot and Indra Presenting Their Gifts

Long ago, after the Teacher had awakened to perfect buddhahood, there was a period of seven weeks in which he showed the manner of not teaching the Dharma. At that time Brahmā of the topknot and Indra, lord of the gods, presented gifts — a golden wheel with a thousand spokes, a white conch that spirals to the right, and the like — and asked that the wheel of Dharma be turned. Just as they did, it is explained, in this verse I too join my palms to the buddhas of the ten directions and, through my speech, ask them to light the lamp of the Dharma.

#### Metaphors and Examples

- **The lamp of dharma** (ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྒྲོན་མེ) → beings' ignorance and suffering are the darkness (མུན་པ); the Dharma taught by a buddha is compared to a blazing lamp that clears that darkness away and reveals the true path. Kunzang Pelden says that "having lit what is like the brightly blazing lamp of the holy Dharma and cleared away the darkness of not knowing, they show the true light of wisdom"; Sabzang Mati Panchen says that "having lit what is like a bright lamp, the light of the holy Dharma, and cleared away the darkness of not knowing, they show the true path"; and Minyak Kunzang Sonam says that "having lit the great lamp of the holy Dharma and cleared away the darkness of not knowing, they make everything that is to be taken up and given up appear and become clear just as it is" — each putting it in his own way.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Joining the Palms to the Buddhas of the Ten Directions and Asking Them to Light the Lamp of Dharma

All the commentaries agree in the end on the meaning: to the buddhas, the blessed ones, who abide in all the world-realms of the ten directions, one joins the palms with the body and petitions with the speech, asking that the lamp of the Dharma be lit.

###### 2. Those the Request Is Made For Are Beings Who Suffer and Who, in the Darkness of Ignorance, Are Bewildered and Do Not Know What to Take Up or Give Up

Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam explain that those beings know nothing at all of the path and what is not the path, or of what is to be taken up and what given up, because the darkness of ignorance has obscured and clouded over their stream of knowing.

###### 3. "The Lamp of Dharma" Means the Dharma of Scripture and Realization, Which Makes the Path to Liberation Clear

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen explains that the lamp of the Dharma is the Dharma of scripture and of realization, which makes clear the path to liberation for beings bewildered in the darkness.

###### 4. This Request That the Wheel of Dharma Be Turned Is Made With an Eye to the Story of Brahmā and Indra Asking the Buddha to Teach

Kunzang Pelden and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk both cite the account of how, during the seven weeks in which the Teacher remained after his awakening without teaching the Dharma, Brahmā and Indra offered a golden wheel and a white conch and asked that the wheel of Dharma be turned; and by it they show the reason for the petition made in this verse.

###### 5. One Also Imagines Offering the Buddhas a Golden Wheel or a Dharma Conch — the Gift That Goes With the Request

Kunzang Pelden, Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam all three speak of the offering made along with the request, imagining that one presents something like a golden wheel or a dharma conch; but Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and His Holiness the Dalai Lama do not mention this in their commentaries.

###### 6. The Result of Lighting the Lamp of Dharma Is That the Darkness of Not Knowing Is Cleared Away and the True Path to Liberation Is Shown

Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both explain that by lighting the lamp of the Dharma the darkness of not knowing is cleared away and the true path to liberation is shown.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows how one joins the palms before all the buddhas who abide in all the world-realms of the ten directions and asks them to light the lamp of the Dharma for beings who suffer, and who, in the darkness of ignorance, are bewildered and cannot tell the path from what is not the path. All the commentaries agree in the end that this is the petition urging the buddhas to turn the wheel of Dharma.

**Key points.**

- One joins the palms to the buddhas of the ten directions and asks that the lamp of the Dharma be lit for beings bewildered in the dark.
- The lamp of dharma is the Dharma of scripture and realization, which makes clear the path to liberation.
- The reason for the petition rests on the story of how, long ago, during the seven weeks in which the Buddha remained after his awakening without teaching, Brahmā and Indra offered a golden wheel and a white conch and asked that the wheel of Dharma be turned.
- Some commentators say that while making the request one also imagines offering a golden wheel or a dharma conch; others do not mention this. ⚑
- The result of lighting the lamp of dharma is that the darkness of not knowing is cleared away and the true path to liberation is shown.
