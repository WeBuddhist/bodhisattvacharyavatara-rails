## Today's Verse

> Suffering arises from unwholesome deeds.
> "How will I definitely be freed from that?"
> It is right for me to think
> only of this, day and night. ^2-62

> Whatever misdeeds I committed,
> whether inherently harmful or prohibited,
> due to my ignorance and delusion,
> I confess all of them. ^2-63

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 62 and 63 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Suffering comes from wrongdoing and bad actions. That is certain. Under the power of not knowing and confusion we have built up a great deal of wrongdoing. Some of it is harmful by its very nature. Some of it is wrong because it was forbidden. These verses call on us to confess all of it with regret, and to keep thinking, all the time, about the way to freedom.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give a comparison that is valuable, easy to understand, and encouraging.

Khenpo Kunzang Palden said, with an example, that confessing wrongdoing is one of the most important preparations in Buddhist practice. Before a cloth is dyed, it first has to be washed clean. That washing matters a great deal.

In the same way, if the qualities of hearing, thinking and meditating are to grow in us as something new, the mind has to be washed clean first, with strong regret. What gets washed away is everything we have built up through not knowing and confusion, both the kind that is harmful by nature and the kind that was forbidden. Once that has been cleaned away, the qualities of the Dharma can finally hold on in us. The commentaries make the point clearly.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will bring up regret for the wrongdoing I have built up, knowingly and unknowingly, and confess it.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ All suffering comes from wrongdoing and bad actions. So, as the verse says, we should think day and night about how to get free from them. Confessing with regret the wrongdoing we have built up is the best way there is to cut off the cause of suffering.
