## Today's Verse

> Why did I not previously
> bear the meaningless suffering of hard ascetic practices,
> faith, and the feelings of being burned or torn apart,
> for the sake of liberation? ^6-13

> There is nothing
> that cannot be made easy through habit.
> Therefore, by becoming accustomed to minor harm,
> endure great harm. ^6-14

> Have you not seen
> the meaningless suffering
> of snakes, flesh-flies,
> hunger, thirst, and itching? ^6-15

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the thirteenth, fourteenth, and fifteenth verses of the sixth chapter. We endure the meaningless suffering of flies and itching. In the same way, today we get our mind used to harm, starting with small harms.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable story, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Later, the king heard Minister Gorawa singing as he carried a load of thorns out of a rocky gorge. The king asked, "Before, you recited mantras to the beat of a drum. Now, why do you sing under a load of thorns?" He answered, "Before, I slept on seven layers of silk and ate tasty food, but I was not happy. Now I am used to this hardship. So even carrying thorns out of a rocky gorge, I feel no suffering. Happiness and suffering do not come from the body. They come from the habits of the mind." The king said, "That is true," and gave him the post of minister again. This shows that if we first get used to small harms, we can slowly bear great harms. Whatever we get used to becomes easy.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will bear small hardships, such as hunger, thirst, and being bitten by flies.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ People bear many small, meaningless sufferings. So we should put on the armor of a great mind and, for a meaningful purpose, bear hardships, starting with small ones. If we get used to suffering, it is easy to bear. If we do not get used to it, it is hard to bear.
