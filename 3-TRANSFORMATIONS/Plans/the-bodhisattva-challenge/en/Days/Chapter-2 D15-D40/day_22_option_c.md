# Day 22 — Offer the city as you pass through it

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Offer the finest thing you pass today instead of wanting it
> **Detail:** Shantideva offers grand palaces in his mind. Today, offer the impressive things around you as you go.

## Opening

Today's offerings are the grandest yet, whole palaces and jewelled parasols, all raised up in the mind and given to the buddhas and bodhisattvas. The quiet invitation underneath is close to home. The impressive things are not only in the imagination. Some of them are the buildings, cars, and beautiful objects you will walk past today.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

> I always offer to the lords of sages
> Beauteous, jeweled parasols with golden staves,
> Fine shaped, upright, and pleasing to the eye,
> Their rims festooned with winsome ornaments.

## From the Tradition

When something impressive catches our eye, the usual reflex is a small tug of wanting, or a quiet wish that it were ours.

The verse offers palaces and parasols, and Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, pictures them in lavish detail, glowing until they decorate the open sky. The point the tradition draws out is not that you must imagine grander things. It is that the impressive things already around you can be lifted up the same way. A fine building, a beautiful car, an elegant object in a shop: instead of letting the tug of wanting take hold, you offer it in your mind to the buddhas. Nothing changes but the direction of your attention, which turns from getting to giving. A walk through town becomes a steady, quiet practice, and the habit of grasping gets a little weaker each time.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

As you move through your day, each time something impressive catches your eye, a grand building, a nice car, a beautiful object, offer it in your mind to the buddhas and bodhisattvas before walking on. You keep nothing and lose nothing; you simply turn a moment of wanting into a small gift.
