## Traditional Interpretation

### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Palden (Tibetan)

Verse 1-8 draws the practical conclusion from 1-7: since bodhicitta is the sole means for all benefit, it must never be abandoned. KKP structures the verse around three motivational orientations, each addressed: (1) for those wishing to overcome the hundreds of samsaric sufferings (*srid pa'i sdug bsngal brgya phrag gzhom 'dod* — the srāvaka/pratyekabuddha motivation); (2) for those wishing to remove the complete unhappiness of all beings (*sems can mi bde bsal bar 'dod* — the great-scope bodhisattva motivation); (3) for those wishing to enjoy manifold happinesses and glories (*bde mang brgya phrag spyod par 'dod* — those seeking happiness through practice). In all three cases, the conclusion is the same: bodhicitta must never be abandoned (*rtag tu gtang bar mi bya*). KKP cites a sūtra to the effect: one should not abandon bodhicitta even at the cost of one's life.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-8)

### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Tibetan)

GDR reads the verse as addressing anyone who has any of the three types of aspiration — whether for self-liberation from samsaric suffering (middling scope), removal of beings' suffering (great scope), or enjoyment of abundant happiness (lesser scope). In all cases, the means to accomplish what is desired is precisely bodhicitta; therefore it must be held (*bzung bar bya*) and never let go (*rtag tu gtang mi bya*). GDR emphasises the universality: verse 1-8 leaves no motivated practitioner uncovered — all three goals converge on bodhicitta as their instrument.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-8)

### ngulchu-thogmed — Ngulchu Thokme Sangpo (Tibetan)

NTS gives a compact reading: those of lesser scope (wishing happiness), middling scope (wishing to escape samsara), and greater scope (wishing to remove beings' suffering) — practitioners of all three scopes depend on bodhicitta for the fulfillment of their respective aims. Therefore bodhicitta must never be abandoned. NTS notes that the order of the three clauses does not reflect their relative merit — the verse lists them in order of increasing scope (samsara-overcoming → beings'-unhappiness-removing → happiness-enjoying) but the hierarchy of merit runs in the opposite direction: great-scope > middling > lesser.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-8)

### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Mi Nyag Kunzang Sönam (Tibetan)

MKS gives the most detailed three-scope analysis. The verse's three clauses map precisely to the lamrim's three types of practitioner (*skyes bu gsum*): (1) "wishing to overcome hundreds of samsaric sufferings" = the middling practitioner (*skyes bu 'bring*) who seeks liberation from saṃsāra for themselves; (2) "wishing to remove all beings' unhappiness" = the great practitioner (*skyes bu chen po*) motivated by universal compassion; (3) "wishing to enjoy hundreds of manifold happinesses" = the lesser practitioner (*skyes bu chung ngu*) who seeks mundane happiness. In all three cases bodhicitta is the indispensable means; hence it must always (*rtag tu*) be kept and never cast away. MKS also notes that bodhicitta here means primarily *aspiring bodhicitta* (*smon sems*), since even lesser-scope practitioners can aspire; one need not have fully generated engaging bodhicitta (*'jug sems*) for this verse to apply.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-8)

### khenpo-kunga — Ngakwang Kunga Wangchuk (Tibetan)

NKW reads the verse as applying to all practitioners without exception: whether one's motivational scope is to overcome samsaric suffering (for oneself), to remove others' unhappiness, or to enjoy manifold happiness — the means to accomplish all these aims is bodhicitta. Therefore bodhicitta must be held always and never abandoned, even at the cost of one's life. NKW notes the implication: since bodhicitta is the instrument for *all* worthy goals, abandoning it is self-defeating regardless of one's particular motivation.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-8)

### Divergences

**Three clauses — mapping to the three scopes**: All commentaries read the three clauses as corresponding to different motivational types. The mapping is near-unanimous: (1) samsara-overcoming = middling scope; (2) beings'-unhappiness-removing = great scope; (3) happiness-enjoying = lesser scope. NTS observes the verse lists these in reverse order of merit (lesser scope last in the verse, greatest in merit is the second clause). ⚑ MKS specifies that bodhicitta here primarily means aspiring bodhicitta, while other commentaries do not make this distinction explicit.

བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་རང་དོན་དང་གཞན་དོན་གཉིས་ཀའི་རྒྱུ་ཉིད་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། དམིགས་པ་གང་ཡང་རུང་བ་ (① རང་གི་མཐར་ཐུག་མེད་པའི་སྲིད་པའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་གཞོམ་འདོད་ / ② བདག་ཉིད་ཙམ་གྱི་མ་ཡིན་པར་སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་མི་བདེ་བ་མ་མཐའ་དག་བསལ་འདོད་ / ③ མངོན་མཐོ་ངེས་ལེགས་ཀྱི་བདེ་བ་མང་པོ་སྤྱོད་འདོད་) གང་ཡིན་ཡང་སྒྲུབ་ཐབས་ཀྱི་གཞི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་འདི་ཉིད་ཡིན་པས། སྲོག་ལ་ཕར་འཇལ་ཡང་འདི་ཉིད་དུས་རྟག་ཏུ་གཏང་བར་མི་བྱ་སྟེ་རྟག་ཏུ་བཟུང་བར་བྱའོ། །
