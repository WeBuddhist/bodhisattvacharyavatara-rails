# Day 2 — This chance will not come around again

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Name one thing you keep putting off
> **Detail:** This human life is rare. Today, name one thing that matters but you keep delaying.

## Opening

Shantideva makes his first argument: a human life with the freedom to practise is extraordinarily rare. Today's two verses say how rare it is, and how briefly the wish to do good tends to flicker in us.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## Today's Verses

> དལ་འབྱོར་འདི་ནི་རྙེད་པར་ཤིན་ཏུ་དཀའ། །
> སྐྱེས་བུའི་དོན་སྒྲུབ་ཐོབ་པར་གྱུར་པ་ལ། །
> གལ་ཏེ་འདི་ལ་ཕན་པ་མ་བསྒྲུབས་ན། །
> ཕྱིས་འདི་ཡང་དག་འབྱོར་པར་ག་ལ་འགྱུར། །
>
> This precious human life of leisure and opportunity is extremely difficult to find. Now I have this chance to accomplish my goals. If I do not use it to do good, how will I ever find such an opportunity again?

> ཇི་ལྟར་མཚན་མོ་མུན་ནག་སྤྲིན་རུམ་ན། །
> གློག་འགྱུ་སྐད་ཅིག་རབ་སྣང་སྟོན་པ་ལྟར། །
> དེ་བཞིན་སངས་རྒྱས་མཐུ་ཡིས་བརྒྱ་ལམ་ན། །
> འཇིག་རྟེན་བསོད་ནམས་བློ་གྲོས་ཐང་འགའ་འབྱུང་། །
>
> A flash of lightning on a dark, cloudy night reveals everything for a single instant. Similarly, the power of the Buddha occasionally inspires a brief moment of virtue in this world.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Je, a fifteenth-century teacher, points to a famous image from Nagarjuna. Picture a blind turtle that rises to the ocean's surface once every hundred years. Drifting somewhere on that same ocean is a wooden yoke with a single hole in it, blown about by the wind. The chance of being born human, Nagarjuna says, is smaller than the chance of that turtle surfacing with its neck through the hole. The image is not meant to cheer you up. It is meant to make you stop and take this rare life seriously.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Think of one thing that truly matters to you, not a chore, but something about how you want to live, that you keep putting off. Today, just ask yourself, honestly, what you are waiting for.
