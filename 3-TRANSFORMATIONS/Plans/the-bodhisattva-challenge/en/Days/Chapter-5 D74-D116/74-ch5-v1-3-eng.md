## Today's Verse

> Those who wish to uphold the training
> must guard their minds with great effort.
> If this mind is not guarded,
> the training cannot be upheld. ^5-1

> Just as an unleashed mind-elephant
> inflicts harm like that of the lowest hell,
> even a wild, intoxicated elephant
> does not inflict such harm here. ^5-2

> If the mind-elephant is firmly bound
> with the rope of mindfulness,
> all fears will cease,
> and all good deeds will come to hand. ^5-3

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the first three verses of the fifth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Let us think about why watching over our own mind is the root of our happiness, day by day.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

In an ancient Indian battle, an elephant was made wild with liquor. A wheel of weapons was tied to the tip of its trunk, and it was sent into battle. It could destroy an enemy's city and kill many people. This caused great harm. Yet even that elephant cannot do what an untamed mind, ruled by the afflictions, can do. It cannot burn people alive in the lowest hell and cause every kind of harm found there. So we must work hard to guard our own mind.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will bind my own mind firmly to the side of doing good with the rope of mindfulness, and I will not let it wander.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ If we let our own mind wander freely, like an elephant let loose, much wrongdoing and suffering will follow. So if we tie our own mind to doing good with the rope of mindfulness, every fear will disappear and every good action will come about naturally.
