## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three commentators agree that Verse 1-14 forms the climax of the section praising the common benefits of generating the mind of enlightenment (bodhicitta). This verse presents the fifth and final core analogy—the world-consuming fire at the end of an eon—and concludes by establishing canonical scriptural authority through the dialogue between Maitreya and Sudhana in the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*.

### 1. The Analogy of the Aeon-Ending Fire
*   **The Metaphor of Universal Destruction:** The commentators explain the cosmic scale of the analogy: just as the fire at the end of an eon (*yugāntakālānalavat* / *དུས་མཐའི་མེ་*) completely consumes the physical vessel of the world up to the first concentration (the first dhyāna) without leaving a trace of ash, generating the mind of enlightenment has the supreme power to burn and destroy great sins in an instant (*kṣaṇena* / *སྐད་ཅིག་གཅིག་གིས་*).
*   **The Nature of "Great Sins" (*mahānti pāpāni* / *སྡིག་ཆེན་*):** Sazang Mati Panchen and Ngulchu Thokme specifically identify these great sins as the five actions of immediate retribution (*ānantarya-karma*), which ordinarily lead directly to hell. Gyaltsab Je defines them as "extremely fierce negativity such as harming the Three Jewels or the five heinous crimes."
*   **Doctrinal Distinction: Overcoming vs. Destroying Negativity:**
    *   **Ngulchu Thokme's Reconciliation:** Thokme provides a crucial distinction to prevent confusion between the fourth analogy (the heroic escort in Verse 1-13) and this fifth analogy (the aeon-ending fire). He explains that the heroic companion represents "overcoming" or protecting a practitioner from experiencing the ripening maturation of negativity, whereas the aeon-ending fire represents "destroying negativity from its very root."
    *   **Progressive Realization:** Thokme explains that there is no conflict between these two benefits because they arise progressively as the practitioner attains higher and higher levels of generating the mind. Sazang Mati Panchen similarly notes that this complete destruction occurs "in a single instant of obtaining great patience or the instant of completing the action."
    *   **Purification of Definite Karma:** Gyaltsab Je and Sazang Mati Panchen cite the explanation of Kṣemadeva, who asserts that because one does not perceive any object of apprehension in a single instant of great patience, the mind of enlightenment definitely burns even karma that is "definitely to be experienced" (which would otherwise ripen without the meditation on the opponent powers).

### 2. Scriptural Proof: Maitreya's Explanation to Sudhana
*   **The Canonical Source:** All three commentators identify the scriptural source referenced in the second half of the verse as the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra* (often translated in Tibetan commentaries as the *Array of Stalks Sūtra* or *སྡོང་པོ་བཀོད་པའི་མདོ་*). In this text, Maitreya (the Loving Protector, *Maitreyanātha*) expounds on the limitless benefits of generating the mind of enlightenment to the young truth-seeker Sudhana (Nor-bzang).
*   **Narrative Context:**
    *   **Sazang Mati Panchen's Detailed Account:** Sazang explains that Sudhana, the son of a merchant guild leader, first generated the mind of enlightenment in the presence of Mañjuśrī. After seeking the conduct of awakening and relying on many spiritual friends, he eventually arrived at the palace of Maitreya. Sazang quotes the sūtra directly, where Maitreya welcomes Sudhana: "Behold this completely pure aspiration of Sudhana, the son of the extremely firm Nor-bzang, who seeks the holy conduct of awakening, and has excellently come into the presence of me, the Protector."
    *   **Ngulchu Thokme and Gyaltsab Je:** Both commentators confirm that the wise Protector Maitreya, possessing great wisdom, literally explained these immeasurable benefits and praises to the young disciple Sudhana during their meeting.
*   **Analogy Citations from the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*:**
    The commentators cite several specific analogies used by Maitreya in the sūtra to illustrate the qualities of the mind of enlightenment:
    *   **Seed:** It is like the seed of all the Buddhadharmas (Gyaltsab Je, Sazang Mati Panchen, Ngulchu Thokme).
    *   **Field:** It is like a field because it makes all the wholesome qualities of wandering beings grow (Gyaltsab, Sazang, Thokme).
    *   **Earth:** It is like the earth because it supports the entire world (Sazang, Thokme).
    *   **Vaiśravaṇa (God of Wealth):** It is like Vaiśravaṇa because it cuts off all poverty (Sazang).
    *   **Wish-Fulfilling Jewel:** It is like a wish-fulfilling jewel because it accomplishes all purposes (Sazang).
    *   **Excellent Vase:** It is like an excellent vase because it completely fulfills all aspirations (Gyaltsab, Sazang).
    *   **Spear:** It is like a spear to defeat the enemy of afflictions (Gyaltsab).
