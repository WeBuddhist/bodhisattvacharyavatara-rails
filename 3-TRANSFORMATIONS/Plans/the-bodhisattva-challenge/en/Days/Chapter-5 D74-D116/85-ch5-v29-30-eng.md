## Today's Verse

> Therefore, never let mindfulness
> depart from the gate of the mind.
> Even if it departs, recall the harms of lower realms
> and bring it back close. ^5-29

> From associating with a teacher,
> from the instructions of a master,
> and from fear, mindfulness easily arises
> in those who are fortunate and respectful. ^5-30

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the twenty-ninth and thirtieth verses of the fifth chapter. Here, mindfulness is shown clearly as a gatekeeper standing at the door of our own mind's house, guarding against the thieves of the afflictions.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us an example that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Our own mind is like a house. Mindfulness is like a gatekeeper stationed at the door of that house. This gatekeeper must always guard the door, so the thieves of the afflictions do not enter the house of the mind. So we must never send this gatekeeper of mindfulness away from the door.

If mindfulness weakens and the gatekeeper is gone, the thieves of the afflictions will destroy us. So the _Letter to a Friend_ says: mindfulness is the greatest strength of the body. The Buddha showed it as the one path. I will guard it with all my effort, because when mindfulness is lost, thieves bring ruin.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will not let mindfulness leave the door of my mind. If it does wander, I will remember the harm of the lower realms and bring it back at once.

**Explanation:** _(Diligence Practice)_ This practice is the best method for guarding our own mind stream. It keeps wrongdoing and mistakes from arising through a distracted mind. If we rely on mindfulness at the door of the mind, and if it wanders, we remember the suffering of the lower realms and bring mindfulness back at once, our mind can stay calm and remain firmly turned toward doing good. This brings great benefit.
