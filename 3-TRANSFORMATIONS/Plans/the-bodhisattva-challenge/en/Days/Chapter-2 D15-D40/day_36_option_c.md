# Day 36 — A prescription does nothing on the shelf

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Take one remedy you already know works, today
> **Detail:** Today's verse names the healer who pulls out every thorn. Medicine only helps if you take it.

## Opening

Shantideva names the doctor at last: the Omniscient One, the healer who, as the verse puts it, removes every pain. He pictures the buddha pulling out the thorn of suffering the way you would draw a splinter from a foot. And then he says something almost blunt: to have such a physician and such a cure, and decide not to follow it, is the height of foolishness. The medicine exists. The only question is whether we take it.

## From the Tradition

Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, calls the buddha the supreme of all physicians, the one who draws out every thorn of suffering. It is a warm image: not a judge, but a healer leaning in to remove what is hurting you. But the commentators are united on the catch, and it is the point of the verse. A prescription left in the drawer cures nothing. A patient who nods at the doctor and never takes the medicine does not get well, however good the medicine is. We do this with the dharma all the time. We collect teachings, agree with them, even find them beautiful, and then do not actually apply them when the affliction flares. The verse is not scolding us for being bad; it is pointing out something almost comic: we are standing next to the cure, holding it, and not swallowing it. The remedy only works once taken.

## Today's Practice

Think of one remedy you already know helps you, a few slow breaths, a short walk, recalling a line of teaching, pausing before you reply, and that you keep skipping anyway. Today, when the moment comes, actually take it. Not learn a new one, not promise to do it later. Just fill the prescription you are already holding, once, and notice that it works because you used it.
