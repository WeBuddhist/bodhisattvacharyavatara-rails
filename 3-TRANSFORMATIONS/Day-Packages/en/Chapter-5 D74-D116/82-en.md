## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the twenty-first, twenty-second, and twenty-third verses of the fifth chapter. We feel deep inspiration seeing how Master Shantideva himself joins his palms and pleads with us from the depths of his heart.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a point that is easy to understand and encouraging.

When Master Shantideva joins his palms and pleads with his followers to guard their minds, this is not just a devotional gesture. It is because guarding both mindfulness and awareness is so important that we must protect them even at the cost of our life.

It is not enough for those who wish to guard their minds simply to sit alone with the body, or to withdraw from crowds and stay by themselves. They must rely on mindfulness and awareness at all times. This is just as Jowo Atisha said:

> With mindfulness, awareness, and carefulness, always guard the doors of the senses. Again and again, day and night, three times each, examine the flow of your own mind.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, I will examine my own body and mind again and again, and I will not forget mindfulness and awareness.

**Explanation:** *(Meditation Practice)* Those who wish to guard their minds must first work hard to guard both mindfulness and awareness. Mindfulness keeps us from forgetting what to accept and what to reject. Awareness lets us examine the state of our own body and mind again and again. By doing this, we do not let our own mind fall under the power of what is harmful. Instead, we can guide it onto the good path.

*Plain English translation (source: `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-translate/BCA-Full-Plain-English.md#^5-21`):*

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

This teaches that if one dwells by means of conduct such as this that guards the mind, then whether one dwells among wicked people who are like rākṣasa demons, or dwells among women who are like the daughters of gods, the very stability and clarity of the mind that strives at the vows does not decline.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains that if one dwells by means of conduct of this kind that guards the mind, then even upon encountering objects that give rise to attachment and aversion, no fault arises, owing to one's stable discipline of guarding the mind — and further, that even a fully ordained monastic committed to celibacy who dwells among objects such as women will incur no fault, provided he is held by that same mindfulness and awareness.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-21]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

He explains that if one dwells by means of conduct of this kind that guards the mind, then whether one dwells among wicked people or dwells among women, one's steadfast diligence in the vows — that is, one's conscientiousness — does not decline.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-21]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

If one dwells continuously by means of conduct of this kind that curbs the afflictions, then whether one dwells among wicked, angry people, or dwells among alluring women, the resolve of one who is diligent in the vows does not decline. Gyaltsab Je specifies that "wicked people" refers to the condition that gives rise to anger, and "women" to the condition that gives rise to attachment, thereby showing that no matter which of the two afflictions' conditions one meets, the mind committed to the vows does not decline.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-21]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

If, at all times and in every circumstance, one dwells continuously by means of conduct such as this that guards one's own mind through mindfulness and awareness, then whether one dwells among wicked, malicious people — an object that gives rise to anger — or dwells among beautiful women — an object that gives rise to desire — one's firm commitment to strive at guarding the precepts of one's training will not decline on account of such conditions: such is the benefit. His outline indicates that this verse sets out the benefit of the mindful "taking hold" spoken of in verse 5-20.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-21]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If one dwells continuously by means of conduct such as this that guards one's own mind, then whether one dwells among a crowd of wicked, malicious people — an object that gives rise to anger — or dwells within a mansion among beautiful women — an object that gives rise to desire — one's steadfastness in striving to guard the bodhisattva vows does not decline from conscientiousness with regard to the trainings, because it never goes astray.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-21]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

If, relying on mindfulness and awareness, one continually dwells by means of conduct such as this that guards one's own mind from the wound of the afflictions, then whether one dwells among many wicked people who commit various kinds of misconduct — an object that gives rise to anger — or dwells among many beautiful, seductive women — an object that gives rise to desire — the steadfast, conscientious diligence with which one respectfully and joyfully guards the precepts of one's own training does not decline or crumble, because the firm application of mind that serves as the antidote to the wish to abandon the training does not waver even in the slightest.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-21]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

If a person is able to dwell by means of conduct such as this — conscientious, mindful, and aware — that guards the mind, then whether that person dwells among wicked people, like rākṣasa demons, who give rise to intense anger, or dwells among beautiful women, like the daughters of gods, who give rise to desire, the very firm resolve produced by diligently striving to thoroughly guard the precepts of one's own training does not decline in any respect: such is the benefit intended here.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-21]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. A Truly Stable Discipline of Guarding the Mind Is Unaffected by External Circumstance

Once one has attained a truly firm discipline of guarding the mind, one's commitment to the vows remains undiminished regardless of what external circumstance one encounters.

###### 2. "Wicked People" and "Women" Represent the Two Principal Conditions for Anger and Desire

Gyaltsab Je specifies that the root verse's "wicked people" and "women" represent, respectively, the two principal conditions that give rise to anger and to desire.

###### 3. This Verse States the Benefit of Guarding the Mind Taught in Verse 5-20

This verse sets out the benefit of the conduct of guarding the mind through mindfulness and awareness taught above in verse 5-20.

###### 4. The Teaching Applies to Both Lay and Ordained, with a Special Note for the Celibate Monastic

This applies generally to both the ordained and the lay; His Holiness the Dalai Lama specifically points out that even a fully ordained monastic committed to celibacy incurs no fault dwelling among women, provided mindfulness and awareness are present.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Relying on conduct that guards the mind through mindfulness and awareness, the commentators agree that no matter what environment gives rise to anger or desire, one's steadfast commitment and conscientiousness with regard to the vows does not decline.

**Key points.**

- **The benefit of guarding the mind.** Once a truly firm discipline of guarding the mind is attained, one's commitment to the vows and one's conscientiousness are unaffected by any external circumstance.
- **The meaning of the terms.** In the root verse, "wicked people" signifies the condition for anger, and "women" the condition for desire — the two principal conditions, per Gyaltsab Je.
- **The connection in the text.** This verse sets out the benefit and purpose of the conduct of guarding the mind through mindfulness and awareness.
- **A special note for the ordained.** Even a fully ordained monastic committed to celibacy dwelling among women incurs no fault, provided he is held by mindfulness and awareness.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

This teaches: let my gains — such as robes and the rest — and my honor — such as being respectfully bowed to — and even my body and my life-sustaining livelihood be lost; and let other virtues of body and speech decline as well; but the virtue of the mind must never be allowed to decline.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^5-22]]

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains that even if one's gains, one's honor — in short, one's body and resources — decline, and likewise even if other virtues of body and speech decline, this is acceptable; but the virtue of the mind must never be allowed to decline — this is simply not permissible.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-22]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Let my gains — such as offerings received — and my honor — such as being bowed to or having a seat spread for me — and the support of body and life, my own livelihood such as food and clothing, be lost; in short, even should I come to die, this is acceptable. And let other virtues, any virtue whatsoever of body and speech, decline as well — this too is acceptable. But for the sake of guarding the training, this discipline of guarding my own mind at all times through mindfulness and awareness must never, at any time, be allowed to decline — it is simply not to be done.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-22]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Let my gains — such as food and clothing — and my honor — such as being bowed to — and the very condition that sustains my body and life, my livelihood, be lost; this is acceptable. And let other virtues that are unconnected with guarding the mind decline as well; this too is acceptable. But the mind of the Great Vehicle must never be allowed to decline. Gyaltsab Je specifies that "other virtues" here refers only to virtue unconnected with guarding the mind, thereby showing that guarding the mind itself is greater than everything else.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-22]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Let my alms and other gains be lost, and let honor such as being bowed to be lost, and let my own body and livelihood decline; and let other virtue, virtue for one's own purpose, decline as well — so says Vairocana. Gelha explains it as: "This bodhicitta of the ten [grounds] must never be allowed to decline." The Great Commentary [by Prajñākaramati] says "the virtue of mind" — that is, in brief, "other virtue" may even refer to actions of body and speech. Thus he sets out, for the meaning of the term "mind," the differing ways it is explained individually by these three earlier masters. ⚑ *(Editorial note: "Vairocana" and "Gelha" render the Tibetan names རྣམ་སྣང and དགེ་ལྷ as cited within Ngulchu Thogme's own commentary — proper names of earlier exegetes, not commentator ids in this project's locked table; their precise identity is uncertain and flagged for specialist review.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-22]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Let my gains — such as offerings received — and my honor — such as having a seat spread for me — and the support of body and life, the necessities of livelihood such as food and clothing, all be lost; this is acceptable. And let other virtue, whether virtue of body and speech or other good qualities, decline as well; this too is acceptable. But for the sake of guarding the training, this discipline of guarding one's own mind must never, at any time, be allowed to decline. His outline explains this verse as the summary conclusion of the section beginning with verse 5-20.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-22]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Let my gains — alms, robes, food, and clothing — and my honor — being bowed to, having a seat spread for me, and the like — and the livelihood that serves as the condition for the continuous well-being of my body and life, such as the necessities of food and clothing, or even my very life itself, all decline and be lost; this is acceptable. And let any other virtue of body and speech that does not serve as a means of guarding the intention to benefit others decline as well; this too is acceptable. In brief, one must generate the firm conviction that guarding this virtuous intention — the mind of awakening — without letting it decline, is the chief task to be accomplished by all who have entered the Great Vehicle.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-22]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Even if the offerings and gains of this present life, and the wealth and resources one might have received, are lost through never having been obtained, this is acceptable. Even if honor — such as being bowed to, having one's hat doffed for one, or having a seat spread for one — is lost, this is acceptable. Even if this very body itself declines and is lost, this is acceptable. Even if the other circumstances of livelihood — food, clothing, dwelling, and the rest — are lost through their absence, this is acceptable. And even if other virtue — circumambulation, recitation, and other practices of body and speech — declines through one's inability to perform them, this is no cause for astonishment; it, too, is acceptable. But the mind must never be allowed to decline: the discipline of guarding one's own mind, together with mindfulness and awareness, must never in any respect be allowed to decline, and must be guarded.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-22]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Everything Else May Be Lost, But the Discipline of Guarding the Mind Must Never Decline

Gains, honor, body and life, and every other virtue of body and speech may be lost — this is acceptable — but the discipline of guarding the mind, that is, bodhicitta, is the essential point that must never be allowed to decline.

Minyak Kunzang Sonam adduces, as scriptural proof that the mind is the root of the Dharma, quotations from the *Advice to the King* and the *Letter to a Friend*.

###### 5. This Verse Is the Summary Conclusion of the Section on Guarding the Mind Begun at Verse 5-20

By way of the topical outline, this verse serves as the summary conclusion of the section on guarding the mind that begins with verse 5-20.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** That gains, honor, body, life, or any other virtue of body and speech may be lost is acceptable to every commentator; that the discipline of guarding the mind — or the virtue of the mind — must never be allowed to decline is the single point on which they all speak with one voice.

**Key points.**

- **The essential point.** Gains, honor, body and life, and every other virtue of body and speech may all be lost; the discipline of guarding the mind must never be allowed to decline.
- **The meaning of "other virtues."** Gyaltsab Je specifies that this refers only to virtue unconnected with guarding the mind, showing that guarding the mind is greater than everything else.
- **⚑ Differing exegeses of "mind."** Ngulchu Thogme records that three earlier masters (Vairocana, Gelha, and the Great Commentary) explain the term "mind" differently here.
- **Where this verse falls.** By way of the topical outline, Sabzang Mati Panchen explains this verse as the summary conclusion of the section on guarding the mind beginning at verse 5-20.
- **Scriptural proof.** Minyak Kunzang Sonam shows that the mind is the root of the Dharma by citing the *Advice to the King* and the *Letter to a Friend*.

### Verse 5-23

> **Rail source:** `2-RAILS/Verses/5-23-summary.md` &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Rail status:** `draft`

#### Root Verse

*Plain English translation (source: `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-translate/BCA-Full-Plain-English.md#^5-23`):*

> To all who wish to guard the mind I join my palms and say: guard mindfulness and awareness with every effort.

---

<!-- sub:interlinear -->
#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

This teaches: to all who wish to guard the mind, I say: "Guard, through every avenue and with all effort, the mindfulness that does not forget what is to be adopted and what is to be discarded, and the awareness that repeatedly examines the state of body and mind." For the sake of urging this guarding, I join my palms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^5-23]]

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains that since guarding the mind in this way is a root of practice, it is not enough for those who wish to guard the mind merely to sit alone with the body, or merely to withdraw from crowds and dwell in solitude. Rather, one must guard the mind by continually relying, at all times, on the mindfulness and awareness that guard the mind. We must understand that this — the great bodhisattva Śāntideva himself, with joined palms, speaking these words — is being spoken to us as well, here and now.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-23]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

For the reasons just stated above, to those of my followers who wish to guard this mind of theirs, I say: "Guard, through every avenue and with all effort — even at the cost of your very life — the mindfulness that does not forget what is to be adopted and discarded, and the awareness that examines well before engaging in the conduct of the three doors [body, speech, and mind]." Thus, I, Śāntideva, verbally implore you and join the palms of my hands together — and this is not spoken out of mere devotion toward us; it is spoken because the matter is of the utmost importance.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-23]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Since one cannot guard the mind apart from mindfulness and awareness, to those who wish to guard the mind I say: "Guard, without letting it decline even at the cost of your life, the mindfulness that does not forget the object of virtue, and the awareness that examines how matters stand with the three doors [body, speech, and mind]." Thus, I, Śāntideva, join my palms and implore you. ⚑ Since some editions read "exhorts" instead of "join my palms," it is explained that way as well.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-23]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Those who wish to guard the mind should, even at the cost of their life, guard the mindfulness that does not forget the object of what is to be adopted and what is to be discarded, and the awareness that examines one's own mind-stream and knows what is to be adopted and abandoned within it. ⚑ Thus I exhort you in this way.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-23]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Thus, to those who wish to guard the mind in this way, I say: "Guard, with every effort and even at the cost of your life, the mindfulness that does not forget the distinction between what is permitted and what is prohibited, and the awareness that examines well before engaging in the circumstances of the three doors." Thus I join my palms and respectfully exhort you in this way.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-23]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

For the sake of guarding the training, to those disciples who wish to guard the mind I say: "Guard, in every respect and with all effort, both the mindfulness that does not forget how matters of engagement and disengagement truly are, or that recollects, as appropriate, what is permitted and prohibited, and the awareness that, having separately examined how the circumstances of the three doors are turning out, engages accordingly — not merely one or the other of these two, but both together." Thus, I, Śāntideva, join my palms and implore you in this way. This shows that since one cannot guard the mind apart from mindfulness and awareness, and since without guarding the mind one cannot guard the training, this matter is of the utmost importance.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-23]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

For the sake of guarding the training, to those disciples who wish to guard this very mind of theirs from the afflictions such as desire, I say: "Guard, in every respect and with intense effort, without letting it decline, the mindfulness that does not forget what is to be adopted and discarded, and the awareness that repeatedly examines and investigates the state of body and mind." Thus, I, Śāntideva, the author of this treatise, for the sake of urging this guarding, join the palms of my hands and give this instruction to my disciples — such is the meaning.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-23]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Joining of Palms Is Not a Mere Gesture of Devotion

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that Śāntideva's joining of his palms to plead with his own followers is not a mere gesture of devotion, but is spoken because mindfulness and awareness are of such crucial importance that they must be guarded even at the cost of one's life.

###### 2. ⚑ A Textual Variant: "Exhorts" in Place of "Joins Palms"

Gyaltsab Je notes that some editions read "exhorts" in place of the root verse's closing "joins palms"; Ngulchu Thogme's and Sabzang Mati Panchen's own commentaries likewise rely on the reading "exhorts."

###### 3. Mindfulness and Awareness Are Both Required Together

Minyak Kunzang Sonam firmly establishes that mindfulness and awareness are both required together — not merely one or the other.

###### 4. Scripture Defines Mindfulness and Awareness Precisely

Minyak Kunzang Sonam clarifies the definitions of mindfulness (from the *Abhidharmasamuccaya*) and awareness (from the Mother sutra) by citing them directly.

###### 5. This Instruction Applies to Us as Listeners Today

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that it is not enough merely to sit alone with the body or to withdraw into solitude; one must continually rely on mindfulness and awareness — and teaches that Śāntideva's instruction here applies equally to listeners today.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** To all who wish to guard the mind, Śāntideva joins his palms and, with intense effort, instructs them to guard mindfulness and awareness; this plea is spoken not out of mere devotion but because guarding these two, even at the cost of one's life, is of the utmost importance.

**Key points.**

- **Not a devotional gesture.** Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that the joining of palms is spoken because guarding mindfulness and awareness must be maintained even at the cost of life.
- **⚑ A textual variant.** Some editions and several commentaries read "exhorts" in place of "joins palms" at the close of the root verse.
- **Both are required together.** Mindfulness and awareness must both be maintained; neither alone suffices, and without them neither the mind nor the training can be guarded.
- **Scriptural definitions.** Mindfulness is defined, via the *Abhidharmasamuccaya*, as non-forgetting of a familiar object; awareness, via the Mother sutra, as the repeated examination of body and mind.
- **Universal application.** His Holiness the Dalai Lama teaches that mere physical solitude is not enough — continuous reliance on mindfulness and awareness is needed — and that this instruction applies to us as listeners today, just as it did to Śāntideva's own followers.
