# Day 38 — The two things we bend our principles for

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Notice the two pulls today, and don't bend for either
> **Detail:** Today's verses name what we override our values for: pleasures and people. Neither stays.

## Opening

Across these two verses, Shantideva names the two great forces that pull us off course. One is pleasure, the things we enjoy. The other is people, the friends we cling to and the rivals we resent. For the sake of these two we routinely set aside what we know is right. The verses make one quiet point about both: they do not last. The pleasures perish, and the people are left behind when we go.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, keeps the two verses tied together, because they make a single case. The first looks at pleasures: of everything you have enjoyed and lost, he says, not even a little of substance remains, yet you clung to that empty thing and went against the guidance you had accepted. The second looks at people: you will leave them all and go alone, so the clinging to allies and the resenting of rivals comes to nothing. His conclusion is not gloom but redirection. If the two things we keep compromising ourselves for are both temporary, then the sane response is to stop spending our integrity on them and to put our energy, one-pointedly, into what actually lasts, the practice itself. The verses clear away two false priorities so that a true one has room.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Today, watch for the two pulls. When a pleasure tempts you to cut a corner you would rather not, or when liking or disliking someone tempts you toward something you would not otherwise do, notice it and simply do not bend this once. Then give that freed-up moment, one-pointedly, to something that lasts: a few minutes of practice, an honest kindness, a quiet breath of refuge.
