## Combined Commentary Summaries
The three commentators provide a consistent structural and theological analysis of the opening homage in Verse 1-1, which is traditionally dedicated to the Three Jewels and other worthy objects of veneration. This act of worship serves to purify obstacles, generate immense merit, and prepare the author and audience for the presentation of the Bodhisattva path.

### 1. The Purpose and Function of Homage
All three commentators agree that paying homage to supreme spiritual objects is essential for ensuring that noble endeavors reach successful completion. 
* **Gyaltsab Je** structures the homage into purpose, condensed meaning, and word meaning. He explains that praising and taking refuge in supreme objects clears away obstacles to composing the treatise, establishes the author as a holy being, and ultimately leads to definitive goodness while enabling following disciples to accumulate merit.
* **Sazang Mati Panchen** focuses on how hearing about the Buddhas' qualities generates clear faith, which in turn inspires engagement in the path and text. He cites the *Sūtra of Making Many Buddhas* to show that even minor actions directed toward the Victorious Guides lead to higher rebirths and the deathless state of nectar.
* **Ngulchu Thokme** similarly explains that the homage is performed to accomplish temporary and ultimate happiness, citing the *Sūtra Praising the Qualities of the Buddha* to establish that small offerings to the Teacher enable one to traverse the happy realms of gods and humans and attain immortality.

### 2. The Definition of "Sugata" (The Blissful One / Gone to Bliss)
The commentators unpack the name "Sugata" (*bde bar gshegs pa*) using classical scholastic frameworks of perfect abandonment and perfect realization:
* **Gyaltsab Je** and **Ngulchu Thokme** provide detailed breakdowns of Sugata's aspects of abandonment and realization:
  * *Perfect Abandonment:* Both cite three analogies for abandonment: 
    1. *Leaving beautifully/gracefully* (like a handsome person), because of having abandoned all afflictive obscurations. This distinguishes the Buddha from non-Buddhist ascetics who merely suppress attachment.
    2. *Leaving in a way that one does not return* (like a disease completely cured), because of having eliminated the seeds of ignorance and afflictions so that he does not return to cyclic existence. This distinguishes him from Stream-enterers and Once-returners.
    3. *Leaving completely/without exception* (like a filled vase), because of having abandoned non-afflictive ignorance and all habit-energies. This distinguishes him from the Arhats of the lesser vehicle. (Thokme attributes this threefold schema to Locana).
  * *Perfect Realization:* Both explain that the Buddha is "Gone to Bliss" because of directly knowing the reality of the two types of selflessness, knowing firmly, and knowing without exception. This distinguishes him from non-Buddhist ascetics, and lesser vehicle disciples and solitary realizers. (Thokme attributes this to Kṣemadeva).
* **Sazang Mati Panchen** explains "Sugata" in terms of self-benefit and other-benefit. Self-benefit is completed through perfect realization of the unconditioned realm of reality, while other-benefit is completed by guiding sentient beings out of compassion and possessing the power to eliminate their suffering. He cites the *Supreme Continuum* (*Uttaratantraśāstra*) to define Buddhahood as unconditioned, spontaneously present, and endowed with the two benefits.

### 3. The Three Jewels
The commentators align the elements of the homage verse with the Three Jewels:
* **The Buddha Jewel:** The Sugata who possesses the Dharmakāya.
* **The Dharma Jewel:** 
  * **Gyaltsab Je** defines it as the Truth Body (Dharmakāya), which is the naturally pure sphere free from all stains.
  * **Sazang Mati Panchen** describes the Dharma Jewel as possessing the direct realization of emptiness endowed with the supreme of all aspects (Truth Body), the cessation of desire, the inconceivable truth of cessation, and the truth of the path. He cites the *Supreme Continuum* definition of the Dharma as "inconceivable, non-dual, free from conceptualization, pure, clear, and in the class of remedies."
  * **Ngulchu Thokme** cites the *Uttaratantra* to divide the Truth Body into two: the completely stainless dharmadhatu (representing realization) and its natural cause, the profound and diverse ways of teaching (representing teachings). The realized Truth Body is inseparable from the pure dharmadhatu and stainless primordial wisdom.
* **The Sangha Jewel ("Sons of the Sugatas"):**
  * **Gyaltsab Je** identifies them simply as the noble Heroes of Enlightenment (Bodhisattvas).
  * **Sazang Mati Panchen** explains that they are the great Heroes of Enlightenment born from the mind of the Buddha, who possess the wisdom of knowing the realm of reality directly and conventional reality as it is, making them the irreversible noble ones of the Great Vehicle.
  * **Ngulchu Thokme** cites the *Uttaratantra* to describe them as heirs whose seed is faith in the supreme vehicle, mother is wisdom, womb is the bliss of concentration, and wet-nurse is compassion, born in the footsteps of the Sages.

### 4. Other Worthy Objects and the Manner of Homage
* **Worthy Objects:** All three commentators agree that homage is also paid to others who are high in qualities and worthy of honor, such as preceptors, masters, and teachers, even if they are not the ultimate Mahayana objects of refuge. Sazang also mentions images, stupas, Hearers, and Solitary Realizers. Sazang quotes the *Uttaratantra* to explain why the Three Jewels are called "Jewels"—because they are rare, stainless, powerful, the ornament of the world, supreme, and changeless.
* **Manner of Homage:** Sazang explains that homage is paid through all three doors: bowing with the body, praising with speech, and recollecting qualities with the mind.
* **Grammatical Transition:** Gyaltsab Je and Ngulchu Thokme point out that the Tibetan coordinating particle *te* ("having paid homage...") functions to connect the preliminary act of homage with the subsequent vow of composition, comparable to saying "having bathed, one eats."
