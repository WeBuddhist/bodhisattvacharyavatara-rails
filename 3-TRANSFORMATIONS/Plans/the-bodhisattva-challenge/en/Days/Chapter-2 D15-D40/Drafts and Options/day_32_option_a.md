# Day 32 — Where do you look when you're scared?

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Notice where you reach when something rattles you today
> **Detail:** Today's verses show panic searching everywhere for rescue. The question is where you turn.

## Opening

These are the darkest verses so far, and they are meant to be. Shantideva imagines himself at the very end, gripped by illness and fear, casting around in every direction for anyone who might save him, and finding no one. He is not leaving us there. This is the bottom of the chapter, and the next move, the one the whole confession has been heading toward, is to turn and take refuge.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, stays with the eyes. The dying person's eyes are wide with terror, darting to all four directions, looking for anyone who can step in and help. He points out that this is not some strange far-off state. We do a smaller version of it most days. The moment something frightens us, the mind starts scanning for rescue, reaching for the phone, the snack, a reassuring voice, anything to make the fear stop. The verse simply turns the volume all the way up, to the one moment when none of those things can answer. Ngulchu does not leave the search unanswered, though. He says the cry for help finds its true direction when it turns to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, the ones whose compassion does not look away. The point of seeing the panic clearly is to learn, before the end, where it was always meant to turn.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Once today, when something small rattles you, a piece of bad news, a worry, a sudden stress, notice the very first thing you reach for to feel safe again. Just see it, without judging it. Then, for one breath, turn the same reaching toward the Buddha, the dharma, and the sangha instead. You are teaching the habit now, while the stakes are low.
