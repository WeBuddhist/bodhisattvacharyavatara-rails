## Combined Commentary Summaries
The three commentators provide a highly complementary and unified analysis of Verse 1-6, which contrasts the extreme weakness of ordinary worldly virtue with the immense, unbearable power of negativity, establishing the mind of enlightenment (Bodhicitta) as the only force capable of overcoming such negativity.

### 1. Structural Outlines and Contextualization
Each commentator contextualizes this verse within their respective outlines of Chapter One, highlighting different angles of the same doctrinal point:
* **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen** explains the "lightning in the dark" metaphor (spanning verses 1-5 and 1-6) under section **1.2** (the advice that it is proper to abandon negativity and accomplish virtue). He subsequently explains the unique power of Bodhicitta to overcome negativity under section **1.4** (overcoming great negativity).
* **Sazang Mati Panchen** treats the verse under section **1.1**, specifically focusing on "holding the mind-generation firmly because it eliminates great negativity."
* **Ngulchu Thokme** analyzes this under section **1.6** (actual benefits divided into common and exceptional) and section **1.7** ("overcoming negativity" as the first of the five common benefits of Bodhicitta).

### 2. The Metaphor of Lightning and the Weakness of Virtue
The commentators agree that ordinary virtue is inherently feeble due to its rare occurrence and short duration:
* **Sazang Mati Panchen** explains that ordinary virtue leading to merit is like a sudden flash of lightning on a dark night—it arises only occasionally and lacks continuity, meaning its strength is extremely weak.
* **Gyaltsab Je** notes that we constantly abide in weak virtue because a virtuous mind wishing to perform merit arises only occasionally in worldly beings through the power of the Buddha, rather than arising continuously.
* **Ngulchu Thokme** summarizes this by stating that since the support (leisure and fortune) and the virtuous mind are extremely difficult to find and of short duration, the virtuous mind is perpetually weak and has little strength.

### 3. The Immense Power of Negativity
In contrast to weak virtue, the power of non-virtue is described as pervasive and terrifying:
* **Sazang Mati Panchen** likens negativity to the thick, dense darkness of a cloudy night. This negativity possesses great power to produce the fierce and extremely unendurable sufferings of hell and other bad rebirths.
* **Ngulchu Thokme** explains that because the causes of non-virtue are easily gathered, extremely powerful and fierce negativity arises continuously.
* **Gyaltsab Je** highlights that this powerful negativity produces birth in bad migrations and represents an extremely fierce situation that produces great fear.

### 4. Bodhicitta as the Sole Remedy
All three commentators reach the consensus that only perfect Bodhicitta can conquer and neutralize this massive negativity:
* **Sazang Mati Panchen** defines the perfect mind of enlightenment as that which is "endowed with the essence of emptiness and compassion." He asserts that no other ordinary virtue can overcome and outshines negativity.
* **Gyaltsab Je's Doctrinal Distinction:** Gyaltsab Je introduces a key scholastic distinction: while the ultimate mind of enlightenment (directly realizing the ultimate mode of existence) has the power to abandon the seeds of obscuration, what is primarily praised in this verse is the conventional mind of enlightenment. Conventional Bodhicitta has the power to purify previously accumulated causes of bad migrations and completely cut off future ones.
* **Scriptural Support:** The commentators cite key scriptures to prove Bodhicitta's supreme power:
  * **Sazang Mati Panchen** quotes the *Teaching of the Four Dharmas* (*Caturdharmanirdeśa Sūtra*): "The power of the support is taking refuge in the Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha, and not discarding the mind of enlightenment. By relying on these powerful ones, all negativities are overcome."
  * **Ngulchu Thokme** cites the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra* (*Array of Stalks Sūtra*): "Just as the eyes of a cat instantly overcome mice so they cannot endure, likewise, the eyes of the precious mind of perfect enlightenment also instantly overcome the mice of karma and afflictions so they cannot endure."
