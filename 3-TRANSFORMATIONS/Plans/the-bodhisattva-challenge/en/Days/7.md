# Day 7 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: Benefits of Bodhicitta — Verses 1-19 to 1-21

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Continuous Growth of Goodness (Merit Without Effort):** When you make a strong wish to help all living beings, good energy keeps growing inside you — even when you sleep or are not paying attention. This is very helpful because it means your spiritual progress does not stop. You do not need to work hard every single moment to make progress.

*   **Freedom from Fear of the Path (Confidence in the Journey):** Some people feel afraid when they think about how long and hard the path to full enlightenment is. Today's verses show that the good energy of this wish grows easily and naturally. This helps remove fear and gives you the confidence to keep going without feeling overwhelmed.

*   **The Power of Kind Thoughts (Even Small Wishes Matter):** Even a small kind thought — like wishing to remove someone's headache — carries a lot of good energy. This teaches you that every kind thought has real value. It encourages you to keep choosing kind thoughts in daily life, because even small ones make a big difference.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-19**

> དེང་ནས་བཟུང་སྟེ་གཉིད་ལོག་གམ། །
> བག་མེད་གྱུར་ཀྱང་བསོད་ནམས་ཤུགས། །
> རྒྱུན་མི་འཆད་པར་དུ་མ་ཞིག །
> ནམ་མཁའ་མཉམ་པར་རབ་ཏུ་འབྱུང་། །
>
> *from then on, even while asleep or careless, a vast stream of merit arises continuously, as vast as the sky.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-19**

Once a person truly commits to the wish for enlightenment — not just as a wish but as an active way of living — good energy starts to flow without stopping. This stream of merit keeps growing even when the person is asleep, distracted, or not thinking about the path at all. The reason is clear: the wish to free all living beings from suffering is so vast and deep that it keeps producing good energy on its own, as long as the commitment does not weaken. All three traditional teachers agree that this is the key difference between simply wishing for enlightenment and truly engaging with the path of a Hero of Enlightenment.

---

#### **Verse 1-20**

> འདི་ནི་འཐད་པ་དང་བཅས་པར། །
> ལག་བཟང་གིས་ནི་ཞུས་པ་ལས། །
> དམན་མོས་སེམས་ཅན་དོན་གྱི་ཕྱིར། །
> དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་གསུངས། །
>
> *The Buddha explained this clearly with sound reasoning in the teaching requested by Subāhu. He did this for the sake of those with lesser aspirations.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-20**

The great teacher Śāntideva points to a well-known teaching from the Buddha to prove that good energy keeps growing without stopping. The teaching was given for people who felt afraid of the long path to full enlightenment. These people worried that it would take too much effort and too much time. The Buddha gave this teaching to help them feel less afraid. He showed them that once the heart of enlightenment is truly engaged, good energy builds up easily and naturally, even without constant effort. This was the Buddha's compassionate way of helping people who might otherwise give up and take a shorter path.

#### **Verse 1-21**

> སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས་ཀྱི་ཀླད་ནད་ཙམ། །
> བསལ་ལོ་སྙམ་དུ་བསམས་ན་ཡང་། །
> ཕན་འདོགས་བསམ་པ་དང་ལྡན་དེ། །
> བསོད་ནམས་དཔག་མེད་ལྡན་གྱུར་ན། །
>
> *Even if someone merely wishes to cure a headache for other beings, that kind intention yields immeasurable merit.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-21**

Even a very small kind wish — just wanting to remove a headache from another person — carries a huge amount of good energy. This might seem surprising, but the traditional teachers explain it clearly. The good energy does not come from the size of the wish. It comes from the heart that genuinely wants to help another being. There is even a story of a person who, while suffering greatly in a painful situation, had one sincere thought of wishing to take away others' pain. That single thought was so powerful that it ended the suffering and brought great happiness. If one small kind thought has that much power, the great teacher Śāntideva shows us that the vast wish to free all living beings from all suffering has power beyond measure.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Notice Kind Thoughts Through the Day:** Today, I will pay attention every time I have a kind thought — even a small one, like hoping someone feels better. I will remind myself that these small thoughts have real power and I will not dismiss them as unimportant.

*   **2. Renew My Wish Before Sleep:** Tonight, before I go to sleep, I will take a moment to refresh my wish to help all living beings. I will remind myself that this wish keeps growing even while I sleep, and I will feel encouraged by that thought rather than worried about the length of the path.

*   **3. Be Patient with My Own Progress:** When I feel like my practice is not enough, I will remember today's teaching. I will remind myself that even my small efforts build good energy that does not stop. I will trust the process and not give up because the path feels long or hard.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམར་བར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Merit (བསོད་ནམས།):** This means good energy or positive force that comes from kind thoughts and helpful actions. In today's verses, merit grows and builds up whenever someone has a genuine wish to help others. Even small kind thoughts create this positive energy, and it keeps growing over time.

*   **The Engaged Wish for Enlightenment (འཇུག་པའི་སེམས།):** This is the active, committed wish to reach full enlightenment in order to help all living beings. It is not just a quiet hope — it is a strong resolve that shapes how a person lives and acts. Today's verses show that this committed wish produces a constant flow of good energy.

*   **Those with Lesser Aspirations (དམན་མོས་སེམས་ཅན།):** This refers to people who feel afraid of the vast effort needed to reach full enlightenment and so aim for a smaller, more limited goal instead. The Buddha gave the teaching in Verse 1-20 specifically for such people, to help remove their fear and encourage them to take the fuller path.

*   **Sky-like (ནམ་མཁའ་མཉམ་པར།):** This phrase describes the size of the merit that comes from the engaged wish for enlightenment. Just as the sky has no edges and no end, the good energy produced by this wish is said to be truly boundless. It is a way of saying the benefit is beyond what any number can describe.

*   **Headache (ཀླད་ནད།):** In today's verses, a headache is used as an example of a small, ordinary kind of suffering. The point is that even wishing to help someone with something as small and everyday as a headache creates great good energy. This example helps make the teaching feel personal and close to daily life.
