# Day 10 — We want to be happy, and run the other way

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Name what you were really reaching for
> **Detail:** We chase happiness and, confused, run from it. Today, name what you actually wanted.

## Opening

Today's verses say something striking: just wishing to help others is worth more than the grandest gifts laid before the buddhas. Then they turn to something sad. People want to be happy, yet keep walking straight into what hurts them, not because they want pain, but because they are confused about what leads where.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

> Although beings wish to abandon suffering, they run directly into suffering itself. Although they desire happiness, out of ignorance they destroy their own happiness like an enemy.

## From the Tradition

These three verses show that the awakening mind is like a wish-fulfilling jewel, and like the finest medicine, the nectar that removes all the suffering of beings. If merely wishing to help beings brings more merit than making offerings to countless Buddhas, then think how much greater it is to actually work for the good of others.

Why do beings need such compassion and such an awakening mind? Because, even though they do not want suffering, they rush toward harmful actions like moths flying into a flame. And even though they want happiness, out of confusion and ignorance they destroy the very causes of their own happiness, as if those causes were enemies. For these beings — whose thoughts and actions are both so mixed-up — there is no refuge anywhere greater than compassion and the awakening mind.

## Today's Practice

**Practice**: Today, I will stop doing one habit that I know causes me suffering.

**Explanation**: Today's verse explains that even though people want to escape suffering, they often run right toward it. Out of confusion, we accidentally destroy our own happiness as if it were an enemy. By choosing to stop one harmful habit today, you are letting go of this confusion and using wisdom to protect your own joy.
