# Video Creation Playbook
### Distilled from "Marketing The Dharma" Masterclass (Days 1–7) — for the Bodhisattva Challenge video-idea assistant

This playbook keeps only what's directly useful for generating and producing video content. General business/monetisation detail, platform pricing, and team-org material from the source workbooks have been left out except where it directly shapes a video decision.

---

## 1. Content Formats — What Container to Use

Every idea should be assigned a format before it's scripted, because format determines pacing, length, and hook style.

**Short-form video** (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) — under 60 seconds, one idea, one emotional payoff. Best for discovery and new audiences. Vertical, muted-first (captions mandatory).

**Long-form video** (YouTube talks, Q&A, documentary-style) — 5–30 minutes, full story arc, deeper teaching. Builds an evergreen "library" — content that keeps gaining views for years.

**Livestreams** — real-time teaching, pujas, Q&A. Builds trust and community fastest but content has short shelf life.

**Documentary-style** — cinematic journeys (pilgrimages, a day in a life), narrative-driven.

**Animated / AI-generated explainers** — good for illustrating abstract concepts (visualising deities, historical events, "invisible" ideas like karma or emptiness).

**Guided-practice video** — meditation/chanting people return to repeatedly; strong for building a library of "reusable" content.

Rule of thumb: pick one message, then decide the *shortest* format that can carry it, and a *longer* version for depth. Almost every idea should be produced in at least two lengths (a 60-second cut and a 5-minute version).

## 2. Niches / Content Pillars

A niche is the "doorway" people first connect through — it doesn't limit the teaching, it gives it an entry point. For the Bodhisattva Challenge, useful niche buckets (from the masterclass's 15 Buddhist content niches) include:

1. **Sacred pilgrimage / place-based storytelling** — history, ritual, and "why this place is holy."
2. **Storytelling from scripture** — parables/jataka tales retold and connected to a modern struggle.
3. **Wisdom for modern life** — "Ask a Monk"-style answers to everyday problems (stress, grief, relationships).
4. **Monastic / practitioner lifestyle** — behind-the-scenes of the practice itself.
5. **Mindfulness & meditation instruction** — practical, replicable practices.
6. **Rituals & sacred arts** — showing the "why" behind a practice, not just the "what."
7. **Interfaith dialogue** — comparing/contrasting with other traditions.
8. **Age-tailored content** — different framing for kids/teens/young adults.
9. **AI-recreated content** — using AI to recreate stories from the tradition, visualise deities/sacred places, or animate key teachings.
10. **Animated storytelling** — short films/animation to make teachings visually "cool."
11. **Collaborations** — cross-pollinating with creators outside the niche.
12. **Comparative/"myth vs new-age wellness"** content.
13. **Conversational/podcast format** — interviews, including appearances on non-Buddhist podcasts.
14. **History & socially-engaged action** — documenting compassion in action.
15. **Myth-busting** — correcting common misconceptions in a clear, non-defensive way.

A good video idea usually sits at the intersection of **(a) a specific teaching/practice** and **(b) a modern pain point** — e.g., not "meditation" in general, but "one breath to calm exam-panic."

## 3. Idea Validation — Don't Guess, Listen First

Before scripting, run every idea through this filter so it's rooted in real demand, not assumption:

- **Personal validation** — Has this been lived/practiced/witnessed firsthand? ("I know this works because I've seen it.")
- **Market validation** — Is there already proof people want this? (Check what's trending on YouTube/Google, bestselling books in the space, top Reddit threads, paid courses on the topic.)
- **Consumer validation** — What exact words do real people use to describe this struggle? (Pulled from interviews, comments, forums — use their language, not doctrinal language.)

### The Pain → Path → Gain framework
Every piece of content is a journey:
- **Pain** — the specific suffering, named concretely (not "dukkha" — "racing thoughts at 2am," "doom-scrolling," "loneliness eating alone").
- **Path** — the specific teaching/practice offered as the way through.
- **Gain** — the felt transformation ("racing thoughts → inner calm").

A useful one-line generator for any video concept:
> "I help [specific person] go from [specific pain] to [specific gain], so they can [desired feeling]."

## 4. Hooks, Titles & Thumbnails

**The first 3 seconds decide everything.** If the hook doesn't land, nothing else in the video matters.

### Hook types (use for opening line / on-screen text)
- **Contradiction** — "The happiest people aren't the richest."
- **Question** — "What happens if a monk loses his phone?"
- **Vulnerability/emotion** — "I nearly gave up meditation because of this…"
- **Value promise** — "Yes, monks get angry too — here's what we do with it."

### 10 title psychology triggers
Contradictory curiosity · tangible timeframe ("in 30 days") · insider authority ("Former monk reveals…") · using negativity ("biggest mistakes") · weight of regret ("I wish I knew this before…") · personal victory · cliffhanger question · broad relatability · buzzwords · sensational promise.

### Title best practices
Front-load keywords · keep under 60 characters (mobile-safe) · use power words ("secret," "proven," "mistake") · numbers/lists ("7 Steps to…") · tease without misleading · title + thumbnail + description must tell the same story.

### Thumbnails
Treat as ~50% of the effort (design before filming so the video matches the promise). Success is ~80% psychology, ~20% design polish — create a *curiosity gap* (moment/story/result/transformation/novelty). Scroll-stoppers: faces with big expressions, numbers, bright contrast, simplicity. Max 5 words of text, never repeating the title. Always produce 3 thumbnail variants and be ready to swap.

## 5. Storytelling Structure

Facts inform; stories transform — they trigger mirror neurons and stick in memory. Every video, short or long, should follow the same six-beat arc, just compressed for short-form:

1. **Hook / Exposition** — relatable situation + a surprising or emotional opening line.
2. **Inciting moment (conflict)** — name the specific struggle honestly.
3. **Rising action** — show failed attempts; this is what makes the resolution feel earned.
4. **Climax (turning point)** — the "aha" moment, delivered simply and emotionally, not doctrinally.
5. **Falling action (integration)** — show the before/after in daily life, concretely.
6. **Resolution + CTA** — one clear, memorable sentence of wisdom + an invitation to act (comment, try, share).

Short-form compression: **Hook → Conflict → Resolution** in under 60 seconds.
Long-form: full six-beat arc across 5–10+ minutes, with more anecdote and layered takeaway.

### Sticky-idea checklist (SUCCES)
Simple (one idea, not ten) · Unexpected (break a pattern) · Concrete (sensory, specific imagery, not abstraction) · Credible (lineage, personal experience, a graspable number) · Emotional (evoke a feeling, not just information) · Story-driven.

## 6. Style, Resonance & Relatability

- **Style is a fingerprint, not a formula** — study how admired creators deliver (hooks, humour, urgency, vulnerability) but don't copy their persona, only the *technique*. Authenticity (quirks, pauses, accent, imperfection) is what makes content memorable.
- **Resonance bridges "monk life" and "ordinary life."** Distance creates admiration; shared humanity creates trust. Everyday anchors that work well: food/daily routines, pets, exercise, small struggles, humour.
- **Why it works (the science, briefly):** mirror neurons make viewers feel what they watch you feel; self-disclosure of small personal details builds intimacy fast; showing your process (not just the polished result) makes viewers feel invested (the "IKEA effect").
- **Imperfection is a bridge, not a flaw** — a stumble followed by a smile builds more trust than a flawless take.

## 7. What Makes Content Spread (use when the goal is reach)

Virality = felt value × easy hand-off. A few compact frameworks worth applying to any idea before publishing:

- **STEPPS** (why people share): Social currency, Triggers (daily-life cues that resurface the idea), Emotion (high-arousal beats neutral), Public/visible behaviour, Practical value ("save this"), Stories. Aim for at least 2 of these per post.
- **Hooked loop** (why people return): Trigger → Action (lowest-friction possible) → Variable reward (fresh angle each time, same relief) → Investment (comment/save/share).
- **MAYA** (Most Advanced Yet Acceptable): pair a *familiar* frame (a universal pain point) with one *novel* twist (something surprising from the practice) — too familiar is boring, too novel feels unsafe.
- **Purple Cow**: one deliberately remarkable, pattern-breaking element per video (a signature ritual opener, an unexpected visual, a sticky metaphor) — without turning it into a gimmick.
- **StoryBrand**: the *viewer* is the hero, not the creator. The creator is the guide (like Yoda, or a doctor) — clarify the problem in the viewer's words, offer 1–3 simple steps, and show the cost of inaction gently.
- **Reduce friction**: captions/subtitles always on, vertical/square crop, one specific CTA ("send this to one friend who needs calm today") beats a generic "share this."

## 8. AI-Assisted Production Workflow

Treat AI as a fast, tireless assistant — it provides structure and speed; the human provides lived truth and soul. A repeatable pipeline:

**Stage 1 — Planning & scripting** (ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini/Perplexity)
Feed it audience research (pains/gains, real language from interviews or comments) → generate 10–20 hook variants → pick 2–3 → expand the best into a full script using the Hook→Conflict→Rising Action→Climax→Integration→Resolution+CTA arc, with timestamps, on-screen text (≤9 words/beat), and B-roll notes.

**Stage 2 — Media generation**
- AI voice/narration (for multilingual dubbing of the same talk) — always disclose when a clone/avatar is used.
- AI music/soundbeds for meditation content (royalty-free, mood- and tempo-matched).
- AI image/video generation for b-roll and thumbnails (visual metaphors like "waves of thought," "light breaking through clouds").

**Stage 3 — Post-production**
- Transcript-based editing to cut filler words/pauses.
- Auto-captioning (critical — most viewers watch muted).
- AI clipping tools to turn one long talk into 5–10 short-form clips.

**Stage 4 — Distribution**
- Keyword/title optimisation tools to match how people actually search.
- Auto-resizing/reformatting one master video for each platform's aspect ratio and caption style.
- Scheduling tools to keep a steady publishing cadence without manual daily effort.

**Quality gate before filming/publishing** — ask AI to self-audit: score the hook 1–10 for curiosity/clarity/honesty, quote the exact line that maps to the target pain/gain, list 3 likely viewer objections and how the script addresses them. Only proceed once the hook scores ≥8/10.

## 9. Delivery & Camera Presence

The camera magnifies whatever energy is already present — mirror neurons mean a calm, warm presenter makes viewers feel calmer; a rushed, tense one transmits tension.

Before recording: 3 slow breaths, roll shoulders back/open the chest, a short vocal warm-up, and a one-line intention ("who am I serving right now?") — this shifts focus from self-consciousness to service and visibly changes tone.

Structure vs. spontaneity: outline in 3 beats (Hook → Teaching → Practice step), rehearse once, then deliver freely from the outline rather than memorising word-for-word.

Body language carries most of the message: open hands/visible palms (signals honesty), genuine smile, and deliberate pauses after a key line (a 3-second pause lets the point land).

## 10. Iteration — The 1% Loop

Treat every published video as feedback, not a verdict.

- **Feedback loop**: Publish → wait 48–72 hours for data → identify the single weakest point (usually the hook, audio clarity, or a relatability gap) → change exactly one variable in the next video → log it.
- **What to look at per platform**: YouTube — retention graph (a sharp drop in the first 10–30 seconds = weak hook) and CTR (healthy range ~6–10%); Instagram — saves and shares (saves are the strongest "this was useful" signal, more meaningful than likes); TikTok — average watch/completion percentage (aim for 90%+ on short clips) and shares.
- **Never chase every fix at once.** One small, specific improvement per post compounds — small daily gains compound multiplicatively over a year, not just additively.
- **Consistency beats frequency.** Two well-made posts a week outperform seven rushed ones; "consistently bad" erodes trust faster than posting less often but better.

---

## 11. Quick-Reference: Idea → Video Checklist

For every new video idea, confirm before greenlighting:

1. Pain and Gain are both stated in plain, specific, everyday language (not doctrinal terms).
2. A hook exists that lands in ≤3 seconds and uses one of the trigger types above.
3. The story arc is mapped (even for a 30-second short): Hook → Conflict → Resolution (+CTA).
4. At least one everyday/relatable anchor is present (routine, humour, a small honest struggle).
5. Format and length are chosen deliberately (short cut + long cut where possible).
6. Title (≤60 characters) and 3 thumbnail concepts are drafted before filming.
7. A single, specific call-to-action is defined (not a generic "like and subscribe").
8. A plan exists to check performance at 48–72 hours and note one thing to improve next time.
