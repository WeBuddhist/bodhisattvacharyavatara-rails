## Today's Verse

> I rejoice in the good deeds
> that bring rest from the suffering of all beings in the lower realms,
> and in the happiness of those who suffer. ^3-1

> I rejoice in the good deeds accumulated
> that are the cause of awakening.
> I rejoice in the definite liberation
> of embodied beings from the suffering of cyclic existence.
> I also rejoice in the awakening of the protectors
> and the bodhisattvas on the paths. ^3-2

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 1 and 2 from the third chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. We have finished confessing our own faults. Today is where something else begins: taking heartfelt joy, with no jealousy in it, in the good that others do. That runs from the smallest act which eases someone's suffering all the way up to the awakening of a buddha.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses tell us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago King Prasenajit, wanting to benefit others, made vast offerings to the Buddha and the Sangha for seven days. The Buddha said that each day he would dedicate in the name of whoever had done the most good.

At the palace gate was a beggar called Sada. He rejoiced at the king's giving from the depths of his heart. The good he gained by rejoicing turned out to be greater than the king's giving. So for six days the Buddha dedicated the beggar's good rather than the king's.

On the seventh day the ministers contrived to have the beggars beaten. Sada was upset, and his rejoicing left him. That day the Buddha dedicated the king's giving instead, and the king received a prophecy.

The story shows that rejoicing in what someone else does can bring us more good than our own giving.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when I see others doing good and finding happiness, I will rejoice without jealousy.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ This practice settles the jealousy and competitiveness in our own mind. If we cultivate heartfelt joy at other people's good and at their happiness, we gain great merit ourselves without any effort. This is the actual practice of rejoicing that the first verse teaches.
