## Combined Commentary Summaries

Here the text turns from invoking protectors to admitting the fault directly. All three commentators read "your command" as the Buddhas' basic teaching — epitomized, as Sazang notes by citing the verse "Commit no wrong whatsoever; perfectly cultivate virtue" — which the speaker admits having disregarded in the past.

Gyaltsab and Sazang both frame the plea as twofold: an acknowledgment of past transgression paired with a present resolve to act henceforth exactly as instructed regarding what is to be adopted and abandoned. Because the speaker now vividly sees the terror of endless saṃsāra and the lower realms, refuge is taken with real urgency, and the plea is for the Buddhas to dispel that fear swiftly. This verse opens what Gyaltsab Je's outline calls the section on striving at the antidote to wrongdoing, structured around the analogies of illness and of a precipice that follow.
