# Day 9 — Even Brahma's vast goodwill falls short of this

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Push your kindness past where it usually stops
> **Detail:** Even a god's goodwill stops short of this wish. Today, take one wish all the way.

## Opening

Chapter One's case closes today. These verses search every kind of being, parent, sage, god, even Brahma, and find that this wish has never arisen anywhere in samsara. A mind this rare is also beyond measure in what it does.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

> This jewel of the mind is the cause of joy for all wandering beings, and the medicine that cures their suffering. How can its immeasurable merit ever be measured?

## From the Tradition

Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, describes Brahma as someone who has mastered the widest goodwill samsara holds, wishing every being well. And yet, he says, it is still not this mind. The difference is what it aims at. Brahma wishes beings comfort, peace, a better life. The bodhisattva wishes something else entirely: that every being, without exception, awaken fully. Even goodwill on a cosmic scale still works inside samsara. This wish steps outside it, which is why the verses can search everywhere and find it nowhere.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

When you wish someone well today, a stressed colleague, a struggling friend, notice where the wish usually stops, somewhere near "I hope things get better for them." Try taking one such wish further, all the way to: may they be completely free.
