# Registers for the Bodhicaryāvatāra English versions

## Academic
- Readers: students and practitioners reading a careful study translation; university readers of Buddhist texts.
- Faithful and precise; one locked rendering per term (per sense, where a Tibetan term has clearly distinct senses in this text).
- Use established English Buddhological equivalents. Keep a Sanskrit word untranslated only where academic English normally does (e.g. bodhicitta, bodhisattva, Sugata, saṃsāra, nirvāṇa), with IAST diacritics.
- Proper names in their standard Sanskrit form with IAST diacritics (Śāntideva, Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, Avalokiteśvara); names of philosophical schools in their usual English or Sanskrit form (Madhyamaka, Cittamātra, Sāṃkhya).
- Rendering is a noun-phrase head form (singular, lower case unless a name or title); the translator inflects it in context.

## Children's
- Readers: children aged 8–12 reading or being read a retelling of Śāntideva's poem in English.
- Plain everyday words; short. A technical idea becomes a simple phrase that keeps the meaning, never a different idea (do not turn emptiness into "nothing").
- No Sanskrit except names; names without diacritics (Shantideva, Manjushri). "Buddha" stays "Buddha".
- One rendering per term (per sense), used every time.
