## Today's Verse

> Herons, cats, and robbers,
> who move stealthily and silently,
> accomplish their desired aims.
> The Sage always practiced likewise. ^5-73

> Skillfully encouraging others,
> and speaking unsolicited beneficial words,
> one should respectfully accept them with bowed head,
> and always be a student to everyone. ^5-74

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the seventy-third and seventy-fourth verses of the fifth chapter. Today we think about walking silently like a cat. We also think about bowing our head with respect and becoming a student of everyone.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Just as a heron kills fish, a cat kills mice, and a thief steals goods, going silently and getting what we want is an example of getting things done through gentle conduct. Bodhisattvas always practice silent and calm conduct. The purpose of this is to give up the thorns of deep meditative concentration, and to become skilled in the ways of accomplishing the benefit of others. We must learn the kind of conduct that makes beings happy as soon as they see us. His Holiness the Dalai Lama taught that followers of the Buddha must learn the way of being a friend to everyone.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when someone gives me helpful teaching, I will listen with respect and without pride.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ If we listen with respect to the helpful teaching of others, our pride becomes calm. If we are a student of everyone, we gain new good qualities. This sets our mind on doing good.
