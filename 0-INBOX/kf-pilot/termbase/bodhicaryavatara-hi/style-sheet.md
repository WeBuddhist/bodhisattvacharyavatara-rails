# Style sheet: Hindi (Devanagari) termbase of the Bodhicaryāvatāra

## Policies

1. Script and punctuation: Devanagari only, never Roman transliteration or IAST in a rendering; sentence-final stop is the danda (।), ordinary commas and quotation marks, Western digits for numbers in running text but numerals inside a term are spelled out (तीन लोक, दस दिशाएँ). Reason: one script for both registers and clean matching in the later translation.
2. Spelling of Sanskrit loans: anusvāra before a consonant (संसार, संवृति, संघ, संवर, गंगा), but keep the conjunct म् before प/ब/भ (सम्प्रजन्य, सम्पदा, क्षण-सम्पदा, सम्बोधि), keep ञ् in मञ्जुश्री and पञ्च, use त्त्व (सत्त्व, बोधिसत्त्व), ऋ for ṛ (ऋषि), and in the academic register the visarga (दुःख) and the final halant of the stems आत्मन्, मनस्, भगवान्; the children's register writes दुख and भगवान. Reason: registers-hi.md already spells सम्प्रजन्य, क्षण-सम्पदा, मञ्जुश्री, संवृति this way, and one spelling per word avoids false variants.
3. Academic register, evidence order: (a) the terms listed in registers-hi.md; (b) the established Hindi Buddhist tatsama that the Gemini zero-shot also uses (in the lines checked it uses सत्त्व, क्लेश, पुण्य, संसार, संवृति, परमार्थ, सम्प्रजन्य, तथागत, सुगत, जिन); (c) the rendering already approved for the same Tibetan term in the Diamond Sutra and Ratnaguṇasaṃcayagāthā glossaries; (d) where Gemini differs, the Sanskrit tatsama that matches the Sanskrit equivalent in overview.md; (e) everyday Hindi only when the tatsama is opaque or misleading. Reason: Gemini is evidence of attested usage, not an authority; it follows the Sanskrit well but merges senses and spells loosely.
4. Gemini is overridden when it (i) merges two Tibetan words into one Hindi word (अहङ्कार for ང་རྒྱལ, which would clash with अहंकार for བདག་ཏུ་འཛིན་པ; भाव for དངོས་པོ, which in Hindi means feeling), (ii) uses a Hindi word with another everyday meaning (स्वार्थ "selfishness" for རང་དོན), (iii) uses a Hindu-devotional or Urdu-Persian word (आत्मा, मोक्ष, see 5), or (iv) varies within the text (बोधिचर्यावतार and बोधिसत्त्वचर्यावतार for the title, मञ्जुघोष and मञ्जुश्री for one name). Reason: the glossary must keep the sense distinctions of the approved English glossary and give one rendering per term.
5. Never use Hindu-devotional or Urdu-Persian substitutes for technical terms in the academic register: no आत्मा (ātman is आत्मन्), मोक्ष (liberation is मुक्ति or विमोक्ष), प्रभु, अवतार, फ़रिश्ता, दुनियावी, ज़िन्दगी. Two exceptions, each tied to its own Tibetan term: पाप only for སྡིག་པ / སྡིག (the direct tatsama of pāpa, as in the chapter title पापदेशना and Ratnaguṇa's पापमित्र; མི་དགེ་བ stays अकुशल, never पाप); ईश्वर and प्रधान only for དབང་ཕྱུག and གཙོ་བོ, the opponents' own terms in chapter 9. Reason: the registers file forbids substitutes that import another doctrine, but a word that is the Sanskrit term itself imports nothing. The children's register may use everyday Hindustani words (दुनिया, आज़ादी, नफ़रत) because the registers file asks only for simple common Hindi there.
6. The senses of ཆོས stay apart by the sense label in the packet, not by Gemini's single धर्म: the Buddha's teaching = धर्म (children's शिक्षा), written in the singular; the plural "teachings" (5-89) = धर्म-देशनाएँ (children's शिक्षाएँ); things as such (9-105) = धर्म only in the plural or with सभी/सर्व (सभी धर्मों) and in the compounds धर्मता and धर्मधातु (children's दिखने वाली चीज़); quality of a Buddha = गुण (children's गुण). Reason: Hindi has no capital letter to separate "Dharma" from "phenomenon", and the Diamond Sutra and Ratnaguṇa glossaries use these same word choices.
7. Compounds with ཆོས follow the sense of ཆོས inside them: teaching compounds keep धर्म (सद्धर्म for དམ་པའི་ཆོས / དམ་ཆོས, children's पवित्र शिक्षा; धर्मकाय for ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ, children's शिक्षा का शरीर); buddhadharma (སངས་རྒྱས་ཆོས) is बुद्ध-गुण (children's बुद्ध के गुण), never बुद्धधर्म, which in Hindi means Buddhism; अधर्म is never used. Reason: the English glossary resolves each compound by sense and Hindi बुद्धधर्म or अधर्म would change the meaning.
8. Epithets of the Buddha: academic uses the Sanskrit tatsama in the nominative with no vocative ending, each epithet always the same word and kept distinct (जिन for རྒྱལ་བ and རྒྱལ, सुगत for བདེ་གཤེགས and བདེ་བར་གཤེགས, तथागत for དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ and དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས, भगवान् for བཅོམ་ལྡན, मुनि for ཐུབ་པ, नाथ for མགོན་པོ and མགོན, मार्गदर्शक for འདྲེན་པ, मुनीन्द्र for ཐུབ་དབང, जिनपुत्र for རྒྱལ་སྲས); children's keeps भगवान, बुद्ध and मुनि and renders the meaning of the rest in plain Hindi (विजेता, भली-भाँति गए हुए, इसी तरह गए हुए, रक्षक, रास्ता दिखाने वाला, मुनियों के स्वामी). Reason: the epithets are separate terms in the text and the translation must not blur them; the forms match the Diamond Sutra and Ratnaguṇa glossaries.
9. Titles and honorifics: आर्य for འཕགས་པ (also in आर्य समन्तभद्र), आचार्य for སློབ་དཔོན, उपाध्याय for མཁན་པོ, गुरु for བླ་མ, भिक्षु for དགེ་སློང, योगी for རྣལ་འབྱོར་པ, कल्याणमित्र for དགེ་བའི་བཤེས་གཉེན; the children's register keeps the everyday ones (आर्य, गुरु, भिक्षु) and uses a short phrase for the rest (मुझे दीक्षा देने वाले गुरु for མཁན་པོ). An honorific variant (ཐུགས་རྗེ for སྙིང་རྗེ) takes the rendering of the plain word, except that སྐུ "body of a Victor" is काय, not शरीर. Reason: it keeps the monastic register without Hindu forms like श्री or स्वामी, and one term keeps one rendering.
10. Names: Indian names in Sanskrit form in Devanagari in both registers and never translated (शान्तिदेव, मञ्जुश्री for both འཇམ་དབྱངས and འཇམ་པའི་དབྱངས even where the Sanskrit is mañjughoṣa, समन्तभद्र, अवलोकितेश्वर, आकाशगर्भ, वज्रपाणि, पद्मपाणि, मैत्रेय, सुधन, सुबाहु, ब्रह्मा, यम, मार); places and objects in the standard Hindi form (मेरु पर्वत, जम्बूद्वीप, अवीचि नरक, शाल्मली); a hell with a Sanskrit name keeps it and a Tibetan descriptive name is rendered by the meaning of the English academic gloss in both registers; Tibetan-only names (the translators) are written as pronounced in Devanagari. Reason: registers-hi.md says names keep their Sanskrit form; children recognise them as names.
11. The six perfections are one fixed set in the academic register: दान, शील, क्षान्ति, वीर्य, ध्यान, प्रज्ञा, and each full perfection is the stem plus पारमिता written as one word (दानपारमिता, शीलपारमिता, क्षान्तिपारमिता, वीर्यपारमिता, ध्यानपारमिता, प्रज्ञापारमिता); the children's register uses देना, अच्छा आचरण, धैर्य, खुशी से की गई मेहनत, शान्त एकाग्रता, समझदारी, with पूर्णता for pāramitā (देने की पूर्णता, अच्छे आचरण की पूर्णता, पूर्ण समझदारी). Reason: all six must read as one list, and the stems क्षान्ति, वीर्य, ध्यान, प्रज्ञा and पारमिता are in registers-hi.md (दान and शील are added from the two approved glossaries).
12. The work, its chapters and its titles: the academic title is the Sanskrit बोधिसत्त्वचर्यावतार (also for the Tibetan transliteration of the Sanskrit title), the children's title जागृति-वीर के मार्ग में प्रवेश; a chapter is परिच्छेद (children's अध्याय); chapter titles follow the approved English gloss: 2 पापदेशना (children's हानिकारक कामों को खुलकर मान लेना), 4 अप्रमाद (सावधानी), 8 ध्यान (शान्त एकाग्रता), 9 प्रज्ञापारमिता (पूर्ण समझदारी), and the other chapter titles use the same stem renderings (बोधिचित्तानुशंसा, बोधिचित्तपरिग्रह, सम्प्रजन्यरक्षण, क्षान्ति, वीर्य, परिणामना). Reason: Gemini already renders the chapter headings this way, and a title built from the stem terms keeps the chapter title and the term identical.
13. The seven-limb practice words are fixed: शरण (སྐྱབས, children's सुरक्षित सहारा), शरण में जाना (སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི, children's सहारे के लिए जाना), वन्दना (ཕྱག་འཚལ, children's झुककर प्रणाम करना), पूजा (མཆོད and མཆོད་པ, children's भेंट), देशना (བཤགས, children's खुलकर मान लेना), अनुमोदना (རྗེས་སུ་ཡི་རང, children's खुश होना), परिणामना (བསྔོ་བ, children's भलाई बाँटना). Reason: these terms come from the same set of practices and the Hindi must keep them from merging.
14. Children's register simplifies a technical term by replacing it with a short plain phrase that translates its parts or carries the approved English children's gloss, never by substituting a different doctrinal idea (निर्वाण becomes शोक से मुक्ति, पुण्य becomes जमा हुई भलाई, सम्प्रजन्य becomes सजग रहना); a one-word loan that is already everyday stays (बुद्ध, करुणा, भगवान, मुनि, गुरु, भिक्षु); names stay; no Hindu-devotional word is introduced. Reason: registers-hi.md; the child must reach the same meaning as the scholar.
15. Children's wording avoids clashes: each plain phrase belongs to one term only, so जीव is སེམས་ཅན and प्राणी is འགྲོ་བ; दुख is སྡུག་བསྔལ and शोक is མྱ་ངན; सुख is བདེ་བ; अच्छा काम is དགེ་བ, बुरा काम is མི་དགེ, and नुकसान पहुँचाने वाला काम is སྡིག་པ; शिक्षा is only the teaching (ཆོས), training is अभ्यास; in the children's register the word ध्यान appears only as "attention" (ध्यान में रखना), while dhyāna is शान्त एकाग्रता. Reason: one rendering per term and per sense, so a reader of the translation can tell the Tibetan terms apart.
16. Compounds take their parts' renderings: where the parts are core terms the compound is built from them, with a hyphen in the academic register when the Hindi tradition has no single word (बुद्ध-गुण, क्षण-सम्पदा, आत्म-ग्राह, आत्म-आसक्ति), as one word when the established Sanskrit compound exists (बोधिचित्त, सद्धर्म, धर्मकाय, जिनपुत्र, बुद्धपुत्र, गुणसागर, सत्त्वधातु, लोकधातु, पापदेशना), and as a plain "X का Y" phrase in the children's register (जागृति का मन, जागृति-वीर, अच्छी खूबियों का सागर). Reason: it keeps terms predictable across the 741 entries.
17. Repeated-stem sets keep one stem: बोधि / जागृति for བྱང་ཆུབ in every compound (बोधिचित्त, बोधि-प्रणिधान-चित्त, बोधि-प्रस्थान-चित्त, बोधिचर्या), चित्त / मन for སེམས (and सत्त्व for སེམས་ཅན inside compounds), दुःख / दुख for སྡུག་བསྔལ, आत्म- for བདག་ in བདག་ཏུ་འཛིན་པ and བདག་ཏུ་ཞེན་པ, कुशल / अकुशल for དགེ / མི་དགེ, and पाप only for སྡིག་པ. Reason: these stems occur in many compounds and in the chapter titles.
18. Kinds of beings and the mind words stay apart: སེམས་ཅན is सत्त्व, འགྲོ་བ and འགྲོ are जगत् के प्राणी, ལུས་ཅན is देहधारी, སེམས་ཡོད is सचेतन प्राणी, སེམས་དམྱལ is नरक का प्राणी; སེམས is चित्त, ཡིད is मनस् (compound ཡིད་སྒོ is मनोद्वार), སེམས་པ is चेतना, ཤེས་པ is ज्ञान and རྣམ་ཤེས is विज्ञान (children's both जागरूकता), བློ is बुद्धि. Reason: the Hindi must keep Tibetan distinctions even where the English glossary uses one word ("mind", "being", "consciousness"), as the Ratnaguṇa glossary does.
19. Welfare words: རང་དོན, རང་གི་དོན and བདག་གི་དོན are स्वहित, གཞན་དོན and གཞན་གྱི་དོན are परहित, and དོན in the sense "welfare" is हित (children's भलाई), while the sense "object" of དོན is अर्थ, distinct from विषय for ཡུལ; स्वार्थ is not used. Reason: in Hindi स्वार्थ means selfishness and would reverse the Mahāyāna meaning, which Gemini's zero-shot does.
20. Doctrinal pairs and sets: ཀུན་རྫོབ is संवृति and དོན་དམ is परमार्थ (full forms संवृति-सत्य and परमार्थ-सत्य, children's रोज़ का सच and सबसे गहरा सच); སྟོང་ཉིད, སྟོང་པ་ཉིད and the noun སྟོང་པ are शून्यता (children's खालीपन), the adjective སྟོང་པ is शून्य (children's खाली); the three roots are आसक्ति (ཆགས་པ, ཆགས), द्वेष (ཞེ་སྡང) and मोह (རྨོངས་པ), with གཏི་མུག as अज्ञान; དལ་བ is अवकाश and དལ་འབྱོར is क्षण-सम्पदा; བདེ་འགྲོ is सुगति and ངན་འགྲོ and ངན་སོང are दुर्गति. Reason: these words come in fixed pairs or sets in the verses and must stay a set.
21. The opponents of chapter 9 are named in their own terms: དབང་ཕྱུག is ईश्वर (children's सृष्टि का रचयिता), གཙོ་བོ is प्रधान, བདག as the opponents' ātman is आत्मन्, the Sāṃkhya sattva, rajas and tamas (སྙིང་སྟོབས, རྡུལ, མུན་པ) are सत्त्व-गुण, रजस् and तमस् to keep सत्त्व-गुण apart from सत्त्व (being), and ཚད་མ is प्रमाण. Reason: the verses refute these positions by name, and the glossary must not translate them into Buddhist terms.
22. Head forms and verbs: nouns in the nominative singular without case endings; verbs of practice take the infinitive in ना (शरण में जाना, चित्त उत्पन्न करना, देशना करना), and the translation inflects them as the sentence needs. Reason: one lemma per term, and Hindi verb endings vary with person, number and gender.
23. Variant spellings, truncated forms and metrical short forms in the Tibetan (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔ, མྱ་ངན་འདས་པ, ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན, སྡིག, དམྱལ, བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས) take the rendering of the standard form, and a note records the variant; a mis-cut span marked in overrides.json is not rendered as a term. Reason: the same term must not receive two renderings because of spelling or metre.
