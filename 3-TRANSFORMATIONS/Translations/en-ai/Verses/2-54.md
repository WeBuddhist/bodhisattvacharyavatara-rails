## Combined Commentary Summaries

All three commentators unpack this verse through the traditional Buddhist medical analogy: just as one obeys a physician's instructions even for an ordinary illness caused by imbalance of wind, bile, and phlegm (traditionally enumerated as 404 kinds of disease), so much more should one follow the Buddha's instructions — the "physician's" instructions — for treating the far more dangerous and pervasive illness of the three poisons (desire, hatred, and delusion) and the "hundred faults" that have afflicted beings since beginningless time.

Sazang explicitly cites the classical Indian commentarial tradition (Prajñākaramati's Pañjikā) for the numerical parallel between the 404 physical illnesses and the 404 mental afflictions. Gyaltsab and Thokme both draw the same a fortiori conclusion: if diligence is called for with ordinary illness, then how much more so with the afflictions that are the "source of hundreds of thousands of faults".
