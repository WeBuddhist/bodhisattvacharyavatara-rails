# Requirements — Bodhicaryāvatāra, Thai, academic

## Target language
Thai, in Thai script. Modern standard written Thai; spaces only between phrases and sentences, as Thai is written.

## Audience and register
Students and practitioners reading a careful study translation; university readers of Buddhist texts (see `audience.md`). Faithful, precise, readable Thai; no archaism.

## Source and grounding
- Translate from the Tibetan. Use the aligned Sanskrit and the aligned commentary passages to settle the meaning of each verse; the checked English academic translation (../en-academic/) is given to confirm the meaning, not as the text to translate; the Thai machine draft may be consulted but is not an authority.
- Where the commentaries read a verse differently, follow the reading they share; if they truly split, follow the Tibetan's most literal sense.
- Translate the whole verse, in order: no omissions, no additions, no summary.
- Never add explanations, glosses or notes in the translation itself.

## Vocabulary
- Every term in `termbase.md` is rendered with its locked academic rendering, inflected for number and grammar as needed. Where a term has several senses, the sense assigned to that occurrence is used.
- Established Thai Buddhist vocabulary (Pāli and Sanskrit loans as Thai readers know them), as the termbase sets it.
- Proper names: the customary Thai Buddhist forms (ศานติเทวะ, มัญชุศรี, สมันตภัทร).

## Form and style
- A four-line Tibetan verse becomes four lines of Thai free verse (no rhyme, no metre), separated by line breaks, following the Tibetan line order wherever Thai syntax allows. Longer or shorter verses keep their own line count. Prose blocks (title, homage, colophon) stay prose.
- Standard modern Thai punctuation; each line must read naturally and the verse must parse as a whole.
- Keep Śāntideva's first-person voice ("I", "my mind") and his rhetorical questions as questions.
- In chapter 9, an opponent's objection stays recognisable as an objection; do not merge it into the reply.
- Headings: translate the Tibetan chapter title literally and concisely.
