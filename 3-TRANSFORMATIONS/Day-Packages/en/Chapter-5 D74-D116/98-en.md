## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the sixty-second, sixty-third, and sixty-fourth verses of the fifth chapter. If we cut open our own skin, flesh, and bones step by step with the sharp knife of wisdom, is there any hope of finding anything inside in the end?

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us the following key point, which is easy to understand and encouraging.

If we think this body has something clean and pleasing, we first separate the seven outer layers of skin with our own mind, one by one. We check whether anything clean, pleasing, or valuable is there. Next, we use the weapon of wisdom to set the flesh and sinews inside the skin to one side, separating them one by one, from among the three hundred and sixty pieces of bone. Again we check whether anything clean, pleasing, or valuable is there.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, I will examine what essence there is in my own body.

**Explanation:** *(Wisdom Practice)* If we know there is no essence inside the body, attachment grows less. This frees our own mind from confusion. These words teach us that we must examine the nature of the body again and again.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* teaches that, in order to examine whether this body, which we cherish, is clean and has an essence, we must use our own mind, as the blade of wisdom, to separate the layers of skin, the flesh, and the network of bones one from another and examine them.

**Key points.**

- To dispel the doubt, "Though it is unclean, there is an essence worth guarding," the skin and flesh are separated with the blade of the mind's wisdom and examined; it is taught that nothing is found there but what is unclean, foul-smelling, and fearsome — no essence at all.
- Even when the parts of the body are separated one by one and examined — "this is skin, this is flesh, this is bone" — it is taught that no essence of any kind is found.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that the seven layers of skin, the five hundred pieces of flesh, the sinews, and the network of the three hundred and sixty bone-joints should each be separated with the blade of wisdom and examined for whether anything clean and pleasing is present.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama teaches that, after the skin is separated, the flesh and fat beneath it, which cling to the network of bones, should be separated one by one with wisdom and examined.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* teaches that, having divided the bones of the body into joints and parts and examined them with wisdom all the way to the inner marrow, one must examine for oneself whether this body has any cleanliness or essence at all.

**Key points.**

- It is taught that when the bones are divided into parts and examined, not even a trace of an essence that withstands investigation, or one that could be trusted, is found.
- It is held that the joints and ribs of the bones should be separated, and that one must examine whether there is, anywhere outside or inside, an essence worthy of the praise of the wise.
- It is taught that with individually discerning wisdom one should look for an essence everywhere, inside and out, and examine whether anything clean and pleasing is present.
- It is held that, from the hair of the head to the tips of the toenails, and from the outer skin to the inner marrow, one should examine again and again whether a pleasing essence or cleanliness is present.
- It is taught that when the bones of the head, hands, and feet are examined substance by substance from the outer skin to the inner marrow, there is no essence worthy of being cherished and no great value — only a nature that is unclean and nauseating.

If one says, "What has been said in this way is well said" — then: even though you have striven in this way to search, if you see no essence at all in it, why, still, knowing that it has no good qualities, do you guard this very body full of pus, in a manner of attachment?

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Even though one sees no essence in this way, one still holds this body extremely dear and cherishes and tends it by way of [the words བདེ་སྡུག་གཏམ་ངན་ཁྱད་དུ་བསད་པ — unclear in the rail, left untranslated]; for the sake of this body, there are food and clothing, and these costly and of fine color; and on top of that, those who have ornaments hang various kinds on the ears and nose. All of that is, within confusion, a further delusion — such is the meaning.

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Having striven in this way and searched for whatever desirable, pleasing thing there is, if you, mind, see no essence or desirable, pleasing thing at all in that body — while not seeing it, why, still, in a manner of fondness and attachment to this body, do you, mind, guard this unclean, rotten, foul-smelling body? It is not fitting to do so.

##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Having striven in this way and searched for an essence, if you, mind, have not seen an essence in that body, why, still, in a manner of attachment and fondness, do you, mind, guard this body? It is not fitting to be especially fond of it.

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Having made great effort in this way and searched with wisdom, if you see no essence at all in that body, why, still, knowing that there are no good qualities, in a manner of attachment and fondness, do you guard and maintain this body? It is not fitting to do so.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Having striven in this way with great effort and searched minutely throughout, inside and outside, if you, the mind that searches, see not even the slightest essence to be found in that body which is the object of the search — then, not only did you formerly guard it, presuming through confusion that it had an essence; now, even though you are certain that there is no essence and understand this, why, still, for what reason, in a manner of attachment and fondness, do you, mind, guard this body just as before? "Say what the reason is for doing so!" — that is, because there is no reason to be stated.

*(Editorial note: Minyak Kunzang Sonam's transclusion tags for 5-62, 5-63, and 5-64 appear together with one shared prose block following; this portion, verified by content, belongs to 5-64 — see 5-62 for the earlier portions.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-64]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

As explained above, having striven with the mind to investigate in this way and searched again and again, if you, mind, see not even the slightest essence — a pleasing, valuable thing — in that body, why, still, in a manner of attachment and fondness, do you, mind, bound up with self-grasping (བདག་འཛིན), guard this very body? It is not fitting to do so; such is the meaning.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. At the End of the Investigation: Having Found No Essence, It Is Not Fitting to Go On Guarding the Body Out of Attachment

At the close of the investigation, this verse clearly sets down that, knowing no essence has been found in the body, it is not fitting to go on guarding the body in a manner of attachment.

###### 2. His Holiness the Dalai Lama Names Ear and Nose Ornaments as a Further Delusion

His Holiness the Dalai Lama separately sets down that such things as hanging ornaments on the ears and nose, as an example of intensely cherishing the body, are a further delusion within confusion.

###### 3. Minyak Kunzang Sonam: Formerly Confusion, Now Guarding the Body While Knowing There Is No Essence Is Even Less Fitting

Minyak Kunzang Sonam specifically shows that whereas formerly one guarded the body, presuming through confusion that it had an essence, now that one is certain there is no essence and knows it, guarding the body is all the more unfitting.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** If, having striven with the mind and with wisdom to search this body in detail, one sees not even a trace of an essence — of anything desirable or pleasing — then for what reason does one still guard this unclean body out of fondness and attachment? It is not fitting. Even though one is certain there is no essence and understands this, to cherish and guard this body dearly is, within confusion, a further delusion.

**Key points.**

- Though one searches with effort, one sees no essence at all, and — knowing that it has no good qualities — why still, in a manner of attachment, does one guard this very body full of pus?
- While seeing no desirable, pleasing thing at all, it is not fitting for the mind to guard this unclean, rotten, foul-smelling body in a manner of fondness and attachment.
- Even after searching with wisdom and great effort, if one sees no essence in the body, then, knowing there are no good qualities, it is not fitting to go on guarding and maintaining it with attachment and fondness.
- Having searched minutely, outside and inside, and being certain there is no essence and understanding this, to guard the body just as before out of fondness and attachment cannot be justified, because there is no reason that could be stated.
- Having examined and searched with the mind and seen nothing pleasing or valuable at all, it is not fitting for the mind bound up with self-grasping to guard this very body.
- While seeing no essence, to cherish and tend the body with food and clothing, costly things, and various kinds of ornaments hung upon it — all of that is, within confusion, a further delusion.
