## Per-Commentary Summaries

### [[en-AI-generated-commentary-gyaltsab(old)|Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen]] Summary
Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen explains the expression of humility in three parts: expressing humility by not primarily making it for the sake of others, the reasons for rejoicing in composing the treatise, and showing how it will also benefit others of equal fortune.
- **Humility:** Śāntideva states that he did not compose the treatise for others who can already easily understand the scriptures on their own. He asserts that there is no subject matter in this text that has not already appeared in previous scriptures, and that he lacks skill in poetic composition.
- **Self-benefit / Purpose:** The primary purpose of writing is to familiarize his own mind with what has already been understood, preventing decline and promoting increase. By setting down these thoughts, he can continuously meditate on vast virtues, which temporarily increases and develops his faith, wisdom, and compassion.

### [[en-AI-generated-commentary-sazang(old)|Sazang Mati Panchen]] Summary
Sazang Mati Panchen Lodro Gyaltsen describes Śāntideva's purpose in composing the text in terms of humility and self-benefit.
- **Humility:** Śāntideva abandons pride regarding words and meaning by asserting that there is nothing new in this text that does not already exist in the pure Mahayana scriptures, and that he lacks scholarship in poetic composition. Thus, he did not write it to please others or make them engage in it.
- **Self-benefit / Purpose:** The primary focus is to cultivate and practice the aspiring mind of bodhicitta and the six perfections in his own mind. Setting these realizations down in writing helps temporarily increase and develop his conviction/faith (conviction in karma, clarity toward qualities, and aspiration toward ability) and other virtues (diligence, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom).

### [[en-AI-generated-commentary-thokme|Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo]] Summary
Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo explains the expressing of humility and self-benefit through the following points:
- **Humility:** Śāntideva states that there is no new meaning in this text that has not appeared in previous scriptures, and he lacks skill in poetic composition. Therefore, he did not compose it for the sake of others.
- **Self-benefit / Purpose:** The treatise was written solely to familiarize his own mind with bodhicitta. By setting down his exceptional realizations, his conviction and faith will temporarily increase and develop further, leading to the gradual increase of diligence, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom. Stating his humility also shows that he is free from pride, and he asks for forgiveness for any faults.

## Combined Commentary Synthesis

### Consensus
All three commentators agree that Verse 1-2 represents Śāntideva's expression of humility and statement of the primary purpose of his writing, which is self-benefit (mind-cultivation/familiarization) rather than altruism or scholastic pride.
- **Humility:** They agree that Śāntideva rejects any claim to originality (there is nothing new that has not been said in the scriptures) or literary/poetic skill (composition, wordplay). This humility serves to abandon pride and pre-emptively seek forgiveness for any faults in the text.
- **Self-familiarization:** They agree that the main goal of composing the text is for Śāntideva to habituate or familiarize his own mind with the virtues of *bodhicitta* and the perfections, preventing decline and fostering the growth of faith, conviction, wisdom, and other spiritual qualities in his own continuum.

### Divergences and Unique Insights
- **Gyaltsab** provides a highly structured breakdown, focusing on how writing down realizations allows one to continuously meditate on vast virtues and develop faith, wisdom, and compassion.
- **Sazang** explicitly categorizes the three types of faith that are increased by writing: conviction in karma and its fruit, clarity toward those with qualities, and aspiration toward spiritual ability.
- **Thokme** notes that expressing humility serves a psychological and social purpose: it demonstrates that the author is entirely free from pride, making readers more sympathetic and willing to forgive any structural or linguistic faults in the treatise.
