# Bodhicaryāvatāra, Chapter 1 — Verses 1–5 (Plain English)

## ^1-1

I bow with respect to the Buddhas — who have realized the truth-body — and to their spiritual heirs, and to everyone else worthy of honor. Now, following the scriptures, I will briefly explain how to enter the way of life that the Buddhas' heirs, the bodhisattvas, practice.

## ^1-2

There's nothing new here that hasn't been said before, and I have no particular skill as a writer. So I'm not writing this because I think it will help other people — I'm writing it simply to train my own mind.

## ^1-3

Still, writing it will strengthen my faith for a while and help me get used to living virtuously. And if someone else, whose situation is similar to mine, happens to read these words, it may be worthwhile for them too.

## ^1-4

This life — free and well-supported enough to actually practice — is extremely hard to come by. Now that I have it, if I don't use it to do something meaningful, how could I ever expect to have a chance like this again?

## ^1-5

Just as a flash of lightning briefly lights up everything on a pitch-black, moonless, starless night, in the same way, through the power of the Buddhas, a moment of clarity and merit rarely — but occasionally — arises in the minds of ordinary beings in this world.

## 3. Notes on the one flagged item

- **1-3, Style/Audience, Minor:** the commentary specifies the "virtue" being cultivated is bodhicitta (awakening mind) specifically, not virtue in general. The plain English says "living virtuously," which is accurate to the root verse's own wording (དགེ་བ་) but loses the commentary's added specificity. This is a deliberate audience-level compression, consistent with a "plain English for ordinary people" brief, and is logged as Neutral-leaning-Minor rather than penalized as a mistranslation — no doctrinal content is wrong, only under-specified.
