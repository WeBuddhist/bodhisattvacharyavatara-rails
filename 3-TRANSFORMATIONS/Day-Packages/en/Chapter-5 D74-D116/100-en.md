## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the sixty-eighth and sixty-ninth verses of the fifth chapter. Does anyone pay wages to a hired worker who never does any work? Or is our own body also like a servant?

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us the following explanation, which is easy to understand and encouraging. The meaning of "let it do its own work" is this. For a bodhisattva, accomplishing the benefit of beings is itself one's own benefit. Apart from that, there is no other benefit of one's own at all. The *Ornament of the Sutras* says, "Others' benefit is one's own benefit." So this body must be put to work doing good for the benefit of others. For example, we pay a servant only the wages for the work done. In the same way, it is fine to give the body only what it needs, such as food and clothes. It is not right to give it everything it wants.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, I will give my body only enough food and clothes. Then I will work for the benefit of others.

**Explanation:** *(Doing good)* This is the practice of giving the body enough food and clothes, and then working for the benefit of others. If we keep the body's wishes in check, the mind becomes peaceful. If we help others, both we and others find happiness.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Even to a servant who cannot be employed, / if one does not give clothes and so on, / if this body, though pampered, goes elsewhere — / you, why do you nourish it at such cost? — this says: for example, in the world, among servants to whom one gives food and wages and whom one keeps, if there is one who cannot be urged on to work and put to work, then giving that one clothing and so on is unneeded or, as one recognizes, without benefit. In the same way, however much one has pampered and nurtured this body from birth until now, in the end it is of a nature that one must leave it behind and go. Therefore, "you, why should you exert yourself to nourish it at such cost, with great weariness?" It is not right.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-68]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Even to a servant who cannot be put to work — who does not do the work well — if the master or lord does not give food, clothing, and so on, then, Mind, though you pamper and nurture this body with food, clothing, and so on, you and the body will in the end part and each go elsewhere, so that it is of no benefit to you. Mind, why should you nourish it at such cost — that is, with great hardship — by feeding and clothing it? It is not right.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-68]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

For example, in the world, if a master punishes a servant who cannot be made to do the work of the household by giving him no food, clothing, and so on, then — this body, though you exert yourself to nurture and pamper it, is not under your control and goes elsewhere. You, yes you — why do you nourish it at such cost? That is not right.

##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

For example, if one does not give clothing, food, and so on even to servants and the like who cannot be employed in the lord's household, then in the same way, though one pampers this body with food, drink, and so on and nurtures it, in the end it will inevitably be separated and go elsewhere, and so it is of no benefit to oneself. Mind, why do you, by seeking provisions, nourish it at such cost, wearing yourself out? It is not right to exert yourself to guard and nurture it.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-68]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

If one thinks, "Even so, what is unreasonable about nurturing it with mere food and clothing?" — it is unreasonable. How so? In the world, to servants who cannot be put to any work of the lord, or who do no work at all, why would one give clothing, food, and so on? That is, it would not be right to give them anything at all, and not only that, it would be fitting even to punish them. In just the same way, though one pampers this body, giving whatever food, drink, and so on is wanted, extolling it, dressing it in whatever clothing is wanted, and nurtures it with effort, in the end, Mind, it is not under your control; it will inevitably separate and, on dying, go elsewhere. You — you, who have sought provisions with great hardship — why do you nourish it at such cost? To nurture it in that way is utterly unreasonable.

*(Editorial note: Minyak Kunzang Sonam adds here a brief textual note: this verse's third line reads variously "མི་བཏུབ་པའང" or "མི་བཏུབ་ལའང" across editions, glossing that "འང" extends the point a fortiori — if a servant who does no work at all still is not given food or clothing, how much more so a body that does no virtuous work.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-68]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Even to a servant who cannot be employed, / if one does not give clothes and so on, / if this body, though pampered, goes elsewhere — / you, why do you nourish it at such cost? — this means: in the world, even to a servant — one who cannot be put to work as a worker, or who has not done the work well — if the patron does not give clothing and so on, that is, food, wages, and so on, as is due; then this body: though in a single lifetime the mind covers it with food and clothing, nurtures it, and pampers it, in the end, at the moment of death, it separates from the mind, scatters apart, and becomes food for birds, dogs, and the like. So now you, mind of self-grasping — you, why do you nourish this body at such cost? "At such cost" means: until the flesh is worn thin and the blood dried up, by way of toil and weariness — it is not right to nourish it that much. Such is the meaning.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Servant Analogy: A Body That Will Leave Cannot Rightly Be Nursed

Through the example of not paying wages to a servant who does no work, this verse shows that, however much one nurtures this body, it will in the end separate from the mind and go elsewhere, and so it is not right to nourish it at such cost.

###### 2. Minyak Kunzang Sonam: Not Only Unpaid but Even Punished

Minyak Kunzang Sonam, drawing out by implication that a servant who does no work is not only left unpaid but may even be punished, extends the example and its meaning together, and sets down the conclusion that it is not right to exert oneself in nurturing the body.

###### 3. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and His Holiness: However Long It Is Nurtured, One Must Leave It Behind

Both Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and His Holiness the Dalai Lama set down the conclusion that, since however much one nurtures the body from birth until now one must in the end leave it behind and go, it is not right to nourish it at such great cost.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Just as, in the world, one does not give food and clothing to a servant who cannot be put to work, so however much one pampers and nurtures one's own body with food, drink, clothing, and so on, it will in the end inevitably separate and go elsewhere, and so it is of no benefit to oneself. It is therefore taught that it is most unreasonable for the mind of self-grasping to seek provisions with great hardship and weariness and to nurture this body at such cost.

**Key points.**

- Just as one does not give clothing and food to servants who cannot be put to work, so, since this body, though pampered and nurtured, inevitably goes elsewhere, it is not right to nourish it at such cost.
- Just as one does not give food and clothing to a servant who does not do the work well, so, since mind and body in the end part and each go elsewhere, and this body is of no benefit to oneself, it is not right to nurture it at great hardship.
- Just as in the world one punishes by withholding food and clothing a servant who cannot be put to the household's work, so, if this body, though one exerts oneself to nurture it, is not under one's control and goes elsewhere, it is not right to nourish it at such cost.
- Just as one does not give clothing and so on to a servant who cannot be put to work, so, if this body, though pampered and nurtured, will surely go elsewhere, why nourish it at such cost?
- Just as one does not give food and clothing to a servant who cannot be employed in the lord's household, so, though one pampers this body with food and drink, in the end it will inevitably be separated and go elsewhere, and so it is not right to exert oneself, by seeking provisions, to nourish it at such cost, wearing oneself out.
- Just as in the world one does not give food and clothing to a servant who does none of the lord's work, and even punishes him, so, though one nurtures this body with effort, in the end it is not under the mind's control, will inevitably separate, and goes elsewhere after death; it is utterly unreasonable to seek provisions with great hardship and nurture it at such cost.
- Just as one does not give food and wages to a servant who has not done the work well, so, though this body is nourished with food and clothing for a lifetime, at the time of death it scatters apart from the mind and becomes food for birds, dogs, and the like; therefore the mind of self-grasping should not nourish it through toil and weariness until the flesh is worn thin and the blood dried up.
- Just as one recognizes that giving clothing and so on to a servant who cannot be put to work is without benefit, so, since however much this body is nurtured it is in the end of a nature that one must leave it behind and go, it is not right to exert oneself to nourish it with great weariness.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Having paid the body its wages, / let it now work for its own purpose; / without benefit, to this / one should not give everything — this says: giving this body something like wages, let it accomplish its own purpose, that is, actions that benefit others. If that happens, not only does it benefit oneself, it will also benefit future lives. Without accomplishing a purpose that benefits — that is, one that has essence — one should not, just as one pleases, give this body all favorable conditions.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-69]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Therefore, as before, without clinging to the body — just as one entrusts work after paying wages — one gives this body, as the wages and pay for well accomplishing the sacred Dharma, a livelihood of food, clothing, and so on: just enough to fill the belly and protect, without falling into the two extremes. Having given that, now let it do one's own purpose, that is, hearing, reflecting, and meditating on the sacred Dharma and so on. Without the purpose of accomplishing sacred Dharma that benefits future lives, one should not give this body all the food, clothing, and so on that one has — that is, one should not devote oneself to this body's belly and back alone.

Having given this body, as wages, the conditions for living — food, clothing, and so on — now let it do one's own purpose, virtuous action. Without even a little benefit, one should not give this body all the food, clothing, and so on.

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Therefore, just as one entrusts work after paying wages, having given this body, as mere wages for entrusting virtuous work to it, a livelihood of food, clothing, and so on, now let it do the purpose that benefits one's own mind. Without doing anything that lastingly benefits oneself, one should not give this body all the food, clothing, and so on, nor guard it.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

"Because of the reason it is not right to nurture it if it does not do the needed work": therefore, just as one pays a servant wages and sets him to work, so to this essenceless body, having given merely wages — blameless food, clothing, and so on, the conditions of livelihood, whether as the price of upkeep or as its sustaining "medicine" (རྐྱེན་རྩི་, རྐྱེན་སྨན) — now, Mind, you quickly do one's own purpose, that is, the purpose of others: virtuous action of merit. Without even the slightest benefit to oneself or others, whether immediate or lasting, one should not give this body all the food, drink, and other needs — just as with servants and the like, it is fitting to give only wages measured by how much work they have done, and not right to give them everything they have earned and everything they need.

"Let it do its own purpose" — as the *Ornament of the Sutras* says, "Others' benefit is one's own benefit" — means that for a bodhisattva, accomplishing the benefit of beings is itself his own benefit, and apart from that there is no other benefit of his own whatsoever; therefore it is said in this way.

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Having paid the body its wages, / let it now work for its own purpose; / without benefit, to this / one should not give everything — this means: "Then how should one proceed?" For this body of one's own, now, give merely wages — the favorable condition for accomplishing the sacred Dharma, that is, food and clothing; not so extremely meagre and wretched that it cannot sustain the body, nor so very much more than is needed, extravagantly plentiful, nor like spending whatever wealth one has for the body's sake. For the time being, having given merely wages, now, Mind, let it properly do one's own purpose — hearing, reflecting, meditation, and practice of the sacred Dharma and so on. At all times and in every way, since this body is of no benefit, temporary or ultimate, to oneself and others, one should not give to this body all wealth, goods, and possessions — one should not give it all of that. Rather, what must be done above all is to devote them to others' purposes: above, making offerings to the Three Jewels; below, giving to beggars and the like. Such is the meaning.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Pay the Body Mere Wages and Put It to Virtuous Work

This verse sets down that, having given the body only the wages of food and clothing, one should put it to work for virtue, and teaches that one should not give it all the goods that bring it no benefit.

###### 2. Minyak Kunzang Sonam: "Others' Benefit Is One's Own Benefit"

Minyak Kunzang Sonam, citing the *Ornament of the Sutras* — "Others' benefit is one's own benefit" — sets down the conclusion that a bodhisattva's own benefit is the very accomplishing of beings' benefit.

###### 3. The Right Measure of Wages, and Benefit in This Life and the Next

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk sets out in detail that the body should be given wages of a proper measure, neither too meagre nor too much; His Holiness the Dalai Lama teaches that if one does so, it benefits not only this life but future lives as well.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Having given this body, like wages, only the livelihood-conditions of food and clothing, one must put the mind to its own purpose — the sacred Dharma and virtuous action that benefits others. Without benefit to oneself or others, and without essence, one should not give this body all possessions and favorable conditions merely for the sake of its belly and back.

**Key points.**

- Having given this body only the wages of food and clothing, one must now put the mind to doing its own purpose, the practice of Dharma; without benefit, one should not give this body all material things.
- Without clinging to the body, as one entrusts work after paying wages, one gives food and clothing — just enough to fill the belly and protect, without falling into the two extremes — and must put the mind to hearing, reflecting, and meditating on the sacred Dharma; without the purpose of accomplishing Dharma that benefits future lives, one should not devote oneself to this body's belly and back alone.
- Having given this body, as wages, the conditions of livelihood — food, clothing, and so on — one must now put it to doing its own purpose, virtuous action; without even a little benefit, one should not give this body all food, clothing, and so on.
- Having given this, as mere wages, blameless food, clothing, and so on, one must put the bodhisattva to his own purpose, that is, the benefit of others; without a purpose that benefits others, one should not give this all of it.
- As one entrusts work after paying wages, having given food, clothing, and so on as mere wages for entrusting it with virtuous action, one must put it to doing what benefits its own mind; without doing anything that lastingly benefits oneself, one should not give this body all food, clothing, and so on, nor guard it.
- As one pays a servant wages and puts him to work, having given this essenceless body, as mere wages, blameless food, clothing, and other conditions of livelihood, one must quickly put it to its own purpose — virtuous action of merit for the sake of others; without even the slightest benefit, immediate or lasting, to oneself or others, one should not give it all the food, drink, and other needs.
- Having given this body, as mere wages, the favorable conditions of food and clothing for accomplishing the sacred Dharma, one must put the mind to doing its own purpose — hearing, reflecting, meditating, and practicing the sacred Dharma and so on; without benefit to oneself or others, one should not give this body all wealth and goods.
- Having given this body something like wages, one must put it to accomplishing its own purpose, actions that benefit others; without accomplishing a purpose that has essence, one should not give this body all favorable conditions just as one pleases.
