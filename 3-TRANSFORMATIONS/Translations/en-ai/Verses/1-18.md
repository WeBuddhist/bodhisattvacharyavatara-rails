## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three commentators maintain an absolute consensus on the meaning and doctrinal implications of Verse 1-18, which marks the exact moment the practitioner transitions from the aspiring mind to the engaging mind of enlightenment (*bodhi-prasthāna-citta*), triggering a vast and unceasing stream of merit.

### 1. The Continuous Flow of Merit
The commentators agree that once the engaging mind is generated, a vast, inexhaustible force of merit arises and flows continuously:
*   **The Moment of Generation:** Both **Sazang Mati Panchen** and **Gyaltsab Je** emphasize that the stream of merit begins the exact moment one correctly takes up the engaging mind of enlightenment through the proper ritual or vows.
*   **Irreversible Resolve:** This mind is defined by an irreversible resolve to completely liberate the infinite, boundless realms of sentient beings from the prison of cyclic existence. Sazang describes this prison as characterized by the three afflictions (desire, anger, and delusion), while **Ngulchu Thokme** describes it as the "noose of karma and afflictions."
*   **Active in Careless States:** A defining characteristic of this merit is that it continues to arise uninterruptedly even when the practitioner is asleep, distracted, unmindful, faints, or is intoxicated. Sazang adds that this flow occurs regardless of physical posture, continuing whether one is walking, standing, or sitting.
*   **Vastness:** This unceasing force of merit is described by all commentators as being equal to the expanse of space (`nabhaḥsamāḥ`).

### 2. The Condition of Non-Degeneration
*   **Ngulchu Thokme's Caveat:** Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo introduces a crucial caveat to this continuous flow of merit: it arises from that moment onward *if the mind of enlightenment does not degenerate* (`དེ་མ་ཉམས་ན་`). This highlights the absolute necessity of maintaining the vows and actively training in the Bodhisattva precepts after formal adoption.

### 3. The Functional Distinction: Cause vs. Fruit
The commentators explain the underlying mechanism that allows the engaging mind to produce such a continuous stream of merit:
*   **Seeking the Fruit vs. Accomplishing the Causes:** **Gyaltsab Je** explains that the aspiring mind is merely an attitude that *seeks the fruit* (Buddhahood), whereas the engaging mind is actively *conjoined with accomplishing the complete, actual causes* of Buddhahood (the practice of the perfections). Thus, only the engaging mind can sustain an uninterrupted flow of merit.
*   **Conjoining with Moral Discipline:** Sazang Mati Panchen specifies that this continuous force of merit arises specifically from conjoining the mind with the *three kinds of moral discipline* (restraining non-virtue, gathering virtue, and benefiting sentient beings).
