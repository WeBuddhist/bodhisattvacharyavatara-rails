# Day 1 — Shantideva wrote this text for himself alone

## Opening

Chapter 1 makes the case for why bodhicitta — the wish to awaken for the benefit of all beings — is worth everything. But before that, Shantideva begins quietly. He bows, admits he has nothing original to offer, and says he wrote this book only to train his own mind. That honesty is where today's reading starts.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

*Mind Training (recite 3 times)*

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in immeasurable equanimity, free from attachment and aversion.

*Refuge (recite 3 times)*

> Until I reach the heart of awakening, I take refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.

*Taking the Bodhisattva Vow (recite 3 times)*

> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And trained step by step in the bodhisattva conduct,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And train step by step in that same conduct.

## From the Tradition

When Shantideva says writing this will increase "the strength of his faith," Sazang Mati Panchen points out that faith here isn't one thing. He names three kinds that will grow: trust that your actions actually have consequences; genuine admiration for people who embody good qualities; and confidence that you yourself are capable of practice. What's striking is the chain Sazang traces from there. As these three strengthen, they pull other qualities along with them — diligence, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom — one leading to the next. In this reading, Shantideva isn't just writing a book. He's setting up a practice.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious and supreme bodhicitta —
> Where it has not yet arisen, may it arise;
> Where it has arisen, may it not decline,
> But grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of this practice,
> May all wandering beings, without exception,
> Enter the path of the bodhisattva.

## Today's Practice Challenge

Sazang says the first kind of faith that this practice builds is simply trusting that what you do has consequences. Most of us believe this in theory. The next time you are about to snap at someone you live with, or let a small lie slide because it is easier, ask yourself whether you actually believe it in that moment. That question is the practice.
