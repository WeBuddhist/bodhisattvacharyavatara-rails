# Day 1 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: The Benefits of the Mind of Enlightenment — Verses 1-1 to 1-3

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Releasing Pride (Humility as a Foundation):** The great teacher Śāntideva's act of denying any originality or literary skill shows how letting go of intellectual pride clears the mind for genuine learning. When pride is set aside, the mind becomes open and receptive, making real spiritual growth possible. This quality of humility protects against the subtle arrogance that can quietly block one's progress on the path.

*   **Strengthening Personal Faith (Self-Cultivation):** Writing down or expressing virtuous intentions — as the great teacher Śāntideva did — temporarily strengthens the three aspects of faith: conviction, admiration, and aspiration. This process helps stabilize one's spiritual motivation and prevents it from fading under the pressure of daily life. Even a small, sincere act of recording one's commitments can cause faith to grow steadily over time.

*   **Inspiring Fellow Practitioners (Shared Benefit):** When a practitioner genuinely trains their own mind without pretension or pride, others who share the same sincere aspiration naturally benefit from that honest example. Authentic, humble practice creates a quiet ripple effect, encouraging those with similar good fortune to engage more deeply with the path. One person's genuine effort thus becomes a source of support for the whole community of practitioners.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-1**

> བདེ་གཤེགས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་མངའ་སྲས་བཅས་དང་། །
> ཕྱག་འོས་ཀུན་ལའང་གུས་པར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏེ། །
> བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་ནི། །
> ལུང་བཞིན་མདོར་བསྡུས་ནས་ནི་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ། །
>
> *I pay respectful homage to the Blissful Ones who possess the Truth Body, and to their heirs. I also bow to everyone who is worthy of honor. I shall now briefly describe how to enter the commitments of the Buddha's heirs, in accordance with the scriptures.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-1**

The great teacher Śāntideva opens by bowing with deep respect to the enlightened Buddhas, their spiritual heirs, and all others who are worthy of honor. This act of homage is not merely ceremonial — it clears mental obstacles, gathers positive energy, and connects the author to an authentic lineage of wisdom. Even a small act of sincere respect directed toward such holy objects plants the seed of liberation in the heart of the practitioner.

---

#### **Verse 1-2**

> སྔོན་ཆད་མ་བྱུང་བ་ཡང་འདིར་བརྗོད་མེད། །
> སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་མཁས་པའང་བདག་ལ་ཡོད་མིན་ཏེ། །
> དེ་ཕྱིར་གཞན་དོན་བསམ་པ་བདག་ལ་མེད། །
> རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་ངས་འདི་བརྩམས། །
>
> *I have nothing new to say here, and I do not possess any skill in writing poetry. Therefore, I have no expectation of benefiting others. I wrote this solely to train and cultivate my own mind.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-2**

The great teacher Śāntideva expresses deep humility by denying any claim to originality or poetic genius. This modest attitude helps him overcome pride and intellectual arrogance before his peers. He writes this text primarily to train his own mind and prevent his spiritual growth from declining. Setting these realizations down in writing acts as a powerful support, causing his faith, wisdom, and compassion to continuously increase.

---

#### **Verse 1-3**

> དགེ་བ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་བདག་གི་དད་པའི་ཤུགས། །
> འདི་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་རེ་ཞིག་འཕེལ་འགྱུར་ལ། །
> བདག་དང་སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཀྱང་། །
> ཅི་སྟེ་འདི་དག་མཐོང་ན་དོན་ཡོད་འགྱུར། །
>
> *By writing this, the strength of my faith to cultivate virtue will temporarily increase. If anyone of equal fortune should see these words, perhaps they will also find them useful.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-3**

The great teacher Śāntideva recognizes that the very act of writing down his reflections will cause his own faith and spiritual qualities to grow stronger over time. Three types of faith are nourished by this process: conviction that good actions lead to good results, heartfelt admiration for those who embody noble qualities, and a personal aspiration to practice. While self-cultivation remains the immediate goal, those who share the same sincere aspiration and good fortune may also find genuine benefit in these pages.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Practicing Humility Before Study:** Today, I will begin any reading or learning by setting aside pride about what I already know. Before opening a book or entering a conversation, I will remind myself that my true goal is to cultivate my own mind, not to impress others with my knowledge.

*   **2. Writing Down a Virtue:** Following the example of the great teacher Śāntideva, I will take a few minutes today to write down one quality or aspiration I want to strengthen in my own mind. By putting my intention into writing, I make it more concrete and help my faith grow a little stronger each day.

*   **3. Recognizing Fellow Practitioners:** Today, I will notice at least one person who shares a sincere wish to live more kindly or wisely. I will hold the quiet thought that my own honest effort to improve myself may gently encourage them, even without a word being spoken.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **The Blissful One (བདེ་གཤེགས):** This name refers to the fully enlightened Buddhas who have achieved both perfect abandonment of all faults and perfect realization of all knowables. The name points to their having traveled completely beyond suffering to a state of stainless peace and wisdom, never to return to confusion. Bowing to the Blissful Ones at the opening of this text acknowledges them as the supreme objects of respect and the source of the entire teaching.

*   **Truth Body (ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ):** The Truth Body is the deepest nature of an enlightened being — the completely purified sphere of reality, free from all obscurations and stains. It is not a physical form visible to ordinary eyes but the living wisdom of full awakening itself. Paying homage to the Blissful Ones "who possess the Truth Body" acknowledges that their true greatness rests in this ultimate, stainless realization.

*   **Commitments (སྡོམ):** This term refers to the training guidelines that govern the life of a Hero of Enlightenment on the path. Entering these commitments means making a sincere and sustained pledge to live in a way that benefits all beings, guided by the teachings of the Buddhas. The entire text of the Bodhisattvacharyavatara is a detailed guide on how to take up and sustain this way of life.

*   **Cultivate / Familiarize (བསྒོམ):** To cultivate here means to bring the mind repeatedly into contact with virtuous qualities until they become natural, stable, and effortless. This is not a matter of thinking about something once — it is a process of deep familiarization, like carving a smooth groove through repeated practice. The great teacher Śāntideva wrote this text as his own personal tool for exactly this kind of sustained inner cultivation.

*   **Faith (དད་པ):** In this context, faith does not mean blind belief but a clear, grounded confidence in the value of the path. Three aspects are highlighted: conviction that virtuous actions lead to genuine happiness, admiration for those who embody noble qualities, and a personal aspiration to practice oneself. This kind of faith grows stronger when it is expressed and recorded in writing, rather than left vague and unexamined.

*   **Equal Fortune (སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ):** Those of "equal fortune" are fellow practitioners who share the same aspiration, the same spiritual family, and the same sincere wish to follow the path of a Hero of Enlightenment. The great teacher Śāntideva acknowledges that while his immediate purpose is to cultivate his own mind, others with similar good fortune may also find genuine and lasting benefit in these pages.
