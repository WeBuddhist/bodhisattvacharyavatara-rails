3. Second, the explanation of the title: To attain perfect enlightenment in which all abandonment and realization are completed, this text teaches how to engage in its methods. ## 1. Chapter One: Explaining the Benefits of the Mind of Enlightenment (ལེའུ་དང་པོ། བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ཀྱི་ཕན་ཡོན་བཤད་པ།)

### 1.1 Regarding the first of these three parts, first, holding it firmly because it eliminates great negativity: Leisure and opportunity are difficult to find, and the mind of Dharma is also rare;

91. Even if it is born, it does not remain for long. Therefore: ^1-1-1

92. Virtue that leads to merit is like a flash of lightning, arising only occasionally, and thus its strength is extremely weak. Negativity, on the other hand, is like the thick darkness of a cloudy night, possessing great power to produce the fierce and extremely unendurable sufferings of hell and so forth. What other ordinary virtue, which is not the perfect mind of enlightenment endowed with the essence of emptiness and compassion, can overcome and outshine such negativity? None can. ^1-1-2

93. However, the mind of enlightenment does overcome it, so it is proper to hold it firmly. ^1-1-3

94. As the *Teaching of the Four Dharmas* states: "The power of the support is taking refuge in the Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha, and not discarding the mind of enlightenment. ^1-1-4

95. By relying on these powerful ones, all negativities are overcome." This is how it is. Second, holding it firmly because it accomplishes benefit and happiness: The Lords of Sages, the perfect Buddhas, who contemplated only the methods to benefit sentient beings for many countless ages (such as three countless eons), saw that this very mind of enlightenment accomplishes all purposes of benefit and happiness. For this reason, it enables infinite, countless assemblies of beings to easily obtain the supreme happiness of higher rebirth and definite goodness without depending on fierce hardships or arduous, mediocre efforts. Third, holding it firmly because it accomplishes one's aspirations: Anyone possessing intelligence who wishes to destroy hundreds of sufferings of cyclic existence, such as those of the hell realms, and engages in their remedies; who wishes to clear all the miseries of sentient beings, such as birth, old age, sickness, and death; and who wishes to enjoy hundreds of temporary and ultimate happinesses for themselves and all others—since this very mind of enlightenment is the method to definitely accomplish these, they should never discard it at any time, but must hold it firmly. ^1-1-24

96. Those who wish to destroy the hundreds of sufferings of cyclic existence, who wish to clear all the miseries of every single sentient being, and who wish to enjoy hundreds of temporary and ultimate happinesses for themselves and all others, should never discard this very mind of enlightenment at any time. ^1-1-6

Second, how one becomes an heir of the Victorious Ones: If the exceptional thought of the mind of enlightenment is generated in anyone, in that very instant of its generation, even those sentient beings who were previously tormented by various sufferings, bound by the fetters of karma and mental afflictions in the prison of cyclic existence, undergo a change of name and are called "Heroes of Enlightenment," the heirs of the Blissful Ones. 127. he taught that it is endowed with these qualities and other limitless distinctions of qualities. For this reason too, it is said: "Why do conscientious beings not rely on it?" Second, the distinctions: of the three parts, first, the division into aspiring and engaging: Although the basis of division—the mind of enlightenment which possesses the benefits explained before—can be divided into many categories by way of family, stages, analogies, companions, and so forth, if condensed, the number of divisions should be understood as twofold: ^1-1-37

128. The aspiring mind of enlightenment, whose essence is the desire to attain great awakening, and the engaging mind of enlightenment, which actually engages in the conduct of awakening motivated by that aspiration. ^1-1-38

129. As the *Sūtras* state: "Those sentient beings who aspire in their minds for unexcelled, perfectly complete awakening are rare. ^1-1-39

130. Even more so, those sentient beings who engage in unexcelled, perfectly complete awakening are extremely rare." ^1-1-40

131. This is how it is. Second, applying these to analogies: Just as one understands the distinction of thought and action between a person who desires and seeks to travel to a desired destination and one who has entered the road and is actually traveling, in the same way, the wise who unerringly realize the way things are should understand the distinction between these two—the aspiring mind of enlightenment, which is the desire to travel to great awakening, and the engaging mind of enlightenment, which is entering the path of the six perfections and actually traveling. ^1-1-41

132. Third, how the engaging mind is exceptionally superior: Although generating merely the aspiring mind toward awakening, even without the actual practice, brings great fruits such as obtaining the perfect splendor of gods and humans while wandering in the cycle of life, it does not produce a continuous, uninterrupted stream of merit or virtue in the way that the engaging mind does, which is associated with the diligent practice of generosity and so forth. ^1-1-42

133. From the moment the engaging mind of enlightenment is generated—when one correctly takes up the engaging mind of enlightenment through the proper ritual with an irreversible resolve for the sake of others, intending to completely liberate the infinite, boundless realms of sentient beings from the cycle of existence characterized by the three afflictions—then, from that time onward, even if one falls asleep, has the mind distracted, is unmindful, faints, becomes intoxicated, walks, stands, or sits, a vast, inexhaustible, and continuous force of merit arising from the three disciplines (moral discipline) constantly arises, equal to space. Because this is so. Second, the proof: ^1-1-43

137. This continuous arising of merit from the engaging mind of enlightenment is established with valid reasons in the *Subāhuparipṛcchā Sūtra* (Sūtra of the Inquiry of Subāhu): ^1-2-1

138. "If one dons the armor for the sake of the benefit and happiness of the infinite realms of sentient beings, ^1-2-2

139. their boundless virtuous roots—even if they become unmindful, have their minds distracted, or fall asleep—will grow, increase, and become fully perfected in every single instant of the mind, day and night; ^1-2-3

140. by generating the boundless accumulations of awakening, they will not find it difficult to attain perfect awakening." This is how it is. Second, the logical proof: ^1-2-4

#### How immeasurable merit is obtained, and how it is supreme among all.

### 1.3 Of the four parts of the first, first, how it is obtained through immeasurable objects and aspects; this is shown along with an analogy:

143. Formerly, the daughter of a merchant leader named Mdz'a-bo'i Bu-mo kicked her mother's head and went to fetch jewels. When that action ripened as a visible result in the temporary hell realm, she experienced the fierce suffering of a burning iron wheel revolving on her head. At that time, she thought: "May the suffering of all sentient beings who would experience such pain ripen upon me!" By that thought, she died and was reborn as a god. In the same way, if even the thought of wanting to clear just a headache of sentient beings—who are the objects of focus—is endowed with immeasurable and infinite merit, such as rebirth in the Tuṣita heaven, ^1-2-5

144. what need is there to mention the infinite merit of the mind of enlightenment that wishes to clear the boundless sufferings and unhappiness of every single countless sentient being equal to space, and wishes to establish each and every one of them in immeasurable temporary and ultimate qualities? ^1-2-6

145. Second, how it is obtained due to being the supreme beneficial thought: Does any ordinary person—whether a father, a mother, a sibling, or a close friend who wishes to benefit others—possess such a beneficial thought of wanting to establish all sentient beings in great awakening? None do. Do even the world-guarding gods, the truth-speaking sages, or Brahma who has trained in the four immeasurables and wishes to be the grandfather of all, possess such a beneficial thought? None do. ^1-2-7

146. The reason is that such ordinary sentient beings have never before generated or even dreamed of such an exceptional mind even for their own sake. ^1-2-8

147. If so, how could it ever arise for the sake of other sentient beings in their waking state? It is extremely difficult to arise. As the *Ornament* states: "Just as the heirs of the Victorious Ones remain here with a beneficial thought to ripen all wandering beings, in the same way, fathers, mothers, and relatives do not remain with such goodness toward their children and kin." Third, how it is obtained due to being a marvelous virtue: Let alone for the sake of others, this mind that wishes to attain Buddhahood for the sake of all sentient beings does not arise even for one's own sake in other ordinary beings. In whichever sentient being's stream of being this arises, they become equal to a supreme, precious wish-fulfilling jewel that grants all needs and aspirations. This is an unprecedented, marvelous distinction that has arisen in the Heroes of Enlightenment; hence, they are certain to obtain great benefits. ^1-2-9

148. Fourth, how it is obtained due to its outstanding activity: It acts as the cause for the temporary and ultimate joy and happiness of all wandering beings without exception, and is like a great medicine or elixir that clears all the illnesses of suffering of the cycle of existence and the hell realms for sentient beings, and like a precious jewel that clears all degradations. How could the merit of this mind of enlightenment ever be measured by signs, analogies, or numbers? It cannot be measured, and thus one obtains infinite benefits equal to space. ^1-2-10

149. Second, how it is supreme among all: of the four parts, first, how it is supreme among all offerings: Even the mere thought of wanting to benefit sentient beings with a mind of love, even when devoid of actual practice, as the *Lamp of the Moon Sūtra* (*Candrapradīpa Sūtra* / *Samādhirāja Sūtra*) states: "Even if one makes immeasurable, various, and numerous offerings to the Victorious Ones in as many billions of Buddha fields as there are, they cannot equal even a fraction or a drop of the mind of love." ^1-2-11

150. Since it is taught to be far superior to making infinite offerings to limitless Buddhas, ^1-2-12

151. what need is there to mention the exceptional merit of engaging with diligent practice in accomplishing the great temporary and ultimate happiness of all sentient beings without exception, who are equal to space? ^1-2-13

152. As the *Inquiry of Vīradatta Sūtra* also states: "If someone fills as many Buddha fields as there are grains of sand in the Ganges with the seven precious jewels, ^1-2-14

153. and offers them to the Protectors of the world, and if someone else folds their hands with devotion ^1-2-15

154. and generates the mind of enlightenment, ^1-2-16

155. this offering is far superior, ^1-2-17

156. and has no limit." ^1-2-18

157. This is how it is. Second, how it is supreme among all virtues: Although foolish sentient beings have the thought of wanting to discard suffering, for the sake of pacifying sufferings such as hunger, they commit non-virtues such as killing, and thus run directly toward the sufferings of hell, like moths leaping into a flame. In the same way, although they have the thought of wanting to obtain the distinctions of happiness, they are ignorant of its methods and, due to confusion, engage in various non-virtues, thereby destroying their own happiness as if it were an enemy. ^1-2-19

158. Therefore, for those wretched ones who are destitute and impoverished of happiness along with its causes, and who possess abundant sufferings along with their causes and are tormented, ^1-2-20

159. what other virtue of equal strength is there to this motivation of the mind of enlightenment—which satisfies them with all the happiness of higher rebirth and liberation through love, cuts off all the sufferings of the cycle of existence and the hell realms through compassion, and clears even their confusion by showing them the way of adoption and avoidance through wisdom? None exists. ^1-2-21

160. What other trustworthy spiritual friend or companion equal to this is there? None exists. What other accumulation of merit equal to this that benefits sentient beings is there? There is none equal to it. ^1-2-22

161. Third, how it is supreme among all those worthy of praise: If even an ordinary person who returns a benefit to someone who previously benefited them is considered worthy of praise and respect in the world as "one who does what is proper," ^1-2-23

162. what need is there to mention how worthy of praise, homage, offerings, and respect is an heir of the Buddha endowed with this mind-generation—who, without any prior benefit received and without being asked by others, benefits all sentient beings out of compassion, establishing them in goodness and virtue without expecting any return or maturation of action? ^1-2-24

163. Fourth, how it is supreme among all benefactors: If a benefactor who, focusing on a limited object—a few wandering beings of a specific area—provides ordinary food on an annual or monthly basis, giving merely mediocre food for a brief moment without making offerings, and with mediocre application, such as treating them with contempt, fighting, and beating them, and providing only a mediocre benefit of satisfying them for half a day, ^1-2-25

164. is praised and respected by many people in the world as "one who performs great virtue," ^1-2-26

165. what need is there to mention how worthy of praise and respect are those endowed with the mind of enlightenment—the great benefactors who, focusing on a vast object, the infinite and countless realms of sentient beings, constantly give for a vast duration as long as space remains and the cycle of existence is not emptied, providing the supreme object of the unexcelled happiness of the Blissful Ones, with peaceful, disciplined, and respectful application, in a manner that completely fulfills all the various desires in their minds? ^1-2-27

166. The Sanskrit text, instead of "the unexcelled happiness of the Blissful Ones," ^1-2-28

167. has: "As long as space and wandering beings are not completely exhausted." ^1-2-29

168. This has the meaning of "as long as space remains and the cycle of existence is not emptied." ^1-2-30

169. Third, how they are worthy of homage: establishing them as the supreme field, and paying homage and taking refuge in them. ^1-2-31

170. Regarding the first, establishing them as the supreme field, of the two parts, first, how they are taught to be the supreme field of negativity: Let alone physical or verbal harm, if any low sentient being generates even a single moment of harmful thought such as anger toward an heir of the Buddha—the supreme benefactor endowed with the qualities explained above—the Lord of Sages taught that the perpetrator will experience great suffering and remain in hell for as many aeons as there were moments of harmful thoughts generated. ^1-2-34

171. As the *Sūtra of the Miracle of the Complete Pacification of Certainty* states: "Mañjuśrī, if an heir of the Buddha generates a mind of anger or contempt toward another heir of the Buddha, they must don the armor of remaining in hell for that many aeons." ^1-2-35

172. This is how it is. ^1-2-36

173. Second, showing them to be the supreme field of virtue: On the other hand, if a fortunate person generates clear faith and devotion toward one who possesses the mind of enlightenment, the fruit of that faith increases far more than the fruit of the harmful thought. ^1-2-37

174. Because this is so. Even if others obstruct or make great efforts to hinder the profound Dharma of the heirs of the Buddha, no negativity such as harm toward sentient beings ever arises in them; rather, virtues such as love and compassion naturally increase more and more. Second, paying homage: To whichever person this precious, supreme mind of enlightenment—which clears all degenerations and grants all fortunes like a wish-fulfilling jewel—has been generated, we pay homage to their body with devotion through the three doors at all times. Not only does an heir of the Buddha bestow happiness when benefited, but even when harmed through contempt and so forth, they connect them with and bestow temporary and ultimate happiness through great compassion. Having promised to always rely on that noble being, who is the sole source of all happiness, we place our trust and take refuge in them. If one generates harmful thoughts toward such a noble object, it is not contradictory that they fall into hell,

175. and it is also not contradictory that they are eventually connected with great happiness through the skillful methods and the power of the aspirational prayers of those endowed with great compassion. Therefore, by recollecting the benefits of the mind of enlightenment, one should also have devotion toward those noble beings who possess it. ^1-2-37

176. From the *Bodhisattvacaryāvatāra*, this is the first chapter, the explanation of the benefits of the mind of enlightenment, which is the aspiring mind. ^1-2-38
