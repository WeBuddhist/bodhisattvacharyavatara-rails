## Today's Verse

> I should not be delicate
> about heat, cold, rain, wind,
> illness, bondage, or beatings.
> If I am, the harm will increase. ^6-16

> Some people, seeing their own blood,
> become even more courageous.
> Some people, seeing the blood of others,
> collapse and faint. ^6-17

> This comes from the mind's steadfastness
> and from past habits.
> Therefore, disregard harm
> and be untouched by sufferings. ^6-18

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the sixteenth, seventeenth, and eighteenth verses of the sixth chapter. We build steady courage in the mind, so that we are not afraid even when we see rain and wind, or blood.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. In war, brave warriors grow even braver when they see their own blood flowing. Cowards become afraid and faint, even when they see the blood of others. This difference does not come from the strength of the body or of weapons. It comes from the steadiness of the mind and from getting used to cowardly ways. If we develop courage and make the mind steady from the start, even great suffering becomes a friend on the path.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will not make a fuss about the suffering of heat, cold, rain, and wind. I will endure it.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ If we make a fuss about heat, cold, and so on, our endurance becomes smaller and the harm becomes bigger. If we react to small harms, the feeling of suffering grows bigger. So while we train our mind in bodhicitta, we should get used to not losing heart, whatever hardship comes.
