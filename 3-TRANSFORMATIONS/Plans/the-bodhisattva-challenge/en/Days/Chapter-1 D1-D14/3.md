# Day 3 — What the Buddhas spent eons looking for

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Turn a hard moment into a wish for others
> **Detail:** When something hurts today, remember others feel it too, and quietly wish them well.

## Introduction

Yesterday's verses made the case for urgency. Today's three verses make the case for bodhicitta as the answer. The argument moves fast: ordinary virtue is simply too weak against the weight of our negativity; the Buddhas searched for eons and found this one thing works; and so if you want to end suffering — yours and everyone else's — this is the path.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

*MIND TRAINING (recite 3 times)*

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in immeasurable equanimity, free from attachment and aversion.

*REFUGE (recite 3 times)*

> Until I reach the heart of awakening, I take refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.

*TAKING THE BODHISATTVA VOW (recite 3 times)*

> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And trained step by step in the bodhisattva conduct,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And train step by step in that same conduct.

## Today's Verses

> དེ་ལྟས་དགེ་བ་ཉམ་ཆུང་ཉིད་ལ་རྟག །
> སྡིག་པ་སྟོབས་ཆེན་ཤིན་ཏུ་མི་བཟད་པ། །
> དེ་ནི་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མིན་པ། །
> དགེ་གཞན་གང་གིས་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་པར་འགྱུར། །
>
> Therefore, ordinary virtue is always extremely weak. Negativity is immensely powerful and unbearable. Except for the perfect mind of enlightenment, what other virtue can overcome such great evil?

> བསྐལ་པ་དུ་མར་རབ་དགོངས་མཛད་པ་ཡི། །
> ཐུབ་དབང་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་འདི་ཉིད་ཕན་པར་གཟིགས། །
> འདིས་ནི་ཚད་མེད་སྐྱེ་བོའི་ཚོགས་རྣམས་ཀྱིས། །
> བདེ་མཆོག་བདེ་བླག་ཉིད་དུ་ཐོབ་པར་བྱེད། །
>
> The Wise Sages contemplated this for many eons and saw that this mind alone is beneficial. Through it, countless multitudes of beings easily attain the highest happiness.

> སྲིད་པའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་བརྒྱ་ཕྲག་གཞོམ་འདོད་ཅིང་། །
> སེམས་ཅན་མི་བདེ་བསལ་བར་འདོད་པ་དང་། །
> བདེ་མང་བརྒྱ་ཕྲག་སྤྱོད་པར་འདོད་པས་ཀྱང་། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ཉིད་རྟག་ཏུ་གཏང་མི་བྱ། །
>
> Do you wish to overcome the hundreds of sufferings of worldly existence? Do you wish to remove the unhappiness of sentient beings and enjoy hundreds of joys? If so, you must never abandon the mind of enlightenment.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Je uses an agricultural image to unpack verse 1.7. Bodhicitta, he says, is like a seed — it carries within it everything needed for the final result. What's striking is what he says about the path itself: unlike approaches that depend on severe physical hardship, this one proceeds from happiness to even greater happiness. He quotes an old verse: "Going from bliss to bliss, what intelligent person would be lazy?" Sazang Mati Panchen echoes this — bodhicitta lets beings reach the highest happiness "without depending on fierce hardships or arduous effort." This is not what most people expect from a teaching about overcoming suffering.

## Aspiration

> May the precious and supreme bodhicitta —
> Where it has not yet arisen, may it arise;
> Where it has arisen, may it not decline,
> But grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of this practice,
> May all wandering beings, without exception,
> Enter the path of the bodhisattva.

## Today's Practice Challenge

Verse 1.8 is addressed to anyone who wants to end suffering and find happiness — which is everyone. Sazang grounds this in something concrete: birth, aging, sickness, and death. The next time you feel something difficult in your own body or life — tiredness, a moment of real pain, the weight of a bad day — pause long enough to notice that every other person is experiencing some version of this too. Let that recognition be the beginning of a wish.
