# Fact-Check Report: NTS (Ngulchu Thokme) × Marathi Children's Translation

Prepared by: commentary-fact-check skill  
Commentary: `1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md`  
Translation: `AI_translation/marathi/bca-marathi-children.md`

Severity key:
- **ERROR** — translation contradicts or inverts the commentarial meaning
- **SOFTENING** — translation reduces intensity, scope, or doctrinal precision in a way that could mislead
- **NOTE** — editorial observation; no error

---

## Chapter 1 — Introduction / Bodhicitta Aspiration

### ERROR — verse 1-1
**Marathi:** "तू श्रद्धाळू आहेस, तर बोधिसत्त्वाच्या आचरणात प्रवेश घे."  
**NTS commentary:** The chapter opens with homage and a statement of purpose — the author declares he will briefly explain *entry into the conduct of the bodhisattvas* following scripture. The first verse is not addressed to a "you" (reader/listener) but is the author's own dedication of purpose.  
**Issue:** Marathi converts the author's dedicatory statement into an exhortation addressed to the reader. This shifts the voice from first-person authorial intent to second-person instruction, misrepresenting the verse's function as a dedication/introduction.

### SOFTENING — verse 1-5
**Marathi:** "जसा आनंद आणखी वाढत राहतो."  
**NTS commentary:** NTS explains that bodhicitta surpasses the merit of the seven limbs, and specifically compares it to the wish-fulfilling jewel (*yid bzhin nor bu*) that grants all wishes — this is the image anchoring the joy analogy.  
**Issue:** Marathi retains the sense of growing joy but drops the wish-fulfilling-jewel imagery that the commentary treats as essential for understanding why bodhicitta surpasses all other virtues.

### SOFTENING — verse 1-28
**Marathi:** "पापांची माफी मागणे सुद्धा कठीण आहे."  
**NTS commentary:** The verse specifies that evil done to bodhisattvas specifically impedes liberation for eons (*bskal pa*); it is not a general difficulty of confession but a time-scaled karmic consequence.  
**Issue:** Marathi omits the temporal scope (eons) and the specific object (bodhisattvas) making the consequence seem general rather than calibrated.

## Chapter 2 — Confession of Misdeeds

### SOFTENING — verse 2-1
**Marathi:** "मी मनापासून चांगली पूजा अर्पण करतो."  
**NTS commentary:** NTS enumerates six qualities of perfect offering: (1) with intention for enlightenment, (2) for the sake of beings, (3) with pleasing substances, (4) with purity of three spheres, (5) free from opposing conditions, (6) dedicated to great awakening.  
**Issue:** Marathi retains the act of offering but drops the sixfold structure entirely. For a children's translation this is appropriate simplification, but it should not be used to generate a scholarly translation.

### SOFTENING — verse 2-24
**Marathi:** "माझे आई, वडील, गुरू यांना दुखावले."  
**NTS commentary:** The verse specifically lists harming the Buddha (*sangs rgyas*) as the gravest object, followed by the Dharma and Sangha, then parents and teachers — the order of severity is doctrinally significant.  
**Issue:** Marathi omits the Three Jewels as objects of harm, listing only parents and teachers. Given that harm to the Buddha is foregrounded by both commentaries as the most severe case, this is a substantive omission.

### NOTE — Chapter 2 overall
Both NTS and KKP contain detailed commentary only on selected verses of Chapter 2; many verses (2-3 through 2-20) have sparse or no NTS commentary, making full verse-by-verse audit impossible from these sources alone.

## Chapter 3 — Taking Bodhicitta

### SOFTENING — verse 3-6 / 3-7
**Marathi (3-6):** "मी त्यांना औषध आणि डॉक्टर बनेन."  
**NTS commentary:** The verse says "may I become medicine, a doctor, and a nurse (*snye ma*)" — the word *snye ma* means "grain/grass" used as an image of a nurse who tends without expecting anything. NTS additionally explains that this aspiration follows the pattern of the earth, wind, fire, water, medicine analogies (inanimate benefactors) — the key point being effortless, unconditional benefit.  
**Issue:** Marathi omits "nurse/grass-tender" and drops the inanimate-benefactor framing. The threefold structure (medicine/doctor/nurse) is collapsed to two.

### SOFTENING — verse 3-33
**Marathi:** "त्या सर्वांना बोधीचे भाग्य लाभो."  
**NTS commentary:** NTS specifies two levels: (1) immediate happiness *for now* and (2) ultimately *entering into the conduct of bodhisattvas* / attaining the happiness of buddhahood (*bde gshegs nyid*). The Marathi captures only the ultimate aspiration.  
**Issue:** The immediate/ultimate distinction is collapsed. For a children's translation acceptable; worth noting for scholarly use.

## Chapter 4 — Heedfulness

### SOFTENING — verse 4-14
**Marathi:** "बंधने, कापले जाणे, तुकडे होणे असे भोगावे लागेल."  
**NTS root text cited:** "In the lower realms — disease, bondage, cutting, splitting and similar [sufferings] will be experienced."  
**Issue:** Marathi omits disease (*nad*) as the first item in the sequence. The list in NTS opens with disease in the lower realms; Marathi jumps straight to bondage and cutting.

### NOTE — Chapter 4 overall
Chapter 4 is well rendered. The key doctrinal warnings about heedlessness (*bag med*) and the consequences of losing bodhicitta are faithfully conveyed in simplified Marathi.

## Chapter 5 — Vigilance

### NOTE — Chapter 5 overall
NTS provides detailed commentary on guarding the mind (*sems srung*) and the simile of the wounded man walking through a crowd. The Marathi chapter captures the key metaphors (wounded man, elephant-mind, guard at the gate) appropriately for children. No ERRORs detected against NTS.

## Chapter 6 — Patience (Kṣānti)

### SOFTENING — verse 6-1 / 6-2 (chapter opening)
**Marathi (6-1):** "एका रागानेच नष्ट होते."  
**NTS root text cited:** "Whatever good conduct has been accumulated over a thousand eons — generosity, offering to the Tathāgatas, and so forth — all of it is destroyed by a single flash of anger."  
**Issue:** Marathi captures "anger destroys" but strips the scale entirely: *a thousand eons of merit* (*bskal pa stong du bsags pa yi*) is the specific quantity. Dropping this removes the gravity the commentary explicitly calls central.

### NOTE — verse 6-10 / 6-11 (fixable / unfixable logic)
**Marathi (6-10):** "तर दुःखी होऊन काय उपयोग?"  
**NTS root text cited:** "If it is remediable, why be unhappy? If it is not remediable, what does being unhappy achieve?"  
The Marathi captures one horn of the dilemma (if unfixable, no point) but compresses the symmetrical two-part argument into a single line. For children this is acceptable but worth noting.

## Chapter 7 — Diligence (Vīrya)

### SOFTENING — verse 7-1
**Marathi:** "तसेच उत्साहाशिवाय पुण्यही निर्माण होत नाही."  
**NTS root text cited:** "Without diligence neither merit (*bsod nams*) nor wisdom (*ye shes*) arises, just as without wind there is no movement."  
**Issue:** Marathi renders "merit" (*bsod nams*) but omits "wisdom/knowledge" (*ye shes*). NTS commentary explains this refers to the accumulation of the two collections (*tshogs gnyis*) — merit and wisdom. Dropping wisdom weakens the soteriological claim to only one of the two essential accumulations.

### SOFTENING — verse 7-2
**Marathi:** "आणि स्वतःला कमी लेखून खचून जाणे."  
**NTS commentary:** NTS enumerates three forms of laziness: (1) laziness of inertia (*snyom las*), (2) laziness of attachment to negative activities, and (3) laziness of self-deprecation (*bdag nyid brnyas pa*). The verse names all three; Marathi renders only the third.  
**Issue:** Two of the three specific forms of laziness are dropped. This is acceptable simplification for children but the threefold structure is doctrinally important.

## Chapter 8 — Meditation (Samādhi)

### SOFTENING — verse 8-4
**Marathi:** "जी जगाच्या आसक्तीचा त्याग करून, मनापासून आनंदाने साधता येते."  
**NTS root text cited:** "Knowing that afflictions are fully overcome by calm abiding (*zhi gnas*) endowed with insight (*lhag mthong*), first seek calm abiding — this is achieved with joy by one not attached to the world."  
**Issue:** The paired technical terms *śamatha* + *vipaśyanā* (calm abiding and insight meditation) are the structural core of this verse and the chapter. Marathi renders "meditation practiced joyfully" but omits the śamatha/vipaśyanā distinction entirely. NTS and KKP both emphasize that vipaśyanā without śamatha cannot eliminate the afflictions — the sequence matters doctrinally.

### NOTE — Chapter 8 overall
Chapter 8 contains highly condensed philosophical material (two-truths, dependent origination, exchange of self and other). The Marathi simplifies appropriately for children. No outright ERRORs beyond the śamatha/vipaśyanā omission above.

## Chapter 9 — Wisdom (Prajñā)

### SOFTENING — verse 9-2
**Marathi:** "कारण बुद्धी स्वतःच व्यावहारिक सत्याचाच भाग आहे."  
**NTS root text cited:** "The ultimate (*don dam*) is not the object of discursive mind (*blo*); discursive mind is said to be conventional (*kun rdzob*)."  
**Issue:** Marathi renders this as "wisdom/intelligence (*buddhi*) itself is part of conventional truth." The Tibetan source distinguishes *blo* (discursive cognition) as conventional from *don dam* (ultimate truth) as its object. The Marathi use of *buddhi* (a positive cognitive term) for *blo* (ordinary discursive mind) is somewhat misleading — the point is that *ordinary intellect* is limited to the conventional, not that *wisdom/prajñā* is conventional. For children, simplified; but *prajna* should not be conflated with *blo*.

### NOTE — Chapter 9 overall
Chapter 9 is the most philosophically dense chapter. The Marathi reduces the two-truths debate, the refutations of Sāṃkhya/Mīmāṃsā opponents, and the Madhyamaka arguments to highly compressed one- or two-line stanzas. This is appropriate for the target audience but makes the chapter unsuitable as a source for scholarly translation without extensive cross-checking against NTS/KKP.

## Chapter 10 — Dedication

### NOTE — Chapter 10 overall
Chapter 10 consists of aspirational dedication verses. NTS commentary on most verses is brief (glossing the root text closely). The Marathi renders the aspiration accurately and warmly. The key metaphors (rain of merit, liberation of all beings from lower realms, wish-fulfilling trees arising in hells) are simplified but not inverted.

No ERRORs detected against NTS in Chapter 10.
