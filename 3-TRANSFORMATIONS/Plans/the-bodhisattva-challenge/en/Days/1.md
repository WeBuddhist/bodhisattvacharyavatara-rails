# Day 1 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: The Benefits of the Mind of Enlightenment — Verses 1-1 to 1-3

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Releasing Pride (Humility):** The great teacher Śāntideva shows humility by saying he has no special skill or new ideas. Letting go of pride makes the mind open to real learning. When arrogance is released, the spiritual path becomes much easier to walk.

*   **Building Merit Through Respect (Devotion):** Paying homage to the Buddhas and their heirs helps remove obstacles in the mind. This respectful act builds great spiritual merit that supports all future practice. Even a small act of genuine respect can bring lasting positive results.

*   **Strengthening Faith Through Practice (Self-Cultivation):** Writing down spiritual teachings helps the strength of faith in virtue grow over time. When faith increases, other good qualities like diligence and wisdom naturally follow. This inner growth leads to real and lasting change in daily life.

---

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-1**

> བདེ་གཤེགས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་མངའ་སྲས་བཅས་དང་། །
> ཕྱག་འོས་ཀུན་ལའང་གུས་པར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏེ། །
> བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་ནི། །
> ལུང་བཞིན་མདོར་བསྡུས་ནས་ནི་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ། །
>
> *I pay respectful homage to the Blissful Ones who possess the Truth Body, and to their heirs. I also bow to everyone who is worthy of honor. I shall now briefly describe how to enter the commitments of the Buddha's heirs, in accordance with the scriptures.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-1**

In this opening verse, the great teacher Śāntideva bows to the Buddhas, the teaching, and the Heroes of Enlightenment. This act of homage through body, speech, and mind helps remove obstacles and builds great merit for all who practice. Paying respect to these supreme spiritual objects also prepares the mind to receive the teachings, and it enables future readers to accumulate good spiritual qualities.

---

#### **Verse 1-2**

> སྔོན་ཆད་མ་བྱུང་བ་ཡང་འདིར་བརྗོད་མེད། །
> སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་མཁས་པའང་བདག་ལ་ཡོད་མིན་ཏེ། །
> དེ་ཕྱིར་གཞན་དོན་བསམ་པ་བདག་ལ་མེད། །
> རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་ངས་འདི་བརྩམས། །
>
> *I have nothing new to say here, and I do not possess any skill in writing poetry. Therefore, I have no expectation of benefiting others. I wrote this solely to train and cultivate my own mind.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-2**

The great teacher Śāntideva shows deep humility by saying he has nothing new to teach and no special skill in writing. This text contains nothing that is not already found in the sacred scriptures, so those who can easily understand them directly do not need this text. His main goal is to train his own mind and keep his wish for enlightenment strong, because writing down these teachings prevents his practice from declining and helps his faith and wisdom grow over time.

---

#### **Verse 1-3**

> དགེ་བ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་བདག་གི་དད་པའི་ཤུགས། །
> འདི་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་རེ་ཞིག་འཕེལ་འགྱུར་ལ། །
> བདག་དང་སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཀྱང་། །
> ཅི་སྟེ་འདི་དག་མཐོང་ན་དོན་ཡོད་འགྱུར། །
>
> *By writing this, the strength of my faith to cultivate virtue will temporarily increase. If anyone of equal fortune should see these words, perhaps they will also find them useful.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-3**

By writing this text, the great teacher Śāntideva's faith grows stronger in three distinct ways: trust in cause and effect, admiration for noble spiritual beings, and confidence in one's own ability to practice. As faith grows in these ways, other important qualities such as diligence, clear awareness, and wisdom also develop naturally. If another person with the same wish for enlightenment reads this text, it may also help them walk the path and find real benefit for their own mind.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Begin Each Day with Homage:** Today, I will start my morning by bowing three times to the Buddhas and the path of awakening. This simple act helps me set a clear intention for the day and opens my mind to practice with sincerity.

*   **2. Let Go of Pride When It Arises:** When I notice pride or arrogance appearing — about my knowledge, skills, or opinions — I will pause and recall the great teacher Śāntideva's example of humility. Releasing that pride, even briefly, helps my mind become more open and kind.

*   **3. Write to Strengthen My Faith:** Today, I will write one short reflection in my journal based on these three verses. Writing down my understanding helps my faith in the practice grow stronger and keeps my intention for enlightenment clear and alive.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **The Blissful Ones (བདེ་གཤེགས་):** This term refers to the Buddhas — those who have completely freed themselves from all suffering and reached perfect wisdom. They are called "blissful" because they have fully left behind pain and achieved the highest happiness, never to return to suffering again. In this verse, paying homage to them at the very start sets the foundation for the entire teaching.

*   **Truth Body (ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་):** This refers to the ultimate nature of a Buddha's awakened mind — completely pure, free from all faults, and filled with wisdom. It is not a physical form but the state of perfect realization itself. Honoring this quality helps the practitioner understand what full awakening truly means and points toward what is possible.

*   **Heroes of Enlightenment (བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་):** These are great practitioners who have deeply committed to freeing all living beings from suffering. They are called "heirs" or "sons" of the Buddhas because they follow the same path and carry on the work of awakening in the world. In verse 1-1, they are included in the homage as part of the spiritual community.

*   **Commitments (སྡོམ་):** This word refers to the vows and ethical commitments that a Hero of Enlightenment takes on. Entering these commitments means formally dedicating oneself to the path of awakening for the benefit of all beings. The entire text is presented as a guide to understanding and living by these commitments.

*   **Faith (དད་པ་):** In this context, faith includes three distinct qualities: trust in the law of cause and effect, admiration and respect for those who have noble qualities, and confidence in one's own ability to practice. In verse 1-3, the great teacher Śāntideva explains that writing down these teachings helps all three aspects of faith grow stronger over time.
