# Style sheet: English termbase for the Tibetan Bodhicaryāvatāra

Shared conventions for the Academic and Children's registers (see `termbase/registers.md`). Every choice is grounded in the renderings attested by DharmaMitra (DM) and Gemini (G) in `overview.md`; "new" in a Reason column marks a rendering that departs from both. Renderings are singular noun-phrase heads, lower case unless a name or title.

## Policies

1. **Awakening, not enlightenment, for བྱང་ཆུབ (both registers).** Both translations attest awakening, and it keeps བྱང་ཆུབ, བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས and བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ visibly one family; "enlightenment" (DM's majority) is dropped so compounds do not alternate.
2. **Bodhicitta stays Sanskrit in the academic register; the children's register uses "awakening mind".** Academic English keeps bodhicitta (register rule, DM attests it); children get the attested "awakening mind" because no Sanskrit is allowed. Never "mind of enlightenment".
3. **Aspiring and engaging bodhicitta are fixed forms.** Academic: "aspiring bodhicitta" (བྱང་ཆུབ་སྨོན་པའི་སེམས) and "engaging bodhicitta" (འཇུག་པའི་སེམས, བྱང་ཆུབ་འཇུག་པ); children: "mind that wishes to awaken" and "mind that sets out to awaken". Both pairs build on the attested "aspiring mind" and "engaging mind".
4. **Afflictions, never defilements (academic); "troubling emotion" for children.** Both translations use "affliction" (ཉོན་མོངས, ཉོན་མོངས་པ, ཉེ་ཉོན་མོངས = secondary affliction); "defilement" is unattested. The adjective is "afflicted", and "afflictive" only in the set phrase for the obscurations.
5. **ཆོས is one Tibetan word with two senses, split by context.** (a) the Buddha's teaching: academic "Dharma" (capitalised), children "teachings"; (b) things as such, in the chapter 9 philosophical argument: academic "phenomenon", children "thing that appears". Decide by context: teaching/doctrine/sublime/holy = (a); things analysed, existing or empty = (b). Never "dharma" lower case and never "dharmas".
6. **The six perfections are fixed in this order of names.** Academic: generosity, ethical discipline, patience, diligence, meditative concentration, wisdom; the word "perfection" is the head of ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན ("perfection of generosity"). Children: giving, good conduct, patience, joyful effort, calm focus, wisdom. "Heroic perseverance" is not used because neither translation attests it; "discipline" alone is avoided because it is ambiguous with བརྟུལ་ཞུགས (ascetic discipline).
7. **Mindfulness and introspection are different terms.** དྲན་པ is "mindfulness" and ཤེས་བཞིན is "introspection" (academic); "vigilance", "alertness" and "clear comprehension" are not used. Children: "keeping in mind" and "watchfulness". བག་ཡོད is "heedfulness" (chapter 4 title) so it does not collide with either.
8. **Two truths: conventional and ultimate, never relative.** ཀུན་རྫོབ is "conventional" (DM, and G's "conventional reality"), though G also uses "relative"; དོན་དམ is "ultimate". Noun head forms are "conventional truth" and "ultimate truth" (བདེན་པ་གཉིས, བདེན་གཉིས = "two truths"). Children: "everyday truth" and "deepest truth". དེ་ཉིད stays "reality" in both registers so it is not confused with དོན་དམ.
9. **Philosophical schools and tenets (chapter 9) use usual Sanskrit or English names.** Academic: Cittamātra (not "Mind Only"), Sāṃkhya, Madhyamaka (the last is not in the glossary list but fixed for the text); tenet-terms Pradhāna, sattva, rajas, tamas, Īśvara stay Sanskrit with IAST. Children: "Mind Only", "Samkhya"; tenet-terms become plain words (see core terms). མུ་སྟེགས་པ is "non-Buddhist" (both agree); never "heretic" or "tīrthika".
10. **Emptiness is never "nothing".** སྟོང་ཉིད and སྟོང་པ་ཉིད are "emptiness" in both registers (both translations agree); negations such as དངོས་མེད use "non-existent" / "non-entity", not "nothingness".
11. **Hells, realms and beings.** དམྱལ་བ is "hell" (singular or plural by context); མནར་མེད is "Avīci hell" (academic) / "Avichi" (children); དམྱལ་བའི་སྲུང་མ is "hell guardian". The six-realm terms: ལྷ "god", ལྷ་མིན "asura" (children "demigod"), ཡི་དྭགས "hungry ghost", དུད་འགྲོ "animal". ངན་འགྲོ and ངན་སོང are both "lower realm"; བདེ་འགྲོ is "happy realm" (children use "unhappy realm" for the lower ones).
12. **Yama and the Lord of Death are distinct.** གཤིན་རྗེ is always "Yama" (G, consistent); འཆི་བདག is always "Lord of Death" (both). DM's use of "Lord of Death" for གཤིན་རྗེ is not followed, so the two figures stay separable; གཤིན་རྗེའི་ཕོ་ཉ is "messenger of Yama".
13. **Beings: three terms, three renderings (academic).** སེམས་ཅན "sentient being", འགྲོ་བ and འགྲོ "wandering being" (G's majority; DM's bare "beings" would erase the distinction), ལུས་ཅན "embodied being". Children: "living being", "being", "being with a body".
14. **Sin vocabulary is avoided.** སྡིག་པ, སྡིག is "misdeed" (attested by both) rather than "sin"/"evil"/"sinner" (DM), which import Christian framing; མི་དགེ་བ is "non-virtue" and ཉེས་པ is "fault". Children: "harmful deed" and "bad deed".
15. **Sanskrit left untranslated in the academic register, with IAST:** bodhicitta, bodhisattva, Buddha/buddha, Dharma, Sugata, Tathāgata, saṃsāra, nirvāṇa, karma, Mahāyāna, sūtra, stūpa, mantra, yogi, Saṅgha, Māra, asura, Śrāvaka, Pratyekabuddha, Dharmakāya, Pradhāna, sattva/rajas/tamas, and the names of classes of beings (rākṣasa, ḍākinī, garuḍa). All other terms are translated. Children: none of these appear except as names (Buddha, Manjushri); epithets are translated by meaning.
16. **Diacritics.** Academic uses full IAST for all Sanskrit (Śāntideva, Mañjuśrī, Īśvara, Avīci, Ākāśagarbha, Kṣitigarbha, Māra, saṃsāra, nirvāṇa, sūtra, Tathāgata, Mahāyāna); never the DM variants "samsara", "nirvana", "Bodhichitta", "Mahayana". Children: no diacritics anywhere (Shantideva, Manjushri, Avalokiteshvara).
17. **Capitalisation.** Capitalise: Buddha, Dharma (sense a), Three Jewels, Saṅgha, Sugata and Tathāgata when used as titles of a Buddha, and the epithets Victor, Protector, Sage, Guide, Lord of Death, Yama, and names of people, places and buddha-fields. Lower case: bodhisattva, sūtra, mantra, karma, saṃsāra, nirvāṇa, hell, god, merit, and every ordinary noun. Only chapter titles and the work's title use Title Case. The translator capitalises sentence and line starts in context.
18. **Epithets of the Buddha.** རྒྱལ་བ "Victor" (DM; G's "Conqueror" is dropped, and "Victorious One" is allowed as a long form), ཐུབ་པ "Sage", མགོན་པོ "Protector", འདྲེན་པ "Guide". All are capitalised when they refer to a buddha, and are singular or plural by the Tibetan context.
19. **Compounds take their parts' renderings.** Never invent a new word for a compound whose parts are core terms: སྡུག་བསྔལ་རྒྱ་མཚོ is "ocean of suffering", ཉོན་མོངས་ཆུ་བོ is "river of afflictions", བསོད་ནམས་རྒྱ་མཚོ is "ocean of merit", རྒྱལ་བའི་ཞིང is "field of the Victors", སེམས་ཅན་ཞིང is "field of sentient beings". Where the two translations render the head differently, the core-term choice wins. Exception: a fixed technical or title phrase (e.g. ཉོན་མོངས་ཤེས་བྱའི་སྒྲིབ་པ = "obscuration of afflictions and of objects of knowledge") keeps its attested whole.
20. **Variant spellings share one rendering.** བདེ/བདེ་བ, ཆགས/ཆགས་པ, ཁྲོ/ཁྲོ་བ/ཁྲོས, སྡིག/སྡིག་པ, དམྱལ/དམྱལ་བ, བཟོད/བཟོད་པ, སྲེད/སྲེད་པ, སྒྱུ/སྒྱུ་མ, བྱིས/བྱིས་པ, ཤེས/ཤེས་པ, དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས/དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ, ཐུགས་རྗེ/སྙིང་རྗེ (honorific), མཆོད/མཆོད་པ, བསྔོ/བསྔོ་བ and the like get the same English as the full form; honorific and plain Tibetan registers are not distinguished in English.
21. **Merit, virtue and quality.** བསོད་ནམས is "merit"; དགེ་བ is "virtue" (never "wholesome" or "merit"); ཡོན་ཏན is "good quality" (G's majority; DM's "virtue" would collide with དགེ་བ); ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པ is "excellence". Children: "store of goodness", "good deed", "good quality".
22. **Fixed ones for the key verbs of practice.** བཤགས "confess", རྗེས་སུ་ཡི་རང "rejoice", ཕྱག་འཚལ "pay homage", སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི "take refuge", བསྔོ་བ "dedication"; the Seven-limb names follow these (Confession of Misdeeds, not "Sins" or "Negativities").
23. **Welfare for དོན in the self/other pairs; benefit only for ཕན་ཡོན.** རང་དོན, བདག་གི་དོན "own welfare"; གཞན་དོན, གཞན་གྱི་དོན, འགྲོ་དོན "welfare of others"; ཕན་ཡོན "benefit". DM's "welfare" is more consistent than G's mix of benefit/purpose. A third sense of དོན is "object" (see core terms).
24. **Mind terms are kept apart.** སེམས "mind", ཡིད "mind" (they cannot be distinguished by an attested English word; both translations render both "mind", so ཡིད appears mostly in fixed compounds such as ཡིད་སྒོ "gateway of the mind"), བློ "intellect", ཤེས་པ and རྣམ་ཤེས "consciousness", ཚོར་བ "feeling". Children: "mind", "mind", "thinking mind", "awareness", "feeling".
25. **Titles and the work's name.** The Tibetan title is "Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva" (both translations' modal form); the Sanskrit title transliterated in the text is "Bodhisattvacaryāvatāra" (G's form; DM's "Bodhicaryāvatāra" is a different, shorter title). Chapter and section titles keep their Tibetan words: བག་ཡོད་བསྟན་པ "Heedfulness", བཟོད་པ་བསྟན་པ "Patience", བསམ་གཏན་བསྟན་པ "Meditative Concentration", with the generic head "chapter" supplied by the translator.
26. **Names of people.** Indian names in IAST (Nāgārjuna, Sarvajñādeva, Sumatikīrti, Dharmaśrībhadra); Tibetan names in the simple phonetic form without diacritics (Rinchen Zangpo, Loden Sherab, Śākya Lodrö in academic only). Colophon information (translators, editors) is academic-only; the children's retelling omits it.
27. **Number and tense.** Core terms are singular lower case in the table; plurals, verb forms and adjective forms (angry/anger, afflicted/affliction, patient/patience) follow from the one rendering and the Tibetan inflection. Do not use a second English lexeme (e.g. "angry" for ཁྲོ and "rage" for ཁྲོ་བ).
28. **Names of the three poisons and key mental factors.** ཆགས་པ "attachment", ཞེ་སྡང "hatred", གཏི་མུག "ignorance" (the three roots); རྨོངས་པ "delusion" is kept separate. ང་རྒྱལ "pride" and རྒྱགས "arrogance", སྲེད་པ "craving", ཕྲག་དོག "envy", ལེ་ལོ "laziness", བག་མེད "heedlessness" each have one fixed word.
29. **Conditions of existence.** འཁོར་བ is "saṃsāra" (academic); སྲིད་པ is "existence" and is never "cyclic existence" in academic text (DM's "cyclic existence" for འཁོར་བ is dropped, and G's use of it for སྲིད་པ once is not followed), so the two terms do not merge. Children: "cycle of rebirth" and "life".
30. **Plain equivalents in the children's register must keep the idea.** Emptiness stays "emptiness", not "nothing"; the self ("I") stays "self"; karma becomes "action", not "luck" or "fate"; merit becomes "store of goodness", not "points"; hell is not softened to "bad place".
