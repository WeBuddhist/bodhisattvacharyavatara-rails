## Today's Verse

> One should not give up this body
> without pure compassion.
> By all means, in this and future lives,
> it should be used as a cause for accomplishing great aims. ^5-87

> Do not teach the teachings to the disrespectful,
> to those with covered heads who are not ill,
> to those with umbrellas, staffs, or weapons,
> or to those with covered heads. ^5-88

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the eighty-seventh and eighty-eighth verses of the fifth chapter. We think about when we must give up our own body, and when we must not. We also think about why we do not teach the Dharma to people holding umbrellas and staffs.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable quotation, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Master Aryadeva taught about the body in the _Chatuhshataka_ as follows. "Although we see the body as an enemy, even so we must protect it. Living long with ethics creates great merit from it." Although this body is like an enemy because it brings harm, living long with ethics accomplishes great merit. For this reason, we protect this body.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, to benefit others, I will take good care of my own body by eating and resting well.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ Without pure compassion, we must not harm our own body. If we protect this body well, we can bring great benefit to others. Living a long life becomes a cause for gathering great merit.
