# Day 9 — A wish no one in the world has had

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Take one kind wish further than usual
> **Detail:** This wish is rarer than any ordinary love. Today, take one wish all the way.

## Opening

Today's verses look around the whole world for a wish like this one and cannot find it. Not in parents, who love their children. Not in the wise, not in the gods, not even in their highest god. No one, the verses say, has ever wished this for others. Most have never even wished it for themselves, not even in a dream. That is how rare it is.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

**Ngulchu Thokme**, a fourteenth-century teacher, points to the love of a parent as the strongest, most natural goodwill we know. Then he quotes an old line: even parents, friends, and relatives do not wish for their loved ones what a bodhisattva wishes for every living being.

It is not that a parent's love is small. It is that it reaches only so far, to the few people they hold dear. This wish reaches everyone, with nothing left out. That is what no one in the world had thought of before.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

When you wish someone well today, notice where the wish usually stops, somewhere around "I hope things get easier for them." Try taking one wish all the way: may you be completely free.
