## Chapter One: The Benefits of Bodhicitta ^1-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-1]]

To the Sugatas, who have the Dharmakāya, and to their sons,
And to everyone else who deserves homage, I bow with respect.
How to enter the vows of the sons of the Sugata
I will now state briefly, following scripture. ^1-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-2]]

Nothing is said here that has not been said before,
And I have no skill in composition.
So I have no thought of benefiting others.
I wrote this to cultivate my own mind. ^1-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-3]]

So that I can cultivate virtue, the force of my faith
Will grow for a while through these words.
And if others who are as fortunate as I am
Should read them, they will find them meaningful. ^1-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-4]]

This life with leisure and endowment is extremely hard to find.
Now that we have gained the means to accomplish human purpose,
If we do not use it to do what is beneficial,
How will we ever find it again later? ^1-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-5]]

Just as on a dark night, inside a mass of clouds,
A flash of lightning lights everything up for an instant,
In the same way, through the power of the Buddha,
A mind that turns to merit now and then arises, briefly, in the world. ^1-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-6]]

So virtue is always feeble,
While misdeeds are extremely powerful and unbearable.
Except for the bodhicitta for perfect awakening,
What other virtue could overcome them? ^1-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-7]]

The Lords of Sages thought deeply for many eons
And saw that this alone is beneficial.
Through it, limitless crowds of beings
Will easily reach supreme happiness. ^1-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-8]]

Anyone who wishes to overcome the hundreds of sufferings of existence,
Who wishes to dispel the unhappiness of sentient beings,
And who wishes to enjoy many hundreds of joys,
Should never give up bodhicitta. ^1-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-9]]

When bodhicitta is born, in an instant,
Those wretched ones bound in the prison of saṃsāra
Will be called children of the Sugatas,
And the world, with its gods and humans, will pay them homage. ^1-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-10]]

Like the finest gold-making elixir,
It takes this impure body and transforms it
Into the priceless jewel of a Victor's body.
So hold firmly to what is called bodhicitta. ^1-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-11]]

The one caravan leader of wandering beings, whose intellect has no limit,
Has examined it well and found it to be very precious.
So those who wish to be free from the realms of wandering beings
Should hold firmly to precious bodhicitta. ^1-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-12]]

All other virtues are like the plantain tree:
Once they have produced a result, they are used up.
But the tree of bodhicitta always
Yields a result, is never used up, and keeps growing. ^1-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-13]]

Even if someone has committed extremely unbearable misdeeds,
Just as relying on a hero frees us from great fear,
By relying on this they are freed in an instant.
Why do those who take care not rely on it? ^1-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-14]]

Like the fire at the end of time, it
Certainly burns up great misdeeds in a single instant.
Its immeasurable benefits
Were explained to Sudhana by the wise Protector Maitreya. ^1-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-15]]

In brief, that bodhicitta
Should be understood to be of two kinds:
Aspiring bodhicitta
And engaging bodhicitta itself. ^1-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-16]]

There is a difference between wishing to go and going.
Just as we understand that difference,
In the same way a wise person should understand
The difference between these two, in their order. ^1-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-17]]

From aspiring bodhicitta,
A great result may arise while one is in saṃsāra,
But, unlike from engaging bodhicitta,
Merit does not arise continuously. ^1-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-18]]

From the moment when, in order to free completely
The infinite realm of sentient beings,
With an irreversible mind,
One rightly takes up that mind, ^1-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-19]]

From this moment on, whether one is asleep
Or has fallen into heedlessness, a force of merit,
Unbroken and many-sided,
Keeps arising, equal to space. ^1-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-20]]

The reasoning for this is given too,
In the Question of Subāhu,
Where, for sentient beings with lesser inclinations,
The Tathāgata himself taught this. ^1-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-21]]

If just by thinking, “I will relieve
The mere headaches of sentient beings,”
One has a beneficial intention
And gains immeasurable merit, ^1-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-22]]

Then wishing to dispel the immeasurable discomforts
Of each and every sentient being,
And wishing to bring about immeasurable good qualities
For each one—what need is there to mention it? ^1-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-23]]

Is there any father, or even any mother,
Who has such a beneficial intention?
Do any gods or seers have it?
Does even Brahmā have this? ^1-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-24]]

If, for those very sentient beings, before now,
Such a mind for their own sake
Was never even dreamed of in a dream,
How could it arise for the sake of others? ^1-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-25]]

The wish for the welfare of sentient beings
Does not arise in others even for their own sake.
This extraordinary jewel of a mind
Is born as a wonder never seen before. ^1-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-26]]

It is the cause of joy for all wandering beings
And the medicine for the suffering of sentient beings.
The merit of such a precious mind—
How could anyone measure it? ^1-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-27]]

If even the mere intention to be of benefit
Is greater than making offerings to the Buddhas,
Then, for all sentient beings without exception,
What need is there to mention striving for their happiness? ^1-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-28]]

Although they wish to be rid of suffering,
They rush straight toward suffering itself.
Although they desire happiness, because of ignorance
They destroy their own happiness as if it were an enemy. ^1-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-29]]

For those who have no happiness
And who have many sufferings,
That which fills them with all happiness
And cuts through all suffering, ^1-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-30]]

And also clears away their ignorance—
Where is there any virtue equal to that?
Where is there a friend like that?
Where is there merit like that? ^1-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-31]]

If someone who repays a benefit done to them
Is worthy of some praise,
Then what need is there to mention the bodhisattva
Who does good without being asked? ^1-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-32]]

Someone who gives a few wandering beings a regular meal,
Giving only food that lasts a brief moment,
And keeps them fed for half a day, with contempt,
Is honored by people as one who does virtue. ^1-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-33]]

To limitless numbers of sentient beings, for a long time,
One gives the unsurpassed happiness of the Sugatas,
Giving constantly, so as to fulfill every wish in their minds—
What need is there to mention one who gives like this? ^1-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-34]]

If anyone, toward such a benefactor, a child of the Victors,
Forms a malicious thought, that person,
For as many eons as the malicious thoughts formed,
Will stay in hell. So the Sage has taught. ^1-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-35]]

But if anyone develops a mind of deep devotion,
The result grows even greater than that.
Even when great hardship falls on the children of the Victors,
Misdeeds do not arise, and virtue grows by itself. ^1-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-36]]

Anyone in whom that supreme, precious mind
Has arisen—I bow to their body.
And there are those for whom even doing harm leads to happiness.
I go for refuge to those sources of happiness. ^1-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-a]]

This is the first chapter of Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva. It is called Explaining the Benefits of Bodhicitta. ^1-a

## Chapter Two: Confession of Misdeeds ^2-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-1]]

So that I can take hold of that precious mind,
I make excellent offerings to the Tathāgatas and the sublime Dharma,
To the stainless Jewel, and to the children of the Buddhas,
Who are oceans of good qualities. ^2-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-2]]

All the flowers and fruits there are,
All the kinds of medicine there are,
All the jewels there are in the world,
And all the pure and pleasing waters there are, ^2-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-3]]

Mountains of jewels, and likewise
Quiet, delightful forest lands,
Trees decorated with flowers,
And trees whose branches bend low with fine fruit, ^2-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-4]]

Also those in the worlds of the gods and others: fragrances,
Incense, wish-fulfilling trees, and jewel trees,
All kinds of crops that grow without being ploughed,
And other ornaments worthy of being offered, ^2-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-5]]

Lakes and ponds decorated with lotuses,
With swans that have very sweet and pleasing voices,
Reaching the limits of the infinite realm of space,
All those things that belong to no one, ^2-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-6]]

I take them up in my mind, and to the Sages, the supreme among persons,
Together with their children, I offer them well.
May those supreme worthy recipients of offerings, those of great compassion,
Think of me with love and accept these things of mine. ^2-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-7]]

I am without merit and extremely poor,
And I have no other wealth at all to make offerings with.
Therefore, may the Protectors, who think of the welfare of others,
Accept this for my sake through their own power. ^2-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-8]]

To the Victors and their children,
I shall offer my whole body forever.
Supreme beings, please accept me completely;
With devotion I will become your servant. ^2-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-9]]

Because you have completely accepted me,
I will be fearless in existence and act for the benefit of sentient beings.
I will fully go beyond my earlier misdeeds,
And I will not commit any further misdeeds from now on. ^2-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-10]]

In very fragrant bathhouses,
With clear and shining crystal floors laid out,
With pleasing pillars of blazing jewels,
And with canopies of radiant pearls spread above, ^2-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-11]]

To the Tathāgatas and their children,
I offer a bath with many precious vases of scented water,
Filled to the brim with pleasing things,
And with many songs and music. ^2-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-12]]

Their bodies, with matchless garments,
Clean and well perfumed, I dry.
Then I offer them robes,
Beautifully dyed, very fragrant, and of the finest kind. ^2-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-13]]

With many kinds of fine garments, thin and soft,
And with hundreds of those finest ornaments,
I shall also adorn the noble Samantabhadra and Mañjuśrī,
Avalokiteśvara, and others. ^2-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-14]]

With the finest perfumes, whose fragrance spreads
Throughout the three-thousandfold world system,
I shall anoint the bodies of all the Lords of Sages,
Shining and blazing like polished, refined gold. ^2-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-15]]

To the Lords of Sages, the highest objects of offering,
I make offerings of pleasing mandāra flowers and lotuses,
Blue lotuses, and all other sweet-smelling flowers,
And of beautifully strung, pleasing garlands. ^2-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-16]]

From the finest incense, whose captivating fragrance spreads far,
I also offer them clouds of smoke.
With meals of many kinds of food and drink,
I shall also offer them divine feasts. ^2-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-17]]

Set in golden lotuses arranged in rows,
I shall also offer precious lamps.
On the smooth ground, anointed with perfume,
I shall scatter pleasing loose flowers. ^2-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-18]]

Immeasurable palaces with pleasing songs of praise,
Shining beautifully with hanging ornaments of pearls and jewels,
Which have become ornaments of boundless space:
These too I shall offer to those whose nature is compassion. ^2-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-19]]

Beautiful jeweled parasols with golden handles,
With pleasing ornaments all around them,
Well shaped, lovely to look at, and held up high:
These too I shall always offer to the Lords of Sages. ^2-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-20]]

Besides these, there are many other offerings,
With music and pleasing, sweet melodies.
May the clouds that soothe the suffering of sentient beings
Stay in their own places. ^2-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-21]]

On all the jewels of the sublime Dharma,
On the stūpas and the images,
May a rain of jewels, flowers, and the like
Fall without stopping. ^2-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-22]]

Just as Mañjuśrī and the others
Make offerings to the Victors,
In the same way I make offerings to the Tathāgatas,
The Protectors, together with their children. ^2-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-23]]

I praise those who are oceans of good qualities
With oceans of verses of sweet-sounding praise.
May those sweet clouds of praise
Surely arise everywhere for them. ^2-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-24]]

To all the Buddhas who have gone in the three times,
Together with the Dharma and the Supreme Assembly,
With as many bodies as there are atoms in all the fields,
I bow down and pay homage. ^2-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-25]]

To the bases of bodhicitta
And to the stūpas, I pay homage.
To the preceptors and also the masters,
And to the supreme ascetics, I pay homage. ^2-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-26]]

Until I reach the essence of awakening,
I take refuge in the Buddhas.
In the same way, I take refuge
In the Dharma and in the assembly of bodhisattvas. ^2-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-27]]

To those who live in all directions,
The fully awakened Buddhas and those with bodhicitta,
Who have great compassion,
I join my palms and pray: ^2-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-28]]

From beginningless saṃsāra,
In this life or in other lives,
Any misdeeds that I have done without knowing
Or have caused others to do, ^2-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-29]]

Overpowered by the confusion of ignorance,
Whatever I have rejoiced in,
Having seen those mistakes,
I confess them to the Protectors from the depths of my heart. ^2-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-30]]

To the Three Jewels,
To my parents, or to other teachers,
Through the door of the afflictions, with body, speech,
And mind, whatever harm I have done, ^2-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-31]]

I am stained by many faults.
Whatever misdeeds I, one burdened with misdeeds,
Have done that are extremely unbearable,
I confess them all to the Guides. ^2-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-32]]

Without the purification of my misdeeds,
I may die before that happens.
So that there is definite liberation from this,
Please protect me, and do it quickly. ^2-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-33]]

This Lord of Death cannot be trusted.
He does not wait to see what has been done or not done.
Whether we are sick or healthy,
No one can trust this life, which can end suddenly. ^2-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-34]]

I must leave everything behind and go,
But I did not understand this.
For the sake of friends and enemies,
I have done many kinds of misdeeds. ^2-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-35]]

Enemies will cease to be,
And friends will cease to be.
I too will cease to be,
And in the same way everything will cease to be. ^2-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-36]]

Like the experiences of a dream,
Whatever things I have enjoyed
Will become only things I remember.
All that has passed will not be seen again. ^2-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-37]]

Even in this very life,
Many friends and enemies have passed away.
The unbearable misdeeds done for their sake
Still remain before me. ^2-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-38]]

Because I did not realize
That I am transient in this way,
Through ignorance, attachment, and hatred,
I have done many kinds of misdeeds. ^2-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-39]]

Without a pause by day or night,
This life is always getting shorter.
Since nothing is added from anywhere else,
Why would someone like me not die? ^2-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-40]]

While I lie on my bed,
Even if I am surrounded by all my relatives and friends,
The sensations of my life being cut off
Will be felt by me alone. ^2-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-41]]

When the messengers of Yama seize me,
What help are relatives, and what help are friends?
If at that time merit alone is a refuge,
I have not relied on even that. ^2-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-42]]

Protectors, through my heedlessness,
I did not know of such terrors as these.
For the sake of this impermanent life,
I have done many misdeeds. ^2-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-43]]

Even a person who is led today
To a place where their limbs will be cut off is terrified.
If their mouth is dry, their eyes are distressed, and so on,
And they look different from how they were before, ^2-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-44]]

Seized by the messengers of Yama
With their terrifying bodies,
And struck down by the disease of great terror,
What need is there to speak of their utter misery? ^2-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-45]]

"Who will protect me well
From this great terror?"
With eyes wide open in panic,
I will search the four directions for a refuge. ^2-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-46]]

When I see no refuge in the four directions,
I will then fall into utter despair.
If there is no refuge in that place,
What shall I do at that time? ^2-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-47]]

So I turn to the Victors, Protectors of wandering beings,
Who work hard to protect wandering beings,
The mighty ones who remove all fears.
From this very day, I take refuge in them. ^2-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-48]]

In the Dharma they have realized,
Which removes the fears of saṃsāra,
And in the assembly of bodhisattvas,
I also take refuge fully. ^2-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-49]]

I am terrified by fear.
I offer myself to Samantabhadra.
To Mañjuśrī, I myself
Shall offer my body. ^2-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-50]]

The conduct of the protector Avalokiteśvara never errs, through compassion.
To him as well
I cry out with an anguished voice:
"Please protect me, one possessing misdeeds!" ^2-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-51]]

To noble Ākāśagarbha,
To Kṣitigarbha,
And to all the Protectors with great compassion,
I cry out from my heart, seeking refuge. ^2-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-52]]

When the messengers of Yama
And other malicious beings see him,
They flee in terror in the four directions.
I take refuge in the Vajra-wielder. ^2-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-53]]

Before, I went against your commands.
Now I have seen great fear,
And I take refuge in you.
Please quickly remove my fears! ^2-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-54]]

Even when we are afraid of an ordinary illness,
We must act on the doctor's words.
When we are constantly struck by the disease
Of a hundred faults such as desire, what need is there to say more? ^2-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-55]]

If even just one of them can destroy
All the people living in Jambudvīpa,
And no other medicine to cure them
Can be found anywhere in any direction— ^2-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-56]]

Then, for the Omniscient Physician
Who draws out all pain,
The thought of not following his commands
Is extreme ignorance, and it deserves blame. ^2-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-57]]

Even at a small, ordinary precipice,
We must stay careful.
What need is there to speak of falling a thousand yojanas
Into a precipice that lasts a long time? ^2-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-58]]

Thinking, "I will not die this very day,"
It is not right to stay at ease.
The time when I will cease to be
Will surely come. ^2-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-59]]

Who has given me fearlessness?
How will I definitely escape from this?
If I will surely cease to exist,
How can my mind stay at ease? ^2-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-60]]

Of what I experienced before and has now passed away,
What is left for me?
Because I was so attached to it,
I went against the teacher's commands. ^2-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-61]]

If I must leave behind this lifetime, and also
My relatives and friends,
And go somewhere alone,
What use are all my friends and non-friends? ^2-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-62]]

Suffering comes from non-virtue.
"How will I definitely escape from that?"
Day and night, constantly, it is right for me
To think only about this. ^2-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-63]]

Because I did not know, and through ignorance,
Whether natural misdeeds or
Whatever proscribed misdeeds there are,
Whichever of these I have committed, ^2-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-64]]

Right in front of the Protectors,
With joined palms and a mind afraid of suffering,
I pay homage again and again,
And I confess all of these. ^2-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-65]]

I ask the Guides to take my misdeeds
As faults.
Since this is not good,
From now on I will never do it. ^2-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-a]]

The second chapter of Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, Confession of Misdeeds. ^2-a

## Chapter Three: Fully Adopting Bodhicitta ^3-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-1]]

In the virtue of all sentient beings
That gives rest from the suffering of the lower realms,
And in suffering beings living in happiness,
I rejoice with joy. ^3-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-2]]

In the virtue that has been gathered and becomes a cause of awakening,
In that I rejoice.
When embodied beings are freed from the suffering of saṃsāra,
In their definite liberation I rejoice.
In the awakening of the Saviours,
And in the levels of the children of the Victors too, I rejoice. ^3-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-3]]

Bringing happiness to all sentient beings,
In the ocean of virtue of the generation of bodhicitta,
And in the deeds that benefit sentient beings,
I rejoice with joy. ^3-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-4]]

To the Buddhas of every direction,
I join my palms and pray:
Sentient beings are lost in the darkness of suffering.
Please light the lamp of the Dharma for them. ^3-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-5]]

To the Victors who wish to pass into nirvāṇa,
I join my palms and pray:
Please do not leave these wandering beings in blindness.
Please stay for countless eons. ^3-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-6]]

I have done all of this.
Whatever virtue I have gathered by it,
Through that virtue, may all the suffering
Of all sentient beings be removed. ^3-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-7]]

For as long as wandering beings are sick,
Until they are cured of their illness,
May I become their medicine and their doctor,
And care for them as their nurse. ^3-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-8]]

May I rain down food and drink,
And take away the suffering of hunger and thirst.
In the intermediate eon of famine,
May I myself become food and drink. ^3-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-9]]

For sentient beings who have nothing and are poor,
May I become an inexhaustible treasure.
May I be there before them, close at hand,
As all the many things they need. ^3-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-10]]

My body and my possessions too,
And all the virtues of the three times,
To bring about the welfare of all sentient beings,
I shall give away without any sense of loss. ^3-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-11]]

By giving up everything, one reaches nirvāṇa,
And my mind aims to achieve nirvāṇa.
Since I must give up everything anyway,
It is best to give it to sentient beings. ^3-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-12]]

Since I have already given
This body to all embodied beings for their pleasure,
Let them do as they please with it,
Whether they keep killing and insulting it, or strike it and so on. ^3-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-13]]

Whether they play with my body
Or make it a target of ridicule and jokes,
I have already given this body away.
What use is it to worry about it? ^3-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-14]]

Whatever action does not harm them,
Let them do it, whatever it is.
When they focus on me, at any time,
May it never be meaningless for anyone. ^3-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-15]]

For those who focus on me,
If a mind of anger or faith arises in them,
May that very mind always become for them
The cause for achieving all their goals. ^3-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-16]]

Some people speak badly of me.
Others cause me harm.
Others also make fun of me.
May they all have the fortune of awakening. ^3-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-17]]

May I become a Protector for those who have no Protector.
May I be a guide for those who travel on the path.
For those who wish to cross over,
May I become a boat, a ship, and a bridge. ^3-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-18]]

May I be an island for those who look for an island.
May I be a resting place for those who want a place to rest.
For any embodied beings who want a servant,
May I become a servant to them all. ^3-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-19]]

May I become a wish-fulfilling jewel and an excellent vase.
May I become an accomplished knowledge-mantra and a great medicine.
May I become wish-granting trees,
And a cow of plenty for embodied beings. ^3-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-20]]

Just as the earth and the other great elements support all,
And as space is always there,
For immeasurable sentient beings,
May I also be the ground of many kinds of sustenance. ^3-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-21]]

In the same way, reaching to the limits of space,
For the realm of sentient beings, in every way,
Until they have all attained nirvāṇa,
May I also be the cause of their sustenance. ^3-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-22]]

In the same way that the Sugatas of former times
Generated bodhicitta,
And step by step
Lived by the trainings of the bodhisattvas, ^3-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-23]]

So too, for the benefit of wandering beings,
I will generate bodhicitta,
And in the same way, in the trainings,
I will train step by step. ^3-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-24]]

In this way, a wise person
Who has taken up bodhicitta with clear and calm faith
Should lift up their own mind like this,
So that it may grow even further: ^3-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-25]]

Today my life has borne fruit.
I have gained a human existence, and gained it well.
Today I am born into the family of the Buddhas.
Today I have become a child of the Buddhas. ^3-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-26]]

From now on, whatever it takes,
I must do the actions that fit this family,
So that this flawless, noble family
Is not stained. This is how I will act. ^3-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-27]]

Imagine a blind person
Finding a precious jewel in a heap of rubbish.
In the same way, by some sheer chance,
This bodhicitta has been born in me. ^3-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-28]]

This is the supreme nectar
That destroys the Lord of Death for wandering beings.
This is the inexhaustible treasure
That removes the poverty of wandering beings. ^3-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-29]]

This is the supreme medicine
That fully heals the illnesses of wandering beings.
This is the tree where wandering beings can rest,
Weary from roaming the path of existence. ^3-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-30]]

This is the universal bridge that frees
All wandering beings from the lower realms.
This is the rising moon of the mind
That soothes the torment of the afflictions of wandering beings. ^3-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-31]]

This is the great sun that clears away
The dimness of the unknowing of wandering beings.
This is the essence of butter
That comes from churning the milk of the sublime Dharma. ^3-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-32]]

Wandering beings are guests travelling the path of existence,
And they wish to enjoy happiness.
This is waiting close by for them as the supreme happiness,
Satisfying the great guests, the sentient beings. ^3-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-33]]

Today, in the presence of all the Saviours,
I have invited wandering beings as my guests, to Sugatahood itself,
And to happiness in the meantime.
May gods, asuras, and others rejoice! ^3-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-a]]

This is the third chapter of Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, called “Fully Taking Hold of Bodhicitta.” ^3-a

## Chapter Four: Teaching Heedfulness ^4-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-1]]

A child of the Victors, having in this way
Firmly taken hold of bodhicitta,
Should always, without lapsing,
Strive not to go against the trainings. ^4-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-2]]

Anything that is begun rashly,
Or that has not been well examined,
Even if I have made a promise about it,
It is right to examine it and decide whether to do it or let it go. ^4-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-3]]

The Buddhas and their children
Examined this with great wisdom,
And I myself have examined it again and again.
So why would I put it off? ^4-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-4]]

If I make this promise
And then do not carry it out through my actions,
I will have deceived all those sentient beings.
What kind of wandering being will I become? ^4-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-5]]

Take even a small, ordinary thing.
Suppose a person decides in their mind to give it,
But then does not give it.
If it is taught that such a person will become a hungry ghost, ^4-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-6]]

To unsurpassed happiness—
I have sincerely invited them there.
If I deceive all wandering beings,
How will I go to a happy realm? ^4-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-7]]

Even if someone gives up bodhicitta,
The fact that they still attain liberation
Is an inconceivable working of karma,
Known only to the Omniscient One. ^4-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-8]]

For a bodhisattva, this
Is the heaviest of all downfalls.
If it happens,
One falls short in working for the good of all sentient beings. ^4-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-9]]

If anyone else, even for a single moment,
Blocks the merit of this bodhisattva,
That bodhisattva then falls short in working for the good of sentient beings.
And that other person’s lower realms will have no end. ^4-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-10]]

Harming even the happiness of one sentient being
Would bring one’s own ruin.
The embodied beings are as limitless as all of space.
Need I say anything about destroying their happiness? ^4-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-11]]

So if powerful downfalls
And the powerful bodhicitta
Take turns in saṃsāra,
It will take a long time to reach the grounds. ^4-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-12]]

Therefore, as I have vowed,
I must carry it out with respect.
If I do not apply diligence starting today,
I will sink lower and lower. ^4-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-13]]

Countless Buddhas who benefited all sentient beings
Have passed away.
But because of my own faults,
I did not become someone they could heal. ^4-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-14]]

If I still act in this way,
The same will happen again and again:
In the lower realms I will go through illness and bondage,
Being cut, split open, and more. ^4-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-15]]

The coming of a Tathāgata,
Faith, and gaining a human body,
And the ability to cultivate virtue:
These are rare, so when will I gain them again? ^4-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-16]]

Today I am free of illness,
I have food, and no harm threatens me.
Still, life betrays us in an instant,
And the body is like something borrowed for a short time. ^4-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-17]]

With the way I behave,
I will not even gain a human body.
If I do not gain a human body,
There will be only misdeeds, and no virtue. ^4-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-18]]

Even when I have the good fortune to practice virtue,
If I do not practice virtue,
I will be completely bewildered by the suffering of the lower realms.
What will I be able to do then? ^4-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-19]]

If I do not practice virtue
And instead pile up misdeeds,
Then for a hundred ten-million eons
I will not even hear the word “happy realm.” ^4-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-20]]

For this very reason, the Blessed One said
That gaining the human state is extremely hard.
It is like a turtle’s neck slipping into the hole
Of a yoke drifting on the great ocean. ^4-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-21]]

If a misdeed done in a single instant
Can lead to an eon in the Avīci hell,
Then with the misdeeds I have piled up in beginningless saṃsāra,
Need I even say that I will not go to a happy realm? ^4-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-22]]

Even after going through that much,
One still will not attain liberation.
This is because, while going through it,
Other misdeeds are born in full force. ^4-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-23]]

I have found a leisure like this.
If I do not train myself in virtue,
There is no greater deception than this,
And no greater bewilderment than this. ^4-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-24]]

If I understand this,
But out of bewilderment I am still lazy later on,
Then at the time of my death,
Great sorrow will rise in me. ^4-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-25]]

Suppose the unbearable fire of hell
Burns my body for a long time.
The blazing fire of unbearable regret
Will certainly torment my mind. ^4-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-26]]

This state of benefit is extremely hard to find.
I have found it by some chance.
If I know all this myself,
And yet I am led back again into hell, ^4-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-27]]

It is as if a mantra had bewildered me.
I am left with no mind here.
I do not even know what bewilders me.
What is there inside me? ^4-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-28]]

Enemies such as hatred and craving
Have no hands, no feet, and so on.
They are neither brave nor wise. Yet how is it
That they have made me like a slave? ^4-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-29]]

The afflictions live within my mind
And harm me whenever they please.
To feel no anger even toward them, and to practice patience,
Is patience in the wrong place, something to be criticized. ^4-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-30]]

Even if the gods and asuras
All rose up against me as enemies,
Even they could not lead me
And throw me into the fire of the Avīci hell. ^4-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-31]]

This powerful enemy, the afflictions,
Throws me in a single instant
Into a place where, on contact,
Even Mount Meru would not remain as ashes. ^4-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-32]]

These enemies of mine, the afflictions,
Have lasted a very long time, as if with no beginning or end.
No other enemies
Can last as long as that. ^4-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-33]]

If we serve and honor others kindly,
They all bring us benefit and happiness.
But if we serve the afflictions,
They bring more suffering and harm. ^4-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-34]]

So these long-standing enemies, always with me,
The only cause of a great increase of many harms,
May truly make their home within my heart.
How could I be fearless and joyful in saṃsāra? ^4-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-35]]

These are the guards of the prison of saṃsāra,
Who become the executioners in hell and in other places.
If they stay in the net of attachment that lives in my mind,
How could I be happy? ^4-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-36]]

So, as long as I have not clearly
And surely destroyed this enemy, I will not give up my effort here.
For example, people swollen with pride
Get angry even at a small wrongdoer, and they cannot sleep until they have destroyed that enemy. ^4-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-37]]

There are wretched enemies who are made to suffer by natural death.
Some people want to destroy them by force when lined up in battle.
They ignore the suffering of being struck by arrows, spears, and weapons.
They do not turn back and run away before they reach their goal. ^4-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-38]]

The constant cause of all suffering
Is a natural enemy that I must surely destroy. Today I strive to do so.
Whatever becomes the cause of hundreds of sufferings,
Needless to say, it will not make me despair and lose heart. ^4-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-39]]

Enemies give scars for no meaningful purpose,
Yet people wear those scars on the body like ornaments.
I strive fully to accomplish a great purpose.
Why should suffering harm me? ^4-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-40]]

Fishermen, outcastes, farmers, and others
Think only of their own mere livelihood.
Yet they endure the harms of cold, heat, and so on.
Why should someone like me not endure them for the happiness of wandering beings? ^4-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-41]]

I promised to free from afflictions
The wandering beings who reach to the ends of space in the ten directions.
But when I made that promise,
I myself was not free from afflictions. ^4-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-42]]

I did not even know my own limits.
Isn’t it madness to speak like that?
So from now on, in destroying afflictions,
I will never turn back. ^4-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-43]]

I will cling to this task,
Hold a grudge, and go to battle.
Afflictions of this kind
are an exception, because they destroy afflictions. ^4-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-44]]

Even if I am burned and killed,
Or even if my head is cut off, so be it.
In no circumstance at all
Will I bow down to the enemies, the afflictions. ^4-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-45]]

Ordinary enemies may be driven out of a country.
They live in other lands and take hold there.
They build up their strength and come back from there.
But the enemies called afflictions do not work like that. ^4-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-46]]

Afflictions, wretched afflictions, are to be cast out by the eye of wisdom.
Once driven out of my mind, where will they go?
Where could they stay, to come back and harm me?
The only problem is that my mind is weak and I lack effort. ^4-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-47]]

Afflictions do not live in sense objects, or in the collection of faculties, or in between.
If they are not anywhere else either, where do they live, and how do they harm all wandering beings?
They are like an illusion. So put fear out of your heart, and rely on effort in order to know them.
Why do I pointlessly harm myself in hells and other places? ^4-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-48]]

Having thought about it in this way, I will make an effort
To carry out the training just as it has been explained.
If a patient does not listen to the doctor’s words,
How can anyone who needs to be treated with medicine be cured? ^4-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-a]]

This is the fourth chapter of Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, called Heedfulness. ^4-a

## Chapter Five: Guarding Introspection ^5-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-1]]

Those who wish to guard the training
Must guard the mind with great care.
If this mind is not guarded,
They will not be able to guard the training at all. ^5-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-2]]

Letting the elephant of the mind run loose
Causes harm in the Avīci hell.
An untamed, maddened elephant
Does not cause harm like that here. ^5-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-3]]

If the elephant of the mind is firmly bound
all around with the rope of mindfulness,
all fears will come to an end,
and all virtues will come into my hands. ^5-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-4]]

Tigers, lions, elephants, bears,
snakes, and all enemies,
hell guardians,
witches, and likewise rākṣasas— ^5-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-5]]

By binding this one mind,
all of them will be bound.
By taming this one mind,
all of them will be tamed. ^5-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-6]]

In this way, all fears
and all immeasurable sufferings
come from the mind.
The Speaker of Truth himself taught this. ^5-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-7]]

The weapons in the hell of sentient beings,
who made them on purpose?
Who made the burning iron ground?
From what did those masses of women come? ^5-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-8]]

The Sage taught that all such things
come from the mind of misdeed.
So in the three worlds,
there is nothing to fear other than the mind. ^5-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-9]]

If ending the poverty of wandering beings
Is the perfection of generosity,
Then, since wandering beings are still poor,
How did the Saviours of the past reach perfection? ^5-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-10]]

All possessions, together with their results,
Are given up to all people with a mind of relinquishing.
This is what is taught as the perfection of generosity.
So it is the mind itself. ^5-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-11]]

Where could I drive away fish and other creatures
So that they would not be killed?
When I gain the mind of renunciation,
That is explained as the perfection of ethical discipline. ^5-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-12]]

Sentient beings who will not be tamed are like space;
They can never be fully overcome.
But if I overcome this one mind of anger,
It is as if I had overcome all those enemies. ^5-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-13]]

Suppose I wanted to cover the whole surface of the earth with leather.
Where would I find enough leather?
But with just the leather for the soles of my shoes,
It is as if the whole surface of the earth were covered. ^5-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-14]]

In the same way, I cannot turn back
External entities completely either.
I will turn back this mind of mine.
What need is there to turn back others? ^5-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-15]]

A single clear mind brings a result,
As it did for Brahmā and others.
The result of actions of body and speech
Is not like that, because the conduct is weak. ^5-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-16]]

All recitations and austerities,
Even if practiced for a long time,
Are meaningless if done with a mind distracted elsewhere.
So said the Knower of Reality. ^5-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-17]]

Whoever does not know this supreme principal of the Dharma,
This secret of the mind,
May wish to find happiness and overcome suffering,
But they will wander in vain, without meaning. ^5-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-18]]

Therefore, this mind of mine
I will hold firmly and guard well.
Apart from the ascetic discipline of guarding the mind,
What use are many other ascetic disciplines? ^5-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-19]]

When one lives in the midst of an unruly crowd,
One carefully protects a wound.
In the same way, when living among bad people,
One should always guard this wound of the mind. ^5-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-20]]

Someone afraid of the small suffering of a wound
Takes care to protect the wound.
Why do those who fear the Crushing Hell and the Destroying hell
Not guard the wound of the mind? ^5-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-21]]

If a person lives by this kind of conduct,
whether they live among wicked people
or even among women,
the steadfast practitioner of restraint will not decline. ^5-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-22]]

It is better that I lose my gain and honor,
my body and my livelihood,
and better that my other virtues decline,
but I must never let my mind decline. ^5-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-23]]

To those who want to guard the mind, I say,
"Guard mindfulness and introspection
with all your effort!"
With these words, I join my palms together. ^5-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-24]]

People who are disturbed by illness
have no power to do anything.
In the same way, a mind disturbed by delusion
has no power to do anything. ^5-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-25]]

Someone whose mind lacks introspection
may hear, think over, and meditate on the teachings,
but like water in a leaking vase,
they do not stay in their mindfulness. ^5-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-26]]

Even many people who have learning and faith
and who are committed to diligence
become tainted by the stain of downfalls
because of the fault of lacking introspection. ^5-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-27]]

The thieves of lacking introspection
follow after the decline of mindfulness.
Even when merit has been gathered,
one goes to the lower realms, as if robbed by thieves. ^5-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-28]]

This gang of affliction thieves
looks for a chance to strike.
Once they find a chance, they steal virtue
and destroy even the life of a happy realm. ^5-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-29]]

So I should never let mindfulness leave
the gateway of the mind.
Even if it does leave, I should remember the harms of the lower realms
and firmly set it in place again. ^5-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-30]]

By keeping company with a teacher
and by following the instruction of a preceptor,
fortunate people who act with respect out of fear
find that mindfulness comes easily. ^5-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-31]]

The Buddhas and bodhisattvas
see everywhere without any obstruction.
"I am always living
in the presence of them all." ^5-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-32]]

Thinking in this way, with shame,
respect, and fear, one should keep this in mind.
And so the recollection of the Buddha
will also arise in one again and again. ^5-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-33]]

When mindfulness stays at the gateway of the mind
in order to guard it,
introspection will come at that time,
and even what has gone away will return. ^5-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-34]]

First, when I see that my mind is like this
and has faults,
then I should stay steady and still,
like a log of wood. ^5-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-35]]

I should never look around
in a distracted way, for no purpose.
With a firm mind, I should always
look with my eyes cast down. ^5-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-36]]

To rest my gaze,
I may sometimes look out in the different directions.
If someone comes into my sight,
I should look at them and say, "Welcome." ^5-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-37]]

To check for dangers on the road and the like,
I should look again and again in the four directions.
When I have rested, I should turn and look back,
and look at the area behind me. ^5-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-38]]

After checking what is in front and what is behind,
I should go on or else come back.
In the same way, in every situation,
I should understand what is needed and then act. ^5-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-39]]

Once I have begun an action with the thought,
"My body will stay like this,"
I should then look from time to time
and ask, "How is this body staying?" ^5-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-40]]

The mad elephant of the mind
is tied to the great pillar of reflecting on the Dharma.
So that it does not break free,
I should examine it with all my effort. ^5-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-41]]

So that my striving in concentration
is not lost even for a single moment,
I should examine my mind closely and ask,
"Where is this mind of mine engaged?" ^5-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-42]]

When I am caught up in dangers, festivities, and the like,
if I am not able to keep up my practice, I may act at ease.
For it is taught that at times of generosity,
ethical discipline may be treated with equanimity. ^5-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-43]]

Whatever I have decided on and begun to do,
I should not think of anything else.
With my thought fixed on it,
I should first accomplish that very thing. ^5-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-44]]

In this way, everything is done well.
Otherwise, neither task will be accomplished.
In this way, the secondary affliction
of lacking introspection will not grow. ^5-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-45]]

There are many kinds of idle chatter
and many kinds of amazing spectacles.
When I take part in any of them,
I should give up attachment to them. ^5-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-46]]

If I dig the earth or cut grass for no purpose,
or draw lines on the ground and the like,
I should remember the training of the Sugata
and, out of fear, stop at once. ^5-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-47]]

Whenever I want to move,
or even whenever I want to speak,
I should first examine my own mind,
and then act steadily and properly. ^5-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-48]]

Whenever my own mind feels attachment
or feels anger,
I should not act and should not speak.
I should stay still like a log of wood. ^5-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-49]]

If there is agitation or mockery,
or if I am full of pride and arrogance,
or if I have the intention of exposing others' faults,
or if there is duplicity or a deceptive mind, ^5-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-50]]

whenever I am set on self-praise,
or on disparagement of others,
or I become abusive and full of regret,
at that time I should stay still like a log of wood. ^5-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-51]]

If I want gain, honor, or fame,
or want to look for attendants and retinue,
or if my mind wants to be served,
at that time I should stay still like a log of wood. ^5-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-52]]

If a mind arises that gives up the welfare of others,
wants to pursue my own welfare,
or wants to speak,
then I should stay still like a log of wood. ^5-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-53]]

If impatience, laziness, and fear arise,
and likewise impudence and idle chatter,
or if a mind attached to my own side arises,
at that time I should stay still like a log of wood. ^5-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-54]]

In this way, I should examine my mind
for affliction and for striving after meaningless things.
Then, as a hero, I should use an antidote
to hold it firmly. ^5-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-55]]

With complete certainty and excellent faith,
be steady, respectful, and courteous,
with a sense of shame and with apprehension.
Be peaceful, and work hard to make others happy. ^5-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-56]]

Do not be discouraged by the childish ones,
whose desires clash with one another.
Instead think, "Because afflictions have arisen in them,
this mind of theirs has arisen," and be a compassionate one. ^5-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-57]]

Regarding blameless things,
which are governed by myself and sentient beings,
I should always hold this mind
like an emanation, without an "I". ^5-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-58]]

After a long time, I have found the supreme leisure.
I should think again and again about having gained it,
and hold such a mind
completely unshakable, like Mount Meru. ^5-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-59]]

Vultures crave flesh
and drag the body back and forth everywhere.
O mind, if you are not upset by this,
why do you care for it now? ^5-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-60]]

You have taken this body as "mine."
O mind, why do you guard it?
If you and this body are separate,
what can it do for you? ^5-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-61]]

Mind of delusion, why
do you not take hold of a clean wooden form?
This machine made of a collection of impurities,
what is the use of guarding this rotten thing? ^5-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-62]]

First, take this layer of skin
and separate it with your own intellect.
Then separate the flesh from the lattice of bones
with the weapon of wisdom. ^5-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-63]]

After you have separated even the bones,
look all the way down to the marrow,
and ask yourself,
"What essence is there in this?" ^5-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-64]]

If you search there with such effort
and see no essence,
why do you still guard this body
with attachment? ^5-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-65]]

Impurity is not fit for you to eat.
Blood, too, is not fit to drink.
Entrails are not fit to suck.
So what will the body do for you? ^5-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-66]]

At best, it is worth guarding
as food for foxes and vultures.
Every human body
is only something to be put to use. ^5-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-67]]

Even if you guard it in this way,
the merciless Lord of Death
will snatch it and give it to birds and dogs.
What can you do then? ^5-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-68]]

You would not give even clothes and such
to a servant who cannot be put to work.
This body leaves even when it is fed,
so why do you wear yourself out feeding it? ^5-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-69]]

Once you have paid this body its wages,
make it serve your own purpose now.
Where there is no benefit,
do not give it everything. ^5-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-70]]

See the body as a boat,
as a mere support for going and coming.
In order to accomplish the welfare of sentient beings,
transform it into a wish-fulfilling body. ^5-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-71]]

So take self-mastery,
and always keep a smiling face.
Give up frowning and scowling completely.
Be a friend to wandering beings and speak sincerely. ^5-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-72]]

Do not throw down chairs and the like
recklessly, with a loud noise.
Do not open doors forcefully either.
Always take delight in acting quietly. ^5-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-73]]

Herons, cats, and thieves
go silently and stealthily,
and so they achieve what they want.
The Sage should always act in that way. ^5-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-74]]

When people who are skilled at giving advice speak,
and when words that help are offered without being asked,
accept them respectfully on your crown.
Always become the disciple of everyone. ^5-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-75]]

To everything that is well spoken,
one should say, "Excellently spoken!"
If one sees someone creating merit,
one should bring forth deep joy by praising them. ^5-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-76]]

One should speak of a person's good qualities when they are not present,
and when others speak of good qualities, one should join in.
When one's own good qualities are spoken of,
one should understand this as the other person knowing good qualities. ^5-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-77]]

All undertakings are for the sake of joy,
and joy is rare even if one tries to buy it with a price.
So through the good qualities that others achieve,
I should enjoy the happiness of joy. ^5-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-78]]

In this life I lose nothing,
and in the next life I will have great happiness.
Through faults, there are unhappiness and suffering,
and in the next life there is great suffering. ^5-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-79]]

When one speaks, one's words should be comforting and fit together well,
clear in meaning and pleasing.
Having given up attachment and hatred,
one should speak gently and in moderation. ^5-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-80]]

Even when one looks at sentient beings with one's eyes,
one should think, "By relying on these very beings,
I will become a Buddha,"
and look at them in a direct and loving way. ^5-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-81]]

Whether I am moved by constant, strong, fixed aspiration
or moved by an antidote,
toward those who are fields of good qualities, those who are benefactors,
and those who are suffering, my virtue becomes great. ^5-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-82]]

Having become skilled and full of faith,
I shall always do my actions myself.
In all my actions,
I should not rely on anyone at all. ^5-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-83]]

One should practice the perfection of generosity and the others
in a way that becomes more excellent step by step.
One should not give up the greater for the sake of the lesser.
Above all, one should think of the welfare of others. ^5-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-84]]

Having understood this, one should always stay
diligent for the welfare of others.
The Compassionate One sees far ahead,
so he allows even forbidden acts for such a person. ^5-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-85]]

One should share food with those who have fallen into wretched states, with those who have no protector,
and with those who live in ascetic discipline.
One should eat only a moderate amount,
and give away everything except the three Dharma robes. ^5-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-86]]

This body practices the sublime Dharma,
so it should not be harmed for something trivial.
If one acts in this way, the wishes
of sentient beings will be fulfilled quickly. ^5-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-87]]

While the intention of compassion is not pure,
one should not give away this body.
Instead, one should give it only when,
in this life and in other lives, it becomes a cause for accomplishing a great purpose. ^5-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-88]]

One should not teach the Dharma to people who are disrespectful,
or to those who wear head-wraps when they are not ill,
or to those who hold parasols, staffs, or weapons,
or to those whose heads are covered. ^5-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-89]]

Do not teach the profound and vast teachings to people of lesser capacity,
and do not teach a woman when no man is present.
Toward the lesser and the supreme Dharmas,
always act with equal respect. ^5-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-90]]

Those who have become vessels for the vast Dharma
should not be turned toward the lesser Dharma.
Conduct should not be completely given up,
and they should not be deceived with sūtras and mantras. ^5-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-91]]

When tooth-sticks and saliva
are thrown away, they should be covered.
It is also frowned upon to pass urine and the like
into water or on land that people use. ^5-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-92]]

One should not eat with a full mouth,
making noise, or with the mouth wide open.
One should not sit with the legs stretched out,
or rub the hands together. ^5-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-93]]

In vehicles, on beds and seats, and in dwellings,
one should not be alone with another woman.
After observing and asking, one should avoid
everything that makes the world lose faith. ^5-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-94]]

One should not point with a finger.
Instead, with respect, use the whole
right hand,
and show even the way in this manner. ^5-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-95]]

One should not wave the hands about too much.
If one moves them a little, one should make a sound
and signal by snapping the fingers and the like.
Otherwise, one will lack restraint. ^5-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-96]]

Just as the Protector lay down when he passed into nirvāṇa,
one should lie down facing the direction one wishes.
With introspection, one should firmly set the mind
from the very beginning on the thought of getting up quickly. ^5-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-97]]

The conduct of the bodhisattva
is taught to be measureless.
Still, one should certainly practice
the conduct of purifying the mind, until one is able to practice it all. ^5-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-98]]

Three times by day and three times by night,
one should recite the Three Heaps Sūtra.
By relying on the Victors and on bodhicitta,
one should in this way pacify the remaining downfalls. ^5-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-99]]

Whether one acts under one's own power or another's,
in whatever situation and whatever one is doing,
whatever has been taught as a training,
in that situation one should make effort and train. ^5-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-100]]

There is nothing at all
that is not a training for the children of the Victors.
For the wise person who lives in this way,
there is nothing that does not become merit. ^5-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-101]]

Whether directly or indirectly,
do nothing other than what benefits sentient beings.
For the sake of sentient beings alone,
make a dedication of everything for awakening. ^5-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-102]]

A spiritual friend who is learned in the meaning of the Mahāyāna
and who keeps the supreme ascetic discipline of the bodhisattvas
should always be kept,
not given up even for the sake of one's life. ^5-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-103]]

From the liberation story of Śrīsaṃbhava,
one should train in how to rely on the teacher.
This and the other words the Buddha spoke
should be learned by reading the sūtras. ^5-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-104]]

The trainings appear in the sūtras,
so one should read the sūtras.
The Ākāśagarbha Sūtra
should be looked at first of all. ^5-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-105]]

What we must practice all the time
Is shown at length in that text.
So the Śikṣāsamuccaya too
Should certainly be studied again and again. ^5-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-106]]

Or, for the time being, study the short
Compendium of Sūtras.
And the two works composed by the noble Nāgārjuna
Should also be studied with effort. ^5-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-107]]

Whatever is not forbidden there,
That is what we should practice.
To protect the minds of the world,
One should look at the training and practice it well. ^5-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-108]]

Look at the states of body and mind,
Again and again.
That alone, in short,
Is the definition of guarding introspection. ^5-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-109]]

These things must be practiced with the body.
What can be accomplished by only speaking words?
Will sick people be helped
By only reading a medical book? ^5-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-a]]

This is the fifth chapter of Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, called Guarding Introspection. ^5-a

## Chapter Six: Teaching Patience ^6-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-1]]

Built up over a thousand eons,
There is generosity, offerings to the Sugatas, and so on.
Whatever good conduct there is, all of it
Can be destroyed by a single moment of anger. ^6-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-2]]

There is no misdeed like hatred.
There is no austerity like patience.
So, putting real effort into patience,
We should practice meditation in many ways. ^6-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-3]]

If someone holds on to a mind with the dart of hatred in it,
The mind does not know peace.
They find neither joy nor happiness.
Sleep does not come, and they become unsteady. ^6-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-4]]

Those who have been favored with wealth and honor
And who depend on him
Will even attack to kill
That master who is possessed by hatred. ^6-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-5]]

Because of it, his friends become disheartened.
Even if he gathers them with generosity, they do not stay with him.
In short, someone who has anger and lives in happiness—
There is no such person at all. ^6-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-6]]

The enemy, anger, brings about
these and other such sufferings.
Whoever works hard to destroy anger
is happy in this life and in the next. ^6-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-7]]

When I do what is not wanted
or something blocks what I want, mental unhappiness arises.
Having found this food of mental unhappiness,
hatred grows strong and destroys me. ^6-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-8]]

So I will completely destroy
the food of that enemy,
because this enemy has no task
other than harming me. ^6-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-9]]

Whatever happens,
I will not disturb my joyful mind.
Even if I become unhappy, what I want is not achieved,
and my virtues will decline. ^6-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-10]]

If something can be remedied,
why be unhappy about it?
If it cannot be remedied,
what is the use of being unhappy about it? ^6-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-11]]

I do not want, for myself or for my friends,
suffering, contempt, harsh words
or disrepute,
because for an enemy it is the reverse. ^6-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-12]]

The causes of happiness occur only now and then.
The causes of suffering are very many.
Without suffering, there is no renunciation.
So, my mind, resolve to be steadfast. ^6-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-13]]

Devotees of Durgā and the Karṇapas
Endure the feelings of burning, cutting, and so on
For nothing. For the sake of liberation,
Why then am I a coward? ^6-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-14]]

There is nothing whatsoever
That does not become easy through habit.
So by getting used to minor harms,
Learn to endure great harms. ^6-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-15]]

Snakes and gadflies,
The feelings of hunger, thirst, and so on,
And afflictions such as rashes:
Why do you not see their pointless suffering? ^6-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-16]]

Toward heat, cold, rain, wind, and so on,
Sickness, bondage, beatings, and so on,
I will not be overly sensitive.
If I am, the harm increases. ^6-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-17]]

Some people, when they see their own blood,
Become exceptionally brave and steadfast.
Some, when they see the blood of others,
Become dazed and faint. ^6-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-18]]

This comes from the steadiness of the mind
And from the nature of a coward.
So disregard harm
And make yourself unaffected by sufferings. ^6-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-19]]

Even when suffering arises, the wise
Should not cloud the clarity of the mind.
For in waging war against the afflictions,
There is much harm during the battle. ^6-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-20]]

Having disregarded all suffering,
Those who conquer enemies such as hatred
Are the victorious heroes.
The others only slay corpses. ^6-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-21]]

Furthermore, the good quality of suffering is this:
Disillusionment dispels haughtiness,
Compassion arises for those in saṃsāra,
One shuns misdeeds and delights in virtue. ^6-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-22]]

People are not angry at bile and the like,
Which are great sources of suffering.
So why be angry at beings with a mind?
They too are all pushed along by conditions. ^6-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-23]]

For example, even though we do not wish for it,
This sickness arises.
In the same way, even though we do not wish for it,
The afflictions arise by force. ^6-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-24]]

Without thinking, “I will become angry,”
People become angry at the slightest condition.
Without thinking, “I will produce it,”
Anger arises in the same way. ^6-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-25]]

All faults, however many there are,
And various kinds of misdeeds,
All arise through the power of conditions.
They have no independence. ^6-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-26]]

Even those assembled conditions
Have no intention, “I will produce this.”
And what is produced by them
Has no intention, “I am being produced.” ^6-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-27]]

Whatever is asserted as “Pradhāna,”
And whatever is mentally assigned as the “self,”
Do not arise by purposefully thinking,
“I will come into being.” ^6-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-28]]

If it does not exist before it arises,
Then at that time, what is there that wishes to arise?
Since it would be constantly distracted by sense objects,
It would never cease either. ^6-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-29]]

If that self is permanent,
It is plainly inactive, like space.
Even if it meets other conditions,
What can be done to something that does not change? ^6-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-30]]

If, even while acting, it remains as it was before,
What has the action done to it?
What connection could there be
By which one could say, “This is its action”? ^6-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-31]]

Thus everything is under the power of others,
And by that power it has no power of its own.
Knowing this, one will not become angry
At any entities, which are like emanations. ^6-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-32]]

Someone might object, “By what is anything to be prevented?
Even preventing is unreasonable!”
But depending on that, it is not unreasonable
To wish for sufferings to be cut off. ^6-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-33]]

Therefore, whether it is an enemy or a friend,
If you see them doing something improper,
Think, “This has arisen from such conditions.”
Reflect in this way and remain at ease. ^6-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-34]]

If things were accomplished by their own will,
Then, since no one desires suffering,
For all embodied beings,
Suffering would not arise for anyone. ^6-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-35]]

Through heedlessness, people even harm themselves
With thorns and the like.
In order to obtain women and the like,
They become obsessed and engage in fasting and the like. ^6-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-36]]

Some hang themselves or leap from cliffs.
They eat poison and unwholesome food,
And through non-meritorious conduct
They bring harm upon themselves. ^6-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-37]]

When, under the power of afflictions,
They even kill their own beloved selves,
At that time, how could they not cause harm
To the bodies of others? ^6-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-38]]

For those who, because afflictions have arisen,
Do such things as kill themselves,
If compassion does not even arise for them,
What sense is there in anger? ^6-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-39]]

If causing harm to others
Is the nature of childish ones,
It is not right to feel anger toward them,
Just as it is not right to resent fire for its nature to burn. ^6-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-40]]

Or suppose this fault is adventitious,
And the nature of sentient beings is gentle.
Even then it is not right to feel anger,
Like resenting the sky when smoke rises within it. ^6-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-41]]

Suppose someone uses sticks and the like as the actual instruments,
And you feel anger at the one who throws them.
Since he too is driven by hatred,
Then, in the second place, it is fitting to feel anger at hatred. ^6-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-42]]

In the past, I inflicted harm like this
On sentient beings.
So it is fitting that this harm should arise for me,
A harmer of sentient beings. ^6-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-43]]

His weapon and my body
Are both causes of suffering.
Since he drew the weapon and I took up the body,
At whom should I direct my anger? ^6-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-44]]

Like an abscess in human shape,
Full of suffering, unable to bear being touched,
I, blinded by craving, have grasped it.
When it is harmed, at whom should I direct my anger? ^6-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-45]]

Childish ones do not desire suffering,
Yet they crave the causes of suffering.
When they are harmed by their own faults,
Why should they resent others? ^6-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-46]]

For example, take the hell guardians
And the forest of sword-leaves.
Since this is produced by my own karma,
At whom should I direct my anger? ^6-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-47]]

Impelled by my own karma,
Those who harm me appear.
If because of this those beings go to hell,
Have I not ruined them? ^6-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-48]]

By relying on them,
Through patience my many misdeeds are purified.
But by relying on me, they
Go to the hells of long-lasting suffering. ^6-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-49]]

I am the one who harms them,
While they are the ones who benefit me.
Why, out of perversity,
Do you give rise to anger, you wicked mind? ^6-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-50]]

If I have the good quality of intention,
I will not go to hell.
If I protect myself,
What will happen to them here? ^6-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-51]]

But if I cause harm in return,
They will not be protected,
And my conduct will also decline.
So my austerity will be destroyed. ^6-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-52]]

Because the mind is not an embodied being,
No one can destroy it anywhere.
Because of fixation on the body,
The body is harmed by sufferings. ^6-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-53]]

Contempt and harsh speech,
And those unpleasant words—
Since they do not harm the body,
Mind, why do you react with such intense anger? ^6-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-54]]

Other people's displeasure with me
will not devour me
in this life or in any other life.
So why do I dislike it? ^6-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-55]]

I dislike it
because it is an obstacle to my gain.
But my gain will be left behind here,
while my misdeeds will stay firmly with me. ^6-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-56]]

It is better that I die this very day
than live a long time by wrong livelihood.
Even if I stay alive for a long time,
the suffering of death is exactly the same. ^6-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-57]]

One person wakes up
after enjoying happiness in a dream for a hundred years.
Another person wakes up
after enjoying happiness for just a moment. ^6-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-58]]

For both of them, once they wake,
that happiness does not come back.
In the same way, whether a life is long or short,
at the time of death it is exactly the same. ^6-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-59]]

Even if I gain great wealth
and enjoy happiness for a long time,
I will leave naked and empty-handed,
as if a thief had robbed me. ^6-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-60]]

Suppose I say, "Living on gain
will use up my misdeeds and create merit."
But if I feel anger for the sake of gain,
will my merit not be used up and misdeeds arise? ^6-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-61]]

I live for a certain purpose.
If that very purpose is destroyed,
what is the use of a life
that does nothing but commit misdeeds? ^6-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-62]]

Suppose you say, "I feel anger at people who speak unpleasantly of me
because it ruins sentient beings."
Then why do you not feel anger in the same way
at those who speak unpleasantly about others? ^6-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-63]]

A lack of faith depends on other things,
and you have patience with people who have no faith.
Speaking unpleasantly depends on the arising of afflictions,
so why do you not have patience with those who speak unpleasantly? ^6-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-64]]

Some people disparage and destroy images,
stūpas and the sublime Dharma.
It is not right for me to feel hatred toward them,
because the Buddhas and others cannot be harmed. ^6-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-65]]

Some people harm my teachers, my relatives
and others, and even my friends.
As before, I should see that this arises from conditions,
and I should turn my anger back. ^6-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-66]]

Both beings with a mind and the insentient
cause harm to embodied beings.
So why blame beings with a mind alone?
Therefore, practice patience toward harm. ^6-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-67]]

Some people commit misdeeds out of delusion.
Others, also out of delusion, react with anger.
Which of them is without fault,
and which is the one at fault? ^6-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-68]]

Why did I earlier commit the karma
by which others now cause harm?
If everything depends on karma,
why should I feel resentment toward them? ^6-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-69]]

Seeing this, I will do all I can
so that everyone has a loving mind for one another.
To bring this about,
I will put my effort into merit. ^6-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-70]]

For example, a house is on fire,
and the fire moves to another house.
Whatever straw or the like it catches on
is pulled out and thrown away. ^6-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-71]]

Likewise, whatever the mind has attachment to,
if the fire of hatred has spread in it,
out of fear that merit will be burned,
it must be thrown away at once. ^6-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-72]]

A person is about to be killed
but has a hand cut off and is set free. Why is that not good?
If, through human suffering,
one is freed from hell, why is that not good? ^6-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-73]]

If I cannot even endure
this much suffering now,
why do I not turn back anger,
the cause of suffering in hell? ^6-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-74]]

For the sake of sensual desire,
I have gone through burning and the like in hell, thousands of times.
Yet I have accomplished neither my own welfare
nor the welfare of others. ^6-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-75]]

This is not so harmful,
and great purposes will also be accomplished.
In suffering that removes the harm of wandering beings,
it is right to feel only joy here. ^6-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-76]]

If others praise those who have good qualities
and so find the happiness of joy,
then, O mind, why do you not also praise them
and rejoice in the same way? ^6-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-77]]

This happiness that comes from your rejoicing
is a source of happiness without blame.
It is permitted by ones endowed with good qualities
and is also the best way to gather others. ^6-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-78]]

Moreover, suppose you think, "In that way the other will be happy,"
and so you do not want this happiness for him.
By giving up the paying of wages and the like,
the seen and unseen will be ruined. ^6-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-79]]

When your own good qualities are spoken of,
you want others to have happiness too.
When the good qualities of others are spoken of,
you do not want happiness even for yourself. ^6-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-80]]

You desire happiness for all sentient beings,
and for awakening you have undertaken the generation of bodhicitta.
If sentient beings find happiness by themselves,
why do you become angry at that? ^6-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-81]]

You desire for sentient beings
awakening as a Buddha, worshipped by the three worlds.
Yet when you see them receive even meager honor and respect,
why are you tormented by it? ^6-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-82]]

Those dependents whom you nurture,
to whom you yourself must give,
if those relatives find a livelihood,
do you become angry instead of being glad? ^6-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-83]]

If you do not want this for wandering beings,
who is it that wants awakening for them?
In someone who becomes angry at another's prosperity,
where is bodhicitta? ^6-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-84]]

Whether he receives it from him,
or it stays in the benefactor's house,
you do not have it in either case.
So what does it matter whether it is given or not? ^6-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-85]]

Why do you throw away merit and faith,
or your own good qualities?
You do not take hold of the gains that occur.
Tell me, why do you not become angry? ^6-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-86]]

You yourself have committed misdeeds,
and not only do you feel no sorrow,
but do you also wish to compete
with others who have performed merit?

The Second Fascicle. ^6-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-87]]

Even if an enemy is unhappy,
what is there for you to rejoice in?
Your mere wishing
will not become a cause of harm to him. ^6-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-88]]

Even if your desire makes that suffering happen,
what is there for you to rejoice in?
If you say, "I will be satisfied,"
what is more ruinous than that? ^6-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-89]]

The fisherman of the afflictions has cast
this hook, an unbearable barb.
Caught by it, I will certainly be boiled
in the cauldrons of hell by the hell guardians. ^6-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-90]]

The honors of praise and fame
do not become merit or long life.
They do not become my strength, and they are not health,
and they do not even make the body comfortable. ^6-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-91]]

If I were to know my own welfare,
what welfare of mine is there in that?
If I desire only mental happiness,
I must also rely on gambling, alcohol and the like. ^6-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-92]]

If, for the sake of fame, I squander my wealth
and even get myself killed,
what is the use of mere words?
Once I am dead, who will be made happy by them? ^6-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-93]]

When a sandcastle collapses,
children weep in despair.
In the same way, when praise and fame decline,
my own mind is like a child's. ^6-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-94]]

First of all, a sound has no mind,
so it cannot possibly intend to praise me.
As for the saying, "Others are pleased with me,"
perhaps that is the cause of joy? ^6-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-95]]

Whether it is directed at another or even at myself,
how does the joy of another benefit me?
That joy and happiness belong to that person alone;
I get no share of it. ^6-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-96]]

If I am happy because they are happy,
then I must do the same toward everyone.
How is it that, when they become happy
by rejoicing in others, I am not happy? ^6-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-97]]

So the joy that arises in me
when I think, "I am praised!"
is not justified either.
It is nothing but the behavior of a child. ^6-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-98]]

Praise and the like distract me,
and they destroy my disillusionment too.
They cause envy toward those with good qualities,
and they also destroy excellence. ^6-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-99]]

So those who are there
to destroy my praise and the like—
are they not working to protect me
from falling into the lower realms? ^6-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-100]]

I seek release,
so I do not need the bonds of gain and honor.
Those who release me from being bound—
how could I be angry with them? ^6-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-101]]

When I wish to enter into suffering,
they are like a blessing of the Buddha:
they become the very door that does not let me enter.
How could I be angry with them? ^6-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-102]]

Thinking, “This is an obstacle to merit,”
It is not right to be angry even at them.
If there is no austerity equal to patience,
Should I not stay with it? ^6-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-103]]

If, because of my own faults,
I do not practice patience toward this,
Then, when the cause of merit is right here,
I am only creating an obstacle to it. ^6-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-104]]

If one thing does not happen without another,
And does happen whenever that other is present,
Then that other is its very cause.
How can it be called an obstacle? ^6-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-105]]

A beggar who arrives at the right time
Does not make an obstacle to generosity.
Someone who grants ordination
Cannot be called an obstacle to ordination. ^6-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-106]]

In the world, beggars are many,
But people who cause harm are few.
For if I do not cause harm to others,
No one will cause harm to me. ^6-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-107]]

Therefore, like a treasure that appears in my house,
One I did not have to work hard to get,
My enemy becomes a helper in the conduct of awakening.
So I should delight in him. ^6-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-108]]

Since this is accomplished by both them and me,
The result of patience
Should first be offered to them,
Because they are the cause of patience. ^6-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-109]]

Suppose you say that this enemy has no intention of bringing about patience,
So he is not to be venerated.
Then why do you venerate even the sublime Dharma,
Which is a fit cause for accomplishment? ^6-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-110]]

Suppose you say that this enemy intends
To harm me, so he is not to be venerated.
But if he strove to benefit me like a physician,
How could my patience be accomplished? ^6-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-111]]

So patience arises,
Depending on a very hateful mind.
Since such a person is the very cause of patience,
He is worthy of veneration, like the sublime Dharma. ^6-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-112]]

Therefore the Sage has proclaimed
A field of sentient beings and a field of the Victors.
By pleasing them, many
Have in this way reached the perfection of excellence. ^6-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-113]]

From sentient beings and from the Victors,
The qualities of a Buddha are accomplished in the same way.
Just as we respect the Victors,
Why not respect sentient beings too? ^6-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-114]]

It is not through the good quality of intention itself,
But through the result, that they are similar.
Sentient beings too have good qualities,
And so they are equal. ^6-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-115]]

Whatever offering is made to someone with a loving mind
Is the very greatness of sentient beings.
Whatever merit comes from faith in the Buddhas
Is also the very greatness of the Buddhas. ^6-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-116]]

Because they have a share in accomplishing the qualities of a Buddha,
They are said to be equal.
But no one at all is equal
To the Buddhas, who have an infinite ocean of good qualities. ^6-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-117]]

Of the unique collection of supreme good qualities,
If merely a small fraction of those good qualities
Appears in someone, then to make offerings to them,
Even offering the three realms would be too little. ^6-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-118]]

A small share of the arising of the supreme qualities of a Buddha
Is present in sentient beings.
So, in proportion to just this share,
It is fitting that sentient beings be objects of offering. ^6-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-119]]

Furthermore, there are those who become friends without deceit
And who do immeasurable good.
Other than pleasing sentient beings,
By what else could they be repaid? ^6-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-120]]

There are beings for whose sake they give up their bodies and enter the Avīci hell.
Benefiting them becomes a repayment.
So even if these beings cause great harm,
I should act toward all of them with complete goodness. ^6-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-121]]

Even my own lords
Do not care even for their own bodies for the sake of these beings.
So how could a fool like me
Act with pride toward them and not act as their servant? ^6-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-122]]

There are beings whose happiness pleases the Sages,
And whose harm brings the Sages displeasure.
By bringing joy to them, I please all the Sages,
And by harming them, I harm the Sages. ^6-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-123]]

Just as when one's body is completely on fire,
No desirable things can make the mind happy,
In the same way, when harm is done to sentient beings,
There is no way to please those with great compassion. ^6-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-124]]

Therefore, whatever harm I have done to wandering beings,
Which has displeased all those with great compassion,
That misdeed I confess one by one today.
I pray that the Sages forgive whatever displeasure I have caused. ^6-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-125]]

In order to please the Tathāgatas,
From today on I will be a servant to the world.
Even if many wandering beings strike my head with their feet
Or kill me, I will not retaliate. May the Protectors of the World be pleased! ^6-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-126]]

Those whose very nature is compassion
Have made all these wandering beings into themselves; of this there is no doubt.
These very ones, seen in the form of sentient beings,
Are those Protectors themselves. Why do I not respect them? ^6-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-127]]

This alone is what pleases the Tathāgatas.
This alone fully accomplishes my own welfare.
This alone is also what removes the suffering of the world.
So I will always practice this alone. ^6-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-128]]

For example, suppose one of a king's men
Causes harm to many people.
Farsighted people,
Even if they are able to, do not harm him in return. ^6-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-129]]

This is because he is not alone;
The might of the king is his might.
In the same way, a weak person who does harm
Should not be looked down on at all. ^6-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-130]]

This is because the hell guardians
And the compassionate ones are his forces.
So, just as subjects please a fierce king,
I should please sentient beings. ^6-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-131]]

Even if a king were to become angry,
Consider what will be experienced
By displeasing sentient beings.
Could he inflict that harm of hell? ^6-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-132]]

Even if a king were to be pleased,
He could not possibly give Buddhahood itself,
Which is what is attained
By pleasing sentient beings. ^6-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-133]]

Never mind the future accomplishment of a Buddha,
Which arises from pleasing sentient beings.
Why do you not see that in this very life
Great glory, fame, and happiness will come? ^6-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-134]]

While in saṃsāra, through patience one gains beauty and the like,
Good health, and renown,
An extremely long life,
And the vast happiness of a universal monarch. ^6-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-a]]

This is the sixth chapter of Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, called “Patience.” ^6-a

## Chapter Seven: Teaching Diligence ^7-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-1]]

So, having patience, we should take up diligence,
Because awakening rests in such striving.
Just as nothing moves without wind,
Merit does not arise without diligence. ^7-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-2]]

What is diligence? It is delight in virtue.
Its opposing factors will now be explained:
laziness, attachment to what is base, and
despondency, which is self-contempt. ^7-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-3]]

Through tasting the pleasure of idleness
and through the craving that relies on sleep,
and from feeling no weariness toward the suffering of saṃsāra,
laziness comes to arise. ^7-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-4]]

Snared by the fowler of the afflictions,
caught in the net of birth,
and gone into the mouth of the Lord of Death—
how is it that you still do not know? ^7-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-5]]

Your own group is being killed one by one.
Have you not seen this?
Yet you who rely on sleep
are like a buffalo beside its outcaste butcher. ^7-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-6]]

With the paths completely blocked
and the Lord of Death watching you,
how can you take joy in eating,
and how can you take joy in sleeping like this? ^7-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-7]]

Because you will die very quickly,
you should gather the accumulations until then.
Even if you give up laziness at that time,
what can you do when it is not the right time? ^7-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-8]]

"This is not done, this has only just begun,
and this is only half done" —
when the Lord of Death suddenly arrives,
I will think, "Alas, I am ruined!" ^7-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-9]]

The eyes of my relatives will be red and swollen with grief,
with tears streaming down their faces.
They will have lost all hope,
and I will gaze upon the faces of the messengers of Yama. ^7-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-10]]

Tormented by the memory of my own misdeeds
and hearing the sounds of hell,
I will soil my body with filth in terror.
When I become delirious, what will I do? ^7-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-11]]

If you, like a live fish writhing about,
are gripped by fear in this life,
and you have committed misdeeds, what need is there to mention
the unbearable sufferings of hell? ^7-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-12]]

When touched by hot water,
you with your tender flesh are badly scorched.
Having created the karma of hell,
why do you stay at ease like this? ^7-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-13]]

You want the result without any effort.
You are fragile and open to many harms.
Death has seized you, yet you act like a god.
Alas, you are destroyed by sufferings! ^7-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-14]]

Rely on the boat of a human body
and cross over the great river of suffering!
This boat will be hard to find later,
so, O fool, do not sleep at this time! ^7-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-15]]

You have abandoned the supreme joy of the sublime Dharma,
which is a boundless cause of joy.
Why do you delight in distraction by the causes of suffering,
and in agitation and the like? ^7-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-16]]

Without despondency, with a host of forces,
with earnest application and self-mastery,
practice the equality of self and others
and the exchange of self and others! ^7-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-17]]

Do not fall into despondency
thinking, "How could I attain awakening?"
For in this way the Tathāgata, the Speaker of Truth,
has spoken this truth: ^7-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-18]]

Flies, gadflies and bees,
and likewise anything that has become a worm—
even they, if they generate the power of diligence,
will attain unsurpassable awakening, which is hard to attain. ^7-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-19]]

Someone like me has been born as a human
and recognizes what is beneficial and what is harmful.
If I do not abandon the conduct of awakening,
why should I not attain awakening? ^7-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-20]]

If I think, "But I must give away my arms, legs
and so forth, so I am afraid,"
I have not analyzed what is heavy and what is light.
I am afraid merely because of delusion. ^7-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-21]]

For countless tens of millions of eons
I will be cut many times over,
pierced, burned and split apart,
yet I will not attain awakening. ^7-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-22]]

The suffering of my accomplishing awakening
has a limit.
It is just like the suffering of cutting open the body
to remove the harm of a splinter destroying it from within. ^7-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-23]]

Even physicians, all of them, use medical treatment
to cure illness by means of discomfort.
Therefore, to destroy many sufferings,
I should endure a minor discomfort. ^7-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-24]]

The Supreme Physician does not use
ordinary medical treatments like these.
By an extremely gentle method
he cures immeasurable great diseases. ^7-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-25]]

The Guide first has one practice generosity
even with vegetables and the like.
Having become accustomed to that, later on,
one gradually gives away even one's own flesh. ^7-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-26]]

When, toward one's own body,
an attitude arises that it is like vegetables and the like,
at that time, in giving away flesh and so on,
what difficulty is there in that? ^7-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-27]]

Because misdeeds have been abandoned, there is no suffering.
Because one is a wise person, there is no unhappiness.
In this way, wrong conceptions
and misdeeds harm the mind and body. ^7-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-28]]

If the body is at ease through merit
and the mind is at ease through being a wise person,
then even while remaining in saṃsāra for the welfare of others,
why would the compassionate ones become weary? ^7-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-29]]

Through the power of bodhicitta, this bodhisattva
exhausts previous misdeeds
and gathers an ocean of merit.
For this reason he is said to be superior to the Śrāvakas. ^7-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-30]]

Therefore, having mounted the horse of bodhicitta,
which dispels all weariness and fatigue,
and going from happiness to happiness,
what intelligent person would fall into despondency? ^7-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-31]]

To accomplish the welfare of sentient beings, the forces
are aspiration, steadfastness, joy and relinquishment.
Aspiration is generated through fear of suffering
and by contemplating the benefits of that aspiration. ^7-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-32]]

Thus, having abandoned the opposing factors,
through aspiration, pride, joy and relinquishment,
and the powers of earnest application and mastery,
I should strive to increase diligence. ^7-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-33]]

The faults of myself and others
are immeasurable, and I must destroy them.
For each and every one of these faults
an ocean of eons would be exhausted. ^7-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-34]]

If I do not see in myself
even a fraction of the effort to exhaust those faults,
though I am an abode of immeasurable suffering,
why does my heart not burst? ^7-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-35]]

The good qualities of myself and others
are many, and I must accomplish them.
If for each and every good quality
I must familiarize myself for an ocean of eons, ^7-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-36]]

For even a fraction of a good quality
I have never generated familiarization.
Having somehow found this birth,
it is astonishing that I have made it meaningless. ^7-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-37]]

I have not made offerings to the Blessed One
or provided the happiness of great festivals.
I have not performed service for the teaching
or fulfilled the wishes of the poor. ^7-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-38]]

If I have not given fearlessness to the frightened
and have not given happiness to the distressed,
I have merely produced the pangs
and suffering of my mother's womb. ^7-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-39]]

Because I have been devoid of aspiration for the Dharma,
both before and even now,
such destitution has come about.
Who would forsake aspiration for the Dharma? ^7-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-40]]

The Sage has stated that aspiration
is the root of all virtuous factors,
and the root of aspiration is always
meditating on the ripened result. ^7-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-41]]

Suffering and mental unhappiness,
various kinds of fears,
and separation from what is desired
will arise from engaging in misdeeds. ^7-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-42]]

By performing virtue that one intends in the mind,
wherever one may go,
there, through that merit,
one will be openly honored with the offerings of its results. ^7-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-43]]

Although those who commit misdeeds desire happiness,
wherever they may go,
there, through those misdeeds,
they are completely destroyed by the weapons of suffering. ^7-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-44]]

One lives in the core of a vast, fragrant, cool lotus.
One's splendor is nourished by the food of the Victor's sweet words.
One has a supreme body, born from a lotus opened by the Sage's light.
Through virtue one becomes a son of the Sugata, living before the Victor. ^7-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-45]]

Yama's henchmen skin one completely, and one is utterly tormented.
Molten copper, melted by intense fire, is poured onto one's body.
Blazing swords and short spears pierce one, and one's flesh is scattered into a hundred pieces.
Through many non-virtues one falls onto the iron ground, which blazes fiercely. ^7-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-46]]

So one should aspire to virtue
and cultivate it with reverence.
By the method of the Vajradhvaja,
once one has begun, one should cultivate pride. ^7-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-47]]

First, one should examine one's resources.
Then one should either begin or not begin.
It is best not to begin at all,
but once one has begun, one should not turn back. ^7-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-48]]

If one grows used to that in another life,
misdeeds and suffering will increase.
Another deed, and the time of its result,
will become worse, and that other deed too will not be accomplished. ^7-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-49]]

Karma, afflictions, and capacity:
One should cultivate pride in these three.
Thinking, "I shall do it alone,"
this is pride in action. ^7-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-50]]

This world is powerless because of the afflictions
and cannot accomplish its own welfare.
Wandering beings are not as capable as I am,
so I shall do this. ^7-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-51]]

If others perform inferior actions,
how can I myself sit idle?
I do not do this out of pride;
for me, to be without pride is best. ^7-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-52]]

When a crow finds a dead snake,
even it acts like a garuḍa.
If I myself am weak,
even a minor downfall will do me harm. ^7-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-53]]

For someone who is despondent and gives up effort,
is there any liberation from destitution?
By generating pride and effort,
one becomes hard to overcome, even by great downfalls. ^7-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-54]]

So, with a steadfast mind,
downfalls must be conquered.
If a downfall defeats me,
my desire to conquer the three realms becomes a laughingstock. ^7-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-55]]

I shall conquer all,
and no one shall conquer me.
I am a child of the Victor Lion,
and I shall abide in this pride. ^7-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-56]]

Any being overcome by pride
is under the sway of affliction; that is not one who possesses pride.
One who possesses pride does not fall under the power of the enemy.
Those beings fall under the power of the enemy that is pride. ^7-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-57]]

Those puffed up by the affliction of pride
are led by pride to the lower realms.
Their human joy is destroyed,
and they become slaves who eat the food of others, ^7-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-58]]

foolish, ugly, and weak,
and they are despised everywhere.
If ascetics inflated with pride
are also counted among the proud,
tell me, what must the lowly be like? ^7-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-59]]

Whoever holds pride in order to conquer the enemy that is pride
is both the proud one and the victorious hero.
Whoever surely destroys even the thriving enemy that is pride
perfects the result of victory for wandering beings, as they wish. ^7-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-60]]

If I dwell in the midst of the faction of the afflictions,
I shall endure in a thousand ways.
Like a lion among foxes and the like,
I shall not be harmed by the host of afflictions. ^7-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-61]]

Even if great peril arises,
just as a person protects their eyes,
in the same way, even if peril arises,
I shall not fall under the power of the afflictions. ^7-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-62]]

Just as one longs for the happiness that comes from play,
whatever action this one has to do,
one should be devoted to that action
and, never getting enough of it, find joy in it. ^7-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-63]]

Even if one acts for the sake of happiness,
it is uncertain whether happiness will result.
But for one whose very action is happiness,
how can there be happiness without doing that action? ^7-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-64]]

If one is never satisfied by sensual desires,
which are like honey smeared on a razor's edge,
then by merit, whose ripening is happiness and which brings peace,
how could one ever be satisfied? ^7-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-65]]

So, in order to complete the action,
tormented by the midday sun,
just as an elephant that finds a lake plunges into it,
one should also throw oneself into that action. ^7-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-66]]

If it brings a decline in strength,
one should set it aside in order to resume it later.
When it is well completed, desiring the next
and the next, one should leave it behind. ^7-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-67]]

Just as a veteran warrior, together with enemies,
clashes swords on the battlefield,
one should dodge the weapons of the afflictions
and strike to destroy the enemy afflictions. ^7-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-68]]

Just as someone who drops a sword in battle
picks it up quickly out of fear,
so too, if the weapon of mindfulness is lost,
one should quickly take it up again, remembering the fears of hell. ^7-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-69]]

Just as poison, relying on the blood,
spreads and pervades the body,
so too, if an opportunity is found,
a fault will pervade the mind. ^7-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-70]]

Just as someone carrying a vessel full of mustard oil
with a man holding a sword standing nearby
fears being killed if it spills,
an ascetic should likewise stay focused. ^7-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-71]]

Therefore, just as one springs up quickly
if a snake crawls into one's lap,
likewise, if sleep and lethargy arrive,
one should quickly drive them away. ^7-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-72]]

For each and every fault that occurs,
I shall reproach myself and reflect for a long time:
"By all means, I will act in such a way
that this never happens to me again." ^7-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-73]]

Thinking, "How, in these situations,
will mindfulness become a habit?"
For this reason, one should seek out the company of others
or suitable actions. ^7-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-74]]

Before performing an action,
I should have the capability for everything.
So, recalling the discourse on heedfulness,
I should make myself rise lightly. ^7-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-75]]

Just as the wind, by going and
coming, controls a tuft of cotton,
likewise, I should be controlled by enthusiasm.
In that way, success will be achieved. ^7-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-a]]

This is the seventh chapter of Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, called the Exposition of Diligence. ^7-a

## Chapter Eight: The Exposition of Meditative Concentration ^8-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-1]]

Having thus generated diligence,
I should place my mind in concentration.
A person whose mind is completely distracted
lives between the fangs of the afflictions. ^8-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-2]]

Through the solitude of body and mind,
complete distraction will not arise.
Therefore, I should renounce the world
and completely discard discursive thought. ^8-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-3]]

Because of attachment and craving
For gain and so forth, one does not abandon the world.
So one should give these up completely,
And a wise person should look into it in this way. ^8-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-4]]

Special insight that is fully joined with calm abiding
Completely destroys the afflictions. Knowing this,
One should first seek calm abiding, and that
Comes about through joyful delight in non-attachment to the world. ^8-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-5]]

What impermanent being would form
A true attachment to something impermanent?
Because of that, for thousands of lifetimes,
They will not see the one they love. ^8-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-6]]

If they are not seen, one feels no joy,
And the mind cannot rest in meditative equipoise.
Even when they are seen, one is not satisfied,
And craving torments one just as before. ^8-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-7]]

If one becomes attached to sentient beings,
True reality is completely hidden from view.
The mind of disillusionment is also destroyed,
And in the end one will be tormented by grief. ^8-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-8]]

By thinking only about them,
This life will pass away meaninglessly.
Because of impermanent friends and relatives,
Even the eternal Dharma will be destroyed. ^8-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-9]]

If one shares the same fortune as a childish one,
One will certainly go to the lower realm.
If they lead one to a fortune unlike that of the noble ones,
What is the use of relying on a childish one? ^8-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-10]]

In a single instant they become friends,
And in a moment they even become enemies.
They get angry at things that should bring joy,
So ordinary beings are hard to please. ^8-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-11]]

If I speak for their benefit, they become angry,
And they also try to turn me away from what is beneficial.
If I do not listen to their words,
Out of anger they will go to the lower realm. ^8-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-12]]

They envy those above them and compete with equals.
They show pride toward those below them and become conceited when praised.
If spoken to unpleasantly, anger arises.
When is benefit ever gained from a childish person? ^8-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-13]]

If one keeps company with a childish one, then from that childish one
Come self-praise, the disparagement of others,
And talk that delights in saṃsāra and the like.
Non-virtue will certainly arise in every way. ^8-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-14]]

In this way, when oneself and another rely on each other,
The result is only ruin.
They do not achieve my welfare,
And I too will not achieve their welfare, ^8-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-15]]

I shall keep far away from a childish person.
If I meet one, I shall please them with a happy greeting.
Without becoming close friends,
I shall behave well as a mere ordinary person. ^8-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-16]]

Just as a bee takes nectar from a flower,
I shall take only what serves the purpose of the Dharma.
As if I had never seen them before,
I shall stay unattached and unfamiliar with everyone. ^8-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-17]]

"I have much gain and honor,
And many people are fond of me."
If I hold such conceit,
Fear will arise after death. ^8-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-18]]

So whatever the thoroughly deluded mind
Becomes attached to,
All of it gathers together, grows a thousandfold,
And turns into suffering and rises up as suffering. ^8-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-19]]

So a wise person should not form attachment,
Because fear arises from attachment.
Since these things will naturally be left behind,
Stay steadfast and understand this fully. ^8-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-20]]

Even if much gain has been acquired,
And fame and a good reputation have arisen,
Along with the whole heap of gain and fame,
No one can know where they will go. ^8-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-21]]

If there are others who disparage me,
Why should I be pleased by praise?
If there are others who praise me,
Why should I be upset by disparagement? ^8-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-22]]

Sentient beings have many different aspirations.
If even the Victors cannot please them,
Then there is no need to mention a lowly person like me.
So I shall give up thinking about the world. ^8-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-23]]

Sentient beings disparage those who have no gains
And speak unkindly of those who have gains.
How could joy ever come
From people who are so hard to be around by nature? ^8-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-24]]

Childish ones feel no joy
When there is nothing in it for themselves.
So the Tathāgatas have said
That no childish one is a friend. ^8-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-25]]

In the forest, the deer, the birds,
And the trees do not speak unkindly.
When will I live together
With those whose company is pleasant? ^8-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-26]]

I shall make my home in a cave, an empty temple,
Or at the foot of a tree.
When will I never look back
And become free from attachment? ^8-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-27]]

In places that nobody claims as "mine,"
Places that are naturally wide open,
Acting freely and without attachment,
When will I come to live? ^8-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-28]]

Owning only an alms bowl and the like,
Wearing clothes that nobody else wants,
And not even hiding this body,
When will I live without fear? ^8-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-29]]

After going to a charnel ground,
When will I treat the skeletons of others and my body
As equal,
Since all of them are bound to perish? ^8-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-30]]

Even this very body of mine
Will become such that
Because of its stench, even foxes
Will not come near. ^8-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-31]]

This body arose on its own as one,
But the flesh and bones that were born with it
Will fall apart and separate.
So what need is there to mention other loved ones? ^8-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-32]]

When we are born, we are born alone,
And when we die, we die alone.
Others do not take a share of the suffering,
So what is the use of loved ones who get in the way? ^8-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-33]]

Just as travelers on a road
Take up a place to stay,
So too those who have entered the path of existence
Fully take up a place of birth. ^8-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-34]]

While the world grieves all around,
As long as four men
Have not yet carried that body away from there,
Until then, go to the forest! ^8-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-35]]

Make no friends and no enemies.
Let this body live alone in solitude.
Once it is counted as already dead,
No one will feel grief even when it dies. ^8-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-36]]

Since no one is staying nearby
To cause the harm of grief,
No one at all will distract me from
The recollection of the Buddha and so forth. ^8-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-37]]

So in forests that are exceptionally delightful,
Free of hardship and blissful,
Which calm all distractions,
I myself shall live alone. ^8-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-38]]

Letting go of all other thoughts,
With a mind intent only on my own mind,
I shall strive to settle the mind in meditative equipoise
And to subdue it. ^8-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-39]]

In this world and in the next,
Sensual desires bring ruin.
Here, they lead to being killed, bound, and cut up,
And in the next, they bring about the hells and so forth. ^8-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-40]]

In the past, through male and female messengers,
You made many requests for the sake of someone,
And for that person's sake you did not hesitate,
Even over misdeeds or a bad name; ^8-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-41]]

You put yourself in danger
And used up your wealth.
When you embraced them fully,
You were supremely delighted—those very people ^8-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-42]]

Are nothing but skeletons, nothing else.
Since they have no independence and no self,
And you desire and cling to them so intensely,
Why do you not go to nirvāṇa? ^8-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-43]]

What you first lifted with effort—
Even when drawn up, it looked down in modesty.
Whether it had been seen before or not,
That face was covered with a cloth. ^8-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-44]]

That face of your affliction
Has now become plain to see.
Seeing it uncovered by vultures,
Why do you run away from it now? ^8-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-45]]

Even when others merely looked at it with their eyes,
You kept it carefully guarded.
Now that they are eating it,
Why, O miser, do you not guard it? ^8-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-46]]

Seeing this heap of flesh
Being eaten by vultures and others,
Why do you honor what is food for others
With flower garlands, sandalwood, and ornaments? ^8-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-47]]

Seeing it as a mere skeleton,
You are terrified even when it lies still.
So why are you not afraid when something
Makes it move, like a reanimated corpse? ^8-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-48]]

Excrement and saliva
Both come from one and the same food.
Of these two, you dislike the excrement,
So why do you delight in the saliva? ^8-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-49]]

Lustful persons take no delight in a pillow
Of cotton that is soft to the touch,
Saying, "It does not leak a foul odor."
They are deluded about the impure. ^8-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-50]]

If you feel attachment to this even when it is covered,
Why do you not desire it when it is uncovered?
If you have no need for it uncovered,
Why do you embrace it when it is covered? ^8-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-51]]

If you have no attachment to impurity,
Why do you hold in your arms another person—
A cage of bones tied together by sinews
And plastered over with the mud of flesh? ^8-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-52]]

You yourself contain a great deal of impurity,
And you make constant use of it.
Yet you also want other bags of filth,
Because you are greedy for impurity. ^8-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-53]]

"I delight in this flesh,"
You say, and want to touch it and look at it.
But how can you want flesh
That is insentient by nature? ^8-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-54]]

The mind that you desire
Cannot be touched or seen,
And what can be touched or seen is not consciousness.
What is the use of such a meaningless embrace? ^8-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-55]]

Another person's body is of the nature of impurity,
And it is no great wonder that you do not realize this.
But you yourself are impurity itself,
And it is a great wonder that you do not realize this! ^8-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-56]]

You leave behind a fresh lotus
Opened by the rays of a cloudless sun,
And with a mind attached to impurity,
Why do you delight in a cage of filth? ^8-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-57]]

If you do not want to touch a patch of ground
That is smeared with impurity,
How can you want to touch the body
That the impurity came from? ^8-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-58]]

If you have no attachment to impurity,
Why do you hold in your arms another person
Who came from an impure field
And was produced by that seed? ^8-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-59]]

You do not want even a tiny filth-worm
That has come out of impurity,
Yet you want a body that is mostly impurity by nature
And was born from impurity. ^8-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-60]]

Not only do you not look down on
Your own impurity,
But out of greed for impurity,
You also want another bag of filth. ^8-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-61]]

Even pleasant things like camphor,
Or cooked rice and vegetables,
Once they are put into the mouth and spat out,
Make even the ground impure and foul. ^8-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-62]]

If you doubt that there is such impurity as this,
Even though it is plainly evident,
Look at the bodies thrown away in the charnel grounds,
And look at those other impurities as well. ^8-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-63]]

If its skin is peeled away,
Great terror will arise.
Even knowing this, how can joy arise
In that very same thing again? ^8-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-64]]

The scent smeared on the body
Comes from sandalwood and the like, and from nothing else.
Because of the scent of one thing,
Why do you feel attachment to another? ^8-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-65]]

If it naturally smells foul,
Would it not be better to feel no attachment to it?
People in the world who crave for no purpose,
Why do they smear sweet scents on it? ^8-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-66]]

But if the sweet scent is sandalwood,
What comes from the body here?
Because of the scent of one thing,
Why do you feel attachment to another? ^8-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-67]]

If, with long hair and nails,
Yellowish teeth with filth, and soaked in the smell of mud,
The nature of the body
Is truly terrifying when naked, ^8-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-68]]

Like a weapon that harms oneself,
Why do you work so hard to polish it?
Through the efforts of those deluded about the self,
This whole earth is thrown into turmoil by madmen. ^8-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-69]]

If you feel revulsion in the charnel ground
When you see nothing but skeletons,
Do you take delight in the charnel ground of the village,
Filled with skeletons that move about? ^8-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-70]]

Even this thing that has become so impure
Cannot be had without paying a price.
To get it brings exhaustion,
And harm in hell and the like. ^8-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-71]]

A child cannot yet gather wealth.
Once grown up, what happiness is there in that?
If a whole life is used up gathering wealth,
What can an old person do with sensual desire? ^8-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-72]]

Some wretched lustful people
Are completely worn out by a full day's work.
They come home with exhausted bodies
And sleep like corpses. ^8-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-73]]

Some are worn down by travelling in foreign lands
And suffering from going far away.
Though they long for their wives and children,
They do not see them for years. ^8-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-74]]

Completely deluded, they want their own benefit,
And for some purpose they even sell themselves.
Without getting that purpose, they are driven
By the wind of other people's meaningless work. ^8-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-75]]

Some sell their own bodies
And, powerless, are put to work by others.
Even when their wives give birth,
It happens just anywhere, at the foot of a tree or in the wilderness. ^8-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-76]]

Fools deceived by sensual desire
Wish to survive and think, "I will make a living."
They go into battle, though afraid of losing their lives,
And become servants for the sake of profit. ^8-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-77]]

Some lustful persons even have their bodies mutilated.
Some are impaled on the points of stakes.
Some are pierced by short spears.
And some are even seen being burned. ^8-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-78]]

Through the torment of gathering, guarding, and losing,
Know that wealth is endless ruin.
Those who are distracted by attachment to wealth
Have no chance to be freed from the suffering of existence. ^8-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-79]]

For lustful persons, these and other things like them
Are many disadvantages, and the enjoyment is slight,
Just as animals that pull a cart
Eat only a few mouthfuls of grass. ^8-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-80]]

For the sake of that small benefit,
Which even animals have no trouble getting,
This perfect leisure and endowment, so hard to find,
Is destroyed by those tormented by karma. ^8-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-81]]

Sensual desires will surely fall apart
And make one fall into hell and the like.
For the sake of what is not great, at all times,
All the wearisome hardship there has been— ^8-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-82]]

If even a ten-millionth part of that
Hardship were enough to reach Buddhahood,
Then for a lustful person, compared to the conduct of awakening,
The suffering is greater, yet there is no awakening. ^8-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-83]]

If one thinks of the suffering of hell and the like,
Then for sensual desires, weapons,
Poison, fire, cliffs,
And enemies cannot even serve as comparisons. ^8-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-84]]

Having grown weary of desire in this way,
One should cultivate joy in solitude
Within peaceful forests
Free of disputes and afflictions. ^8-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-85]]

The fortunate ones, in pleasant fine dwellings of broad stone slabs
Cooled by moonlight and sandalwood,
Stirred by the breeze of the silent, peaceful forest,
Walk back and forth, thinking of the benefit of others. ^8-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-86]]

In empty houses, at the foot of trees, and in caves,
One stays wherever and as long as one wishes.
Having given up the suffering of owning and guarding things,
One lives carefree, depending on no one. ^8-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-87]]

Acting freely and without attachment,
Tied to no one at all,
Whatever happiness of contentment one enjoys,
Even Indra finds that hard to get. ^8-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-88]]

In these and other ways,
Having thought about the good qualities of solitude,
With conceptual thought fully calmed,
One should meditate on bodhicitta. ^8-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-89]]

The equality of self and others
One should first work hard to meditate on.
Since all are equal in happiness and suffering,
All should be protected as one protects oneself. ^8-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-90]]

Although the hands and other parts are many,
They are one as a single body that must be protected.
In the same way, the many different wandering beings, with their happiness and suffering,
Are all one in wanting happiness equally, just as I do. ^8-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-91]]

If my suffering
Does not harm the bodies of others,
Even so, that suffering is mine,
Unbearable because of clinging to self. ^8-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-92]]

In the same way, although the sufferings of others
Do not fall upon me,
Even so, that suffering is mine,
Hard to bear because of clinging to self. ^8-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-93]]

I should dispel the suffering of others
Because it is suffering, just like my own suffering.
I should benefit others
Because they are sentient beings, just like my own body. ^8-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-94]]

Since both I and others
Are equal in wanting happiness,
What makes me different from them,
That I strive for my own happiness alone? ^8-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-95]]

Since both I and others
Are equal in not wanting suffering,
What makes me different from them,
That I protect myself and not others? ^8-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-96]]

If I do not protect them
Because their suffering does not harm me,
And if future suffering also does not harm me,
Why protect against it? ^8-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-97]]

The thought "I will experience it"
Is a false conceptual thought.
The one who dies is truly another,
And the one who is born is also another. ^8-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-98]]

Suppose whenever anyone has suffering,
That person alone must protect against it.
The suffering of the foot is not the hand's,
So why should the hand protect the foot? ^8-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-99]]

If you say, "Even if it is not logical, here
One acts out of grasping at a self,"
Whatever is not logical about self and other
Is exactly what should be given up, as far as one is able. ^8-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-100]]

What are called a continuum and a collection
Are false, like a garland, an army, and the like.
Since there is no one who owns suffering,
Who, then, will have control over it? ^8-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-101]]

Since suffering has no owner,
All of it is entirely without distinction.
Because it is suffering, it is to be dispelled.
What is the point of making restrictions here? ^8-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-102]]

"Why should the suffering of all
Be averted?" There is no room for debate.
If it is to be averted, all of it is to be averted.
If not, then I too am like sentient beings. ^8-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-103]]

If you ask, "Since compassion brings much suffering,
Why force yourself to generate it?"
When one thinks about the suffering of wandering beings,
How can the suffering of compassion be great? ^8-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-104]]

If a single suffering
Can remove many sufferings,
For a compassionate person, that suffering
Should certainly be brought about, in oneself and in others. ^8-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-105]]

Therefore, Supuṣpacandra,
even knowing the harm that would come from the king,
did not dispel his own suffering,
so that the suffering of many would be exhausted. ^8-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-106]]

Thus, one whose continuum has become habituated,
because of delighting in calming the suffering of others,
like a swan into a lotus pool,
will plunge even into the Avīci hell. ^8-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-107]]

When sentient beings are liberated,
there is an ocean of joy.
Is that itself not enough?
What is the use of desiring liberation? ^8-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-108]]

Therefore, even when acting for the welfare of others,
there is no conceit or amazement.
Because one delights only in the welfare of others,
there is no hope for a ripened result. ^8-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-109]]

Therefore, just as I protect myself
even from the slightest unpleasant word,
likewise a protective mind toward others
and a mind of compassion should be cultivated in this way. ^8-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-110]]

Through habituation, regarding the
drops of semen and blood of others,
even though there is an absence of an entity,
just as one knows them as "I", ^8-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-111]]

Likewise, regarding the bodies of others too,
why are they not taken to be "I"?
To regard my own body as other
is not difficult in that way. ^8-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-112]]

Having known that oneself has faults, and that others too
are oceans of good qualities,
one should meditate on completely giving up self-grasping
and taking up others. ^8-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-113]]

Just as the hands and so on
are accepted as limbs of the body,
likewise, as limbs of wandering beings,
why are embodied beings not accepted? ^8-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-114]]

Just as toward this body of selflessness
the intellect of self has arisen through habituation,
likewise, toward other sentient beings as well,
why would the intellect of self not arise through habituation? ^8-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-115]]

If it is like that, then even after accomplishing
the welfare of others, amazement and conceit do not arise,
just as when one has eaten food oneself,
no hope for a return arises. ^8-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-116]]

Therefore, just as I protect myself
even from minor unpleasantness,
likewise a protective mind toward wandering beings
and a mind of compassion should become a habit. ^8-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-117]]

Therefore the Protector Avalokiteśvara,
out of great compassion,
in order to dispel the fear of wandering beings even in assemblies,
bestowed a blessing even upon his own name. ^8-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-118]]

One should not turn back from hardship,
for through the power of habituation in this way,
even someone whose name was frightening to hear —
without that person, one becomes joyless. ^8-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-119]]

Whoever wishes to protect
themselves and others quickly
should practice the exchange of self and others,
the supreme secret. ^8-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-120]]

Because of attachment to this body of mine,
fear arises even toward small sources of fear.
That body, which gives rise to fear —
who would not hate it as an enemy? ^8-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-121]]

Whoever, wishing to carry out a treatment to cure
the body's illnesses of hunger, thirst, and the like,
kills birds, fish, and wild animals
and lies in ambush along the roads; ^8-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-122]]

If one, for the sake of profit and respect,
kills even one's father and mother,
and, having stolen the wealth of the Three Jewels,
is burned in the Avīci hell because of it, ^8-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-123]]

What wise person would desire,
protect, and venerate that body?
Who would not view this body
as an enemy and despise it? ^8-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-124]]

"If I give, what will I consume?"
Thinking of one's own welfare in this way is the way of a demon.
"If I consume, what will I give?"
Thinking of the welfare of others is the way of a god. ^8-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-125]]

If one harms others for one's own sake,
one will be tormented in hell and so on.
If one harms oneself for the sake of others,
one will obtain all excellence. ^8-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-126]]

Through wishing to exalt oneself,
one will be reborn in a lower realm, base and stupid.
If that very wish is shifted to others,
one will obtain a happy realm and respect. ^8-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-127]]

If one employs others for one's own welfare,
one will experience servitude and the like.
If one employs oneself for the welfare of others,
one will experience lordship and the like. ^8-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-128]]

Whatever happiness there is in the world,
all of it arises from wishing for the happiness of others.
Whatever suffering there is in the world,
all of it arises from wishing for one's own happiness. ^8-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-129]]

What need is there to say much?
The childish one who acts for their own welfare
and the Sage who acts for the welfare of others —
look at the difference between these two! ^8-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-130]]

If one does not truly exchange
one's own happiness for the suffering of others,
Buddhahood will not be achieved,
and even in saṃsāra there is no happiness. ^8-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-131]]

Setting aside the next world,
when a servant does not do their work
and a master does not pay wages,
even the purpose of this life will not be achieved. ^8-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-132]]

Completely abandoning the perfect joy
that achieves seen and unseen happiness,
by inflicting suffering on others,
one takes on unbearable suffering through delusion. ^8-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-133]]

Whatever harm there is in the world, and
however much fear and suffering there may be,
if all of that arises from grasping at a self,
what use is that great demon to me? ^8-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-134]]

If one does not completely let go of the very self,
one cannot abandon suffering,
just as, if one does not let go of fire,
one cannot avoid being burned. ^8-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-135]]

Therefore, to put to rest the harm to myself
and to put to rest the suffering of others,
I should give myself to others
and hold others as myself. ^8-135

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-136]]

"I am under the power of others" —
O mind, you must know this with certainty.
Apart from the welfare of all sentient beings,
you should now think of nothing else. ^8-136

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-137]]

With eyes and so on that are under the power of others,
it is not right to accomplish one's own welfare.
With eyes and so on that are for their sake,
it is not right to act wrongly toward them. ^8-137

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-138]]

Therefore, put sentient beings first.
Whatever you see on my body,
snatch it away
and act for the benefit of others. ^8-138

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-139]]

Having made those who are inferior and so on into oneself,
and having made oneself into others,
with a mind free of conceptual thought,
meditate on envy, rivalry, and pride. ^8-139

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-140]]

This one is honored, but I am not.
I have not obtained what this one has.
This one is praised, but I am disparaged.
This one is happy, while I am in suffering. ^8-140

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-141]]

I do the labors, and
this one stays in happiness itself.
This one is great in the world, and
I am known as inferior and without good qualities. ^8-141

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-142]]

What is the use of being without good qualities?
All of us are indeed endowed with good qualities.
There are those compared to whom this one is inferior, and
there are those compared to whom I am superior. ^8-142

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-143]]

The decline of ethical discipline, view, and so on
is due to the power of afflictions; it is not under my control.
I must be healed as much as possible,
and I will accept even harm. ^8-143

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-144]]

However, if I am not to be healed by this one,
why does he despise me?
What use are his good qualities to me?
This one himself possesses good qualities! ^8-144

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-145]]

He lives in the jaws of the vicious lower realms,
and this one lacks compassion for wandering beings.
Moreover, conceited about his good qualities,
he wishes to challenge wise persons. ^8-145

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-146]]

Seeing someone equal to me,
in order to make myself superior,
my gain and honor
I shall certainly secure, even through dispute. ^8-146

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-147]]

I shall ensure that my good qualities
are made clear to the entire world,
and that, whatever good qualities this one has,
no one hears of them. ^8-147

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-148]]

I shall also conceal my faults.
I shall be venerated, but not this one.
Today I shall acquire excellent gains.
I shall be respected, but not this one. ^8-148

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-149]]

When this one is brought to ruin,
I shall look on with joy for a long time.
I shall make him the laughingstock of all wandering beings
and the object of their mutual derision. ^8-149

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-150]]

It is said that even this miserable one
is competing with me.
But is this one my equal in learning or wisdom,
or in looks, family, or wealth? ^8-150

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-151]]

Having heard my good qualities
proclaimed to everyone in this way,
I shall fully enjoy the happiness
of the thrilling joy that arises. ^8-151

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-152]]

Even if this one acquires some gain,
if he does my work,
I shall give him just enough to live on,
and I shall take it by my power. ^8-152

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-153]]

This one must lose its happiness
And always be on the receiving end of my harm.
For through this one, over hundreds of lifetimes,
I was harmed in saṃsāra. ^8-153

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-154]]

O mind, you wanted to achieve your own welfare,
And countless eons have gone by.
After such great weariness,
You have achieved nothing but suffering. ^8-154

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-155]]

So now you must surely commit yourself
Fully to the welfare of others.
The word of the Sage never deceives,
And later you will see its good qualities. ^8-155

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-156]]

If you had done this
In earlier times,
You could not possibly be in a situation like this,
Apart from the perfect happiness of a Buddha. ^8-156

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-157]]

So, in the same way that, upon drops
Of the semen and blood of other people,
You have been grasping as "I",
Get used to doing the same toward others. ^8-157

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-158]]

After acting as a spy for others,
Take whatever appears on my body,
Snatch those very things away,
And then you must practice what benefits others. ^8-158

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-159]]

"I am happy and others are unhappy,
I am high and others are low,
I do what is beneficial and others do not"—
Why do you not feel envy toward yourself? ^8-159

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-160]]

Cut yourself off from happiness,
And take on the suffering of others.
Ask, "What is this one doing at every moment?"
And examine your own faults. ^8-160

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-161]]

Even when others have committed a fault,
Turn it into your own fault.
And even if a fault of your own is small,
Confess it openly to many people. ^8-161

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-162]]

Speak highly of the reputation of others
And push your own reputation down.
Like the lowest of servants,
Put yourself to work for every purpose. ^8-162

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-163]]

This one has a faulty nature.
Do not praise it for even a slight adventitious good quality.
Whatever its good qualities are,
Make sure that no one at all comes to know them. ^8-163

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-164]]

In short, whatever harm you have done to others
For your own sake,
Let that harm fall upon yourself
For the sake of sentient beings. ^8-164

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-165]]

So that it does not become overbearing,
Do not build up its strength.
Like a new bride,
Keep it restrained with shame and fear. ^8-165

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-166]]

"Do that! Stay like that!
You must not act in that way!"
In this manner, bring it under control.
If it goes beyond that, it must be put down. ^8-166

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-167]]

Yet even when you are instructed like this,
If you, my mind, still do not act that way,
Then since all faults rely on you,
There is nothing left but to subdue you yourself. ^8-167

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-168]]

That former time when you ruined me
Was another time.
Now I see you. Where will you go?
I shall destroy all your haughtiness. ^8-168

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-169]]

"I still have my own welfare."
Throw away that thought!
Since I have sold you to others,
Do not be discouraged. Offer your service. ^8-169

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-170]]

If I fall into heedlessness
And do not give you to sentient beings,
You will certainly hand me over
To the guardians of hell. ^8-170

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-171]]

In that way, you have handed me over again and again,
And I have suffered for a long time.
Remembering those grudges,
I will destroy your thinking about your own welfare. ^8-171

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-172]]

If I wish to be happy,
I should not take delight in myself.
If I wish to protect myself,
I should always protect others. ^8-172

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-173]]

The more this body
Is completely cared for,
The more it falls
Into becoming extremely fragile. ^8-173

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-174]]

Once it has fallen in that way,
If this entire earth
Cannot fulfill its desires,
Who else can fulfill them? ^8-174

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-175]]

Desiring the impossible brings afflictions
And a worn-out, weakened intention as well.
Whoever does not depend on anything,
Their excellence never runs out. ^8-175

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-176]]

So one should not give the desires of the body
An opportunity to grow.
Whatever attractive thing one does not grasp at,
That is an excellent thing. ^8-176

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-177]]

In the end it turns to ashes.
It does not move itself; another moves it.
It is an impure, unbearable form.
Why do I direct grasping at self toward it? ^8-177

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-178]]

Whether it is alive or dead,
What use to me is this machine?
If it is no different from a clod of earth and the like,
Alas, why do I not get rid of my pride? ^8-178

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-179]]

By tending to the body,
I have piled up meaningless suffering.
Attachment and anger
Toward this thing, which is like a piece of wood—what use are they? ^8-179

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-180]]

Whether I nurture it in this way
Or vultures and the like eat it,
It has no attachment and no hatred.
So why do I have attachment toward it? ^8-180

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-181]]

This thing gets angry when it is blamed
And is delighted when it is praised.
If it itself knows nothing,
For whose sake have I toiled? ^8-181

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-182]]

"Whoever desires this body
Is a friend of mine," I might say.
But since everyone desires their own body,
Why do I not delight in them? ^8-182

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-183]]

So, without attachment, I
Shall give up the body to benefit beings.
Although it has many faults,
I shall keep it as a tool for action. ^8-183

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-184]]

So enough of childish behavior!
I shall follow in the footsteps of the wise.
Recalling the discourse on heedfulness,
I shall drive away sleep and mental dullness. ^8-184

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-185]]

So, in order to clear away obscurations,
I shall turn my mind back from mistaken paths
And always rest it in meditative equipoise
On the perfect object of focus. ^8-185

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the eighth chapter, called Meditative Concentration. ^8-a

## Chapter Nine: The Perfection of Wisdom ^9-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-1]]

The Sage taught all these branches
For the sake of wisdom.
So those who wish to bring suffering to peace
Should develop wisdom. ^9-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-2]]

Conventional truth and ultimate truth:
these are held to be the two truths.
Ultimate truth is not within the range of the intellect.
The intellect is said to be conventional truth. ^9-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-3]]

In this regard, two kinds of world are seen:
the world of yogis and the world of ordinary people.
In this regard, the ordinary world
is overruled by the world of yogis. ^9-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-4]]

Even yogis, because their intellects differ,
are overruled by those who are higher, one level after another,
through examples that both sides accept,
because, for the sake of the result, they are not analyzed. ^9-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-5]]

Worldly persons see entities
and conceptualize them as true reality,
not as being like illusions. Therefore, here,
yogis and the world are in dispute. ^9-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-6]]

Even the direct perception of forms and so on
rests on convention, not on valid cognition.
That is false, just as the convention of purity and so on
applied to impurity and so on is false. ^9-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-7]]

For the sake of guiding the world,
the Protector taught entities; in reality,
they are not momentary.
Someone might object, "This contradicts even conventional truth," ^9-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-8]]

There is no fault in the conventional truth of yogis.
In relation to the world, they see reality.
Otherwise, if they regarded women as impure,
the world would invalidate that realization. ^9-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-9]]

Someone might say: "How is there merit from a Victor who is like an illusion?"
It is just the same as for one who is truly existent.
Someone might say: "If sentient beings are like illusions,
how are they born after dying?" ^9-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-10]]

As long as conditions come together,
for that long even an illusion will arise.
Merely because a continuum goes on for a long time,
how can a sentient being be truly existent? ^9-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-11]]

If someone kills an illusory person, for example,
there is no misdeed, because there is no mind.
But toward one who has an illusory mind,
merit and misdeed arise. ^9-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-12]]

Because mantras and the like lack the power,
an illusory mind does not arise.
The illusion arises from various conditions,
and it is itself of various kinds. ^9-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-13]]

Nowhere is there a single condition
that is capable of everything.
Someone might say: "If ultimate truth is nirvāṇa,
and saṃsāra is conventional truth, then ^9-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-14]]

Someone might say: "Since even a Buddha would go on cycling in saṃsāra,
what is the use of the conduct of awakening?"
But as long as the continuum of conditions is not cut off,
even an illusion does not cease. ^9-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-15]]

But when the continuum of conditions is cut off,
it does not arise even as conventional truth.
Someone might say: "When even error does not exist,
by what will an illusion be perceived?" ^9-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-16]]

When for you the illusion itself
does not exist, what is there to perceive?
Someone might say: "If in reality something else exists,
that aspect is the mind itself." ^9-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-17]]

When the mind itself is an illusion,
then what is seen, and by what?
Even the Protector of the world
said that the mind does not see the mind. ^9-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-18]]

Just as the blade of a sword
does not cut itself, so it is with the mind.
Someone might say: "Just as a lamp
perfectly illuminates its own entity …" ^9-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-19]]

A lamp is not something to be illuminated,
because it is not covered by darkness.
Someone might say: "Unlike a crystal, a blue thing
does not depend on another for its blueness." ^9-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-20]]

Someone might say: "In the same way, some things are seen to depend
on others, and some are seen not to depend."
But what is not blue in itself
is not made blue by itself. ^9-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-21]]

If it is known by consciousness, and so one says,
"The lamp makes things luminous,"
then by what is it known, when one says,
"The intellect itself is luminous"? ^9-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-22]]

When it is not seen by anyone at all,
whether it is luminous or not luminous,
it is like the alluring pose of a barren woman's daughter:
even to speak of it is meaningless. ^9-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-23]]

Someone might say: "If self-awareness does not exist,
how does the memory of consciousness occur?"
Memory arises from its connection to experiencing another,
like the poison of a rat. ^9-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-24]]

Someone might say: "Because one endowed with other conditions sees,
it is luminous to itself."
But when accomplished eye ointment is applied,
the vase is seen, but not the eye ointment itself. ^9-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-25]]

Whatever is seen, heard, and known
is not what is to be negated here.
Here, what is to be overturned is the conceptualization of true existence,
which is the cause of suffering. ^9-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-26]]

If an illusion is not other than the mind,
and it is not conceived as not other either,
then if it is an entity, how is it not other?
If it is not other, it does not exist as an entity. ^9-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-27]]

Just as an illusion, though not true,
is something to be seen, so too the mind is the one who sees.
Someone might say: "Saṃsāra is based on an entity.
If it were not, it would be like space." ^9-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-28]]

Since a non-entity depends on an entity,
how could it have a function?
Your mind, without any companion,
would become entirely solitary. ^9-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-29]]

When the mind is free of an apprehended object,
at that time all are Tathāgatas.
Even if that is so, what benefit is there
in setting up Cittamātra by conceptual thought? ^9-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-30]]

Someone might say: "Even if one knows things to be like an illusion,
how will the afflictions be turned back?
After all, toward an illusory woman,
even her creator develops attachment." ^9-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-31]]

Her creator, toward objects of knowledge,
has not given up the latent tendencies of the afflictions.
Therefore, when he sees her,
his latent tendency of emptiness is weak. ^9-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-32]]

Through habituation to the latent tendency of emptiness,
the latent tendency of entities will be given up;
and through habituation to "there is nothing at all,"
that too will be given up later. ^9-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-33]]

When one says, "Such and such does not exist,"
and the entity to be analyzed is not apprehended,
then how can a non-entity that lacks a basis
remain before the intellect? ^9-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-34]]

When entities and non-entities
do not remain before the intellect,
then, since there is no other aspect,
there is thorough pacification in non-apprehension. ^9-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-35]]

Just as a wish-fulfilling jewel and a wish-fulfilling tree
completely fulfill hopes,
in the same way, to the ones to be tamed, through the power of aspiration prayers,
the form of the Victor appears. ^9-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-36]]

For example, when a garuḍa shrine
has been properly made and its maker has passed away,
even though a long time has passed since he died,
it still pacifies poison and so on. ^9-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-37]]

In the same way, in accordance with the conduct of awakening,
the shrine of the Victor, too, has been properly made.
Even when the bodhisattva has passed
into nirvāṇa, it accomplishes all aims. ^9-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-38]]

Someone might say: "If offerings are made to one without a mind,
how can they bring a result?"
This is because, whether he remains or has passed into nirvāṇa,
it is taught to be exactly the same. ^9-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-39]]

Whether in conventional truth or in reality,
it is acceptable, for that result comes from scripture,
just as, for example, toward a true Buddha,
it has a result. ^9-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-40]]

Someone might say: "Liberation comes from seeing the truths;
what is the use of seeing emptiness?"
This is because scripture states that without this path,
there is no awakening. ^9-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-41]]

Someone might say: "If the Mahāyāna is not established,
how is your scripture established?"
The reply: "Because this is established for both of us."
At the start, this was not established for you. ^9-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-42]]

By whatever conditions you come to believe in that,
the same holds for the Mahāyāna as well.
If something is true because any two other parties accept it,
then the Vedas and so on would also be true. ^9-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-43]]

Someone might say: "It is because the Mahāyāna is subject to dispute."
Regarding scripture, there is dispute with non-Buddhists,
and regarding other scriptures, among your own and other schools;
since they are subject to dispute, they would be discarded. ^9-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-44]]

If being a monk is the root of the teaching,
even being a monk is hard to establish.
For those whose minds have apprehension,
even nirvāṇa is hard to establish. ^9-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-45]]

If one is liberated by giving up afflictions,
it would happen right away.
Even without afflictions, in them
the power of karma is seen. ^9-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-46]]

If you say it is certain that, for the time being,
the craving that is the cause of grasping does not exist,
then even if that craving is not afflicted,
why would it not exist, just like delusion? ^9-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-47]]

Craving arises with feeling as its condition,
and feeling also exists in them.
A mind that has apprehension
will stay fixed on something. ^9-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-48]]

A mind that is devoid of emptiness,
though it has ceased, will arise again,
like the meditative absorption of non-perception.
Therefore, one should meditate on emptiness. ^9-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-49]]

If any speech that accords with the sūtras
is held to be spoken by the Buddha,
is it not held that most of the Mahāyāna
matches your sūtras? ^9-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-50]]

If, because of one that is not included,
all become faulty,
then because of one that is similar to a sūtra,
why are they not all spoken by the Victor? ^9-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-51]]

Any speech whose depth was not fathomed
by Mahākāśyapa and the others,
because you do not understand it,
who would treat it as unacceptable? ^9-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-52]]

For the sake of those suffering through delusion,
being free from the extremes of attachment and fear,
remaining in saṃsāra is achieved.
This is the result of emptiness. ^9-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-53]]

Thus, against the position of emptiness,
refutation does not hold up.
Therefore, without doubt,
one should meditate on emptiness. ^9-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-54]]

Emptiness is the antidote to the darkness
of the obscuration of afflictions and of objects of knowledge.
How could one who wishes to become omniscient quickly
not meditate on it? ^9-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-55]]

If fear arises from any thing
that produces suffering,
emptiness pacifies suffering;
how could fear arise toward it? ^9-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-56]]

If any self existed,
one might become afraid of anything;
but since no self exists at all,
who is there to be afraid? ^9-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-57]]

Teeth, hair, and nails are not the self,
and the self is not bones or blood;
the self is not mucus and not phlegm,
and it is neither lymph nor pus. ^9-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-58]]

The self is not fat or sweat,
and the lungs and liver are not the self;
the other internal organs are not the self either,
and the self is not feces or urine. ^9-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-59]]

Flesh and skin are not the self,
and warmth and wind are not the self;
the body's openings are not the self, and in every way
the six consciousnesses are not the self either. ^9-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-60]]

If the consciousness of sound were permanent,
it would grasp sound at all times.
If there is no object of knowledge, what does it know,
and for what reason is it called a consciousness? ^9-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-61]]

If something that does not know were consciousness,
it would follow that a piece of wood is consciousness too.
Therefore it is certain that, without the presence
of an object of knowledge, there is no consciousness. ^9-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-62]]

If that very thing knows form,
why does it not also hear at that time?
If it is because sound is not nearby,
then the consciousness of that is also absent. ^9-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-63]]

Whatever has the nature of apprehending sound,
how could it become the apprehender of form?
One person is designated as both a father and a son,
but this is not true reality. ^9-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-64]]

In the same way, sattva, rajas, and
tamas are neither a son nor a father,
and that apprehender of form is not seen to have a nature
that possesses the apprehension of sound. ^9-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-65]]

If, like an actor, that very thing is seen
in another way, it is not permanent.
If that other way is that very thing,
then that oneness is a oneness never seen before. ^9-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-66]]

If that other way is not true,
then state its own nature.
If you say it is consciousness itself, then in that case
it would follow that all persons are one. ^9-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-67]]

The conscious and the non-conscious, too,
would become one, because their existence is the same.
When specific differences are false,
what then is the basis for similarity? ^9-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-68]]

That which is mindless is also not the self,
because it is mindless, like a vase and so on.
If, however, it knows because it has a mind,
it follows that what does not know perishes. ^9-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-69]]

But if there is no change in the self,
what has consciousness done to it?
Thus, lacking the power to know and without activity,
space would be made into the self. ^9-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-70]]

Someone might say: "If the self does not exist,
the connection between karma and result makes no sense.
Because one perishes after performing a karma,
whose karma will it be?" ^9-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-71]]

That the action and its result rest on different bases,
and that the self does nothing in that,
is accepted by both of us.
So is this debate here not pointless? ^9-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-72]]

That the one who has the cause also has the result:
seeing this is impossible.
It is by relying on a single continuum
that an agent and an experiencer are taught. ^9-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-73]]

The mind of the past and future
is not the self, because it does not exist.
If, however, the mind that has arisen is the self,
when that perishes, there is no self either. ^9-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-74]]

Take the trunk of a plantain tree.
When you divide it into parts, nothing is left.
In the same way, when we search through analysis,
the self is not real either. ^9-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-75]]

Someone might say: "If sentient beings do not exist,
toward whom should one practice compassion?"
Toward whatever is imputed through delusion,
which we accept for the sake of the result. ^9-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-76]]

Someone might say: "If there are no sentient beings, whose is the result?"
That is true, yet it is claimed because of delusion.
To bring suffering to rest,
we should not undo the delusion about the result. ^9-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-77]]

The pride that causes suffering
grows through the delusion of a self.
If you say that even this cannot be undone,
then meditating on selflessness is the best thing to do. ^9-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-78]]

The body is not the feet or the calves.
The thighs and the waist are not the body either.
The belly and the back are not the body.
The chest and the arms are not the body either. ^9-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-79]]

The ribs and the hands are not the body either.
The armpits and the shoulders are not the body either.
The internal organs are not the body either.
If the head and the neck are not the body either,
then what in all this is the body? ^9-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-80]]

Suppose this body stays in all of these parts,
each through its own portion.
The parts would indeed stay in the parts,
but where would the body itself stay? ^9-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-81]]

Suppose the whole body
stayed in the hands and so on.
However many hands and so on there are,
there would be that many bodies. ^9-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-82]]

If the body does not exist outside or inside,
how can the body exist in the hands and so on?
If it does not exist apart from the hands and so on,
how does it exist at all? ^9-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-83]]

So there is no body. Toward the hands and so on,
the idea of a body arises through delusion,
because of a particular arrangement of shape,
just as we take a stump to be a person. ^9-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-84]]

As long as the collection of conditions lasts,
the body appears like a person.
In the same way, as long as that is present in the hands and so on,
the body appears there for that long. ^9-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-85]]

In the same way, because the hand is a collection of fingers,
what would the hand be?
Because the finger too is a collection of joints,
and the joint, too, when it is divided into its own parts, ^9-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-86]]

Even a part can be divided into atoms.
And that atom, too, can be divided into directional parts.
The directional division, too, has no parts,
so it is like space; therefore even atoms do not exist. ^9-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-87]]

Form is like a dream.
What discerning person would be attached to it?
When the body does not exist in this way,
then who is a man and who is a woman? ^9-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-88]]

If suffering exists in reality,
why does it not harm those who are very joyful?
And if happiness exists, for those tormented by grief and the like,
why do delicious things and so on not bring them joy? ^9-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-89]]

If you say it is not experienced
because something more powerful overwhelms it,
then how can something whose nature is not experience
be a feeling? ^9-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-90]]

Someone might say: "Suffering exists in a subtle state."
Has its coarse state not been removed?
If you say, "It is merely a joy different from that,"
then that subtle state, too, is still part of that. ^9-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-91]]

Suppose that, because an opposing condition has arisen,
suffering does not arise.
Isn't it then established that what is called feeling
is nothing but conceptual clinging? ^9-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-92]]

For this very reason, one should practice meditation
on this analysis as its antidote.
The meditative concentration that arises from the field of investigation
is the food of the yogi. ^9-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-93]]

If there is a gap between the sense faculty and the object,
where would they meet?
Even if there is no gap, they are just one,
so what would meet with what? ^9-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-94]]

An atom does not enter another atom.
There is no room, and they are equal.
Without entering, there is no mixing,
and without mixing, there is no meeting. ^9-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-95]]

How could it make sense to say
that something partless can meet?
If you have seen meeting and being partless
together, then show it! ^9-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-96]]

For consciousness, which has no body,
meeting simply makes no sense.
A collection, too, lacks an entity,
just as we analyzed before. ^9-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-97]]

So if contact does not exist,
from what would feeling arise?
For what purpose is all this hard labor?
What would harm what? ^9-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-98]]

When there is no feeler at all,
and feeling does not exist either,
then, seeing this situation,
why does craving not turn away? ^9-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-99]]

Seeing, and even touching,
are by nature like a dream and an illusion.
Because feeling arises together with the mind,
it is not seen by that mind. ^9-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-100]]

Even by what arises before or after,
it is remembered but not experienced.
It does not experience itself,
and it is not experienced by others. ^9-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-101]]

There is no feeler at all,
so feeling does not exist in reality.
Thus, how could this collection, which is only selflessness,
be harmed by this? ^9-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-102]]

The mind does not stay in the sense faculties,
nor in forms and so on, nor in between.
The mind is not inside, and it is not outside,
and it is not found anywhere else. ^9-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-103]]

That which is not the body and is not anywhere else,
which is not mixed and is not anywhere separate,
is nothing at all. Therefore,
sentient beings are in nirvāṇa by nature. ^9-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-104]]

If consciousness exists before the object of knowledge,
What does it focus on as it arises?
If consciousness and the object of knowledge exist together,
What does it focus on as it arises? ^9-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-105]]

But if it exists after the object of knowledge,
Then from what does consciousness arise?
In this way, for all phenomena,
Arising cannot be understood. ^9-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-106]]

Someone might say: "If, in this way, conventional truth does not exist,
How could there be the two truths?
And if that happens through another conventional truth,
How could sentient beings pass into nirvāṇa?" ^9-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-107]]

This is the conceptual thought of another's mind.
It is not one's own conventional truth.
If it is ascertained later, it exists.
If not, conventional truth simply does not exist. ^9-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-108]]

Conceptual thought and what is to be conceptualized,
The two depend on each other.
Relying on what is commonly known,
All analyses are expressed. ^9-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-109]]

Someone might say: "When one analyzes with an analysis
That is itself analyzed,
Then that analysis too,
Because of analysis, has no end." ^9-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-110]]

When the object of analysis has been analyzed,
There is no basis for the analysis.
Because there is no basis, it does not arise,
And that too is called nirvāṇa. ^9-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-111]]

For those who hold that these two are true,
That very position is extremely hard to maintain.
If the object is established through the power of consciousness,
What basis is there for the existence of consciousness itself? ^9-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-112]]

But if consciousness is established through the object of knowledge,
What basis is there for the existence of the object of knowledge?
If they exist through the power of one another,
Both of them would not exist. ^9-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-113]]

If without a son there is no father,
From what does the son himself arise?
Without a son there is no father.
In the same way, those two do not exist. ^9-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-114]]

Someone might say: "A sprout arises from a seed, and
Just as the seed is known by that very sprout,
By the consciousness that arises from the object of knowledge,
Why is its existence not known?" ^9-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-115]]

If a consciousness other than the sprout
Knows that the seed exists,
Then as for that by which the object of knowledge is known,
From what is the existence of consciousness itself known? ^9-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-116]]

For a start, by worldly direct perception,
All causes are seen.
The differences between a lotus stalk and the like
Are produced by differences in their causes. ^9-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-117]]

If you ask what made the differences between causes,
They come from the differences in earlier causes.
Why is a cause able to produce a result?
From the very power of the earlier cause. ^9-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-118]]

If Īśvara is the cause of wandering beings,
For a start, say what Īśvara is!
If you say it is the elements, so be it,
But why tire yourself over a mere name? ^9-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-119]]

However, earth and the like are many,
Impermanent, lifeless, and not divine.
Since they are walked upon and are impure,
They are not Īśvara himself. ^9-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-120]]

Īśvara is not space, because space is lifeless.
He is not the self, because that was already refuted.
And as for a creator who is inconceivable,
What is the point of speaking of the inconceivable? ^9-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-121]]

And what is it that he is said to produce?
Are that self, earth and the like,
And the essence of Īśvara not permanent?
Consciousness arises from the object of knowledge, and ^9-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-122]]

Beginningless happiness and suffering come from karma.
Tell me, what was produced by him?
If the cause has no beginning,
How can the result have a beginning? ^9-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-123]]

Why does he not create all the time?
For he does not depend on anything else.
If there is nothing else that was not made by him,
On what would he depend? ^9-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-124]]

If he depends, then the collection itself
Would be the cause, and not Īśvara.
When the collection is assembled, there is no power not to produce.
Without it, there is no power to produce. ^9-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-125]]

If Īśvara acts without wishing to,
It follows that he is under the control of another.
Even if he wishes to, he depends on his wish.
If he acts, how is he Īśvara? ^9-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-126]]

Those who say that atoms are permanent
Have also already been refuted earlier.
The Sāṃkhyas say that the permanent Pradhāna
Is the cause of wandering beings. ^9-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-127]]

The equilibrium of the good qualities
Known as sattva, rajas, and tamas
Is declared to be the Pradhāna.
Their lack of equilibrium is said to be wandering beings. ^9-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-128]]

For one thing to have three natures
Is illogical, so it does not exist.
In the same way, the good qualities do not exist,
Because each of them also has three aspects. ^9-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-129]]

If the good qualities do not exist, the existence
Of sound and the like becomes extremely remote.
In insentient things such as clothing,
It is not even possible for happiness and the like to exist. ^9-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-130]]

If entities have the nature of their cause,
Have entities not already been analyzed?
Your cause, moreover, is just happiness and the like,
And from that, cloth and the like do not arise. ^9-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-131]]

Happiness and the like arise from cloth and the like.
Since those do not exist, happiness and the like do not exist.
Furthermore, happiness and the like as permanent
Are never observed. ^9-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-132]]

If the manifestation of happiness and the like exists,
Why is the experience not grasped?
If that very thing becomes subtle,
How can it be both coarse and subtle? ^9-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-133]]

Since it gives up the coarse and becomes subtle,
The subtle and the coarse are subject to impermanence.
In the same way, why do you not accept
The impermanence of all entities? ^9-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-134]]

If the coarse is not other than happiness,
Manifest happiness is impermanence itself.
If you say that nothing non-existent whatsoever
Arises, because it is non-existent, ^9-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-135]]

The arising of the manifest that was non-existent
Still stands, even if you do not accept it.
If the result stays in the cause,
Someone who eats food would be eating filth. ^9-135

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-136]]

With the price of a cotton cloth,
Buy cotton seeds and wear them!
If the world does not see this because of delusion,
That very thing is still set down by the knowers of reality. ^9-136

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-137]]

That knowledge exists even in the world.
Since it exists, why is it not seen?
If the world is not valid cognition,
Seeing the manifest is also not true. ^9-137

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-138]]

If valid cognition is not valid,
Is what it measures not false?
In reality, emptiness—
Meditation on it therefore becomes untenable. ^9-138

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-139]]

Without touching the imputed entity,
Its non-entity is not grasped.
Therefore, whatever entity is false,
Its non-entity is clearly false. ^9-139

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-140]]

So when a son dies in a dream,
The conceptual thought that he does not exist
Blocks the conceptual thought that he exists,
And that too is false. ^9-140

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-141]]

Therefore, by analyzing in this way,
Nothing at all exists without a cause,
Nor does it stay within conditions,
Whether they are separate or gathered together. ^9-141

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-142]]

It does not come from somewhere else,
Nor does it stay, nor does it go.
This thing that delusion makes seem true,
What difference is there between it and an illusion? ^9-142

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-143]]

Whatever is conjured up by an illusion,
And whatever is conjured up by causes,
Where has it come from,
And where does it go? Examine this! ^9-143

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-144]]

Whatever is seen when something else is near,
And is not seen when that is absent,
Is fabricated, like a reflection.
How could there be true existence in that? ^9-144

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-145]]

For an entity that exists,
What need is there for a cause?
But even if it does not exist,
What need is there for a cause? ^9-145

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-146]]

Even hundreds of millions of causes
cannot transform a non-entity.
How could that state ever be an entity?
And what else could become an entity? ^9-146

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-147]]

If an entity cannot exist at the time of non-existence,
when will it ever become an existent entity?
As long as no entity has been produced,
it will not separate from that non-entity. ^9-147

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-148]]

If it does not separate from the non-entity,
there is no chance for an entity to exist.
Nor does an entity become non-existent,
because then it would have two natures. ^9-148

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-149]]

So there is no cessation,
and there is no entity either. Therefore
all these wandering beings
are always unborn and unceasing. ^9-149

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-150]]

Wandering beings are like a dream.
When examined closely, they are like a plantain tree.
Between nirvāṇa and not passing into nirvāṇa,
in reality there is no difference. ^9-150

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-151]]

So, for entities that are empty,
what is there to gain, and what is there to lose?
When someone is honored by anyone,
or utterly despised, what is there in that? ^9-151

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-152]]

From what do happiness and suffering arise?
What is there to be upset about, and what is there to be pleased about?
When one searches for them in reality,
who is craving, and what are they craving for? ^9-152

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-153]]

When examined, in this world of the living,
who is it that will die here?
What will come to be, and what has been?
And who is a relative, and who is a friend? ^9-153

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-154]]

May those like me fully grasp
that everything is like space.
Those who want happiness for themselves,
because of causes of conflict and excitement, ^9-154

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-155]]

become greatly agitated and delighted.
With grief, effort and quarrels,
cutting and piercing one another,
and through misdeeds, they live in great hardship. ^9-155

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-156]]

They come again and again to happy realms
and enjoy a great deal of happiness.
After they die, they fall into the sufferings
of the lower realms, which last long and cannot be borne. ^9-156

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-157]]

In existence there are many precipices,
and there, what is not reality is of this kind.
Because things there also contradict one another,
in existence there is no such reality. ^9-157

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-158]]

There too, the ocean of suffering,
which is beyond compare and unbearable, has no limit.
There one has little strength,
and there the lifespan is also short. ^9-158

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-159]]

Even there, through the work of staying alive
and healthy, through the weariness of hunger,
sleep, harm, and likewise
meaningless company with childish persons, ^9-159

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-160]]

life passes quickly and meaninglessly,
and analysis is extremely hard to find.
Even there, as for being used to distraction,
where is the means to reverse it? ^9-160

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-161]]

Even there, into the great lower realm,
Māra works hard to make one fall.
There, wrong paths are many,
and even doubt is hard to overcome. ^9-161

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-162]]

Once again, leisure is hard to find,
and the appearance of a Buddha is extremely hard to find.
The river of afflictions is hard to abandon.
Alas, suffering will follow one after another! ^9-162

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-163]]

Even though they are in such extreme suffering,
those who do not see their own suffering,
these beings dwelling in the river of suffering,
alas, are worth lamenting! ^9-163

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-164]]

For example, some people bathe again and again
and then step into a fire again and again.
In the same way, though they live in extreme suffering,
they take pride in being happy. ^9-164

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-165]]

So, for those who live and act
as if there were no aging and death,
first of all they will be killed,
and then the unbearable fall into the lower realms will come. ^9-165

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-166]]

So, for those tormented by the fire of suffering,
when will I calm them
with a rain of my own accumulation of happiness,
well arisen from the clouds of merit? ^9-166

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-167]]

When, in the manner of non-apprehension,
having respectfully gathered the accumulation of merit,
will I teach emptiness
to those who are ruined by apprehension? ^9-167

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-a]]

This is the ninth chapter, the Perfection of Wisdom, from Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva. ^9-a

## Chapter Ten: Dedication ^10-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-1]]

I have composed
Engaging in the Conduct of Awakening.
Through whatever virtue there is in this, may all wandering beings
Engage in the conduct of awakening. ^10-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-2]]

In all directions, all those, however many,
Who are sick with the suffering of body and mind—
Through my merit, may they
Attain an ocean of happiness and joy. ^10-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-3]]

For as long as they remain in saṃsāra,
May they never lose their happiness.
May wandering beings gain the unbroken
Happiness of a bodhisattva. ^10-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-4]]

In the world systems, wherever there are hells,
However many there are,
In them, may embodied beings
Rejoice in the happiness of Sukhavati. ^10-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-5]]

May those tormented by cold find warmth.
From the great clouds of the bodhisattvas,
Through the boundless rivers that have flowed out,
May those tormented by heat be cooled. ^10-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-6]]

May the forest of sword-leaves also
Become a thick sandalwood forest for them.
May the trunks of the śālmali trees also
Grow into wish-fulfilling trees. ^10-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-7]]

May they be made beautiful by the sweet calls
Of waterfowl, ruddy geese, swans, cranes, and other birds,
And may the regions of hell become delightful
With lakes full of sweet-smelling lotuses. ^10-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-8]]

May those heaps of coals turn into heaps of jewels.
May the scorched earth become a smooth crystal floor.
May the mountains of the Crushing Hell also turn into
Celestial palaces of offerings, filled with Sugatas. ^10-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-9]]

May the rain of embers, burning stones, and weapons
From now on turn into a rain of flowers.
And may the striking of one another with weapons
From now on turn into a playful tossing of flowers. ^10-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-10]]

May those who are sunk in the unfordable river that is like a fire pit,
Their flesh all destroyed and their bones the color of kunda flowers,
Gain the bodies of gods through the power of my virtue,
And stay with the goddesses in the gently flowing river. ^10-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-11]]

"Why are the men of Yama, the crows, and the dreadful vultures terrified here?
Whose good power is this, which drives away the darkness everywhere and brings bliss and joy?"
With this thought they look up, and they see the blazing Vajrapāṇi in the middle of the sky.
Freed from misdeeds by the force of their great joy, may they join him. ^10-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-12]]

As a rain of flowers mixed with scented water falls,
They see the fires and embers of hell put out with a hiss,
And they wonder, "What is this sudden happiness that satisfies us?"
May the hell beings see Padmapāṇi. ^10-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-13]]

“Friends, put your fear aside and come quickly here, to us!
Through his power we are freed from all suffering, and a rush of joy arrives.
He completely protects all wandering beings; in him bodhicitta and affection have been born.
Who is this blazing youth with a topknot who comes to make us fearless?” ^10-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-14]]

“You, look at this one. The crowns of a hundred gods worship his lotus feet.
His eyes are moist with compassion, and a rain of many flowers falls on his head.
He has delightful tiered palaces, and thousands of goddesses sing songs of praise!”
Seeing Mañjuśrī like this, may the hell beings now cry out in joy. ^10-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-15]]

In the same way, through my roots of virtue, when they see Samantabhadra and other
Bodhisattvas' unobstructed clouds, and the blissful,
Cool, fragrant rain that they let fall,
May those sentient beings of hell become fully joyful. ^10-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-16]]

May animals be free
From the fear of eating one another.
Just like the humans of Uttarakuru,
May the hungry ghosts become happy. ^10-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-17]]

From the hands of the noble Avalokiteśvara
A stream of milk flows.
May it satisfy the hungry ghosts,
And after they have bathed, may they always be cool. ^10-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-18]]

May the blind see with their eyes,
And may the deaf always hear sounds.
Just like the goddess Māyādevī,
May pregnant women also give birth without harm. ^10-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-19]]

May the naked find clothing,
And the hungry find food.
May the thirsty find water
And delicious drinks. ^10-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-20]]

May the poor find wealth.
May those who are wretched with sorrow find joy.
May the despondent be restored in mind
And gain perfect steadfastness. ^10-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-21]]

However many sick sentient beings there are,
May they soon be freed from illness.
May the illnesses of wandering beings, without exception,
Never arise again. ^10-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-22]]

May the terrified be fearless.
May the bound be freed.
May the powerless be given power,
And may they be kind to one another in mind. ^10-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-23]]

For all travelers,
May every direction be safe and fortunate.
Whatever purpose they travel for,
May it be accomplished without effort. ^10-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-24]]

May those who board boats and large ships
Accomplish what they have in mind,
And after safely reaching the shore,
May they rejoice together with their relatives. ^10-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-25]]

May those who wander lost on desolate paths
Meet with fellow travellers,
And, without fear of bandits, tigers, and the like,
May they go on easily, without tiring. ^10-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-26]]

In fearful, pathless wildernesses and the like,
Children, the elderly, those with no protector,
And those who are asleep, intoxicated, or utterly intoxicated—
May the gods protect them. ^10-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-27]]

May they be free from all states of non-leisure,
Full of faith, wisdom, and compassion,
And perfect in food and conduct.
May they always remember their lifetimes. ^10-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-28]]

Like the treasury of space,
May everyone have enjoyments that never run out.
May they live without quarrels and without harm,
And be free to act on their own. ^10-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-29]]

Whatever sentient beings have little splendor,
May they have great splendor.
Whatever ascetics have an unattractive appearance,
May they gain a perfect, beautiful appearance. ^10-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-30]]

However many women there are in the world,
May they become men.
May the lowly reach a high status,
And may their pride be overcome. ^10-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-31]]

By this merit of mine,
May all sentient beings without exception
Give up all misdeeds
And always do virtue. ^10-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-32]]

May they never be apart from bodhicitta,
May they devote themselves to the conduct of awakening,
May they be completely embraced by the Buddhas,
And may they give up the actions of Māra. ^10-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-33]]

May all those sentient beings
Have lives that are immeasurably long.
May they always live happily,
And may not even the sound of death be heard. ^10-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-34]]

With groves of wish-fulfilling trees,
And with Buddhas and children of the Buddhas
Who proclaim the sweet Dharma,
May all directions be filled! ^10-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-35]]

Everywhere, may the ground
Be free of pebbles and the like,
As level as the palm of a hand,
And stay soft, with the nature of lapis lazuli. ^10-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-36]]

In all the circles of retinues that exist,
May many bodhisattvas,
Through their own excellence,
Adorn the surface of the earth and remain there. ^10-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-37]]

May all embodied beings hear, from birds,
From trees and from all rays of light,
And even from the sky, the sound of the Dharma
Without a break. ^10-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-38]]

May they always meet with the Buddhas
And the children of the Buddhas.
With boundless clouds of offerings,
May they make offerings to the teacher of wandering beings. ^10-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-39]]

May the gods also send rain at the right time,
And may the harvests be plentiful.
May kings act in line with the Dharma,
And may the world also flourish. ^10-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-40]]

May medicines have real power,
And may the recitation of secret mantras succeed.
May ḍākinīs, rākṣasas, and the like
Have minds full of compassion. ^10-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-41]]

May no sentient being suffer.
May none be afraid or scorned,
And may no one ever be unhappy. ^10-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-42]]

May monasteries, made beautiful by reading
And recitation, be well established.
May the Saṅgha always be in harmony,
And may the purposes of the Saṅgha be fulfilled. ^10-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-43]]

May monks who wish to train
Also find solitude.
Having given up all distractions,
May they meditate with minds that are fit for work. ^10-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-44]]

May nuns have what they need
And give up quarreling and harm.
In the same way, may all who have taken ordination
Never let their ethical discipline decline. ^10-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-45]]

May those whose ethical discipline has been broken grow tired of that,
And always bring their misdeeds to an end.
And when they have reached a happy realm,
May their ascetic discipline not decline even there. ^10-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-46]]

May wise persons be honored
And also receive alms.
May their continuums be completely pure,
And may they be famous in all directions. ^10-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-47]]

May they not experience the suffering of the lower realms,
And may they be without hardship.
With bodies better than those of the gods,
May they quickly become Buddhas. ^10-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-48]]

May all sentient beings repeatedly
Make offerings to all the Buddhas,
And through the inconceivable happiness of the Buddhas,
May they always have happiness. ^10-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-49]]

May the bodhisattvas
Succeed in what they have in mind for the welfare of others.
Whatever the Protectors intend,
May sentient beings receive it. ^10-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-50]]

In the same way, may the Pratyekabuddhas
And the Śrāvakas also be happy. ^10-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-51]]

Through the kindness of Mañjuśrī,
May I, until I reach the Ground of Perfect Joy,
Always remember my past lifetimes
And receive ordination. ^10-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-52]]

Even if I have only simple food,
May I live with strength and vitality.
In all my lifetimes,
May I have the perfect conditions to live in a solitary place. ^10-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-53]]

Whenever I want to see him,
Or even want to ask a small question,
May I see the Protector Mañjuśrī himself
With nothing in the way. ^10-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-54]]

To bring about the welfare of all sentient beings
As far as space reaches in the ten directions,
Just as Mañjuśrī acts,
May my conduct be like that too. ^10-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-55]]

As long as space lasts,
And as long as wandering beings are here,
For that long may I also stay,
To remove the suffering of wandering beings. ^10-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-56]]

Whatever the suffering of wandering beings may be,
May all of it ripen upon me.
Through the Saṅgha of bodhisattvas,
May wandering beings enjoy happiness. ^10-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-57]]

The only medicine for the suffering of wandering beings,
The source of all happiness,
May the teaching, together with gain and honor,
Last for a long time. ^10-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-58]]

Through whose kindness a virtuous intellect arises,
To Mañjuśrī I bow down.
Through whose kindness I have flourished,
To the spiritual friend I also bow down. ^10-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-a]]

The tenth chapter of Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, called Dedication. ^10-a

## Colophon ^a-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^a-1]]

Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, written by the master Śāntideva, is finished. ^a-1

## Note on the Translation ^b-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^b-1]]

It was first revised and finalized by the Indian preceptor Sarvajñādeva and the chief editor-translator Bande Paltsek, working from a manuscript from Kashmir. ^b-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^b-2]]

Later, the Indian preceptor Dharmaśrībhadra and the chief editor-translators Rinchen Zangpo and Śākya Lodrö corrected it, translated it, and finalized it, following a manuscript and a commentary from the central region. ^b-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^b-3]]

Still later, the Indian preceptor Sumatikirti and the chief editor-translator, the monk Loden Sherab, revised it correctly, translated it, and finalized it well. ^b-3
