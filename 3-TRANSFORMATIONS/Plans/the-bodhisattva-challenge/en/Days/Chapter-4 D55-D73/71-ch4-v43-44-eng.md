## Today's Verse

> I must cling to this,
> hold a grudge, and wage battle.
> Such defilements
> are nothing but the destroyers of defilements. ^4-43

> Even if I am burned and killed,
> or my head is cut off,
> I will never surrender
> to the enemy of defilements. ^4-44

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 43 and 44 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Let us step together through this gateway of courage: even if our head is cut off, we will never bow before the enemy of the afflictions.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

The afflictions in our mind and their antidotes, doing good, mindfulness, and wisdom, are like two armies drawn up facing each other. Among these, the afflictions are the enemy's army, and the antidotes are our own army. We must set these two armies fighting one another. Relying on the strength of the army of antidotes, we must defeat the enemy army of the afflictions. We must not stay in the middle and remain uninvolved. We must make up our mind and enter this inner battle. We must hold firmly to the antidotes, and we must hold on to our long-standing grudge against the afflictions that have harmed us since beginningless time.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, even if anger rises in me, I will not speak harsh words. I will not bow my head to the afflictions.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ This verse says that whatever suffering comes, we should not bow our head to the enemy of the afflictions. So when anger or craving arises in our mind, not falling under its power is the best practice of taming the mind. If we do this, our mind will find peace.
