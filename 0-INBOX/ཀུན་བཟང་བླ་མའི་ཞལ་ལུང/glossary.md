# Glossary: English – Tibetan – Definition

| Amoghasiddhi                                                   | དོན་ཡོད་གྲུབ་པ                     | “He who accomplishes that which is meaningful”; the Buddha of the Action family.                                                                                         |

| Anuyoga                                                        | —                                  | The second of the three inner yogas and eighth of the nine vehicles; stress is placed on the perfection phase, particularly the channels and energies.                   |
| Aperture of Brahma                                             | ཚངས་པའི་བུ་ག                       | Skt. brahmarandhra; the point on the top of the head where the central channel ends.                                                                                     |

| Arura and kyurura                                              | ཨ་རུ་ར / སྐྱུ་རུ་ར                 | Chebulic and emblic myrobalan, medicinal plants; arura is the emblem of the Medicine Buddha.                                                                             |
| Aryadeva                                                       | འཕགས་པ་ལྷ                          | (2nd century) The most famous disciple of Nagarjuna, commentator on Madhyamika philosophy.                                                                               |
| Asanga                                                         | ཐོགས་མེད                           | (4th century) One of the Six Ornaments, founder of the Yogachara school; received five teachings from Maitreya.                                                          |

| Atiyoga                                                        | —                                  | The highest of the three inner yogas, summit of the nine vehicles. See Great Perfection.                                                                                 |
| Avalokiteshvara                                                | སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས                      | (Chenrezi) One of the Eight Great Close Sons; essence of the speech of all Buddhas, incarnation of their compassion.                                                     |

| Bindu                                                          | ཐིག་ལེ                             | Lit. circle, sphere, point or drop, with a range of more abstract meanings; also translated as essence.                                                                  |
| Bliss (experience)                                             | བདེ་ཉམས                            | One of the three types of meditation experience. See experiences.                                                                                                        |
| Bliss and emptiness                                            | བདེ་སྟོང                           | Bliss experienced without attachment, as being empty.                                                                                                                    |
| Blissful pure land                                             | བདེ་བ་ཅན                           | Skt. Sukhavati; the Buddhafield of the West, that of Buddha Amitabha.                                                                                                    |
| Bodhicitta                                                     | བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས                   | “Mind of enlightenment”: relatively, the wish to attain Buddhahood for all beings and the practices to achieve it; absolutely, direct insight into the ultimate nature.  |
| Bodhisattva                                                    | བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ                   | A being who has decided to bring all beings to enlightenment and practises the Bodhisattva path; or a sublime Bodhisattva on one of the ten levels.                      |

| Bodhisattva levels                                             | འཕགས་པའི་ས                         | Skt. bhumi; the ten sublime levels of realization reached by Bodhisattvas on the paths of seeing, meditation and beyond learning.                                        |
