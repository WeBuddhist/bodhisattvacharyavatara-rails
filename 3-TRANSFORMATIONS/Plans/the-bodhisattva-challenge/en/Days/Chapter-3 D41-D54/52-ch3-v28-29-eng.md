## Today's Verse

> This is the supreme nectar
> that conquers the Lord of Death for beings.
> This is also the inexhaustible treasure
> that removes the poverty of beings. ^3-28

> This is also the supreme medicine
> that perfectly cures the illnesses of beings.
> It is a resting tree for beings
> who wander and are weary on the road of existence. ^3-29

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 28 and 29 from the third chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. A traveller worn out after a long road finds a cool tree to rest under at ease. In the same way, you should look closely today at the meaning of these two verses.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses tell us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago in Varanasi there was a king called Wangchen De. The king of the southern land gathered an army and made war on Varanasi. So that his own people would not commit wrongdoing, King Wangchen De gave up the kingdom and went to the forest. There he lived on roots and fruit.

At that time a poor brahmin heard that the king gave away whatever was asked of him. So he came to find him. The king had himself bound and carried before the king of the southern land, and told the brahmin to ask for whatever he wanted.

The king of the southern land was amazed. He gave the kingdom back and returned to his own country. The brahmin too was satisfied with wealth, and everyone was set in the ten good actions. The Buddha said that he himself was King Wangchen De at that time.

The king gave his body and his kingdom without holding anything back, and so removed the brahmin's poverty. In the same way, this shows that bodhicitta is the treasure that never runs out, which removes the poverty of all beings.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will comfort one friend who is weighed down by worry, and give them some rest.

**Explanation:** _(Generosity Practice)_ Many beings are worn out on the road of samsara and worn down by the illness of suffering. Bodhicitta is like the medicine that calms that illness, and like the tree that gives them rest. If today we help and comfort someone with love, we accomplish the supreme cause of bodhicitta.
