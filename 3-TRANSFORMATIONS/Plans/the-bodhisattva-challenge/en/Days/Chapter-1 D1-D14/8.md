# Day 8 — Even wishing to cure a headache counts

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Wish one good thing for someone struggling
> **Detail:** Even the smallest wish to help carries great merit. Today, wish clearly for one person.

## Opening

Two verses, one step of reasoning. Even the smallest wish to help, hoping to cure a single headache, brings immeasurable merit. So think what the wish to free every being from all suffering, and bring them every good thing, must carry.

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.

>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, tells the story of a woman named Maitrakanya. She had harmed her mother and was reborn in a hell where a burning iron wheel turned on her head. In the middle of that pain, one thought rose in her: may everyone who would suffer like this have it fall on me instead. With that single thought the wheel vanished, and she passed at once into a happier realm. One real wish to take on another's pain, even in the worst place imaginable, carries the power this verse points to.

## Today's Practice

Think of one person you know who is going through something hard, an illness, a loss, a situation with no way out. Just once, as clearly as you can, wish: may they be free of this. Don't judge whether you did it well. The wish itself is what counts.
