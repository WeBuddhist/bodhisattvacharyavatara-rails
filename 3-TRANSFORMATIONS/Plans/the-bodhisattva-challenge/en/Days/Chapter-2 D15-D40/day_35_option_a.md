# Day 35 — You don't have to win the fight yourself

> **Notification**
> **Title:** When a fear looms today, bring strength near instead of wrestling it
> **Detail:** Today's verse turns to Vajrapani, whose mere presence scatters what threatens us.

## Opening

The refuge crescendo reaches its last and most forceful protector. Shantideva turns to Vajrapani, the bodhisattva who holds the vajra and embodies the buddhas' power. The point of the verse is striking: just the sight of him sends the messengers of death and other menacing forces fleeing in every direction. Refuge here is not only gentle comfort. It is strength.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, lingers on three small words: merely on seeing him. The threatening forces do not have to be fought, argued with, or defeated one by one. The moment Vajrapani's strength comes into view, they scatter on their own. Sazang's point is about what kind of refuge this is. The earlier verses called on compassion, the warm side of help. This one calls on power, and it answers a real human need. Some fears do not respond to being soothed or reasoned with; they only ease when something stronger is present. We know this in small ways, how a frightening room changes the instant a steady person walks in. The verse says the same steadiness is available at a far deeper level. You are not asked to overpower your fear. You are asked to turn toward a strength beside which it cannot hold its ground.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Once today, when a fear or a harsh, looming thought presses on you, notice the urge to fight it head on. Instead, try bringing strength near: picture the steady power of Vajrapani, or simply sense something far stronger than the fear standing with you. You do not have to wrestle the thought down. Let the stronger presence be there, and watch the threat lose some of its size.
