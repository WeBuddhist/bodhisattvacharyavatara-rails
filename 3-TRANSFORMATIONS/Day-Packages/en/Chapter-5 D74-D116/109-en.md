## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the eighty-ninth and ninetieth verses of the fifth chapter. We do not hope only for a quick shortcut. We train our own mind to become a good vessel for the Dharma.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable quotation, which is easy to understand and encouraging. The *Heap of Jewels Sutra* says as follows. It is a mistake of a bodhisattva to teach the vast Dharma of the Buddha to beings who have not become vessels. It is also a mistake of a bodhisattva to teach the lesser vehicle to beings who have deep confidence in the vast Dharma. Therefore, we must teach each person the Dharma that suits them. We do not teach the vast Dharma to those who are not vessels. We do not teach the lesser vehicle to those who have deep confidence in the vast Dharma.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, whether a Dharma teaching is lesser or higher, I will listen with great respect, making no difference between them.

**Explanation:** *(Taming the mind)* If we show equal respect to all the Buddha's Dharma, our pride becomes calm and our good actions increase. By making no difference between lesser and higher Dharma, we tame our own mind, as the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* intends.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

"To the lowly, do not teach the profound and vast, / nor to women without men present." And further: although one must teach sentient beings what to adopt and what to abandon and lead them onto the path, one should not teach the Dharma to those who lack respect. When the Dharma is taught, it must become something of real benefit; it is not like the teaching of a mere speaker of polished composition (ལེགས་སྦྱར). Taking the case of those who are not ill, one should not teach the Dharma to people who have their heads wrapped, who carry umbrellas and staffs, who carry weapons, who wear hats on their heads, or who cover their heads with a cloth, and the like.

Above, having shown general conduct, now comes how to carry the inner thought. If one teaches the profound and the vast to those of lesser intelligence, which does not suit their minds *(the rail's word ཁཱུའི་ before བློ་ is unclear)*, wrong views may arise; and sometimes, even if wrong views do not arise, there is the danger that discouragement will arise — "I could never do that." To that kind of person one should not teach the profound and vast Dharma. Likewise, speaking of a fully ordained monk who keeps his discipline, he should not teach a woman when there is no man as a companion in the discipline. Again, if one is a novice nun, she should not teach the Dharma to a man when there is no woman as a companion in the discipline; extend this to similar cases.

"One should always act with equal respect / towards the teachings of the lesser and the supreme." As was just said, although there are differences between the profound and vast Dharma and the lesser and smaller, it is not acceptable to cling to the good as good, or to belittle the lesser. As the *Ornament of Clear Realization* says, "Those who benefit beings accomplish the aims of the world through knowing the paths themselves." Since bodhisattvas who have pledged to accomplish the benefit of others must know the paths in this way, needless to say they must know their own bodhisattva path. Because the circle of those to be trained includes people of superior, middling, and various faculties, even the shravaka path must be known in order to take others under one's care. According to the element and inclination of each of the many kinds of sentient beings, there is no word of the Buddha — spoken by a buddha who is skilled in means and endowed with great compassion — that cannot accomplish this or that quality and exhaust this or that fault. All of that must be accomplished by a bodhisattva, as is taught in the *Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path* (Lamrim Chenmo). Although, by relative positioning of things, there are lesser and higher teachings, since all of them are needed in order to accomplish others' benefit, there is no word of the Buddha that does not serve the benefit of beings; therefore one should take all of them respectfully upon the crown of one's head. As the Mother Sutra says, "With these, one also does the work of the path" — for it says so.

##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

To those of lesser mind, who are inclined toward the path of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas, one should not teach the profound emptiness of the Great Vehicle, nor the vast [subjects such as] the grounds and paths; and a renunciate, when no man is present as a companion, should not teach the Dharma even to a woman alone — it is not permissible to teach. Even when teaching the Dharma, toward the teachings of the lesser (shravaka and pratyekabuddha) and the supreme (the Great Vehicle and so on), one should not take pleasure in teaching some and be unwilling to teach others, and one should not look on them as better and worse, as when elders and others are given different food *(the rail's phrase རྒན་དང་བྱིན་པ་ཁ་ཟས་ཐ་དད་པ་ལྟར is unclear; rendered tentatively)*. Rather, on the path to liberation, recognizing them as equal or the same, like the flavors of brown sugar and salt, one should act with respect in every way — that is, one must teach. For the *Sutra That Gathers the Weaving of All Dharmas* says, "Mañjuśrī, if someone regards some of the Dharma taught by the Tathāgata as good and some as bad, that is abandoning the sacred Dharma."

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

To those who are not vessels, whose motivation is lesser, one should not teach the profound and vast Dharma, nor to a woman alone when no man is present. The teachings of the lesser and the supreme — such as the Dharma of the greater and lesser vehicles — if one separates them individually into good and bad, proper and improper, whether they become a means to buddhahood or not, and so on, that is said to be abandoning the Dharma *(the rail's wording ཆོས་དང་། སྤོང་དུ་གསུངས་པས is garbled here; rendered by sense)*. Therefore one should practice and take up all of them with equal respect.

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

One should not teach the profound and vast Dharma to lesser individuals, nor teach the Dharma in seclusion to a woman when no man is present. Toward the teachings of the lesser shravakas and of the supreme Great Vehicle, one acts with equal respect. Otherwise one comes to abandon the Dharma, as it is said in the *Sutra That Gathers the Weaving of All Dharmas*: "Mañjuśrī, if someone regards some of the Dharma taught by the Tathāgata as good and some as bad, that is abandoning the sacred Dharma."

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

To disciples of lesser mind, one should not teach the profound and vast Dharma of the ultimate Great Vehicle, because it does not fit their minds and so would become the act of abandoning the Dharma.

A renunciate should not teach the Dharma even to a woman alone, with no other man present, because others would criticize and a downfall would result.

As for the teachings of the lesser shravaka vehicle and of the supreme Great Vehicle, they are profound within the range of each disciple's own mind; therefore, abandoning notions of good and bad toward them, one should act with respect toward all of them, thinking, "All of these were equally spoken by the Buddha." <!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

One should not teach the profound and vast Dharma of the Great Vehicle, whose nature is profound and vast beyond fathoming, to disciples who are not vessels — those of lesser mind, or of an untrained, lesser mind — since it does not suit their minds; if it is taught, it does not fit in their minds, and so they would gather the obscuration of abandoning the Dharma. As the *Sutra of the Essence of the Earth* says:

> Likewise, to non-vessels, followers of the shravaka vehicle, / hearing the Great Vehicle taught, they become bewildered, / and, falling into nihilistic views, fall into the lower realms; / therefore, having examined their faculties, teach the Dharma.

For so it is said; and it is also taught that teaching emptiness to one whose mind is untrained becomes a root downfall.

A renunciate should not teach the Dharma to a woman alone — only a woman, in a secluded place with opportunity, when no suitable man is present as a companion in the discipline — because others would criticize and a downfall would arise for oneself. And as for the teachings of the lesser shravaka vehicle and the supreme Great Vehicle: not knowing that all the Buddha's word, directly or indirectly, shows the means to attain buddhahood, if one grasps some as a means to buddhahood and some as an obstacle to buddhahood, separates them individually into good and bad, proper and improper, and so on, and takes up as something to be rejected, saying "A bodhisattva should train in this, should not train in that," it is taught that one gathers the karma of abandoning the Dharma. Therefore, abandoning the mind that grasps the teachings of the greater and lesser vehicles as good and bad and so on, and knowing that the Dharma spoken by the Buddha is equal — without difference in its function as an antidote to the afflictions — one must act with respect toward all of it; for, relative to each disciple's own mind, all of it is good and extremely profound. In the *Sutra That Gathers the Weaving*:

> Mañjuśrī, the obscuration of the karma of abandoning the sacred Dharma is subtle. Mañjuśrī, whoever regards some of the words spoken by the Tathāgata as good and some as bad is abandoning the Dharma. Whoever abandons the Dharma, by abandoning the Dharma slanders the Tathāgata and speaks ill of the Sangha. If one says, "This is proper; this is improper," it is abandoning the Dharma. If one says, "This was spoken for the sake of bodhisattvas; this was spoken for the sake of shravakas," it is abandoning the Dharma. If one says, "This was spoken for the sake of pratyekabuddhas," it is abandoning the Dharma. If one says, "This is not a training of bodhisattvas," it is abandoning the Dharma.

and much more was said. The fault of abandoning the Dharma is exceedingly great. In the *Moon Lamp*:

> Than one who, in this Jambudvīpa, destroys all the stupas, / the sin of one who abandons the sutras is far greater. / Than one who has killed arhats as many as the sands of the Ganges, / the sin of one who abandons the sutras is far greater.

and many others are also said.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-89]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

"One should always act with equal respect" — this means the following. To such a student at the place of Dharma teaching — one who has become of lesser mind, like those of the shravaka or pratyekabuddha lineage — one should not teach the exceedingly profound emptiness of the Great Vehicle Dharma, nor the vast presentations of the grounds and paths. Likewise, if the Dharma teacher is a renunciate, he should not teach the Dharma to a woman alone, with no man present, inside a single house, because there is the danger that others will disparage him and so on and that faults will arise that harm the teachings.

Likewise — *(the rail has a stray ཞེས་ here, with the verse quotation not given)* at the time of teaching the Dharma, as for the Dharma to be taught: the lesser vehicle such as the Dharma of the shravakas, and the Great Vehicle, the supreme — all of these are the word of the Buddha, who teaches according to whichever disciple is to be tamed; in short, all of them are the same, or alike, in being causes of liberation from samsara. Other than acting with respect after recognizing them in this way, if one shows disrespect toward the Dharma of the lesser vehicle, or clings to the Dharma of the Great Vehicle and so on, then, as the sutra says, "To regard some of the Dharma spoken by the Buddha as good and some as bad is to have gathered the karma of abandoning the Dharma," and so it is taught how great faults arise for that person.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Two Cautions Before Teaching: Capacity of Mind and Seclusion

Do not teach the profound and vast Dharma to those of lesser mind, and do not teach the Dharma, in seclusion, to a woman when no man is present.

###### 2. Equal Respect for the Lesser and the Higher Teachings, and No Abandoning of the Dharma

Act with equal respect toward the teachings of the lesser shravaka vehicle and of the supreme Great Vehicle, and do not abandon the Dharma.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** When teaching the Dharma, it is taught that one should not teach profound and vast Dharma to those of lesser mind, nor teach women when no man is present, and that one must act with equal respect toward all the teachings of the greater and lesser vehicles.

**Key points.**

- One should not teach the profound and vast Great Vehicle Dharma to those of lesser mind, or to disciples who are not vessels.
- A renunciate, or a person of good discipline, should not teach the Dharma to a woman in a secluded place when no man, or no companion in the discipline, is present.
- One must practice and take up with equal respect the teachings of the lesser vehicle of the shravakas and of the Great Vehicle.
- If one regards the Dharma as good or bad and so abandons the Dharma, great faults arise.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

Those who have become vessels for the vast Great Vehicle Dharma should not be linked to the teachings of the lesser vehicle. "Conduct" means what is to be trained in: one should not completely abandon it. Turning away from the training, one should not mislead others by saying, "By merely reading sutras and reciting dhāraṇīs and mantras you will be purified."

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

It seems such people are rare among Tibetans *(in the rail the phrase བོད་པའི་ནང་དཀོན་པོ་འདུག runs on without a break into the verse line)*. "One who has become a vessel for the vast Dharma" — that is, there are a few disciples who, having become vessels for the vast Dharma of the Great Vehicle, can be taught it, and one who is able to put it into practice should not be linked to the lesser Dharma. Not only should one not link them to the lesser Dharma, but, since one must lead them into what they are to train in, one should not abandon it. To one who can practice the great practice, without giving the opportunity to study and train, [offering] only things like reading texts for immediate relief and the mere reciting of mantras — if one could do nothing but that kind of thing, there would be nothing else to be done; but while a person is able to do something else, something vast, one should not set that aside and mislead with sutras and mantras.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-90]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Those of the Great Vehicle lineage with sharp faculties, who have become vessels for the profound and vast great Dharma, should not be linked to the lesser Dharma, the path of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas. If it is applied, it becomes a root downfall, and it is not suitable to make the wise merely chant aloud or to lead the faithful around in circles *(the rail's wording here, ཤེས་རབ་ཅན་སྒྲོག་ཏུ་བཅུག་པ་དང༌། དད་པ་ཅན་མགོ་བསྐོར་མི་རུང་བ, is unclear; rendered tentatively)*.

Apart from occasions when the profound view must be taught as the main thing, one should not completely abandon the conduct of karma and its results — the benefits of virtue, the drawbacks of non-virtue, and so on — since virtue and non-virtue, cause and effect, must be taught above all. To a person who is able to practice the bodhisattva's conduct, one should not mislead or deceive by the literal words saying, "Merely reading a little of a sutra with benefits, or reciting a little of a dhāraṇī or mantra, is enough; by that alone one will be liberated." Rather, one should distinguish between the provisional and the definitive, the implicit intention, and so on, and take care of others by teaching the Dharma in accordance with the vessel.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-90]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Those who have become vessels for the vast Dharma, of the Great Vehicle lineage, should not be linked to the lesser Dharma — for example, by leading them along the shravaka path — because it is taught that a downfall will occur. One should also not completely abandon the conduct of the ethics of the vows: while being fit as a vessel for that, one should not mislead others with sutras and mantras by saying, "Merely reading sutras and mantras will purify you."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-90]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Those who have become vessels for the vast Dharma should not be linked to the lesser Dharma. For in the *Heap of Jewels* it says, "To beings who have not become vessels, teaching the vast Dharma of the Buddha is an error of a bodhisattva; and to beings who have deep confidence in the vast, teaching the lesser vehicle is an error of a bodhisattva." One should also not completely abandon conduct — that is, the training — and, having abandoned it, one should not mislead others by saying, "Merely reading sutras and mantras will bring liberation."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-147]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Those who have become vessels for the vast and profound Dharma of the Great Vehicle should not be linked to the lesser Dharma of the small vehicle. And those who have entered the Great Vehicle should not, thinking "Merely realizing emptiness liberates," completely abandon even the subtle conduct of the bodhisattva's trainings and vows; and even if one does abandon it, one should not entice and deceive others by the literal words "By merely reading a little of a sutra and reciting a little of a mantra, you will be liberated." Rather, one should benefit others with the Dharma that suits each one's own mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-90]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

To disciples of the Great Vehicle lineage who have become vessels for the Great Vehicle Dharma that teaches the profound and vast meaning, one should not apply the lesser Dharma — for example, by showing them the path of the lesser vehicle — for it is also taught that if one applies it, a bodhisattva's error and a root downfall arise. Thus, in the *Questions of Ösrung (Kāśyapa)*:

> Trusting in beings who have not been fully ripened and teaching [them] is an error of a bodhisattva; to beings who have not become vessels, teaching the vast Dharma of the Buddha is an error of a bodhisattva; and to beings who have confidence in the vast *(the rail reads རྒྱུ་ཆེན་པོ་, "great cause," here)*, teaching the lesser vehicle is an error of a bodhisattva — and so on: four errors are taught.

Likewise, one should not cause those who have entered the Great Vehicle to completely abandon the conduct of ethical discipline — the vows against misconduct — or the subtle and vast things a bodhisattva ought to train in, on the grounds that "liberation comes only through realizing emptiness and nothing else is needed." And likewise, though one is a vessel for it, one should not entice or deceive disciples who seek liberation *(the rail reads གདུལ་བྱ་དགུ་འདྲིད་ here; the word དགུ་ is unclear)* by clinging to the mere literal words, and causing them to want and trust that, without training in the conduct of the trainings and vows, merely reading profound sutras and reciting a little of the dhāraṇīs and mantras of great benefit will bring purification and liberation.

In these contexts: among the eight root downfalls likely to occur to ordinary beginner bodhisattvas, the first is the defeat of teaching the profound to non-vessels, the second is the defeat of turning away from the Great Vehicle, and the third is the defeat of abandoning the Prātimokṣa; how much brings them about and how they arise and so on should be known elsewhere. Therefore one must take others under one's care and benefit them by teaching the Dharma that suits each disciple's capacities; as the *Ten Wheels of Kṣitigarbha* says:

> The wise who practice the giving of Dharma / teach the Dharma of the three vehicles according to the vessel. / They do not teach other vehicles to them; / having heard, they do not scorn or revile it. / They teach the sacred Dharma according to the faculty-vessel, / not to those whose faculties are not vessels. / According to what each is inclined toward, / they teach this and that Dharma and please them. / To a person practicing the Great Vehicle / they do not teach the Dharma of the two [lesser] vehicles. / To persons of the two vehicles, middling and supreme, / they teach the Dharma of the middling and supreme vehicles.

And in the Great Commentary as well: "Therefore a bodhisattva must first know the intentions and latent dispositions of other beings and other individuals, and then teach the Dharma gradually in accordance with the sentient beings' intentions," it says. To teach in that way is also difficult without having attained the clairvoyance that knows the disciples' capacities, and without clairvoyance it is hard to benefit others. As the *Lamp for the Path* says:

> Just as a bird whose wings have not grown / cannot fly in the sky, / so, without the power of clairvoyance, / one cannot work for the benefit of beings.

and so on. It is also said that even bodhisattvas on the paths of accumulation and joining, not knowing disciples' intentions as they are, mostly teach the Dharma to no purpose; as in the *Ornament of the Sutras*, "Mostly meaningless and meaningful, and ..." and so on. Therefore, as in the sutra, "Having heard much, in the depths of the forest ..." and so on, and "One should urge one who has heard much to dwell in seclusion" and so on, one who has heard much must first dwell in seclusion and make practice the heart, and must not enter upon speaking the Dharma from the beginning. In the chapter on the accumulation of samadhi too: "Thus, one should prevent a bodhisattva who has heard much from entering upon speaking the Dharma; the Victor has not said that, before one has attained clairvoyance, one benefits others through having merely heard much," and so on; and in the *Compendium of Training* as well: "Thus, one who has heard much should dwell in the wilderness in order to tame the mind," and so on. The praise of hearing much, and how to go about hearing and so on, should be known in the chapter on diligence of that same text. These are the conduct of gathering others while keeping oneself unstained by faults.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-90]]

*(The rail's block opens with the bare particle ཞེས་པ་, the verse quotation not being given.)* That student at the place of Dharma teaching — one of sharp faculties whose Great Vehicle lineage has awakened — who has also become fit as a vessel for the profound and vast Great Vehicle Dharma: such a one should not be linked even to the Dharma of the lesser vehicle, such as that of the shravakas. For it is taught that if one applies it in that way, it becomes a root downfall relating to the bodhisattva vows of the Great Vehicle. In short, as it is said, "Though the view is equal to the sky, / conduct must be fine as barley flour" — the meaning is that one should not abandon the adopting and avoiding of karmic cause and effect, accomplishing virtue and abandoning vice in all their forms.

Again, taking as the reason that some sutras say that if one recites this and this five, ten, or a hundred times and so on, one will gain such and such benefits; and again that some dhāraṇīs and mantras say that if one recites them this many times, such and such results will be obtained — by that means one's sins will be purified, and no other Dharma is needed, and so on: one should not entice or deceive others in these ways either. #### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Do Not Apply the Lesser Teachings to Those Who Are Vessels for the Vast

One should not apply the lesser Dharma to those who have become vessels for the vast Dharma.

###### 2. Conduct and Training Are Not Dropped in Favor of Sutra-Reading and Mantra-Reciting

One should not completely abandon conduct and training, and one should not mislead others into thinking that merely reading sutras and mantras will bring liberation.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** It is taught that one should not apply the lesser Dharma, or the path of the shravakas, to disciples who have become vessels for the profound and vast Great Vehicle Dharma. It is taught that one should not abandon the bodhisattva's conduct and training, and that one should not mislead or deceive others merely by reading sutras and mantras.

**Key points.**

- To teach the Dharma of the lesser vehicle to those who have become vessels for the Great Vehicle Dharma is a bodhisattva's error and a root downfall.
- One should not completely abandon the bodhisattva's vows and the trainings of ethical conduct.
- One should not mislead or deceive others into thinking that, after abandoning conduct, merely reading sutras and reciting mantras will bring liberation.
- To say, "Realizing emptiness brings liberation and nothing else is needed," and so not to give up misconduct, is a root downfall; therefore one must be careful about karma and its results, and the wise teach the three vehicles and the provisional and definitive teachings in accordance with the disciples' vessel and intention.
