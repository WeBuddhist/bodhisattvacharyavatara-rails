## Today's Verse

> it is as if I have been deluded by a spell.
> I simply have no mind here.
> I do not know what deludes me;
> what is within me? ^4-27

> Enemies such as hatred and craving
> do not possess hands or feet.
> Yet, in some way, though not brave or wise,
> they have made me their slave. ^4-28

> While dwelling in my mind,
> they harm me with delight.
> Not to be angry at them, but to endure—
> this is inappropriate patience, a cause for scorn. ^4-29

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 27 to 29 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. The afflictions are an enemy with no hands or feet, yet they harm us from inside our own mind. Let us look again and again at our own mind and ask why we practice patience with them and let them make us their slave.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Kyabje Ling Rinpoche once had a teacup. One day strong anger arose in him, and he threw the teacup to the ground and broke it. When the anger had calmed and his mind was at ease again, he gathered up the broken pieces of the cup, and deep regret arose in him. The affliction of anger had completely broken down his good sense and his self-control.

This story shows us that even though the afflictions have no body or weapon, they live inside our own mind and use us like their slave. It is not right for us to practice patience with an affliction that is harming us. So when the afflictions take control of our mind, we must resist them.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, the moment an affliction arises in me, I will recognize it as an enemy and not welcome it.

**Explanation:** _(Recognizing the afflictions)_ The afflictions are the true enemy of our mind. If we practice patience with our own anger and craving, this is a mistake. If we give them free rein, we ruin ourselves. So the moment an affliction arises, we must recognize it as an enemy and stop it.
