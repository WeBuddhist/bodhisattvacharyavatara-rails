## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the seventieth, seventy-first, and seventy-second verses of the fifth chapter. We regard our own body as a boat. We train in closing doors gently and looking at others with a smile.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. To cross the ocean of samsara, we must see this body of ours as a fine boat. We give the body only the wages of food and clothes. We do not use it for wrongdoing. We use it for the good actions of generating bodhicitta and the six perfections. In the end, we accomplish the form body of a buddha, take many different forms, and accomplish the vast benefit of beings.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, I will give up frowning and speak honestly to everyone, with a smile.

**Explanation:** *(Doing good)* If we always speak honestly with a smiling face, people become happy. If we give up frowning and fake shyness and make friends with a good heart, both we and others benefit.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

As a mere support for going and coming, / regard the body as a boat; / to accomplish beings' aims, / transform it into a wish-fulfilling body — this says: looking at it as a mere support for going and coming, regard the body as a boat, and for the sake of accomplishing the aims of beings, one must use this body. Moreover, one regards it as a boat for travelling to the far shore of the ocean of samsara, to the precious island of the three bodies — the royal seat of great enlightenment, the liberation of lasting happiness; and, putting this body to work in order to accomplish the aims of beings, one must see to it that in the end this body becomes a victor's body.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-70]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

"Regarding the body merely as a boat" — a mere support for going to liberation and omniscience and for coming back for the sake of beings — means that just as a boat is guarded so that it does not fall into two extremes, so this body too should be guarded with food and clothing that do not fall into the two extremes of the good and the bad. In order to accomplish the aims of beings well, generate the good intention, bodhicitta, and the good conduct, training well in the six perfections; then transform it into a body like a wish-fulfilling jewel, a king of power, that gives meaning to all beings who see, hear, remember, or touch it — that is, into the body of a victor.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-70]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

In order to strive at virtue, regard the body, as a mere support for going and coming, as a boat for travelling to the far shore of the ocean of samsara; and in order to accomplish the aims of beings, transform it into a body like a precious wish-fulfilling jewel — the body of a fully accomplished buddha, the fruit.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-70]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Therefore, just as a boat is a support for going and coming on water, so the body is a mere support for taking oneself and others across the ocean of samsara and so on; so regard the body as a boat, and in order to accomplish the aims of beings, transform it into a wish-fulfilling body, that is, into the body of a victor, and relying on it, accomplish that.

*(Editorial note: this is the closing segment of Ngulchu Thogme's continuous block ^5-112/^5-113, shared across 5-66 (second half) through 5-70.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-113]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

For example, just as one builds and holds a boat on a great river as a mere support for going back and forth, so, in order to engage in what is to be adopted and what is to be abandoned, place in mind that this body of one's own too is like a fine boat; and in order to accomplish the vast aim of benefiting beings, having given it mere livelihood, transform it into a body that accomplishes whatever action one wishes in the mind, and be diligent in the aims of others. This is the meaning.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-70]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

For that reason, for example, in the world one gets a fine boat ready and keeps it, and the work of its leak-mending and so on (ཟག་ལྷན; rendering tentative) is only for the sake of a support for going to the far shore of a great river and coming back to this shore; just so,

so also with this body of one's own: as a support for taking oneself and others across the ocean of samsara and, having crossed, for accomplishing the aims of beings and so on, place in mind that it is like a fine boat. From now on, like the boat's leak-mending, give it only the wages of food, clothing, and so on, and do not put it to sinful actions that are causes of harm to oneself and others; put it to virtuous actions — the intention of bodhicitta, the conduct of the six perfections, and so on — and accomplish the aims of beings as far as one can. And finally, in order to bring the two aims to their completion, accomplish the Form Body of a fully awakened buddha — that very wish-fulfilling precious jewel, the source of everything desired and needed — and, transforming the body into the various bodies that tame whomever there is to be tamed by whatever means, come back into samsara and, for as long as space remains, accomplish the aims of beings through effortless, spontaneous enlightened activity. Thus one counsels one's own mind;

this body is the support for, for the sake of oneself and others, turning away from what is not merit and engaging in merit; so relying on it, one properly engages in and turns away from white and black actions; practicing such a path, one accomplishes the state of a buddha; and finally the support for engaging in the aims of beings also is a matter of relying on it. Therefore, because one must take up the body of whoever is to be tamed and engage in others' aims for as long as space remains, it was said: "regard the body as a boat." This shows directly the actual way of taking the essence in what passes away (འདས་དུས་ལ, literally "in the time that has passed").

Her extended closing survey of citations that follows immediately, drawing on the *Śikṣāsamuccaya* and other sources, closes out the whole section 5-53 to 5-70 rather than glossing this verse specifically, and is not reproduced here.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-70]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

As a mere support for going and coming, / regarding the body as a boat, / in order to accomplish beings' aims, / transform it into a wish-fulfilling body — this means: oneself going to the state of a fully awakened buddha beyond the ocean of samsara, and again, once buddhahood has been attained, a mere support for coming back into samsara in order to liberate other beings from samsara — regard this very body as a boat; and in order to accomplish the aims of all beings, both in the interim and ultimately, this very body must in the end be transformed into a body like a wish-fulfilling jewel, a source of all that is desired and needed — that is, the Form Body of a buddha adorned with the major and minor marks. This is what is shown.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Regard the Body as a Boat, Then Transform It into a Victor's Body

This verse shows that one must regard the body as a boat, put it to work to accomplish the aims of beings, and in the end transform it into the body of a victor, like a wish-fulfilling jewel; and so it concludes the position of this whole section on giving up attachment to the body and taking its essence.

###### 2. Minyak Kunzang Sonam Explains the Boat Example in Detail

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains the boat example in detail and sets down the conclusion: relying on the body, one properly engages in and avoids white and black actions, accomplishes the state of a buddha, and then transforms the body into many different forms for the sake of those to be tamed.

###### 3. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and His Holiness: The Boat Serves Both Going and Coming

Both Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and His Holiness the Dalai Lama explain the thought of the boat as a support both for going to the state of a buddha beyond the ocean of samsara and for coming back for the sake of beings.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This body is to be looked after by regarding it as a boat for crossing the ocean of samsara — a mere support for going to liberation and omniscience and for coming back for the sake of beings. Training in the intention of bodhicitta and in the conduct of the six perfections, one must put this body to virtuous action in order to accomplish the vast aims of all beings. In the end it is to be transformed into a victor's body — like a wish-fulfilling jewel, a source of all that is desired and needed, which gives meaning to everything that is seen, heard, remembered, or touched.

**Key points.**

- Regarding this body as a boat — a mere support for going to liberation and for coming for the sake of others — one should look after it, and in order to accomplish the aims of limitless beings, transform it into the very body of a victor, like a wish-fulfilling jewel.
- Having merely regarded the body as a boat — a support for going to liberation and omniscience and for coming for the sake of beings — generate bodhicitta and train well in the six perfections, and transform it into the body of a victor, like a wish-fulfilling jewel, a king of power, which gives meaning to all beings who see, hear, remember, or touch it.
- In order to strive at virtue, regard the body, as a mere support for going and coming, as a boat for travelling to the far shore of the ocean of samsara, and in order to accomplish the aims of beings, transform it into a body like a precious wish-fulfilling jewel — the body of a fully accomplished buddha, the fruit.
- Since a boat is a support for going and coming on water, and the body is likewise a mere support for crossing the ocean of samsara, regard the body as a boat; transform it, to accomplish the aims of beings, into the body of a victor, and relying on it, accomplish that.
- Just as one holds a boat on a great river as a mere support for going back and forth, so, in order to engage in what is to be adopted and abandoned, regard the body as a fine boat, give it only a livelihood so as to accomplish the vast benefit of beings, and be diligent in the aims of others.
- Regarding the body as a fine boat, as a support for crossing the ocean of samsara, give it only the wages of food and clothing, do not put it to sinful actions, but put it to the virtuous actions of bodhicitta and the six perfections; and finally, having accomplished the Form Body of a fully awakened buddha, like a wish-fulfilling precious jewel, transform it into the various bodies that tame whomever is to be tamed, and accomplish the aims of beings.
- In order to go to the state of a fully awakened buddha and, in order to free beings from samsara, to come back, regard the body as a boat — a mere support — and in the end transform it into a body like a wish-fulfilling jewel, a buddha's Form Body adorned with the major and minor marks.
- Regarding the body as a boat for travelling to the far shore of the ocean of samsara, the precious island of the three bodies, put this body to work to accomplish the aims of beings, and see to it that in the end this body becomes a victor's body.

##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

So — one does not let oneself become like the body's servant, but makes oneself independent: one must make this body capable of being put to work for accomplishing virtue. As far as the body is concerned, what no other beings can conceive — the meaning of emptiness — what no one can hear, one lets go, to vanish of itself. If one can cherish others in that way, one has become independent; and when that happens, the mind is filled with joy and courage, and so one must always compose the face and, without needing to show a displeasing look, make it a smiling face; likewise, one completely abandons the frown, with the brows gathered on the forehead like a scowl; one must be close to beings with a warm heart, a friend, and speak honestly to beings.

Always, when looking at others, put on a smiling face. #### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Make Body, Speech, and Mind Independent and Always Wear a Smiling Face

One must completely give up frowning and the dark scowl, and make the radiance of the face bright.

###### 2. Be a Friend to Beings and Speak Honestly

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that independence means not letting oneself become the body's servant, but having the freedom to put the body to work accomplishing virtue.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** With one's body and speech coming under the mind's power and the mind having attained independence, one must completely give up frowning and dark scowling and always look at others with a smiling face, and, in the manner of a friend who helps beings, speak honest words that are true and upright, without deceit or fraud.

**Key points.**

- **Taking hold of independence over body and speech.** Body and speech come under the mind's power, and one must make oneself independent, with the mind itself coming under the power of the antidote.
- **Giving up the expression of anger.** Completely casting away the frown, with the brows drawn together on the forehead, and the dark scowl, one must always look at others with a face that is joyful, smiling, and bright.
- **Becoming a friend to beings.** Like a good friend even to those one does not know, one must help all beings with affection, for no reason, and hold a warm-hearted disposition.
- **The conduct of honest speech.** Without deceit or fraud, with an upright heart, one must say to others, before they speak, things like "Have you come?", and constantly speak only words that are agreeable, upright, and true.
- **Understanding the meaning of independence.** Not letting oneself become the body's servant, but being able to put it to work for accomplishing virtue and for cherishing others, is the mark of being independent.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

When putting down chairs, cushions, and so on, do not do so carelessly with a loud thud; and when closing and opening doors, one must do it gently, not to the extent of startling friends. If one makes such thudding and banging noises, one appears to others like a person of great pride, and the appearance of respect disappears; therefore "always gentle and quiet" means that one must break one's pride and be humble, so it was said.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-72]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

When sitting on or taking up chairs, bedding and cushions, vessels and utensils, and so on, do not, carelessly — that is, without examining — sit or put them down or drop them with noise. If one were to drop them, it would look unseemly in others' eyes and cause them to lose faith, and it would do harm, such as interrupting the concentration of lamas, abbots, and others; therefore do not open the doors of houses and so on forcefully either. Always, reflecting on one's own faults, be "gentle" (གཅོམ); in whatever one does, do not make a loud commotion that others would see or hear, or rather let there be none; one must take delight in being "quiet" (སྐྱུང་བ) — whether staying in a hermitage, in the mountains, in the forest, or wherever, one needs to be such that no one sees or hears anything.

Do not carelessly, without examining, drop chairs and other furnishings with a loud noise, because it harms others. Because it harms those staying inside, do not open doors forcefully either; always behave in a way that is gentle and quiet and gives rise to joy.

Even when gathering up chairs, small stools, bedding and cushions, and the like, do not carelessly, without examining, drop them with a loud noise. Even when opening doors, do not open them forcefully, by main force. In short, always be free of roughness (དྲག་བརྡལ, as the rail spells it) and be gentle and quiet, delighting and pleasing beings.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Even when arranging and gathering up chairs, small stools, bedding and cushions, and other furnishings, do not carelessly, without examining, drop them with heedless clatter, forcefully, with a loud noise, letting them fall with a crash — this means abandoning causes of harm to others. When opening the door of a room, do not push it open forcefully, by force (ང་བཙན་ཐབས་ཀྱིས; the ང་ appears stray in the rail), since it makes those staying inside unhappy. In short, always, in all one's conduct, give up roughness and arrogance altogether, and be without commotion (འུར་ཟ; rendering tentative), gentle and quiet, so that one's conduct in every respect is peaceful and smooth and causes others to be pleased and to take interest — do such [conduct]; it is a means to avoid displeasing others and to avoid being a thorn to others' meditation.

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Do not drop chairs, bedding and cushions, vessels and utensils, and so on carelessly — that is, suddenly — with a loud noise. Moreover, since it becomes a thorn to meditation, do not open even the door-panels of the doors where one's own lama and Dharma friends and others are staying so that they make a loud noise. In short, always be gentle and quiet — in a way as if unseen and unheard by others — that is, rely on a humble nature, or take delight in it.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** The commentaries agree that this verse of the Bodhicaryāvatāra teaches: when setting down or gathering up chairs, bedding, and other furnishings, and when opening doors, one must not carelessly make loud noises, but must always break one's pride and rely on the way of being gentle and quiet, with peaceful and smooth conduct.

**Key points.**

- **Avoiding loud noise when arranging and gathering up furnishings.** When setting down or gathering up chairs, bedding and cushions, vessels and utensils, and so on, one must avoid carelessly, without examining, making thudding or loud noises.
- **Not using force when opening doors.** When opening or closing the door of a house, one must not use main force or violence, but do so gently.
- **Avoiding harm to others and obstacles to meditation.** This is to prevent harms such as loud noise looking unseemly in others' eyes, creating displeasure in those staying inside, and becoming a thorn to the concentration and meditation of lamas and Dharma friends.
- **Breaking pride and relying on gentleness and quietness.** Since making loud thudding noises makes one appear very proud, one must break one's pride and hold a humble nature, and, through peaceful and smooth conduct, cause others to take pleasure and interest.
