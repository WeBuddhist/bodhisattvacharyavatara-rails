## Today's Verse

> If the unbearable fires of hell
> burn my body for a long time,
> my mind will surely be tormented
> by the blazing fire of unbearable regret. ^4-25

> If, having somehow found
> this extremely rare and beneficial ground,
> I, being endowed with knowledge,
> am again led to hell, ^4-26

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 25 and 26 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. If we know right from wrong and still lead ourselves back into the fires of hell, nothing but regret can come of it.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

For example, when a father sets out to sea, he leaves instructions with his relatives for his young child. In the same way, when the Teacher, the Buddha, was about to pass into nirvana, he gave this instruction to the noble Avalokiteshvara and Manjushri: "When the beings now dwelling in hell are one day freed and gain a human body, hand over to them this treasury of Dharma gathered over many eons."

So this precious human body we have gained right now has come to us because we were freed from hell in the past. Having found this rare human body in this way, it is truly unfitting to do wrongdoing and go back to hell again.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when I know that some wrongdoing I want to do is not right, I will give it up and not do it.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ To make this human body meaningful, we must guard our own mind. If we do wrongdoing while knowing right from wrong, deep regret will follow. If we act badly while knowing it is not right, we will go to hell. So it is very important to respect and guard our own wisdom.
