## Today's Verse

> Being skilled and energetic,
> I must always perform tasks.
> I must not rely on anyone
> for any task. ^5-82

> The perfections of generosity and so forth
> should be practiced, advancing higher and higher.
> One should not give up the greater for the lesser.
> Primarily, one should consider the benefit of others. ^5-83

> Having understood this, one should always
> strive for the benefit of others.
> The compassionate ones, who see far,
> permit even what is forbidden in such cases. ^5-84

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the eighty-second, eighty-third, and eighty-fourth verses of the fifth chapter. Without depending on others, we take up the path of doing good actions by ourselves.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable story, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Long ago, the caravan leader Great Compassion killed a bad man with a short spear. He did this to save that man from falling to hell for killing five hundred merchants. Also, to save a merchant leader's daughter from death, the young brahmin Star did not keep celibacy for twelve years. Though these two did forbidden acts out of great compassion, it is said that they completed the accumulation of many eons. Therefore, for those who have compassion and see far, even what is forbidden is permitted.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, without depending on others, I will begin doing one good action by myself, with joy.

**Explanation:** _(Diligence Practice)_ This verse of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_ teaches that we must do good actions by ourselves, with joy, without depending on others. If we take up doing good ourselves, great happiness arises in the mind. This quickly completes the store of merit in our own mind.
