## Today's Verse

> Even if a person abandons the Mind of Awakening,
> the way they are set free
> is inconceivable karma,
> known only by the all-knowing. ^4-7

> For a bodhisattva, this
> is the heaviest of downfalls.
> If this occurs,
> it diminishes the benefit for all beings. ^4-8

> If anyone obstructs this merit
> even for a moment,
> having diminished the benefit for beings,
> their lower realms will be endless. ^4-9

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 7 to 9 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Among the ordinary people around us, we do not know who is a bodhisattva. So we should respect even the small good that others do.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses tell us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago King Candraprabha generated bodhicitta, and he gave away whatever anyone asked of him. Mara took the form of a brahmin and asked the king for his hand. The king cut off his own right hand and offered it with his left hand.

Mara acted as if he were angry. He said, "This left hand of yours is dirty," and would not take it. He threw it on the ground. The king lost heart. Thinking that he could not satisfy a being like this, he gave up bodhicitta. That king was later born as Shariputra, and by relying on the buddha's path of the hearers he reached the fruit of an arhat.

Even something like Shariputra giving up bodhicitta cannot be judged by an ordinary mind. How karma ripens is known only by the all-knowing wisdom of a buddha. So we should take seriously the warning in the ninth verse against getting in the way of the good that others do.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will not get in the way of even the smallest good thing others do. I will support it instead.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ If we obstruct or belittle the good that others do, we reduce their power to benefit beings. We do not know whether that other person is a bodhisattva, so getting in the way of their good is very great wrongdoing. But if we rejoice in even the small good that others do, and support it, our own mind is made clean. And great merit grows.
