# Day 19 — Give the gift that fits the person

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Give one person today the thing that actually suits them
> **Detail:** Shantideva offers each recipient the robe that fits their form. Today, fit your kindness to the person.

## Opening

Shantideva has bathed the buddhas and dried them with soft, scented cloths. Now he dresses them, and he does not hand everyone the same thing. To those who appear as monastics he offers saffron robes; to those who appear as householders he offers fine, soft, coloured cloth. Each receives what suits them. He then adorns the great noble ones, Samantabhadra, Manjushri, Avalokiteshvara, and the rest.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

It is easy to give people what we would like, and call it generosity.

The verse offers fine robes, and at first that sounds like one grand gift for everyone. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, notices that it is not. To those who appear as monastics, Shantideva offers plain saffron robes; to those who appear as householders, soft and colourful cloth. He looks at who is in front of him and gives what suits that person, not what would suit himself. Real giving starts with noticing the other person. And the offering quietly works back on the giver: as you clothe them with care, you become wrapped in the warm sense of your own good conduct.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Pick one person you will see today and give them something shaped to them, not to you: the help they actually need, the words they would want to hear, a little space if that is what suits them. Notice the person first, then give.
