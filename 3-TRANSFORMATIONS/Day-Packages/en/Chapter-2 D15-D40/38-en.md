# Day 38 — Nothing left but the clinging, and I go on alone

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the two verses 60 and 61 from the second chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. Everything we enjoyed in the past has already been used up. Nothing solid is left of it. And when death comes, we leave behind our life and all our relatives and friends. We go on alone. So these verses call on us to drop our attachment to the people we like, and our anger at the people we do not. They call on us to put our effort into the Dharma.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give a comparison that is valuable, easy to understand, and encouraging.

As Khenpo Kunzang Palden says, a man of high rank in this life travels with many attendants. But when death arrives, "a great king and a beggar with his stick" are no different. They are equal. Both leave behind their life, their parents and children, and all their wealth and possessions. Both go on alone, empty-handed and bare. Neither has any choice about it.

At that moment the friend we love cannot help us. The enemy we dislike cannot harm us. Only the good and bad actions we have done follow after us. So the point is clear. From now on we should give up our attachment to friends and our anger at enemies, and put our effort into the Dharma.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when attachment or anger toward another person comes up, I will remember that at death I have to go on alone.

**Explanation:** *(Taming the mind)* In this life we spend a great deal of effort on liking our friends and disliking our enemies. But as the verse says, at the time of death none of that helps us. We go on alone. So from today we should thin out that attachment and that anger, and settle our own mind into peace. That is very important.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

Of the delight in sense objects that I enjoyed in the past — enjoyed, and then gone — what is left to me now? Nothing at all. And yet I still cling to those very objects, and in doing so I have gone against the word of those who stand in the place of a teacher: the buddhas, the bodhisattvas and the spiritual friends. This is a grave fault.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

If, while we are alive, we cannot accomplish the virtue that helps us life after life, and instead give in to putting things off and to laziness, then what good do they do us — the food and clothing, the wealth and property and enjoyments we secured by drawing out every bit of strength in our three doors? They do nothing at all that helps us life after life. However great the worldly abundance of this life may be — food, clothing and the rest — those things are just as the verse says: "enjoyed before and now gone; what is left to me?" They bring no real benefit, and there is nothing left over from them, no guarantee we could count on for the future. And more than that: to disregard wrongdoing and suffering for the sake of perfect food, clothing and enjoyments, and to live by wrong livelihood, telling lies and deceiving people, is something the Teacher, the Blessed One, forbade outright. So, knowing that of everything one enjoyed before there is now not the slightest thing left to show for it, one must not live by wrong livelihood within the Teacher's Dharma, but live one's life well, in accordance with the guru's word.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-60]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

"In the past" means: up to now there is not one of the five objects of desire that one has not made use of. And yet, in that enjoyment, one experienced only a momentary pleasure as object and consciousness met, and then it fell apart and there was nothing; a pleasure that lasts into a second moment is impossible, and there is none. So it has no essence. In the same way, even if one goes on enjoying them now, they fall apart and are gone; so what is left to me — what pleasure unlike the earlier ones, a pleasure that does not fall apart in the second moment? There is none. And though there is none, I still cling to those five objects of desire and am attached to them; and for their sake I shrink from nothing, not from wrongdoing and not from suffering, but disregard great hardship and exhaustion and go against the word of the guru — that is, my own root guru, and the guru of the three worlds, the Buddha, the Blessed One, together with his heirs. Doing so ruins both this life and the next; so do not do it.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-60]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->

It sets out in brief, in the underlying gloss, the manner of it: of the delight in sense objects enjoyed in the past and now fallen apart, what is left to me now? Nothing. And yet I cling to those objects and have gone against the word of those who stand in the place of a teacher — the buddhas, the bodhisattvas and the spiritual friends. This is a grave fault.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^2-60]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Of the enjoyments experienced in samsara in the past — which fell apart and could not be relied upon — what enjoyment with any real substance is left to me? Not the slightest. And yet I cling to that thing without substance and am attached to it, and so have gone against the guru's word. Having done wrong, I feel regret.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^2-60]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Of those enjoyments experienced in the past and now fallen apart, what enjoyment is left to me that does not fall apart? And yet I cling to those enjoyments and have gone against the guru's word — which is not right. So do not do it.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^2-60]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

From beginningless samsara I have experienced a body and enjoyments like these again and again, and they have fallen apart; so what thing is left to me? Not the slightest. And yet I cling to all that contaminated prosperity, low as it is, and so have gone against the word of the guru — the victorious ones together with their heirs. That is not right. From now on, then, I shall make every effort to accomplish what their word says.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-60]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Suppose someone asks: why should I not simply enjoy the objects of desire as I please and stay happily with my relatives? Because: from beginningless samsara I have again and again experienced enjoyments of the qualities of desire like these — enjoyments that cannot be relied upon, that have no essence of their own, and that never satisfy however much they are used — and greater ones besides; and all of them have fallen apart. So what enjoyment is left to me now, better than what I had before, endowed with real substance and able to produce satisfaction and contentment? There is none at all. And yet I have been attached to those unreliable enjoyments and clung to them, and in doing so have overstepped and gone against the word of the guru — the victorious ones together with their heirs — and have amassed great wrongdoing, which is utterly unfitting. Thinking this, one should give rise to fierce regret; and from today onward it is right to make every effort to accomplish what their word says.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-60]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

"Enjoyed before and now gone" means this: even if in earlier lives before this one there were enjoyments of the objects of desire and so forth, they have already fallen apart, and now not even a memory of them remains. And not only that: in the earlier part of this very life too, one enjoyed the objects of desire — beautiful forms, pleasant sounds, sweet smells, delicious tastes, soft things to touch — and tasted them, and they have already fallen apart, already passed. So what is left of them for me now that I could actually taste and enjoy? Nothing at all. And so, since nothing is left, what do I do? I still cling to those same contaminated objects of desire, just as before, and am attached to them, and so I work at wrongdoing and non-virtuous action, and have not properly accomplished virtue by going for refuge to the Three Jewels. As for "the guru's word": it means the word of the guru — the perfect Buddha, the Blessed One — and of abbots, masters, spiritual friends and the like. To have gone against their word too, and not to have accomplished the sublime Dharma: that is a very great loss. This is the meaning.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-60]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. There Is Not the Slightest Essence in the Enjoyments Already Experienced and Now Finished

However often one makes use of the objects of desire, one experiences only a momentary pleasure as object and consciousness meet, and then it falls apart; so all the commentaries agree in the end that nothing with any real substance is left to one.

###### 2. Clinging to Those Enjoyments That Have No Essence Is What Makes One Go Against the Guru's Word

To cling to something without essence and be attached to it, and so to go against the word of the guru — the buddhas, the bodhisattvas and the spiritual friends — is a grave fault.

###### 3. Attachment of That Kind Ruins Both This Life and the Next

Kunzang Pelden explains that clinging to the objects of desire, disregarding great hardship and exhaustion, and going against the guru's word in this way ruins both this life and the life to come.

###### 4. One Should Regret From the Heart What Was Done Before, and From Now on Strive to Accomplish the Guru's Word

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Sabzang Mati Panchen both advise that one should regret having done wrong out of attachment to earlier enjoyments, and from now on make every effort to accomplish what the guru's word says.

###### 5. However Endlessly One Enjoys the Objects of Desire, the Senses Are Never Satisfied

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the *Avadāna of the Seven Maidens* and the *Śiṣyalekha* to show that however much one enjoys, there is no essence in it and the senses are never satisfied.

###### 6. Looked at Concretely, in One's Own Life, Enjoyments Secured by Wrong Livelihood Not Only Fail to Help but Pile Up a Great Deal of Wrongdoing

His Holiness the Dalai Lama cites the five methods of wrong livelihood from the *Precious Garland* and advises that one should give up living by wrong livelihood and live one's life well, in accordance with the guru's word.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows that there is not the slightest essence in the enjoyments of desire already experienced and finished; and all the commentaries explain in one voice that to cling to what has no essence, and so to go against the word of the guru — the buddhas, the bodhisattvas and the spiritual friends — is a grave fault.

**Key points.**

- However often one makes use of the objects of desire, only a momentary pleasure at the meeting of object and consciousness is experienced before it falls apart; so there is no essence in them at all.
- Clinging to those enjoyments without essence and going against the guru's word becomes a cause that ruins both this life and the next.
- One should regret from the heart what was done before, and from now on make every effort to accomplish what the guru's word says.
- The *Avadāna of the Seven Maidens* and the *Śiṣyalekha* likewise show that the senses are never satisfied, however endless the enjoyments.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama advises that enjoyments secured by the methods of wrong livelihood not only fail to help but also increase wrongdoing. ⚑

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

This body while one is alive, and likewise all one's relatives and dear friends — all of it is given up, or one is parted from it; and then one must go on alone to "wherever it may be," that is, to some place entirely unknown and unfamiliar, with no one beside one. And in that situation, whoever they were during one's lifetime, those one loved and those one did not, they cannot help or harm; what does connect with help and harm is certainly the two kinds of action, virtuous and non-virtuous.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-61]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

It is not as it is now, when a guru or a lord travels somewhere with a great retinue of attendants. At the time of death one gives up — or leaves behind — this living life of one's own, and likewise father, mother, children and the other relatives, one's happy companions and dear friends, and one's wealth and enjoyments and whatever else one has. Then, exactly like a great king and a beggar with his stick, one goes alone, stripped of life, naked and empty-handed like a man pressed into forced labour; a terrifying darkness comes to meet one from in front, the red wind of karma drives one on from behind, and the fearsome men of the Lord of Death fall upon one with cries of "Kill! Cut!" Under the power of karma, with no idea where one is going — anywhere from the peak of existence down to the Unrelenting Hell — one must go with no freedom at all. At that time, what use are any of them: close relatives, happy companions and loved ones, and the worldly human enemies one does not love? They do not do the slightest good, nor the slightest harm; so what harm can they do then?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-61]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

This body of mine, which has no essence, this life while I am alive, and likewise my relatives and my friends too — all are left behind, and I must go alone, with no freedom at all, into a quarter about which nothing is certain. If that is so, what use are all those I love and all those I do not? They bring not the slightest benefit; so it is not right to be attached to them.

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Not only the enjoyments: this living life of mine too, and likewise my relatives and my friends, must be left behind, and I must go on alone to wherever it may be. If that is so, what use are all those I love and those I do not? Clinging to them is pointless.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^2-61]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

This life of mine while I am alive, which is like a bubble on water, and likewise father and mother, brothers and the rest of my relatives and friends, and whatever enjoyments I have — all of it is left entirely behind, and I must go alone, with no companion, to wherever it may be, a place about which nothing is certain. If that is so, what use is any of it: attachment to those I love, aversion to those I do not, and all the wrongdoing done on that account? It was all done to no purpose. So now it is right to make every effort in virtue.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-61]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

This life of mine during the time while I am living and alive, like a bubble on water, and likewise my relatives — father, mother and the rest — my dear friends and companions, together with my wealth and enjoyments: all of them are left behind. With not even a companion left, alone and with no acquaintance there, I must certainly go, with no freedom at all, following after my karma, into some quarter about which nothing is certain — wherever it may be. If that is so, what use are they all, those I love and my relatives, and those I do not love and my enemies, and the rest? Not one of them gives me refuge or keeps me company, and enjoyments and the like do me no good whatever. So being attached to them and clinging to them is pointless and not right. From now on, therefore, it is right to make every possible effort to accomplish the path; it is not right to sit idle.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-61]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Right now I am living out my life; but this very life too must one day be given up — I must die. And just as that happens, one leaves behind all one's own relatives and kin — father and mother, brothers and sisters and the rest — and one's dear friends, that is, the companions who are one's relatives and friends. In that condition of having died, with no other companion, one must go alone, as a single being in the intermediate state with no one beside one, along the road to the next life — a road like an empty kingdom one has never known. If it is like that, then how can it be right to cling to those dear friends and to one's relatives, father and mother and the rest, or to all those one does not love, enemies and those who do harm? In every respect it is not right to cling and be attached, nor to hate and to injure. This is the meaning.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-61]]

#### Metaphors and Examples

- **Like a great king and a beggar with his stick** (རྒྱལ་པོ་ཆེན་པོ་དང་སྤྲང་པོ་དབྱུག་བཟུང་གཉིས་འདྲ་བར།) → at the time of death every difference of high and low birth, and every difference between friends and loved ones, falls away. — Kunzang Pelden describes how at death one is driven on alone, stripped of life and naked, by the red wind of karma, with the fearsome men of the Lord of Death coming to meet one from in front; and he explains that at that moment, exactly like a great king and a beggar with his stick, one must go alone with no freedom at all, and that close relatives, happy companions and loved ones, and the human enemies one does not love, can do nothing whatever to help or harm.
- **Life is like a bubble** (ཚེ་སྲོག་ཆུ་བུར་ལྟ་བུ།) → the impermanent, essenceless nature of the life one is living. — Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both describe the life one is living as being like a bubble on water, and show that the relatives and friends who rest upon it have no essence either.
- **Like an empty kingdom one has never known** (རྒྱུས་མེད་པའི་རྒྱལ་ཁམས་སྟོང་པ་ལྟ་བུ།) → the uncertain quarter into which, after death, one must go alone in the intermediate state with no companion. — Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that the road to the next life is like an empty kingdom one has never known, making it clear that one must go as a single being in the intermediate state, with no one beside one.

#### Scriptural Quotations

> Leaving behind relatives, wealth and goods, homeland and castle —
> all that one could not bear to be parted from even for a moment —
> one goes alone down the road of terror.
> However much wealth one gathered, staking wrongdoing to get it,
> it is oneself, the fool, who carries the ripening of that wrongdoing,
> while the wealth one gathered is carried off by one's own children.
> So what good is the wealth one has gathered?

— *Advice to the King* (རྒྱལ་པོ་ལ་གདམས་པ) (cited by Minyak Kunzang Sonam as the authority showing that relatives, wealth and goods do not follow one at the time of death).

> In this terror of the three realms there is no friend,
> and likewise no child, no spouse, no kinsman.
> Father and mother, brothers, children and spouse,
> servants, wealth and the whole company of kin —
> none of them go after the one who has died and gone.
> Actions alone follow after; and so the childish one goes on.

— *Questions of Vīradatta* (དཔས་བྱིན་གྱིས་ཞུས་པ), a sutra (cited by Minyak Kunzang Sonam to show that only karma, and no companion, follows the one who has died).

> At death, wealth does not follow after —
> not kinsmen, not friends, not children.
> Here, when one is born, one is born alone;
> when one dies, one dies alone as well;
> and alone one undergoes suffering here.
> A person has no companion in samsara.

— *Lungzhi* (ལུང་གཞི) (Minyak Kunzang Sonam gives his source under just that name).

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. At the Time of Death One Leaves Behind This Life and All One's Relatives and Friends and Must Go Alone Into an Uncertain Quarter

For that reason, all the commentaries agree in the end that clinging to everyone one loves and everyone one does not is pointless.

###### 2. At Death Every Difference of High and Low Birth Disappears, and the Red Wind of Karma Drives One on Before the Men of the Lord of Death

Kunzang Pelden explains that, exactly like a great king and a beggar with his stick, one must go alone with no freedom at all, and shows that close relatives, happy companions and loved ones, and the human enemies one does not love, can do nothing whatever to help or harm.

###### 3. Life Itself Is Like a Bubble, With No Essence, and the Friends Who Rest Upon It Have No Essence Either

Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both describe the life one is living as being like a bubble, and show that clinging to relatives and friends is pointless.

###### 4. At the Time of Death Actions Alone Follow After; No Relative and No Wealth Goes With One

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites *Advice to the King*, the *Questions of Vīradatta* and other scriptures to show that in birth and in death alike one is born and dies alone, with no companion.

###### 5. The Road of the Intermediate State Is a Companionless Quarter, Like an Empty Kingdom One Has Never Known

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that, since one must go as a single being in the intermediate state, it is not right to cling to those one loves or to hate those one does not.

###### 6. Loved or Not Loved, at the Time of Death None of Them Can Help or Harm; Help and Harm Are Settled by Virtuous and Non-Virtuous Action Alone

His Holiness the Dalai Lama advises that what matters is not clinging to those one loves and hating those one does not, but taking virtuous and non-virtuous action itself seriously.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows that at the time of death one leaves behind this life and all one's relatives and friends and must go, with no freedom at all, alone into a quarter about which nothing is certain; and all the commentaries explain in one voice that, for that reason, clinging to everyone one loves and everyone one does not is pointless.

**Key points.**

- Kunzang Pelden explains that at death every difference of high and low birth, and every difference between friends and loved ones, disappears, and that the red wind of karma drives one on before the men of the Lord of Death.
- Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both explain that life itself is like a bubble, with no essence, and that the relatives and friends who rest upon it have no essence either.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites many scriptural passages to show that at the time of death actions alone follow after, and that no relative and no wealth goes with one.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that the road of the intermediate state is a companionless quarter, like an empty kingdom one has never known.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama advises that, loved or not loved, none of them can help or harm, and that help and harm are settled by virtuous and non-virtuous action alone.
