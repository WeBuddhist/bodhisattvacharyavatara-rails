## Today's Verse

> Today my life is fruitful.
> I have perfectly obtained human existence.
> Today I am born into the family of the buddhas.
> Today I have become a spiritual child of the buddhas. ^3-25

> Now, by whatever means, I will
> undertake actions befitting my family.
> I will act so that this pure, noble family
> is not defiled. ^3-26

> Just as a blind person
> might find a jewel in a heap of rubbish,
> likewise, by some chance,
> this Mind of Awakening has arisen in me. ^3-27

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 25 to 27 from the third chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. A blind man finds a wish-fulfilling jewel in a heap of sweepings at the roadside. Bodhicitta is as hard to come by as that. Together let us look at how precious it is that this mind has arisen in us.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Here the blind man is a person whose eye of wisdom has been blinded by not knowing. The heap of rubbish is the heap of our own afflictions and karma: desire, anger, jealousy, pride and the rest.

The blind man finds a precious jewel in such rubbish. In the same way, this precious bodhicitta has arisen in the middle of the dust of our afflictions. It came by the good fortune of merit and the blessing of the buddhas. That is truly a great wonder.

This precious mind removes all the ruin and poverty of samsara and nirvana. Now that we have found it, we should give rise to great joy, hold it dear, and guard it well.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, instead of thinking only about what I want, I will carry out one act that helps someone else.

**Explanation:** _(Ethics Practice)_ We have entered the family of the buddhas' heirs. So if we give up the thought of pursuing our own aims alone and benefit others instead, our vow becomes pure. And the family of the buddhas is not stained by any fault.
