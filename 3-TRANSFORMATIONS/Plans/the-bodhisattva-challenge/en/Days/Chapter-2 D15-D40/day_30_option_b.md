# Day 30 — The one thing no one can do for you

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Take back one thing you're waiting for someone else to carry
> **Detail:** Today's verses face what we go through alone, and let our grip on others ease.

## Opening

Shantideva pictures his own deathbed. He imagines being surrounded by every relative and friend he has, all of them close, and then sees plainly that the feeling of his life being cut is something he will go through entirely on his own. No one can take a share of it. He is not trying to feel lonely. He is noticing something true that quietly changes how tightly we hold on to the people around us.

## From the Tradition

Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, draws out one quiet consequence of the deathbed scene. A room full of people who love us cannot take even a sliver of the experience of dying; we go through it alone. He links this back to how we live. We spend years sorting people into those who are for us and those who are against us, and we do a great deal of good and harm on the strength of that sorting. But at the end we leave all of them behind and go on by ourselves. Seen against that, the constant ranking of friend and enemy starts to look less solid, and our grip on it can ease. He adds the last turn honestly: when death comes, only the good we have built protects us, and he regrets relying on it so little. Loosening the hold on others is not coldness. It is what makes room for the even care the practice is after.

## Today's Practice

Notice one place today where you are quietly waiting for someone else to carry something that is really yours: to fix your mood, settle a worry, or make a choice you keep avoiding. Just for a moment, take it back gently as your own. Some things each of us carries alone, and remembering that can let us lean on others a little less heavily, and resent them a little less too.
