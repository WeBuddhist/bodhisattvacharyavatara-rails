## Today's Verse

> That is why, starting today, I take refuge in the buddhas — the protectors of the world, who work so hard to keep every being safe, and whose great power can dispel any fear. ^2-47

> In the same way, I take refuge in their teaching too — the teaching that clears away all the fears of this life — and in the whole community of bodhisattvas. ^2-48

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on verses 47 and 48 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. A new section starts here. Yesterday's verse was the power of regret, the first of the four powers of confession; these two verses are the second, the power of reliance. Yesterday the man searched every direction and found nothing. Today Shantideva answers his own question. He goes for refuge, and he goes now rather than later, to the Three Jewels, which are the Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha. Today asks us to look at why these three and not something else, and then to actually rest our weight on them.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

Yesterday nobody could help. Today Shantideva turns to someone. So the useful question is what makes something worth turning to at all.

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk, a later commentator on the text, reads three qualifications out of verse 47, and he says all three have to hold together. First, the buddhas are guardians of beings, so their concern includes us. Second, they strive to the end at the work of protecting, so the concern is not a mood that passes. Third, they have the power to dispel all fear, so the wish is backed by capacity.

Verse 48 adds the other two Jewels. The Dharma is the teaching the buddhas actually realized, and the commentaries call it the antidote to the afflictions, which is why it removes the fear rather than covering it. The Sangha here is the assembly of bodhisattvas who have reached the noble stages and do not turn back.

Then one word carries the weight. The commentaries gloss "truly" as going for refuge with no pretence and no doubt, and they add: starting today, not once things get worse. Which is why the verse says today.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when someone near me is frightened or in pain, I will not let my own heart shrink away from them. I will go for refuge on their behalf as well as mine, and stay with them from there.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ The Tibetan for today's practice says whatever fear or suffering comes, mine or another person's, do not let the mind shrink. The second half is easy to skip. When someone else is in trouble we often pull back a little without admitting it, because their fear is uncomfortable to sit next to. Today I will do the opposite. I will stay in the room, bring the Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha to mind, and place my trust there for both of us before I say anything. This will not fix their problem, and it is not meant to. It changes what I am standing on while I am with them, and someone who is not being pulled away by their own discomfort is a great deal easier to be frightened next to.
