# Day 2 — This chance won't come around again

## Introduction

Shantideva has finished his introduction. Now he makes his first argument: this human life — with the time and conditions to practice — is extraordinarily rare. These two verses give the case in two moves: a direct statement of how rare it is, and then an image of just how briefly virtue tends to appear in us.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

*MIND TRAINING (recite 3 times)*

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in immeasurable equanimity, free from attachment and aversion.

*REFUGE (recite 3 times)*

> Until I reach the heart of awakening, I take refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.

*TAKING THE BODHISATTVA VOW (recite 3 times)*

> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And trained step by step in the bodhisattva conduct,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And train step by step in that same conduct.

## Today's Verses

> དལ་འབྱོར་འདི་ནི་རྙེད་པར་ཤིན་ཏུ་དཀའ། །
> སྐྱེས་བུའི་དོན་སྒྲུབ་ཐོབ་པར་གྱུར་པ་ལ། །
> གལ་ཏེ་འདི་ལ་ཕན་པ་མ་བསྒྲུབས་ན། །
> ཕྱིས་འདི་ཡང་དག་འབྱོར་པར་ག་ལ་འགྱུར། །
>
> This precious human life of leisure and opportunity is extremely difficult to find. Now I have this chance to accomplish my goals. If I do not use it to do good, how will I ever find such an opportunity again?

> ཇི་ལྟར་མཚན་མོ་མུན་ནག་སྤྲིན་རུམ་ན། །
> གློག་འགྱུ་སྐད་ཅིག་རབ་སྣང་སྟོན་པ་ལྟར། །
> དེ་བཞིན་སངས་རྒྱས་མཐུ་ཡིས་བརྒྱ་ལམ་ན། །
> འཇིག་རྟེན་བསོད་ནམས་བློ་གྲོས་ཐང་འགའ་འབྱུང་། །
>
> A flash of lightning on a dark, cloudy night reveals everything for a single instant. Similarly, the power of the Buddha occasionally inspires a brief moment of virtue in this world.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Je and Ngulchu Thokme both reach for the same ancient image to explain verse 1.4. They cite Nagarjuna: imagine a blind turtle that surfaces from the ocean once every hundred years. Floating somewhere on that same ocean is a wooden yoke with a hole in it, blown around by the wind. Nagarjuna says the chance of finding a human birth from a lower realm is even smaller than the chance of the turtle surfacing with its neck through the yoke. The comparison is not meant to be encouraging. It is meant to make you stop and take the rarity seriously.

## Aspiration

> May the precious and supreme bodhicitta —
> Where it has not yet arisen, may it arise;
> Where it has arisen, may it not decline,
> But grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of this practice,
> May all wandering beings, without exception,
> Enter the path of the bodhisattva.

## Today's Practice Challenge

Ngulchu Thokme quotes the sutras directly: do not let this opportunity go to waste. Think of one thing you keep meaning to do that actually matters — not a task on a list, but something to do with how you want to live. Something you believe in but keep deferring. Ask yourself honestly what you are waiting for.
