## Today's Verse

> Furthermore, the benefits of suffering are
> that weariness removes arrogance.
> Compassion arises for those in cyclic existence.
> One avoids misdeeds and delights in virtue. ^6-21

> Why be angry at the great causes
> of suffering such as bile,
> but be angry at sentient beings?
> All of them are driven by conditions. ^6-22

> Just as this illness
> arises even though one does not want it,
> likewise, defilements arise forcefully
> even though one does not want them. ^6-23

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the twenty-first, twenty-second, and twenty-third verses of the sixth chapter. We do not get angry at an illness that comes without being invited. So why do we get angry at a person who harms us?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Illness does not come because it wants to harm us. It comes from conditions such as the season and food, so we do not get angry at it. In the same way, the person who harms us is pushed by the conditions of past karma and the afflictions, and acts without free choice. The afflictions arise by force, against the person's own wishes. So if we are angry, we should be angry not at the person but at the afflictions. We do not get angry at an illness, which arises without free choice. In the same way, there is no reason to be angry at a person who acts without free choice.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when people harm me, I will think that they are pushed by conditions, and I will not get angry.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ We do not get angry at great sources of suffering such as illness. People who harm us are also pushed by the conditions of the afflictions and karma, and act without free choice. Therefore, it is right to be angry at the afflictions rather than at the person.
