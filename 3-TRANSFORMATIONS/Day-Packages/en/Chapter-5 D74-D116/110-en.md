# Day 110 — Small Habits That Protect Others' Faith: Discreet Waste, Quiet Eating, Respectful Sitting, and Propriety

**Date:** Oct 23

<!-- sec:challenge -->
## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the ninety-first, ninety-second, and ninety-third verses of the fifth chapter. We think together about how our small daily habits, such as how we eat and how we sit, show respect to others, or not.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable quotation, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Jowo Atisha taught the following about conduct that causes a lack of faith in others. "If it does not agree with the minds of people of the world, saying, 'This is not my Dharma,' first put a good intention forward, and ask about the customs of the world's places." That is, if some conduct of ours does not agree with the minds of people of the world and causes a lack of faith in them, we must first ask well about the customs of the place, with a good intention. Then we must give up that conduct.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when I eat, I will not stuff my mouth, and I will eat without making noise.

**Explanation:** *(Ethics Practice)* If we are careful about small things in our way of eating and our conduct, others do not feel uncomfortable. This protects the faith and respect in the minds of others. This is a very important ethics practice.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

"Tooth-sticks and saliva" — this means that when one discards tooth-sticks, saliva, and the like, one must cover them with earth. Even in terms of the state of the environment today, this is very important. In former Tibet, since the water and air were clean and the ground dry, diseases and germs found it hard to spread; and although attention to the natural environment has not yet become part of our habits, now we definitely must pay attention. Urine and excrement, which are included under "and so on," and the like, one should not discard just anywhere, into water and the like that is used.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-91]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Third: if a bodhisattva does not guard against others' lack of faith, it becomes a great condition for others' wrongdoing. Therefore, when one discards tooth-sticks, saliva, nasal mucus, spittle, and the like in places where one sits, such as in front of a lama, in a temple, or in a house, one must cover them with earth and the like so that they are not seen. As for urine, excrement, and the like, to discard them into the clean water, or onto the fine ground, that oneself and others use is censured and forbidden, so one should not do it.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-91]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

The common conduct taught in the Vinaya collection that is shared in common must be kept even by ordained bodhisattvas, except in cases where it is permitted, and lay bodhisattvas, too, must guard against giving rise to lack of faith in others. Therefore, when one discards tooth-sticks for cleaning the mouth, and saliva, one should cover them with earth and the like; and discarding excrement, urine, nasal mucus, and the like into the water and onto the ground that are used in common would bring censure from gods and others, so one should give it up.

##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Regarding that, guarding one's bodily conduct: when one discards tooth-cleaning sticks soiled with filth, saliva, mouth-water, spittle, and the like, one should cover them with earth or a small flat stone and the like, so that others do not see them; it is also said that when spitting, one should cover with the hand. As for urine, excrement, nasal mucus, spittle, and the like, discarding them into the clean water and the pleasant ground — places where all, oneself and others, commonly stay, enjoy, and delight — is censured and forbidden by the Dharma and the Vinaya, or becomes something censured by gods and others, so one should not discard them.

##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

This means: one should not discard tooth-sticks that clean away the mouth's filth, the residue of chewed food, and saliva in front of a monastery, the residence of the sangha, or a lama, an abbot, and so on. Moreover, in other circumstances of discarding, one must cover them with earth and the like in order to guard against others' lack of faith. And when discarding excrement, urine, and the like, discarding them into clean water that others use, or onto meadows, or near trees and the like, has become a place of censure, so one must avoid it.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** In order to guard the common conduct of the Vinaya and one's bodily conduct, one must discern what to take up and what to avoid in how one discards tooth-sticks and saliva, and excrement and urine. If one does not guard against others' lack of faith, faults arise with respect to others' wrongdoing and so on.

**Key points.**

- When discarding tooth-sticks and saliva, one should cover them with earth and the like, or make sure that others do not see them.
- Tooth-sticks that clean the mouth's filth, and saliva, are not to be discarded in front of a monastery and the like.
- Discarding excrement and urine into water and onto ground used by others, onto meadows, and near trees is a place of censure and should be avoided.
- This is important even in terms of today's environment.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

That is, even at the time of eating, there are such things as filling the mouth with both cheeks stretched out and eating. Further, when eating food, it is not acceptable to eat with sounds such as "tsuk tsuk," "chak chak," "hu hu," and "phu phu." Further, "Do not eat with the mouth gaping wide": one must act in a [manner described by the words མདོ་དོན་མཛེས་པོ་དང་། འཇུག་པོ་ཞིག]. Likewise, when sitting, too, it is not acceptable to sit with the legs stretched out, being without respect, or in a disrespectful and unbecoming guise. The equivalent term for this means rubbing the upper arms (དཔུང་པ་སྙེས་པ). Those who play at wrestling (སྦེར་ཀ་རྩེད་མཁན) and those who compete in strength become arrogant when they rub their upper arms; since that is a disrespectful guise, one should not act in that way.

*(Editorial note: the words མདོ་དོན་མཛེས་པོ་དང་། འཇུག་པོ་ཞིག in this passage are unclear in the rail and are left untranslated; the renderings "rubbing the upper arms" for དཔུང་པ་སྙེས་པ and "those who play at wrestling" for སྦེར་ཀ་རྩེད་མཁན are tentative, and the Tibetan is kept in parentheses.)*

When one is sitting in a house and the like, one should not sit with the two legs stretched out; and when washing the hands, one should not rub both hands together at once — that is, one washes them one after another in turn.

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

When eating, too, one should not eat with the mouth filled with a large mouthful, with sounds such as "tsuk tsuk," "chak chak," and the clicking of the palate, or with the mouth opened before the food has arrived. When sitting on a bench and the like, too, one should not sit with the legs stretched out in a heedless guise, and should not rub one's two hands or upper arms together at the same time in a proud guise.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

When engaging with food, too, one should not eat with the mouth very full of a large mouthful, with loud sounds such as "chak chak" and the clicking of the palate, nor with the mouth opened before the food reaches the mouth, nor while eating with the mouth gaping very wide. When sitting on a bench, a cushion, and the like, one should not sit with the two legs stretched far out; and while washing both hands, there is no fault in rubbing them in turn or alternately, but one should not rub them together at the same time — so it is taught. It also appears that it is explained as: "in a heedless guise one should not sit with the legs stretched out, and in a proud guise one should not rub the hands or upper arms together at the same time."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-92]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

At the time of eating, one should not do such unbecoming things as filling the mouth very full with mouthfuls, eating with sounds such as the clicking of the palate, or opening the mouth and eating before the food has reached it. When sitting on a bench and the like, too, one should not sit in an unbecoming way, with both legs stretched downward or with one leg stretched downward. And when washing the hands in water and the like, one should not rub them together — that is, one must take care that the washing water does not splash about this way and that — so he taught.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-92]]

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

In conveyances such as chariots, on beds and seats, and in dwellings, one should not be alone together with other women — those who are not like a mother or sister. In short, all conduct through which people of the world have lost faith — one should abandon it, having seen it oneself or having asked others.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^5-93]]

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

That is, when traveling on the road, one should not stay together with a woman in a conveyance, on a bed or seat on which one sits, or in a dwelling or sleeping place; and further, from a woman's side, one must add: "one should not be alone together with a man." "All those who lack faith in the world should be avoided by seeing and inquiring" — in short: having generated the supreme bodhicitta and pledged to accomplish others' welfare, in acting for the sake of other beings, one asks about the customs of whatever place and region one is in, comes to know what in those customs is generally regarded as unbecoming and improper, and must abandon it.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-93]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

On a mount such as a horse, on one bed or seat, or in secluded dwellings: lay people should not be alone together with another's woman, and the ordained should not be alone together with other women who are not close kin; it is not to be done, as it is unsuitable. In short, all conduct through which the other beings of the world mock and lose faith — those one has seen oneself and knows, and also those one has not seen and does not know — one must ask other skilled and knowledgeable persons, and abandon them.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-93]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

In conveyances such as horses, on beds and seats, and in dwellings, one should not stay together with another's woman; and lay bodhisattvas, too, should not stay together with women who are not close kin. In brief, as for any conduct at which the world loses faith: those that one sees directly, and those that one does not know oneself — one should ask in that place and at that time, and abandon them.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-93]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Whether one is ordained or lay, one should not stay together with another's woman in a conveyance, on a bed or seat, or in one dwelling; and the ordained should not do so with any woman at all. In short, all conduct that has become a cause of the world's lack of faith — one should abandon it, having seen it oneself or having asked the learned.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-150]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Even on conveyances such as horses and elephants, on one bed or seat, or in secluded places, one should not stay together with another's woman, or — for the ordained — with any other woman who is able to be relied upon bodily (ལུས་བསྟེན་ནུས). In short, all conduct at which worldly people mock and lose faith — as one sees it oneself, or by asking other skilled persons — one should abandon.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-93]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

When traveling on the road, too, one should not ride together with another's woman on one mount such as a horse or an ox; and when sitting, one should not sit together with another's woman on one bed or seat, or in one secluded dwelling. This is taught as common to lay and ordained bodhisattvas; in particular, one must explain that if the bodhisattva is ordained, he should not share a common place with any other woman at all. Also, in some related explanations it is said that a lay bodhisattva should not do these things together with women who are not close kin, and that the ordained should not do them together with other women who are able to be relied upon bodily (ལུས་བསྟེན་ནུས). In brief, in whatever circumstances of bodily conduct, as for all conduct that involves fault and becomes a cause of the mockery, disparagement, and lack of faith of worldly people — as one sees it oneself, and by well asking about the customs of the place (ཡུལ་ལུགས་ཀྱི་ཆོས) those other persons who are skilled in conduct and who are in that place at that time — one should abandon it. The treatise also says "by seeing, and" (མཐོང་བ་དང་). Moreover, an ordained bodhisattva must keep the common conduct taught in the Vinaya at all times except on occasional times of permission, and conduct that gives rise to others' lack of faith must be guarded even by a lay bodhisattva — how much more so by an ordained bodhisattva! This is what Jowo [said]:

> If it does not agree with the minds of the world — "This is not my Dharma" — first put a good intention forward, and ask about the customs of the world's places.

*(Editorial note: in the rail, this block closes with the quotation above, introduced by "This is what Jowo —" without a verb or name; the Tibetan quotation carries the spelling འ་དྲི་བར་བྱ and a stray space after བཟང་པོ་, kept as given in the Tibetan companion file. The practice-plan file attributes the same lines to Jowo Atisha.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-93]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

If one is a bodhisattva who has the basis of ordination, as a novice or a fully ordained monastic, one should not ride together with other women on one conveyance such as a horse, an elephant, or a chariot. Also, one should not sit together with a woman who is not one's close kin on one bed or seat, or inside one dwelling. In short: all the aspects by which other people of the world would lose faith — if one has oneself directly seen or heard of them, one should abandon them accordingly; and if one does not know or has not heard that something is an aspect in which others lose faith, one should ask people of another locale what the customs of that locale are like, and abandon those aspects of others' lack of faith. Such is the meaning.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-93]]

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** All conduct through which worldly people lose faith and mock must be abandoned, whether one knows it oneself or learns of it by asking. In particular, one should avoid staying together with women in conveyances, on beds and seats, and in dwellings.

**Key points.**

- In conveyances, on beds and seats, and in secluded dwellings, one should not be alone together with another's woman, or with a woman who is not close kin.
- If one is ordained, one should not be alone together with any woman at all.
- As for conduct through which the world loses faith, one should abandon it, having seen it directly or having asked the learned.
- Having asked people of that place about the customs of the place and region, one should come to know what is regarded as unbecoming and improper, and abandon it.
