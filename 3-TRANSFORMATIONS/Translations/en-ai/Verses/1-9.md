## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three classical commentators are in complete agreement regarding the profound, instantaneous, and democratic transformation described in Verse 1-9. They explain that the very instant bodhicitta is generated, an individual undergoes a complete revolution in both **name** and **spiritual status (meaning)**, transitioning from a wretched prisoner of cyclic existence to a revered heir of the Buddhas.

### 1. The Suddenness and Immediacy of Transformation
The commentators place great emphasis on the absolute immediacy of this transformation, corresponding to the Sanskrit term *kṣaṇena* ("in an instant"). 
- **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen** highlights that this occurs "in that very instant" and "the moment they generate this mind," showing that there is no temporal gap or gradual transition required for these benefits to manifest.
- **Sazang Mati Panchen** similarly frames it as occurring "in that very instant of its generation."
- **Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo** reinforces this by stating that the transformation occurs "the moment this precious mind of perfect awakening is born in them."

### 2. The Prison of Samsara
The commentators contrast the sublime status of a Bodhisattva with the wretched condition of ordinary sentient beings to accentuate the power of bodhicitta.
- **The Fettered Condition:** All three commentators identify the starting point of the practitioner as being a "wretched" (*varāka*) being bound in the prison of cyclic existence (*bhavacāraka*).
- **Karma and Afflictions:** Sazang Mati Panchen explicitly defines this prison as being "tormented by various sufferings, bound by the fetters of karma and mental afflictions." Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo similarly describes these beings as "bound by the noose of karma and afflictions." Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen notes that they are bound "under the power of karma and afflictions." 

### 3. The Dual Change of Name and Meaning
A central scholastic theme across the commentaries is the dual shift in **name** (*ming*) and **meaning/status** (*don*):
- **Change of Name:** Upon generating bodhicitta, the individual is no longer designated as a wretched samsaric prisoner. Instead, they undergo a change of name and are called "Bodhisattvas" (according to Sazang Mati Panchen) or "Sons of the Sugatas" (according to Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo).
- **Change of Meaning (Status):** This is not a mere nominal title; rather, they actually obtain the "exceptional meaning of that name." They become genuine objects of veneration, prostration, offerings, and respect for all worldly beings, including gods, humans, and asuras.

### 4. Doctrinal Universality (Democratic Nature of Bodhicitta)
Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen provides a crucial doctrinal clarification regarding the accessibility of this status:
- **No Exclusion:** This extraordinary status and the accompanying veneration are not restricted to advanced, high-level Bodhisattvas who have attained the great bhūmis (spiritual grounds). 
- **Immediate Empowerment:** Instead, even the most wretched, ordinary beings bound in the depths of samsara's prison are instantly empowered and endowed with these identical qualities the very moment they rouse the conventional mind of awakening.
