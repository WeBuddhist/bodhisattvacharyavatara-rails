# Day 20 — The flower you give, the flower that grows

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Offer one beautiful thing you see today, in your mind
> **Detail:** Shantideva offers flowers to the buddhas. Today, offer a beautiful thing and let one grow in you.

## Opening

After the scented oils, Shantideva offers flowers. He gathers the loveliest he can picture, mandarava blossoms, lotuses, sweet-smelling flowers of every kind, and strings them into garlands for the buddhas, the ones most worthy of an offering. It is a simple, glad thing to do: to hand over something beautiful.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.

> I take refuge in the Buddha,

> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

It seems like a one-way thing to hand someone a gift. It leaves you, and that is that.

The verse offers flowers to the buddhas, and Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, lingers on what kind of flowers: the most pleasing ones, gathered and strung with care. The tradition draws a quiet link between the flower in your hand and the mind that offers it. When you offer beauty gladly, without grabbing it back, something flower-like takes root in you: patience, and a settled, calm attention. The flower leaves your hand, but the qualities it asks of you, openness and ease, stay and grow. The one who is changed by the giving is you.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

When something beautiful catches your eye today, a flower, the sky, a clean stretch of light, pause and offer it in your mind to the buddhas and bodhisattvas. Give it freely, without holding on. Notice how letting it go leaves you a little calmer than keeping it would have.
