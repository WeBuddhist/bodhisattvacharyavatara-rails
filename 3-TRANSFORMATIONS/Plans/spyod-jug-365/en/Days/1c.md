# Day 1 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: Benefits of Bodhicitta — Verses 1-1 to 1-3

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Overcoming Pride (Humility as Foundation):** The great teacher Śāntideva opens his text by denying originality and literary skill. Practicing this kind of humility uproots intellectual arrogance and clears the ground for genuine spiritual growth.
*   **Strengthening Faith Through Study (Mind Training):** Writing down and studying these teachings causes the three kinds of faith to grow. Conviction in karma, admiration for noble qualities, and aspiration to practice all increase when the mind engages with the text.
*   **Inspiring Others of Equal Fortune (Altruistic Ripple):** Even when one's primary aim is personal training, sincere practice naturally benefits others who share the same aspiration. The secondary benefit to fellow practitioners is a natural fruit of authentic study.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-1**

> བདེ་གཤེགས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་མངའ་སྲས་བཅས་དང་། །
> ཕྱག་འོས་ཀུན་ལའང་གུས་པར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏེ། །
> བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་ནི། །
> ལུང་བཞིན་མདོར་བསྡུས་ནས་ནི་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ། །
>
> *Paying homage with reverence to the Sugatas — the Dharmakāya and their heirs — and to all who are worthy of veneration, I shall now briefly describe, in accordance with scripture, the practice of entering the discipline of the Sugatas' heirs.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-1**

The great teacher Śāntideva opens by bowing to the Buddhas, their heirs, and all worthy objects of veneration. This act of homage purifies obstacles and generates the merit needed to compose the treatise successfully. The "Sugata" — one who has gone to bliss — is defined through perfect abandonment of all obscurations and perfect realization of the two kinds of selflessness, distinguishing the Buddhas from all other spiritual figures.

---

#### **Verse 1-2**

> སྔོན་ཆད་མ་བྱུང་བ་ཡང་འདིར་བརྗོད་མེད། །
> སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་མཁས་པའང་བདག་ལ་ཡོད་མིན་ཏེ། །
> དེ་ཕྱིར་གཞན་དོན་བསམ་པ་བདག་ལ་མེད། །
> རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་ངས་འདི་བརྩམས། །
>
> *What is written here has not been said before, nor do I possess any skill in composition. Therefore I have no thought of benefiting others — I composed this solely to cultivate it in my own mind.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-2**

The great teacher Śāntideva expresses deep humility by denying both doctrinal originality and poetic skill. This twofold gesture systematically removes intellectual pride and arrogance before his peers. He writes this text primarily to train his own mind, preventing spiritual decline and allowing his realization to grow continuously through the act of setting it down in writing.

---

#### **Verse 1-3**

> དགེ་བ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་བདག་གི་དད་པའི་ཤུགས། །
> འདི་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་རེ་ཞིག་འཕེལ་འགྱུར་ལ། །
> བདག་དང་སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཀྱང་། །
> ཅི་སྟེ་འདི་དག་མཐོང་ན་དོན་ཡོད་འགྱུར། །
>
> *Through these writings, my faith in virtue may grow stronger for a while, and should another of equal fortune encounter this, perhaps it may also be of benefit to them.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-3**

This verse completes the introduction of humility by naming both a personal and an altruistic purpose for the text. Writing down these realizations strengthens three kinds of faith — conviction in karma, admiration for noble qualities, and aspiration to practice — which in turn nourish diligence, mindfulness, and wisdom. Those who share the same spiritual disposition and aspiration may encounter this text and find it equally beneficial for their own path.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Practicing Humility in Conversation:** Today, when I share an idea or opinion with others, I will notice any urge to claim originality or superiority. Instead of seeking credit, I will remind myself that the value of what I say comes from the teachings, not from me.
*   **2. Dedicating Study to Mind Training:** When I sit down to study today, I will set a clear intention: this is for training my own mind, not for gaining status or impressing others. I will hold that intention throughout my reading and reflection.
*   **3. Recognizing the Ripple of Sincere Practice:** Today, I will remember that when I sincerely practice — even quietly, even alone — my effort can benefit others who share the same aspiration. This thought will encourage me to practice wholeheartedly, knowing it is never just for myself alone.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Gone to Bliss (བདེ་གཤེགས་ / Sugata):** This title describes a Buddha who has perfectly abandoned all obscurations and perfectly realized the nature of all things. It is not a poetic phrase but a precise description of complete liberation. The three traditional aspects of "gone" — gracefully, without return, and completely — show how the Buddha's freedom differs from any lesser spiritual achievement.

*   **Dharmakāya (ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་ / Truth Body):** The deepest aspect of a Buddha's being, defined as the naturally pure sphere free from all obscurations. It is not a physical body but the very nature of awakened mind. When homage is paid to the Sugata's Dharmakāya, it is this timeless purity of wisdom and realization that is honored.

*   **Heirs of the Sugatas (བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ / Sons and Daughters of the Buddhas):** These are the great Heroes of Enlightenment — practitioners who have irreversibly dedicated themselves to the path of full awakening for the benefit of all beings. They are called heirs because they carry forward the Buddha's work, born from the union of faith, wisdom, compassion, and meditative joy.

*   **Mind Training (བསྒོམ་པ་ / Familiarization):** In this context, mind training means repeatedly bringing the teachings into one's own experience so that what has been understood does not fade but grows stronger over time. Writing down realizations is treated here as a form of mind training, since it anchors the understanding and prevents spiritual decline.

*   **Equal Fortune (སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་ / Fellow Practitioners of the Same Aspiration):** This phrase refers to other practitioners who share the same spiritual family, capacity, and heartfelt wish to follow the path of the Hero of Enlightenment. The text is secondarily intended for their benefit — those who are neither too advanced to need it nor too new to receive it.
