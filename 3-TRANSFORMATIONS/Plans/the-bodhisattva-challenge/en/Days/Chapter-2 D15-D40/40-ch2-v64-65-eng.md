## Today's Verse

> Before the Protectors, directly,
> with palms joined, and with fear of suffering,
> I repeatedly prostrate,
> and confess all those misdeeds. ^2-64

> May the guides consider my misdeeds
> as faults.
> Since this is not good,
> I will never commit them again. ^2-65

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 64 and 65 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Through not knowing, we have built up many mistakes and much wrongdoing. But we can feel regret for them. We can confess them in front of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and promise not to make such mistakes again. Then every fault becomes clean. So from today we should take up the practice of confessing wrongdoing, and of holding back from it afterwards, with real courage.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give scripture and a comparison that are valuable, easy to understand, and encouraging.

Master Nagarjuna says in the _Letter to a Friend_ that someone who was careless before but becomes careful later grows beautiful, like the moon once the clouds have gone. So if regret arises for the mistakes we have made, and we promise not to repeat them, every fault can be made clean.

Likewise, Khenpo Kunzang Palden says that we confess our wrongdoing to the buddhas and their heirs. They see it with the eye of wisdom. They hear it with ears that reach everywhere. They know it with minds that know everything. The confession is made in their actual presence.

Above all, he says, promising not to do wrong even at the cost of our own life is, among the four powers, the power of restraint. The four powers are regret, reliance, applying the antidotes, and restraint.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will admit my mistakes, and make a promise not to repeat them in future.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ We feel honest regret for the wrongdoing and the mistakes we have made. Then we promise never to make that kind of mistake again. That is the power of restraint. If the promise is a firm one, the wrongdoing we did earlier is made clean, and bad actions will not stain us in future. That is why it matters so much.
