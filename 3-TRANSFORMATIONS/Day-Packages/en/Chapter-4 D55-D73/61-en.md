## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the three verses 17 to 19 from the fourth chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. If we fall into the lower realms, we will not even hear the word "happy realms" for a hundred million eons. Let us think together about this frightening thought.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago, the naga king Sagara invited the Blessed Buddha into the ocean. He asked the Buddha this: "When I was born as a naga in the past, there were only a few nagas. Now there are so many that the ocean cannot hold them all. What is the reason for this?" The Buddha answered: "Whether bound by vows or not, it happens because people commit small wrongdoings and downfalls without care."

What does this story teach us? If we leave even small wrongdoing unwatched, it grows step by step, and we must take countless births in the lower realms. So we should do even small good actions, and be careful even with small wrongdoing.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, while I have the chance to do good, I will actually carry out one good action.

**Explanation:** *(Doing good)* Right now we have a good chance to do good. If we do not do good now, then later, if we are born in the lower realms, regret will come but it will not help. So let us work at doing good today. This brings happiness in this life and in all our future lives.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Present Careless Conduct of This Kind Means Even a Human Body Will Not Be Obtained Again

All the commentaries agree that continuing in conduct which commits wrongdoing and neglects virtue means that, forget liberation, even a higher rebirth as a human or a god will fail to be obtained in the future.

###### 2. Once a Human Body Is Not Obtained, One Has No Opportunity for Virtue in the Three Lower Realms, Only for Wrongdoing

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk both explain clearly that once born in the lower realms, one passes the time almost entirely in the wrongdoing of killing and eating one another, with no opportunity at all to accomplish virtue.

###### 3. A Story Shows How Even a Single Downfall Can Multiply Births in the Lower Realms Without Limit

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden cites the story of the naga king Sagara to show clearly that even a downfall unconnected with any vow becomes a cause for many births in the lower realms.

###### 4. If a Happy Rebirth Is Not Obtained, One Cannot Avoid Passing the Time in Nothing but Wrongdoing

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that if a human body is not obtained, accumulating wrongdoing comes naturally of itself, while accumulating fresh virtue becomes difficult.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that continuing in this kind of careless conduct means that, forget liberation, even a human rebirth will not be obtained again; and once a human body is not obtained, there is no opportunity for virtue in the lower realms, only for wrongdoing. - Khenpo Kunzang Pelden illustrates, with the story of the naga king Sagara, how even a single downfall multiplies rebirths in the lower realms without limit.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Since we ourselves want happiness, we must accomplish its cause, virtue; and this need to accomplish virtue is not a tax or a law imposed from outside — it is simply that we want happiness, and obtaining it depends on the cause, virtue. So there already exists, in our own self-interested regard for ourselves, a reason to accomplish virtue. Given this, even at a time such as the present — a good opportunity when virtue can be practiced — if each of us does not make a point of striving deliberately, then, once we have wandered into the states of the lower realms and become a slave to utter confusion, there is absolutely no way for the ability to accomplish virtue to arise at all. So we must accomplish virtue at this very opportunity.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-18]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The reasoning behind this is as follows: even though, having obtained freedoms and endowments such as these, I have the good fortune to apply the three doors to virtue, if I still do not accomplish even the slightest virtue, then in a future life, tormented by the suffering of the lower realms — the hells and so forth, the result of wrongdoing — and utterly confused about what to adopt and what to abandon, at that time, what virtue will I be able to do? There will be no way to do it at all.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-18]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

If one thinks: even should I abandon this present body, since I will obtain a human body again and again in future lives, it will be enough to accomplish the Dharma then and there — this is not correct. Through the power of carelessness, my present conduct, always engaging in nothing but non-virtue and never turning my mind toward virtue, means that I will not obtain a human body in a future life either, since I have not accomplished the virtue that would be its cause.

Moreover, if I fail to obtain such an excellent basis as a human body, one fit for accomplishing the Dharma, then for the most part I will fall into unfree states such as the lower realms, where I will continually accumulate nothing but wrongdoing and will have neither the fortune nor the opportunity to accomplish even the slightest virtue — so if even a single further human rebirth is hard to obtain, how much more so a continuous succession of them.

Moreover, even obtaining a divine body, though it has great splendor and the like, is not, in general, a superior basis for practicing the Dharma — and in particular for accomplishing the Mahāyāna path — as the human basis is; that is why it is said that the human basis is superior even to that of a god. It is not that obtaining just any basis other than the human one constitutes a superior basis for accomplishing the Dharma. Right now, having obtained a complete human body with the freedoms and endowments, one whose mind is capable and forceful with respect to adopting what is to be done and rejecting what is not, one has the ability to practice or accomplish virtuous Dharma, and one has the fortune for it — yet if, at that time, I do not strive with all my power to accomplish virtue, then later, when born in the lower realms, tormented for a long time by intense suffering, utterly and completely confused about the causes of benefit and harm and about what to adopt and abandon, understanding nothing, incapable in mind, and hence unfit to be applied to virtue, at that time what virtuous action will I be able to do? There will not be the slightest opportunity — so I must strive, right now, at this very occasion when the three doors are fit to be applied to virtue.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-18]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Even when one obtains such a distinctive human body, complete with the eight leisures and ten endowments, and thereby has the fortune and complete conditions to practice or accomplish something such as the three trainings of hearing, reflection, and meditation on the holy Dharma, or the threefold ethical discipline of a bodhisattva, if at that time I do not myself accomplish virtue properly and instead fall under the power of laziness and indolence, then in another rebirth, another life, I will be born in states such as the lower realm of hell, passing the time in sufferings such as heat, cold, hunger, and thirst; and at that time, utterly confused about what is to be adopted and rejected, it is taught that at that time there will be nothing I can do.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-18]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. If One Does Not Practice Virtue While One Has the Fortune to Do So, There Is Nothing One Can Do Once Utterly Confused by the Suffering of the Lower Realms

All the commentaries agree that having the good fortune to practice virtue now, but failing to do so, means that once born in the lower realms and tormented by suffering, utterly confused about what to adopt and reject, there will be no opportunity whatsoever to practice virtue.

###### 2. The Human Body Is a Distinctive Basis for Accomplishing the Dharma, Superior Even to a Divine Body

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that even obtaining a divine body does not surpass the human basis for accomplishing the Mahāyāna path, which is why it is said to be "a basis superior even to a god's."

###### 3. The Suhṛllekha Teaches That the Human Mind's Great Power Is What Is Distinctive

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites this text to show that the vehicle of the Sugata's path is found only through the human mind, not by gods, nāgas, or other such beings.

###### 4. The Madhyamakāvatāra Warns That If One Does Not Take Hold of Oneself While Autonomous, No One Can Lift One Out After Falling into the Abyss

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites this text to counsel that if one fails to take hold of oneself while conditions are favorable, no one will be able to rescue one after falling.

###### 5. Accomplishing Virtue Is a Matter of One's Own Self-Interest, Since One Wants Happiness

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that the need to accomplish virtue rests on one's own wish for happiness rather than on any external law, and counsels striving deliberately at the present opportunity.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that having the good fortune to practice virtue now, yet failing to accomplish it, means that once born in the lower realms and utterly confused by suffering, there will not be the slightest opportunity to practice virtue — so one must strive deliberately at the present opportunity. All the commentaries agree on this basic ground.

**Key points.**

- All the commentaries agree that failing to accomplish virtue while one has the fortune means that, once born in the lower realms and utterly confused by suffering, the opportunity for virtue is gone from the root.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains, citing the *Suhṛllekha*, that the Sugata's path is found only through the human mind's great power, not by any other kind of being.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam also cites the *Madhyamakāvatāra*'s warning that if one does not take hold of oneself while conditions are favorable, no one can lift one out after one falls into the abyss.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that accomplishing virtue serves one's own self-interest, since happiness is what one wants and its cause is virtue.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Since conditioned things do not remain static, one might ask: even if I do not accomplish virtue right now, is there not still room to hope that I might do so gradually later on, if I simply wait it out? But that too is not the case. Once one is born and abiding in the lower realms, virtue can only come about by depending on deliberate striving and effort — and lacking that, wrongdoing, accumulated of its own force out of habituation over many lifetimes, means that even though many millions of eons of time may pass under such conditions, there is no possibility of the lower-realm karma being exhausted at some point and liberation being reached. He teaches that it is extremely difficult to escape from having to fall into boundless states of the lower realms for many hundreds of millions of eons, without any chance even to hear the word "happy rebirth."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-19]]

##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Nor is it possible to turn back from the lower realms in this way: since I have not done even small virtuous deeds, and have instead accumulated various great wrongdoings, then, for a long time — for a hundred million eons — I will pass continuously from one lower realm to another; forget being born in a happy realm, I will not even hear the very word "happy rebirth."

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If I do not perform virtuous acts such as benefiting others, which are the cause of a higher rebirth, and instead accumulate many wrongdoings such as harming others, then, for a long time — for a hundred million eons — I will fall continuously from one lower realm to another; forget obtaining the result of a happy rebirth, I will not even hear the very word for it.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-19]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

It will be extremely difficult to obtain the freedoms and endowments afterward, and for this reason: in this life, I have not performed the virtue that would be the cause of obtaining a happy rebirth, and beyond that, I need not even deliberately seek out the wrongdoing that is the cause of going to the lower realms — it is accumulated immeasurably of its own accord, at every moment of thought and action, throughout an ordinary continuous course of life. Under the power of such wrongdoing, I will be reborn continuously from one lower realm to the next, for a long time — for a hundred million eons, that is, hundreds of thousands of millions of eons, and by the word "even," for longer still — and during that time I will not even hear the word "happy rebirth." Or alternatively, it may be construed: "for a long time, for a hundred million eons, I will not even hear the word 'happy rebirth.'" And by the trailing word "even," it draws the conclusion: if one does not hear even the word "happy rebirth," how much less will one actually obtain the exceptional basis of rebirth as a god or human in the happy realms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-19]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Having, upon a Dharma-endowed human body such as this one, generated the supreme aspiration to enlightenment, if I do not properly accomplish its trainings — such as the six virtuous perfections and the threefold ethical discipline of a bodhisattva — and instead accumulate wrongdoing, non-virtuous karma motivated entirely by faults such as attachment, which are contrary to those trainings, then, in birth after birth, in one life after another, for a hundred million eons — that is, for hundreds and thousands of eons — I will not even hear, by way of my ears, the very word "happy rebirth," the migration of gods and humans; needless to say, then, that I will not obtain the higher body of a happy rebirth at all.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-19]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. If One Does Not Do Good and Accumulates Wrongdoing, One Will Not Even Hear the Word "Happy Rebirth" for a Hundred Million Eons

All the commentaries agree in explaining that failing to do even small virtuous deeds in this life, while accumulating various kinds of wrongdoing, means passing continuously from one lower realm to another for a hundred million eons, without even hearing the word "happy rebirth."

###### 2. Minyak Kunzang Sonam Clarifies That Wrongdoing Accumulates of Its Own Accord Without Deliberate Effort

He counsels that virtuous action depends on deliberate effort, while wrongdoing is completed of its own accord at every moment of ordinary thought and action.

###### 3. Once Fallen into the Lower Realms, There Is No Possibility of Naturally Escaping Once the Karma of Wrongdoing Is Exhausted

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that once born in the lower realms, virtue can only arise through deliberate striving; without that, even after many eons pass under the sheer momentum of wrongdoing, there is no chance of the lower-realm karma being exhausted and liberation being reached.

###### 4. The Phrase "Not Even Hearing the Word 'Happy Rebirth'" Implies, All the More, Not Actually Obtaining It

Minyak Kunzang Sonam draws out the implication that if one does not even hear the word "happy rebirth," then needless to say one will not actually obtain the exceptional basis of a happy rebirth.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that if one does not do good and instead accumulates wrongdoing, one will pass continuously from one lower realm to another for a hundred million eons, without even hearing the word "happy rebirth" — showing the danger that the lower realms can extend without limit even without deliberate effort. All the commentaries agree on this basic ground.

**Key points.**

- All the commentaries agree that failing to do good while accumulating wrongdoing means not even hearing the word "happy rebirth" for a hundred million eons.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam clarifies the distinction that virtuous action depends on deliberate effort, while wrongdoing is completed of its own accord.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that once fallen into the lower realms, there is no possibility of naturally escaping once the karma of wrongdoing is exhausted.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam draws out that not even hearing the word "happy rebirth" implies, all the more, not actually obtaining it.
