## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the two verses 32 and 33 from the third chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. This is the close of the third chapter. Please look at this great promise, made with all the buddhas as witness: to take on all beings as guests for the happiness that comes at the end.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us something valuable that is easy to understand and encouraging.

This is a great promise. It invites all beings as guests to the state of a buddha in the end, and to the happiness of gods and humans for now. It takes that on as one's own responsibility. It is not empty talk. There is a real reason behind it.

To give oneself up from the bottom of the heart and work for the good of others takes courage that is astonishing. So no being in the three worlds is able to find fault with it.

And there is more. The bodhisattva who makes this promise is an ordinary person standing on the first stages of the path. He is still bound by every chain of the afflictions. For now he has no understanding that sees the truth directly. Even so, he gives rise to courage like that for the sake of all beings. That makes it more wonderful still.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, in the Buddha's presence, I will say my promise aloud once more for the happiness of all beings.

**Explanation:** *(Diligence Practice)* That verse shows that we should set down our promise to give happiness to all beings once again, and then make effort. If we hold the promise firmly in this way, saying it with our own voice, bodhicitta will not decline. It will grow higher and higher.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

When the verse says "the guests among wandering beings," the word "guest" here is not the guest of honour. It is the guest who is on the move — the traveller's guest, one who journeys along the road of samsaric existence. For those who travel that road and wish to enjoy the delights of happiness, this stands close by as the supreme means of attaining the happiness of the higher realms and of definite goodness; and so what satisfies all those great guests among sentient beings is this as well.

"Guest" is otherwise the word for a guest of honour whom someone great has invited especially.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-32]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The guests are the limitless wandering beings of the six classes. The road of existence runs from the peak of existence at the top down to the Hell of Unrelenting Torment at the bottom; they travel it, and by travelling it they come to suffer. Out of that they wish to enjoy the delights of happiness, both for the time being and finally. For them, this bodhisattva — by the power of bodhicitta — takes birth in samsara for the sake of everything that was shown above: their attaining the supreme happiness, the deathless peace, and the rest. That is what "stands close by" means: he stands right there beside them, the way a dyer's hand is caught and held by the dye. Beings do not stay put even for a moment; they draw ever nearer to death, and there is no ground they can reach where they might settle for good, and so they have become great guests. That they are filled and satisfied with whatever happiness they wish for is also by the power of bodhicitta. This is taught here as a summary of the meaning.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-32]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

The guests among wandering beings, who roam through samsara, travel the road of the three kinds of existence and wish to enjoy the delights of the happiness of gods and humans. For them this generation of the mind stands close by as the supreme means of accomplishing happiness, and it satisfies the great guests among sentient beings — because it accomplishes both the higher realms and definite goodness for every being.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-32]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

For the guests among wandering beings, who travel the road of existence and wish to enjoy the delights of happiness, this bodhicitta stands close by as the supreme happiness, and it satisfies the many great guests among sentient beings with happiness.

*(Note: this commentary's prose treats this verse's guest-feast simile together with the neighbouring similes of the elixir, treasure, medicine, tree, platform, moon and sun of bodhicitta in one continuous run, following a single combined quotation of verses 3-25–3-32; only the guest material pertaining to 3-32 is drawn on here. The commentary's own internal block numbering for this passage does not track the root verse numbers.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-72]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

The guests are the limitless wandering beings of the six classes. Again and again they travel the hard roads of samsaric existence, and they are worn out by every kind of suffering; and out of that they wish to attain and enjoy, in the end, the inexhaustible delight of great happiness. For them, this bodhicitta stands close by as the very cause that accomplishes, without exception, the supreme happiness both for the time being and finally. And therefore it satisfies the great guests among sentient beings with a perfect hospitality of happiness — so, he says, rejoice!

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-32]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

For a very long time, under the power of karma and the afflictions, wandering beings have been bound to samsara. These guests travel the hard road of the three kinds of existence — or the four — passing from one to the next without a break, and undergoing every kind of suffering they are brought low. Out of that they wish to enjoy a vast measure of the delights of happiness: the happiness of gods and humans for the time being, and the uncontaminated happiness at the end. For them, this precious bodhicitta stands close by as what has become the supreme means of accomplishing that whole perfect happiness.

And for that very reason this bodhicitta satisfies the great guests among sentient beings — those who are forever coming and going among the various abodes of the three realms, who never arrive at a place they have reached for good, who cannot stay even for a moment, whose numbers cannot be counted and whose destinations have no end — it satisfies them with every fine hospitality of happiness, immediate and long term. So, he says, give rise to joy, and guard it with care so that it does not decline.

As for the words "this is the supreme happiness": since this generation of the mind is the cause of happiness, the name of the result is given to the cause — just as, when the rain falls, people say "the rice crop has grown."

*(Note: this commentary observes that some other commentaries attach this verse to the "generating joy for others" section rather than to "generating joy for oneself.")*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-32]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

"The guests among wandering beings" means those beings who circle from one state to the next — birth, aging, sickness, death and the rest — in the abodes of samsara, the three realms of existence, and so keep travelling again and again. Whichever of them wish to enjoy delights of happiness for their own body and mind — the happiness produced by the concentrations and meditative absorptions and the like — for all of them this very generation of bodhicitta stands close by as the supreme happiness among all happiness.

In fact, if the practice of generating bodhicitta is done properly, then for the time being one is able to enjoy the delights of happiness in the higher realms — long life, freedom from illness, resources, influence and the rest. And not only that: it also becomes possible for the unsurpassed uncontaminated happiness of final liberation and omniscience itself to come within one's enjoyment. So it stands close by as the supreme of all happiness; and what satisfies the great guests among sentient beings — those who travel and circle again and again through the abodes of samsara, the three realms of existence — by accomplishing at some point every hope they hold in mind, is this very generation of bodhicitta. That is the meaning.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-32]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Bodhicitta Is the Supreme Happiness That Satisfies the Great Guests Wandering the Road of Existence

This verse applies to bodhicitta the image of the supreme happiness that satisfies the great guests. For beings who travel the road of existence and wish to enjoy the delights of happiness, this bodhicitta stands close by as the supreme of all happiness. In the end all the commentaries agree on this.

###### 2. Beings Are Called "Guests" Because They Cannot Stay Even for a Moment and Roam Again and Again Through the Abodes of Existence

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that beings do not stay put even for a moment, that they draw ever nearer to death, and that there is no ground they can reach where they might settle for good — and so they have become "great guests." Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk likewise explains that through birth, aging, sickness and death they circle from one abode of the three realms to the next, again and again.

###### 3. Bodhicitta Accomplishes Both Present Happiness and Final Happiness

Sabzang Mati Panchen explains that this bodhicitta is the cause that accomplishes, without exception, the supreme happiness both for the time being and finally. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk likewise explains that if it is taken up in practice, everything from the happiness of the higher realms for the time being to the uncontaminated happiness of final liberation and omniscience comes within one's enjoyment.

###### 4. Here "Guest" Does Not Mean a Guest of Honour but a Traveller on the Road of Existence

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that although "guest" is the word for a guest of honour whom someone great has invited especially, here it is taken in the sense of the traveller's guest — those who journey along the road of existence and wish to enjoy the delights of happiness.

###### 5. This Verse Closes the Section on Rejoicing at One's Own Purpose Accomplished, and the Next Verse Begins the Section on Rejoicing for Others

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen places his outline heading "second" at the end of this verse. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk likewise sets at the end of this verse the definite note that "with this much, meditating on joy for oneself has been taught."

###### 6. The Way Beings Roam Through Existence Is Shown in the Lalitavistara and the Letter to a Student, Like a Bee in a Vase or a Potter's Wheel

Minyak Kunzang Sonam uses the image from the *Lalitavistara* together with the verses quoted from the *Letter to a Student* to explain that beings wander endlessly on the wheel of existence, against their own wishes.

###### 7. "Supreme Happiness" Is a Way of Speaking in Which the Name of the Result Is Given to the Cause

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that, just as people say "the rice crop has grown" when the rain falls, so the generation of the mind is the cause of happiness, and the name of the result — happiness — is given to the cause.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse applies to bodhicitta the image of the supreme happiness that satisfies the great guests wandering the road of existence: it stands close by as the supreme means of accomplishing every happiness, both for the time being and finally. All the commentaries are agreed on this common ground.

**Key points.**

- Beings are called "guests" because they cannot stay even for a moment and roam again and again through the several abodes of existence.
- Bodhicitta accomplishes both the happiness of the higher realms for the time being and the uncontaminated happiness of final liberation.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama makes a special point that "guest" here does not mean a guest of honour specially invited, but the traveller's guest — one who journeys along the road of existence.
- This verse stands at the end of the chapter's section on "rejoicing at one's own purpose accomplished," and from the next verse the section on rejoicing for others begins; Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk appear to agree on this.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam illustrates the way beings roam through existence with the image from the *Lalitavistara* and the verses quoted from the *Letter to a Student*.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that "supreme happiness" is a way of speaking in which the name of the result is given to the cause, since the generation of the mind is the cause of happiness.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

So he teaches: today, before the protectors — the buddhas together with their heirs — I shall establish all wandering beings in that very sacred state of the Blissful One, the unsurpassed goal to be attained at the end. And in the meantime, until they have reached that fruit, I shall set them in the perfect higher states, in the good support of a god's or a human's life, so that they travel from happiness to happiness and are at last established on the ground of unsurpassed complete awakening. Having made something like that pledge, I have generated the mind of awakening. So, gods and demigods and all the rest — rejoice!

Is there not a little something like boasting in these words? No — they really do have a reason behind them. To say that one will give oneself up from the very depths of the heart and work for the good of others is simply astonishing; there is no being in the three realms who can find fault with it.

And there is more. A bodhisattva of this kind is an ordinary being on something like the paths of accumulation and joining, and is still bound by every one of his own fetters. Even though at present he has nothing like the direct seeing of the truth, that he gives rise to such courage is all the more worthy of wonder.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-33]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Second. Here the subject is taken up first: this is an invitation. Who invites? I do. When? From today. In what manner? Before the protectors — all the buddhas, the blessed ones, together with the assembly of bodhisattvas — that is, before those who have been set as witnesses. Whom does he invite? All wandering beings, as limitless as space. And what does he give them? The final happiness, the very state of a buddha, the Blissful One; and, until that is attained, the happiness of the present, the perfect happiness of the higher states, of gods and humans. To that he has already invited them as guests. So all the gods on the side of virtue — the seventy-five glorious protectors of the pure realms and the rest — and the demigods and all the others: rejoice! In this way he sets them to meditating on joy. Since they rejoice, they receive a share in the benefit and give protection; and since they have been set as witnesses, this also becomes a support for one's own sense of shame and conscience. That is the purpose it serves.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-33]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Today, before all the protectors — the victorious ones together with their heirs — I have already invited all wandering beings as guests: to the state of the Blissful One itself, in which every final benefit and happiness is complete, and, until that is attained, to the happiness of gods and humans, enough to enjoy to their full satisfaction. So from today onward, gods, demigods, creatures that crawl on their bellies and the rest — rejoice! In this way he sets others to giving rise to joy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-33]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

He gives the outline heading "second: giving rise to joy in others," and then: today, before all the protectors — that is, having set them as witnesses — I have already invited wandering beings as guests to the final happiness of the Blissful One, that is, of a buddha, and to happiness "in the meantime," which is the happiness of the present. So all beings, gods and demigods and the rest — rejoice!

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-75]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

I, who have generated the mind set on great awakening, from today onward, before the protectors — the buddhas and the bodhisattvas themselves, seated as witnesses — have already invited limitless wandering beings as guests to the great happiness of the state of the Blissful One at the end, and, for the time being until that is attained, to the perfect happiness of the higher states. Since I have pledged to accomplish benefit and happiness for all beings, let gods and humans and demigods and all the rest be utterly joyful and glad, with minds full of clear confidence.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-33]]

##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Today, before the protectors — all the victorious ones together with their heirs, who stand as the witnesses for every living creature — that is, in their presence, I have already invited every being throughout the whole of space as my guest: to the unsurpassed uncontaminated happiness of the Blissful One itself, which has reached the very end of all well-being; and, until that is attained, to the vast happiness of the higher states, of gods and humans, enough to enjoy as they please. So now that I have entered upon this ground of producing satisfaction for all beings with benefit and happiness that never runs out, let gods and humans, demigods, yakshas, nāgas and all the rest be openly glad and rejoice, with minds full of clear confidence. In this way he sets others to giving rise to joy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-33]]

##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

I, this person, today, before all the protectors — that is, before the protectors who protect beings from the states of samsara: the perfect buddhas, their heart-sons the bodhisattvas, and the teachers, the spiritual friends, along with them — in the presence of all of these, having set them as witnesses, have pledged to establish all wandering beings in the final state of the Blissful One, the perfect buddha, and have pledged to establish them in the meantime in the present happiness of gods and humans. To that I have already invited them as guests. Therefore, you gods of the desire realm, the form realm and the formless realm, and demigods and the rest — that is, humans, hell beings, hungry ghosts, animals and so on, in short all beings included in the six classes of wandering beings — meditate on joy in your hearts! And since I have already pledged to accomplish for you the aim of happiness, both present and final, meditate on joy at your ease and with a careful mind. In this way he sets others to meditating on joy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-33]]

##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **Khenpo Kunzang Pelden:** under the word "protectors" he counts only two among those set as witnesses — the buddhas, the blessed ones, and the assembly of bodhisattvas. ⚑
- **Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk:** under the word "protectors" he counts not just those two but three, adding the teachers, the spiritual friends, to the buddhas and the bodhisattvas. ⚑

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. This Verse Is the Final Pledge That Completes the Generation of Bodhicitta in Chapter Three

All the commentaries agree that before the protectors — the buddhas and the bodhisattvas — all wandering beings have already been invited as guests to the very state of the Blissful One at the end, and to the happiness of gods and humans in the meantime.

###### 2. The Commentaries Differ Over How Many Witnesses "the Protectors" Includes ⚑

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains it as two — the buddhas, the blessed ones, and the assembly of bodhisattvas — while Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk adds the teachers, the spiritual friends, to those two and explains it as three.

###### 3. "Invited as Guests" Means the Pledge to Establish All Beings in the Final State of the Blissful One and, Until Then, in the Happiness of Gods and Humans

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sabzang Mati Panchen, Minyak Kunzang Sonam and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk all explain it in the same way.

###### 4. The Purpose of Setting Witnesses Is to Build a Support for One's Own Sense of Shame and Conscience

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that setting the protectors as witnesses means the generation of the mind is protected and guarded, and that it also serves as a support for one's own sense of shame and conscience.

###### 5. "Gods and Demigods and the Rest" Covers All Six Classes of Beings and Is Said in Order to Give Rise to Joy in Others

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains the phrase as a name for all beings included in the six classes — the gods of the desire, form and formless realms, demigods, humans, hell beings, hungry ghosts and animals. Ngulchu Thogme names the outline heading of this very verse "giving rise to joy in others."

###### 6. That an Ordinary Person Makes So Courageous a Pledge Without Seeing the Truth Directly Is Deeply Astonishing

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that the pledge of this verse is not boasting but a promise, backed by real reasons, to work for the good of others from the very depths of the heart; and he explains that it is all the more astonishing that an ordinary bodhisattva gives rise to such courage without having seen the truth directly.

###### 7. This Verse Closes Chapter Three, "Fully Taking Hold of Bodhicitta," and Links On to Chapter Four, the Teaching on Carefulness

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sabzang Mati Panchen, Minyak Kunzang Sonam, Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and His Holiness the Dalai Lama all place the name of the third chapter — "Fully Taking Hold of Bodhicitta" — at the close of their comment, though the wording differs a little from one to another.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse states the final pledge that completes the generation of bodhicitta in the third chapter: before the protectors — the buddhas and the bodhisattvas — all wandering beings have already been invited as guests to the very state of the Blissful One at the end, and to the happiness of gods and humans until that is attained. All the commentaries are agreed that this is the meaning.

**Key points.**

- ⚑ The commentaries differ over how many witnesses "the protectors" includes: Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains it as two, the buddhas and the assembly of bodhisattvas, while Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk adds the teachers, the spiritual friends, and explains it as three.
- Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and many others explain in the same way that "invited as guests" means the pledge to establish beings in the final state of the Blissful One and, in the meantime, in the happiness of gods and humans.
- Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that the purpose of setting the protectors as witnesses is that the generation of the mind is protected and that it builds a support for one's own sense of shame and conscience.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that "gods and demigods and the rest" covers all six classes of beings, and that it is said in order to give rise to joy in others.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama speaks of it as a wonder that an ordinary person, without seeing the truth directly, gives rise to such great courage to work for the good of others from the very depths of the heart.
- Many commentaries agree that this verse closes the third chapter, "Fully Taking Hold of Bodhicitta," and links on to the fourth chapter, the teaching on carefulness.
