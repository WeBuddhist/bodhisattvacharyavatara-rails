## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on verses 25 through 27 from the second chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. It teaches us how to respectfully bow to our spiritual teachers, sacred stupas, and all sources of awakening. It also guides us to take complete refuge in the Three Jewels (Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha) until we reach enlightenment, and shows us how to fold our hands in prayer to begin purifying our past mistakes. This practice helps us build humility, trust, and spiritual strength.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

These verses explain three important steps: showing respect through prostrations, taking refuge, and praying to purify negative karma. Master Shantideva begins by bowing down to all the sources of awakening — such as Mahayana scriptures, Buddha statues, and sacred stupas. He also pays deep respect to spiritual preceptors (monastic abbots), teachers who guide us in moral conduct, and all sincere practitioners who hold vows and strive for spiritual freedom.

Next, unlike ordinary approaches that seek protection only for this lifetime, Shantideva takes a vast vow: he takes refuge in the Buddha, the Holy Dharma, and the community of Bodhisattvas from today until he reaches the very essence of enlightenment (Bodhicitta). Following this strong trust, he begins the practice of confession by visualizing all the awakened Buddhas and deeply compassionate Bodhisattvas across the ten directions. Seeing them in the sky before him, he respectfully folds his hands and speaks words of sincere prayer.

Spiritual teachers explain that bowing to our teachers and sacred symbols crushes our ego and pride. Taking vast refuge in the Three Jewels protects us from fears, negative energies, and illness, planting the seeds of ultimate liberation. Finally, folding our hands and praying to compassionate beings gives us the spiritual foundation (the "power of reliance") needed to wash away our past mistakes and purify our hearts.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, relying on the Three Jewels to avoid wrongdoing, I will practice humble patience whenever someone makes me angry.

**Explanation:** *(Patience Practice)* Usually, because of our pride and ego, we quickly lose our temper whenever someone insults us or causes us minor harm, reacting with anger and creating negative karma. Today, following the teaching of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* to "take refuge in the Buddhas until reaching enlightenment," I will place my complete trust in the Three Jewels. When someone says unkind words or provokes me today, I will not fight back with anger. Instead, remembering the compassionate Buddhas and Bodhisattvas, I will respond with humility, kindness, and patient endurance. This practice destroys our pride and anger while building deep spiritual peace and positive energy.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

To the bases of bodhicitta — the bodhisattva collections of scripture, and the places of his complete awakening and the rest — and to **the reliquary shrines of the Tathāgata** as well, I bow. And to **the abbot** who bestows the trainings, and likewise to **the master** who shows what is to be given up and what taken up, and to those of **the highest discipline**, who carry the banner of the saffron robe: to them I bow.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

"The bases of bodhicitta" means the places where the Teacher, in his earlier births — as in the life as King Śibi — performed this or that deed. For example, those places where the Tathāgata set foot and stayed, such as Vajrāsana, Kuśinagara and Vārāṇasī; and those places where, when he was still a bodhisattva, he carried out this or that hard-to-do deed. And further, the shrines in which a portion of the relics of the Tathāgata's body resides: to these too I bow — that is the sense. And "to the abbots, likewise the masters, and to the highest discipline I bow" means bowing to abbots and masters of the kind spoken of in the Vinaya as the two abbots and the five masters; and also to those who abide in the highest discipline, training in the paths of the three vehicles — that is, those who have attained the grounds.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-25]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Likewise, "whatever bases of bodhicitta there are" means images of the Buddha; the collections of the Great Vehicle scriptures; the place where the Teacher was born and the rest; sites such as Semkyé Ling, where the Dharma is taught and heard; and whatever supports of body, speech and mind, and whatever shrines there are, however many — to all of these I too bow. He speaks also of the abbot for the vows of individual liberation, and of the one from whom their scriptural transmission is received. Likewise the masters — the one who instructs in the rites of a novice or a fully ordained monk, the reading-teacher, and so on. And as for "discipline" (brtul zhugs): having tamed (brtul) the former and entered upon (zhugs) the latter, that is discipline; and from those who hold no more than the outward marks of following the Teacher upward, these are the highest discipline — the white-robed, braided-hair order and the ordained saffron-robed order. Moved by unwavering faith, I bow to them with the three doors in reverence: so it is said.

As for the manner of bowing, he explains that it must be done through all three doors of body, speech and mind; and he also sets out the bodily manner of bowing given in the *Prātimokṣa Sūtra* (the Sutra of Individual Liberation) — the two hands joined at the crown like a lotus bud just opening.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-25]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->
##### Zhenphen Jampé Gocha (annotation commentary)

In Zhenga's annotation commentary the underlying gloss is set out from the side of the annotation itself, whether or not a word-commentary is set beside it: bowing to the bases of bodhicitta and to the reliquary shrines of the Tathāgata; and bowing to the abbot who bestows the trainings, to the master who shows what is to be given up and what taken up, and to those of the highest discipline who carry the banner of the saffron robe. Thus the underlying gloss sets it out in brief.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^2-25]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

The basis of bodhicitta is the cause from which that mind arises: the collections of the Great Vehicle scriptures; the persons who act as the condition for that mind to be born, and the places where it was generated; and images of the Buddha and other such shrines — to these I bow. And to the abbots, those who complete the earlier and later portions of the vows of individual liberation and so on; and likewise to the masters, those who bestow the vows and so on; and to those who take up the burden of the highest discipline, striving on the path to liberation — to these too I bow.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^2-25]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

To the bases in dependence on which bodhicitta arises — images of the Buddha, the bodhisattva collections of scripture and the rest — and to the bases where that mind was generated, or meditated on, or taught, and to the shrines as well, I bow. And to the abbot from whom the Dharma is received, and likewise to the masters, and to those possessed of the highest discipline who strive for the sake of liberation: to them too I bow.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^2-25]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Furthermore, to the special sites and places that are the support of generating the mind of enlightenment itself, and to all the shrines that stand at the eight great places and elsewhere, I too bow. And to the abbot, the one from whom the scriptural transmission is received, and likewise to the master who teaches the meaning of liberation, and to those who abide in the highest discipline — the vows of pure conduct and so on — to these too I bow with reverence.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-25]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

"The bases of bodhicitta" are the objects in dependence on which that mind of enlightenment arises and is born: the collections of the Great Vehicle scriptures and images of the Buddha; those such as the Great Vehicle spiritual friend, who act as the dominant condition for that mind to be born; the places where the mind was turned toward enlightenment; and the birthplaces spoken of in the birth stories and the rest. It is explained that the four causes for generating bodhicitta taught in the *Sutra of the Ten Dharmas* are likewise bases of bodhicitta. As for the eight shrines: the Heaped-Lotus shrine, built at the time of his birth; the Subduing-of-Māra shrine, of the occasion of his complete and manifest awakening; the Miracle shrine, of the time he tamed the six teachers; the Reconciliation shrine; the Descent-from-Heaven shrine; the Many-Doors-of-Auspiciousness shrine, of the time he turned the wheel of Dharma; the Complete-Victory shrine, of the time he blessed the formative factors of his lifespan; and the Parinirvāṇa shrine — eight in all. And to the abbots for the earlier and later portions of the vows of individual liberation; and likewise to the reading-master; and to those possessed of the highest discipline — shravakas, pratyekabuddhas, bodhisattvas and ordinary practitioners — or else to those who take up the burden of the highest discipline, such as the refuge commitment and the vows of a lay practitioner: to these too I bow. The benefits of bowing should be followed in detail as they appear in the *Sutra of the Three Heaps*; and the *Vinayavibhaṅga* says that if one bows with reverence, one gathers roots of virtue for being born a universal monarch as many times as there are dust-motes in the ground the body covers.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-25]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

"The bases of bodhicitta": that which makes the generation of bodhicitta be attained where it has not yet been attained, makes what has been attained spread without declining, and makes what has not declined increase further and further — that basis is the bodhisattva collections of scripture. "The shrines": the Heaped-Lotus shrine, built at the time when the Teacher, the perfect Buddha Shakyamuni, was first born in the Lumbinī grove; the Subduing-of-Māra shrine, of the occasion of his complete and manifest awakening at Vajrāsana; the Many-Doors-of-Auspiciousness shrine, of the occasion of his turning the wheel of Dharma at Vārāṇasī; the Descent-from-Heaven shrine, of the occasion of his descending from the abode of the gods of the Thirty-Three to the city of Sāṃkāśya; the Reconciliation shrine, of the occasion when the schism in the circle of the Sangha was healed; the Miracle shrine, of the occasion when he displayed miracles at Śrāvastī; the Complete-Victory shrine, of the time he blessed the formative factors of his lifespan; and the shrine built from the relics of his body, of the occasion of his passing beyond sorrow — to those eight kinds of shrine too I bow, through the reverence of the three doors. Furthermore, to the abbot who bestows the vows of a novice and of a fully ordained monk; and to the reading-master, the master of residence, the master for the lay vows, the master for the novice vows, and the master who bestows the vows of full ordination; and to the highest discipline — those who have tamed misconduct and entered upon good conduct — the ordained order of the Sangha, who carry the banner of the saffron Dharma robe, together with the order of white-robed, braided-hair mantra practitioners: to these too I bow through the reverence of the three doors. This is what is taught as the branch of paying homage: the remedy that cleanses those misdeeds of pride and self-importance by which, from beginningless time until now, one has failed to honour those who have good qualities.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-25]]

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **minyak-kunzang-sonam / khenpo-kunga:** rather than leaving "shrine" in its broad sense of whatever supports of the relics and the rest there may be, they identify it specifically as the eight kinds of shrine, and set the matter out in detail through those eight: Heaped Lotus, Subduing of Māra, Turning the Wheel of Dharma, Descent from Heaven, Reconciliation, Display of Miracles, Complete Victory, and Parinirvāṇa.
- **tenzin-gyatso:** he does not set out the scheme of the eight shrines at all; he gives "shrine" a different sense — the places where the Teacher performed his hard deeds in the birth stories, and the shrines in which a portion of the relics resides.
- **kunpal / khenpo-kunga:** they identify "the highest discipline" as two actual orders: the white-robed, braided-hair order (the white-robed mantra practitioners) and the ordained saffron-robed order.
- **gyaltsab / ngulchu-thogmed:** they name no definite order, but set it out generally as anyone at all who strives on the path to liberation.
- **minyak-kunzang-sonam / tenzin-gyatso:** they explain "the highest discipline" with a specific class of person in view — the noble shravakas, pratyekabuddhas and bodhisattvas who have attained the grounds, or those who train on the paths of the three vehicles and have attained the grounds.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Third Object of the Branch of Paying Homage — the Bases of Bodhicitta and the Shrines

With this verse the branch of paying homage that began at verse 2-24 is completed: it shows how one bows to the special places that support the generation of bodhicitta, and to the shrines.

###### 2. "The Bases of Bodhicitta" Covers the Causes and Conditions for Generating Bodhicitta

The texts of the Great Vehicle collections, images of the Buddha, the person who acts as the condition for the mind to be generated (the spiritual friend), and the places where the mind is generated or taught are all called "bases of bodhicitta."

###### 3. "Shrine" Means a Support of the Relics

Some explain it generally as whatever supports of body, speech and mind there are, while Minyak Kunzang Sonam and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk lay it out in detail through the eight kinds of shrine connected with the Teacher's twelve deeds. ⚑

###### 4. "Abbot" and "Master" Apply to the One Who Bestows the Vows and the One Who Teaches the Trainings

The abbot is the one who bestows the trainings, or who completes the earlier and later portions of the vows of individual liberation; the master is the one who shows what is to be given up and what taken up, or who bestows the vows. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk divides the masters in detail into five: the reading-master, the master of residence, the master for the lay vows, the master for the novice vows, and the master who bestows the vows of full ordination.

###### 5. There Are Differences in How "the Highest Discipline" Is Identified

Kunzang Pelden and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk set out both the white-robed, braided-hair order and the ordained saffron-robed order; Gyaltsab and Ngulchu Thogme explain it generally as anyone who strives on the path to liberation; Minyak Kunzang Sonam and His Holiness the Dalai Lama explain it with a specific class of person in view — the noble ones who train on the paths of the three vehicles and have attained the grounds. ⚑

###### 6. The Purpose of the Branch of Paying Homage — a Remedy for the Misdeeds of Pride and Self-Importance

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains this branch of paying homage as the remedy that purifies the misdeeds of the pride and self-importance by which, from beginningless time, one has failed to honour those who have good qualities.

###### 7. The Method of Bowing — Reverence in All Three Doors of Body, Speech and Mind

Citing the *Prātimokṣa Sūtra*, Kunzang Pelden sets out in detail the bodily manner of bowing: the two hands joined palm to palm at the crown, like a lotus bud just opening.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** With this verse the branch of paying homage that began at verse 2-24 is brought to completion: beyond bowing to the special bases that support the generation of bodhicitta and to the shrines, it explains that one also bows to those who abide in the highest discipline — represented here by the abbots and masters who bestow the vows of individual liberation and teach the trainings.

**Key points.**

- Many commentaries agree in taking "the bases of bodhicitta" to mean the Great Vehicle collections of scripture, images of the Buddha, the spiritual friend who acts as the condition for the mind to be generated, and the places where the mind is generated or taught.
- ⚑ On "shrine," some explain it generally as the supports of body, speech and mind, while Minyak Kunzang Sonam and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk lay it out in detail through the eight kinds of shrine, and His Holiness the Dalai Lama extends it also to the places where the Teacher performed his hard deeds in the birth stories and to the shrines holding a portion of the relics.
- "Abbot" and "master" apply to the persons who bestow the vows and teach the trainings; Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk divides the masters in detail into five.
- ⚑ There are differences in how "the highest discipline" is identified: Kunzang Pelden and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk place it in the two orders, the white-robed and the ordained; Gyaltsab and Ngulchu Thogme explain it generally as anyone who strives on the path to liberation; Minyak Kunzang Sonam and His Holiness the Dalai Lama place it with the noble ones who train on the paths of the three vehicles and have attained the grounds.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk states plainly that the purpose of this branch of paying homage is as a remedy that purifies the misdeeds of pride and self-importance accumulated since beginningless time through failing to honour those with good qualities; and Kunzang Pelden, citing the *Prātimokṣa Sūtra*, also sets out the manner of bowing with the two hands joined at the crown like a lotus bud just opening.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

As to duration: "until I reach the heart of enlightenment," which is to say, until I attain buddhahood. As to the objects: to the buddhas, and likewise to the Dharma comprised in the cessations and paths of the Great Vehicle, and to the bodhisattvas who train in such a Great Vehicle path — that is, to such a Great Vehicle Sangha — I go for refuge.

This going for refuge, which comes before confession, is called "the power of the support": since whatever wrongdoing one has done was accumulated in relation to some object, the confession has to be made in dependence on the Jewels above — hence "the power of the support." And there are two: this relying upward on the Jewels, and, focusing downward on beings, generating bodhicitta and so working to purify, by means of the mind, the wrongdoing done in relation to them.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-26]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The second of the branches of going for refuge: the Great Vehicle refuge. With the intention that all beings, as limitless as space, may attain the state of a perfect buddha, from this moment until I reach the heart of enlightenment I go for refuge to the buddhas, who have the nature of the four bodies and the five wisdoms. And to the sublime Dharma, whose nature is scripture and realization, and to the assembly of the noble Sangha of bodhisattvas, I likewise go for refuge, just as before.

As to the distinctiveness of the objects, they are the Three Jewels special to the Great Vehicle. The Buddha: three qualities of perfect benefit for himself — not compounded by causes and conditions, spontaneously accomplished in his qualities, and realized directly by individually self-knowing wisdom rather than through the conditioning of another — together with three qualities of perfect benefit for others: knowing, loving, and capability. Endowed with these eight qualities, he is the four bodies, or the three bodies. The Dharma: the Dharma of realization, which has the character of the two truths and is endowed with the eight qualities, and the Dharma of scripture, the Dharma of the Great Vehicle collections. The Sangha: the assembly of Great Vehicle noble ones who will not turn back.

The benefits of having gone for refuge in this way are that the seed of liberation is planted, that one is brought into the ranks of Buddhists, that one is protected from fears, that one comes to attain enlightenment, and so on.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-26]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->
##### Zhenphen Jampé Gocha (annotation commentary)

Going for refuge to the Three Jewels: "until I reach the heart of enlightenment" carries the sense of "until complete enlightenment has been attained"; and the Dharma is explained as having the nature of the two truths.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^2-26]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

The meaning of going for refuge is to hold with the mind, and to express in speech, that they are the support which frees one from fear and suffering. Of this there are two: causal refuge and resultant refuge. The first is holding the already-accomplished Three Jewels to be the support that frees one from fear; the second is holding the buddha and the Dharma Jewel that are to be attained in one's own continuum to be the support that frees one from fear.

On this occasion, taking as the objects of focus both the Great Vehicle Three Jewels already accomplished in others and the Three Jewels yet to arise in one's own continuum, from this moment until one reaches the heart of unsurpassable enlightenment — that is, until the Truth Body is made manifest beneath the bodhi tree — I go for refuge to the buddhas; and to the Great Vehicle Dharma Jewel and to the assembly of bodhisattvas, the noble bodhisattvas, I likewise go for refuge. That is, one holds and acknowledges the already-accomplished buddha as the teacher who shows the path, the Dharma Jewel to be attained in one's own continuum as the actual refuge, and the already-accomplished Great Vehicle noble ones as companions for accomplishing refuge.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^2-26]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

As to duration: until I reach the heart of enlightenment. As to the objects: the buddhas, and likewise the Dharma and the assembly of bodhisattvas. As to the manner: "I go for refuge" — that is, "since I rely on you, protect me."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^2-26]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

So that all beings, as limitless as the reaches of space, may be freed, from this moment and for as long as it takes to reach the heart of enlightenment, I go for refuge to the buddhas, the blessed ones. And to the sublime Dharma and to the assembly of the noble Sangha of bodhisattvas I likewise go for refuge, just as before — entrusting my mind to them and acknowledging that I will rely on them.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-26]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Here, in the Great Vehicle context, "from this time until I reach the heart of enlightenment": the Great Commentary says that the word "heart" is used as in the essence of milk or curd, or the essence of butter. Understood in terms of place, the heart of enlightenment is the place where the Truth Body is made manifest — Akaniṣṭha, the place of awakening in the Enjoyment Body, and Vajrāsana beneath the bodhi tree, the place of awakening in the supreme Emanation Body: until one reaches, or goes to, that heart of enlightenment. Understood in terms of realization, the heart of enlightenment is enlightenment itself, since every obscuration together with its habitual tendencies has been abandoned and the way all things are, suchness, has been understood in the heart. Until such unsurpassable, complete enlightenment is reached or attained, I go for refuge to the perfect buddhas, the distinctive objects of refuge; and likewise to the Great Vehicle Dharma Jewel and to the assemblies of the noble Sangha of bodhisattvas — that is, until the heart of enlightenment, I go for refuge.

How does one go for refuge? By holding with the mind and acknowledging with speech that they are the very support which frees one from fear and suffering: the Buddha Jewel as the one who shows the fearless path, the one who shows refuge; the Dharma Jewel as the fearless path itself, the actual refuge; and the Sangha Jewel as the companion for accomplishing the fearless path, the companion for accomplishing refuge — holding them each as such and acknowledging that one will rely on them. He says that holding the Three Supreme Ones of the Great Vehicle already accomplished in others as the support that frees one from fear and suffering is causal refuge, and holding the Three Supreme Ones yet to arise in one's own continuum as that support is resultant refuge.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-26]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Here the meaning of going for refuge is set out clearly under a series of headings. First, the divisions of refuge: the refuge common to the vehicles, and the refuge special to the Great Vehicle. Although shravakas and pratyekabuddhas make the Sangha and the Dharma Jewel respectively their ultimate refuge, practitioners of the Great Vehicle hold the Buddha Jewel to be the ultimate refuge. Second, the difference in duration: shravakas and pratyekabuddhas go for refuge for as long as life or the body lasts, whereas practitioners of the Great Vehicle, by the words "until I reach the heart of enlightenment," go for refuge until the state of complete enlightenment is attained. Third, the difference in cause: shravakas and pratyekabuddhas go for refuge chiefly by way of faith, whereas practitioners of the Great Vehicle go for refuge moved by a fierce compassion that cannot bear the suffering of other beings. Fourth, the difference in purpose: shravakas and pratyekabuddhas do so for their own sake, whereas practitioners of the Great Vehicle go for refuge for the sake of others.

Fifth, the purpose of going for refuge: it serves chiefly the arising of the vows of individual liberation and the rest, for going for refuge to the Three Jewels must come first. Therefore, if one holds the refuge commitment, one is able to be guarded from all the harms of non-human beings — the ghosts, demons and spirits of the world and the rest — and one has great protection from every illness and danger; the obscuration of wrong views, such as holding that there is no karmic cause and effect, becomes thinner; and one is brought into the ranks of Buddhists.

Sixth, as for the trainings that follow from going for refuge, four are common: relying on a lama, a spiritual friend; listening to the Dharma properly; growing accustomed to loving-kindness for beings; and offering to the Three Jewels from time to time. Three are special: having gone for refuge to the Buddha, not to go for refuge to other gods; having gone for refuge to the sublime Dharma, to give up any intent to harm or injure beings; and having gone for refuge to the Sangha, not to bow to those of non-Buddhist schools.

#### Stories and Illustrations

<!-- story:BCACXX_WR -->
##### BCACXX_WR — The Story of the Old Monk

This is anchored directly on the words of this verse, "I take refuge in the buddhas." Long ago a dull-witted old monk went out for alms, and a laywoman patron offered him a pair of cloth lengths and asked him for Dharma. Knowing no Dharma, he said only, again and again, "Suffering, suffering." From this the old woman understood that suffering as a result arises from not knowing its cause, and she saw the truth. Just then a thief set about stealing the cloth from the old monk; the old monk, frightened, forced the thief's hands through the hole of a wooden stock, tied him with rope, and made him take refuge in each of the Three Jewels in turn, beating him once with a stick for each. That thief, knowing only that one refuge and nothing else, took refuge with faith in all of it; and later, when he had hidden beneath a bridge, even a host of non-human beings could not overcome him. He too went forth into homelessness and attained the fruit.

<!-- story:BCACXX_WR -->
##### BCACXX_WR — The Story of the God's Child

It is well known that the five signs of death appeared for the god's son Stainless Jewel, and that after dying he was to be born in Jambudvīpa in the womb of a sow. Indra was unable to protect him, so the Buddha gave him refuge; and having died he was born as a brahmin, and later was born in Tuṣita. Scattering flowers before the Buddha, he spoke this verse: "Whoever goes for refuge to the Buddha will not go to the lower realms; casting off the human body, they will then obtain the body of a god." Thus he showed the benefit of going for refuge to the Buddha, and it is said that the same applies to the Dharma and the Sangha as well.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Great Vehicle Refuge — Going for Refuge to the Three Jewels

All the commentaries finally agree that this verse expresses the pledge to go for refuge to the Three Jewels: the Buddha, the sublime Dharma, and the assembly of bodhisattvas.

###### 2. The Duration of the Refuge — Until I Reach the Heart of Enlightenment

Since the Great Vehicle refuge lasts until complete enlightenment has been attained, Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk states plainly that it differs from the refuge of shravakas and pratyekabuddhas, which lasts only for as long as life lasts.

###### 3. The Nature of the Three Jewels, the Objects of Refuge

The Buddha is the one who has the nature of the four bodies and five wisdoms, the teacher endowed with the eight qualities; the sublime Dharma has the nature of scripture and realization, or the nature of the two truths; and the assembly of bodhisattvas is the noble Sangha abiding on the ten grounds. Each is identified separately.

###### 4. The Division Into Causal Refuge and Resultant Refuge

Both Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam explain that holding the already-accomplished Three Jewels of others to be the support that frees one from fear is causal refuge, and holding as that support the Three Jewels yet to arise in one's own continuum is resultant refuge.

###### 5. Four Differences Between the Great Vehicle and the Lesser Vehicle in Going for Refuge

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains how the Great Vehicle refuge surpasses that of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas by way of three points: the difference in duration (for as long as life lasts, versus until the heart of enlightenment), the difference in cause (faith, versus compassion), and the difference in purpose (one's own benefit, versus the benefit of others).

###### 6. The Trainings That Follow From Going for Refuge — Four Common and Three Special

The four common ones are relying on a lama, listening to the Dharma properly, growing accustomed to loving-kindness for beings, and offering to the Three Jewels from time to time; the three special ones — having gone for refuge to the Buddha, not to go to other gods; having gone for refuge to the Dharma, to give up any intent to harm or injure beings; and having gone for refuge to the Sangha, not to bow to those of non-Buddhist schools — Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains by citing the *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*.

###### 7. The Benefits of Going for Refuge

It is explained that the seed of liberation is planted, that one is brought into the ranks of Buddhists, that one is protected from every harm of non-human beings and from illness and malign forces, and that the obscuration of wrong views becomes thinner; and the *Sūryagarbha Sūtra* and the *Vimala Sūtra* also say that the merit of going for refuge is limitless.

###### 8. This Refuge Serves as "the Power of the Support," the Preliminary to Confessing Wrongdoing

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that among the four powers, the power of the support is precisely this going for refuge: since wrongdoing is accumulated in relation to some object, the confession must be made in dependence upon the Jewels themselves.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse expresses the Great Vehicle refuge: the pledge to go for refuge, until one reaches the heart of enlightenment, to the Three Jewels — the Buddha, the sublime Dharma, and the assembly of bodhisattvas. All the commentaries finally agree that this is the refuge special to the Great Vehicle, surpassing the refuge of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas (which lasts only for as long as life lasts) by its difference in duration: until complete enlightenment is attained.

**Key points.**

- The Three Jewels, the objects of refuge, are identified separately: the Buddha (who has the nature of the four bodies and five wisdoms), the sublime Dharma (whose nature is scripture and realization, or else the two truths), and the noble assembly of bodhisattvas.
- ⚑ On the definition of the sublime Dharma, Kunzang Pelden says "having the nature of scripture and realization," while Zhenga's annotation commentary says "having the nature of the two truths" — so they differ in the enumeration they settle on.
- Both Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam give the division into causal refuge (the Jewels already accomplished in others) and resultant refuge (the Jewels yet to arise in one's own continuum).
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that the Great Vehicle refuge is superior to that of the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas in its duration, its cause (compassion) and its purpose (the benefit of others); and he also sets out more than seven trainings, both common and special.
- The two stories of the old monk and of the god's child show the wondrous benefit of going for refuge to the Buddha.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama places this going for refuge as "the power of the support" among the four powers of confessing wrongdoing, thereby linking it to the branch of confession that follows.

##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

By these words, without any division of place or time — in short, to the perfect buddhas and the bodhisattvas possessed of great compassion who abide in the ten directions and the three times — I join my palms, which is the distinctive act of my body, and the heart of what I ask, of my request, is this.

##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Fourth, the branch of confessing wrongdoing, which has two parts: making the object of confession present, and the actual confession. As to the first: the objects of confession are the completely perfect buddhas, the blessed ones, who abide in all the worlds of the ten directions, limitless as they are, and the great bodhisattvas whose hearts are possessed of great compassion. Visualizing them in the sky before one, joining the palms with the body and making the request with the voice — that is, offering this petition — one says: "In this way…"

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-27]]

##### Zhenphen Jampé Gocha (annotation commentary)

Confessing wrongdoing. To the completely perfect buddhas who abide in all the world-systems of the ten directions, and to however many bodhisattvas possessed of great compassion abide there, I join my palms in reverence and make my request in spoken words. Thus, at the outset of confessing wrongdoing, the underlying gloss sets out in brief the manner of joining the palms in reverence and making one's request to the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the ten directions, the objects of confession.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^2-27]]

##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

To the perfect buddhas who abide in all directions, and to the bodhisattvas possessed of great compassion, I join my palms and make the request that, with regret for the wrongdoing I have done, I may confess it.

##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

The objects of confession are the perfect buddhas who abide in all directions, and the bodhisattvas possessed of great compassion. Before them, joining the palms with the body, one makes the request with the voice, as follows. (This verse is presented together with 2-28 and 2-29, and belongs under the heading "the manner of confessing wrongdoing, taught in general.")

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^2-27]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

The objects are the completely perfect buddhas, the blessed ones, who abide in all the limitless world-systems of the ten directions, and the bodhisattvas, the great beings possessed of great compassion. Visualizing them before one, one joins the palms with the body.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-27]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Making present the objects of confession — the perfect buddhas, the blessed ones, who abide in all the immeasurable world-systems of the ten directions, together with the assemblies of bodhisattvas, the great beings, who are possessed of the great compassion that wishes to free all beings from suffering along with its causes — one then makes one's confession with the mind: with faith and reverence toward those to whom the confession is offered, and with fierce regret for the wrongdoing done before; and in one's bodily conduct one bows the body and joins the two palms; and with the voice one makes the request, letting the words of confession be heard. Thus he teaches that confession must be made with the physical and verbal signs, and with a thoroughgoing intention.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-27]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

With these words, first, making present the objects of the confession of wrongdoing: to all the perfect buddhas, the blessed ones who have brought the two benefits to completion and who abide in all the boundless, endlessly extending world-systems of the ten directions — east, south, west and the rest — and to their heart-sons, the bodhisattvas, chiefly those abiding on the ten grounds, together with those abiding on the path of accumulation and the path of joining, who have become possessed of a great compassion that has no object of focus: to all of these, the person himself joins his palms with his body in reverence and makes his request with his voice in reverence, as follows. Thus, first, the objects of confession are made present.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-27]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. With This Verse the Branch of Confessing Wrongdoing Begins, at Its First Part: Making the Objects of Confession Present

Both Kunzang Pelden and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk state plainly that the branch of confessing wrongdoing has two parts — making the objects of confession present, and the actual confession — and that this is the first of them.

###### 2. The Objects of Confession Are the Perfect Buddhas Who Abide in All the Limitless World-Systems of the Ten Directions, and the Bodhisattvas Possessed of Great Compassion

All the commentaries agree in giving as the objects of confession the perfect buddhas who abide in all the world-systems of the ten directions, and the bodhisattvas possessed of great compassion.

###### 3. Visualizing the Objects of Confession in the Sky Before One

Kunzang Pelden explains that the buddhas and bodhisattvas, the objects of confession, are visualized in the sky before one, and Sabzang Mati Panchen too says that they are visualized in front.

###### 4. The Confession Is Begun by Joining the Palms With the Body and Making the Request With the Voice

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that bowing the body in one's conduct, joining the two palms, and letting the words of confession be heard with the voice means that confession must be made with the physical and verbal signs and with a thoroughgoing intention.

###### 5. A Detailed Account: Those Bodhisattvas Abide on the Ten Grounds, or on the Path of Accumulation and the Path of Joining, and Are Possessed of a Compassion Without Object of Focus

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk sets out clearly that the bodhisattvas who are the objects of confession abide on the ten grounds, or else on the path of accumulation and the path of joining, and are possessed of a great compassion that has no object of focus.

###### 6. Ngulchu Thogme Gathers This Verse Together With 2-28 and 2-29 Under the Heading "the Manner of Confessing Wrongdoing, Taught in General"

In Ngulchu Thogme's general-meaning outline, this verse and the two that follow are said to belong under the heading "the manner of confessing wrongdoing, taught in general."

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows the first part of chapter two's branch of confessing wrongdoing — making the objects of confession present. It explains how one visualizes before oneself the perfect buddhas who abide in all the limitless world-systems of the ten directions, and the bodhisattvas possessed of great compassion, and then joins the palms with the body and makes one's request with the voice.

**Key points.**

- The branch of confessing wrongdoing has two parts — making the objects of confession present, and the actual confession — and this is the first of them.
- The objects of confession are the perfect buddhas who abide in all the limitless world-systems of the ten directions, and the bodhisattvas possessed of great compassion.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk sets out clearly that those bodhisattvas abide on the ten grounds, or on the path of accumulation and the path of joining, and are possessed of a compassion without any object of focus.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam says that joining the palms with the body and making the request with the voice in reverence is the first physical and verbal sign of confession, made through both body and speech.
- Ngulchu Thogme gathers this verse together with 2-28 and 2-29 under the heading "the manner of confessing wrongdoing, taught in general."
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains "in all directions" as admitting no division of either place or time, so that the three times are also included in the sense.
