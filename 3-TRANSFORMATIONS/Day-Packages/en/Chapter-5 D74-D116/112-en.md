## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the ninety-seventh and ninety-eighth verses of the fifth chapter. The training of bodhisattvas is endless. Even so, we must begin by wiping away our own mistakes three times in the day and three times in the night.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. The *Akashagarbha Sutra* says as follows. Just as wind enters through a hole, so from whatever fault arises in the mind, from that very place Mara finds an opening. Therefore, a bodhisattva must keep the mind free of faults. By this, just as wind enters through a hole, Mara finds an opening where a fault arises in the mind. So a bodhisattva guards the mind so that no fault arises.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, three times in the day and night, I will recite the *Three Aggregates Sutra* and confess my own mistakes.

**Explanation:** *(Avoiding wrongdoing)* If we recite the *Three Aggregates Sutra*, relying on the buddhas and bodhisattvas, all the remaining downfalls are pacified. This fully purifies our own mind.

##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

"The conduct of training the mind" — although we must keep a vast and measureless conduct of bodhisattvas, if we ask what we should stress first in practice, we must put the conduct of training the mind into practice. Here Khunu Lama Rinpoche was saying: "If there is 'definitely, for that long,' it seems there ought to be 'for as long as' (ཇི་སྲིད) as well; but this word is also used for 'for now' (རེ་ཞིག), and since 'for now' goes with the meaning of 'at first,' [the line means] 'definitely practice it first.'" There are also other trainings.

##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Regarding that, training the mind in order to clear away the afflictions is as follows. In the scriptures such as the bodhisattva's Individual Liberation, the varieties of doors of skillful-means conduct — the bodhisattva's trainings such as the six perfections and the four ways of gathering — are taught as exceedingly many, without number or measure one could grasp. Though for the time being one cannot accomplish all of them, those practices of training the mind explained earlier, which become the means of cleansing the stains of the afflictions in one's own mind-stream, are the chief of the trainings. They must therefore definitely, without fail, be practiced for as long as it takes for those faults of affliction to be purified, and until one is able to put all those doors of conduct fully into practice.

The *Akashagarbha Sutra* says: "For example, just as wind enters through a hole, so from whatever fault arises in the mind, from that very place Mara finds an opening. Therefore a bodhisattva must keep the mind free of faults." And in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*: "The training of a bodhisattva is this: to thoroughly purify the mind. This is the root of all dharmas." Joining on from this, the passage from the *Cloud of Jewels* (དཀོན་མཆོག་སྤྲིན) cited earlier ("Mind is the forerunner of all dharmas," and so on), passages such as that of the *Perfect Compendium of the Dharma* (ཆོས་ཡང་དག་པར་སྡུད་པ), and so on, establish this point, and at the end it is said: "Therefore, that very thing — thoroughly purifying the mind — is the training of a bodhisattva." So this is how it is established.

Confessing faults, in order to purify sins and downfalls, is as follows —

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

"The conduct of training the mind" — in brief, after bodhisattvas have given rise to the aspiration for supreme awakening, the conduct they are to train in is taught in the Great Vehicle sutras as measureless: the six perfections, the three kinds of bodhisattva ethics, the four ways of gathering disciples, and so on. Of these, since a beginner bodhisattva must mainly train the mind first, the conduct of training one's own mind, as taught in a text like this *Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life*, must definitely be practiced for as long as one has not been able to train the mind well. Such is the meaning.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Although the sutras and their commentaries teach measureless bodhisattva conduct, a beginner must first and definitely apply themselves to the conduct of training their own mind. For as long as one's own mind is not well trained, that very conduct of training the mind must definitely be practiced.

**Key points.**

- Bodhisattva conduct is taught with measureless varieties, such as the perfections, the three ethical trainings, and the four ways of gathering disciples.
- Out of the vast range of bodhisattva conduct, a beginner must mainly put into practice, first of all, the conduct of training their own mind.
- The conduct of training the mind must definitely be practiced for as long as one has not been able to train one's own mind well.
- Here both are explained: training the mind in order to clear away the afflictions (ཉོན་མོངས་སྤྱོད་ཕྱིར, as the rail spells it) and confessing faults in order to purify sins and downfalls; and this is a branch of the perfection of accomplishment.

##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

"Relying on the victors and bodhicitta" — there are slight differences in how the three heaps of this sutra, called the Confession of Downfalls of Bodhisattvas, are drawn out. Some scholars, such as Khunu Lama Rinpoche, match them to confession, rejoicing, and dedication, the three; and there is also a matching of them to confession, rejoicing, and urging the turning of the wheel of Dharma. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen matched them to confession, gathering the accumulations, and increasing — that is, to the three of accumulating, purifying, and increasing — and there is that way as well. In any case, one should recite the Sutra of the Three Heaps three times by day and by night; one should surely have the power of reliance complete, by going for refuge to the buddhas and generating bodhicitta with beings as the focus, that is, taking refuge and generating bodhicitta; and, on top of reciting the Three Heaps and other such sutras, the remaining downfalls must be pacified by that, that is, made nonexistent.

Even if a fault occurs, three times by day and three times by night one should recite the Three Heaps — such as the Confession of Bodhisattva Downfalls — which consist of confessing sins, rejoicing in virtue, urging and entreating the buddhas, and so on. <!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Though one strives to keep the trainings, owing to the many afflictions and so on, if one comes to be affected by faults and downfalls in one's own mind-stream, then, three times by day and three times by night, one should recite that very sutra called the Sutra of the Three Heaps, because it shows the three methods of confessing sins and downfalls, rejoicing in merit, and urging the buddhas. Relying chiefly on the two — going for refuge to the Three Jewels, such as the thirty-five victors, and meditating on bodhicitta — or relying on those two, one confesses by way of having the four antidote powers complete. By that, the remaining downfalls — that is, faults other than the root downfalls, those included among the lesser and middling kinds of "leakage" (ཟག་པ་ཆུང་འབྲིང), the forty-six misdeeds, the so-called eighty misdeeds explained in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*, and so on — are said to be purified; so those are pacified by that method of confession.

In general, if one confesses with the four powers complete, even root downfalls are purified from the start. Yet the method of confessing those is specially stated apart, as the *Śikṣāsamuccaya* says, following the *Akashagarbha Sutra* (ནམ་མཁའི་སྙིང་པོའི་མདོ): "In a dream, sit before the noble Akashagarbha (ནམ་མཁའི་སྙིང་བོ) and confess." So what is called "the remaining" here is what has occurred apart from that. Therefore, setting aside the special methods of confession for heavy downfalls such as defeats, what is explained here is said to be fitting as a method of confessing the other, remaining faults, and so it is taken in this way. The Great Commentary (འགྲེལ་ཆེན) explains that very method of confession as the purifier of downfalls other than the remainder of root downfalls or downfalls committed deliberately — those committed through forgetfulness or without knowing — and it is also acceptable to explain it so here.

Vibhūti (བི་བྷཱུ་ཏི) said that a defeat is to be repaired again through refuge and bodhicitta, while the remainder other than defeats is to be pacified by reciting the Three Heaps. Gelha (དགེ་ལྷ) explained "relying on the victors and bodhicitta" as relying on the victors and bodhisattvas, that is, as the power of reliance, and takes "remaining downfalls" to mean downfalls of body and speech. རྣམ་སྣང་འཚོས་བཤགས་པའི་ལྷག་མ་འཆད་པར་བཤད་དོ *(this clause is unclear in the rail and is left untranslated)*.

This too was taught in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*, from the *Questions of Upāli* (ཉེ་བ་འཁོར་གྱིས་ཞུས་པ):

> Shariputra, bodhisattvas have two downfalls that carry great blame. What are the two? They are: being possessed of hatred, and being possessed of ignorance. Likewise, linking on, Shariputra, an honest bodhisattva should confess the heavy ones among the first ten categories of downfalls. He should confess the heavy ones among the five categories of existing downfalls. Shariputra, taking hold of a woman's hand and looking at her with the eyes, and also harmful intent, are downfalls; he should confess them to one or two persons. For a bodhisattva, the downfall that has the five immediate [crimes], and downfalls arising from women, downfalls arising from the hand, downfalls arising from boys, downfalls arising from stupas, downfalls arising from the Sangha, and the other downfalls apart from these, are to be confessed alone, day and night, before the thirty-five Blessed Buddhas.

— so it is taught in the New Translation (འགྱུར་གསར). In the translation of that same sutra, it appears: "The heavy ones of the first fault should be honestly confessed to an assembly of ten." This is well said: of the two heavy faults explained, the first, being possessed of hatred, is confessed to ten objects. And "the heavy existing downfalls should be confessed to a group of five" is amended, the meaning being that the second heavy fault, being possessed of ignorance, must be confessed to five objects.

Taking a woman's hand is to be confessed to one or two persons. Here, although the Old Translation (འགྱུར་རྙིང་མ) explains it as "later" (ཕྱི་བར), the New Translation explains it as light, and this is well said. Although harmful intent does not appear in the present sutra, it appears in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*, so one does it that way. That being possessed of hatred is explained as a heavy fault while harmful intent is explained as light is not contradictory, since the latter refers to the mere thought of harming with the mind-stream not disturbed, as the Detailed Explanation of the chapter on ethics (ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་ལེའུའི་རྣམ་བཤད) says. And that the heavy downfalls such as the immediate [crimes] are said to be purified by the Confession of Downfalls day and night is intended for the case where the ripening is not purified by merely performing the ritual of repair three times for a person — this too is said in that same text. In the Great Commentary (འགྲེལ་ཆེན),

> What is to be confessed of the heavy and light downfalls of bodhisattvas is taught in the noble *Questions of Upāli*; one should look at those in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya* (བསླབ་པ་ཀུན་ལས་བཏུས). All the downfalls of bodhisattvas are to be confessed alone before the thirty-five Blessed Buddhas.

Prostration is included within the confession of sins. Of these, the power of regret is to feel strong regret, through recognizing the faults, for the sins and downfalls accumulated before. The power of reliance is going for refuge to the Three Jewels, such as the thirty-five buddhas. The power of the antidote is reading the profound sutras, reciting dhāraṇīs and mantras, making stupas and making offerings to them, holding the names of buddhas and bodhisattvas, and meditating on emptiness; these are the six doors of the antidote explained in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*. Meditating on bodhicitta is said to belong to both the power of reliance and the power of the antidote. The power of turning away from the fault is relying on the restraining resolve (སྡོམ་སེམས) not to do it again; and that without this it is hard to purify even what was done before is stated in the *Lion's Roar of Maitreya* (བྱམས་པའི་སེང་གེར་སྒྲར, as the rail has it):

> Maitreya, again, in the last of the last five hundred years, when the time has come for the sacred Dharma to disappear, there will arise some who claim to be bodhisattvas. They will incur an excess of downfalls, and, with body, speech, and mind unrestrained, will commit sinful deeds in excess; thinking that they will become pure by merely confessing the fault, they will not restrain themselves in the future. And because [they have] rightly ... in the future (ཕྱིན་ཆད་ཡང་དག་པར་བསྣམས་པའི་ཕྱིར), they will not restrain themselves either. As for me, I taught the Dharma-enumeration of the Three Heaps in order to exhaust sinful deeds done before; but those foolish people, deliberately producing an excess of downfalls, think that they will become pure just by confessing and purifying the fault, and will not restrain themselves in the future.

— so it is said. In the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*, the two confession teachings, the Sutra of the Three Heaps and the "two-legged buddhas" confession (སངས་རྒྱས་རྐང་གཉིས) and the like, are shown in the context of the power of regret because they chiefly show the power of regret; it is not that they fail to show the power of reliance and the others. For in the Confession of Downfalls, going for refuge to the Three Jewels shows the power of reliance; prostrating to the thirty-five buddhas, the power of the antidote; the part from "in this life of mine" (བདག་གི་སྐྱེ་བ་འདི་དང) up to "I will not conceal" (མི་འཆབ་པོ), the power of thorough repudiation; and "from now on I will cut it off and restrain myself," the power of turning back. And the confession liturgy taught in the *Golden Light* (གསེར་འོད) also shows all four powers complete.

Thus the earlier and later verses together show, in sequence, the way to purify afflictions and sins and downfalls, and these are the purification of the body (ལུས་ཀྱི་དག་པ). From the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*:

> What is the purification of the body? / It is the thorough purification of sin and afflictions. / As taught by the perfect buddhas, / without effort one goes to the lower realms.

##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

"Relying on the victors and bodhicitta" — a beginner bodhisattva, having generated the mind of supreme awakening, must keep from being stained by the root downfalls and the branch misdeeds, which are factors contrary to the bodhicitta vow. For that purpose, three times in the day and three times in the night — that is, as if six times in a single day and night — one should recite the Sutra of the Three Heaps, also called the Confession of Bodhisattva Downfalls: the heap of confessing sins, the heap of rejoicing in merit, and the heap of dedicating roots of virtue for the sake of others. If one recites in this way, together with the thirty-five Blissful Ones and their heirs, the bodhisattvas abiding on the ten grounds — in brief, relying on the victors with their heirs and on the good intention of having generated bodhicitta oneself — then, as for "the remaining downfalls," these are the root downfalls that contradict the bodhisattva's mind-generation vow. For a bodhisattva dependent on a king there are five liable to arise; for one dependent on a minister, five; for one dependent on ordinary people, eight; in sum, fourteen. When those dependent on kings and ministers are distinguished individually, eighteen root downfalls are taught. The remainder of those — the kinds such as major and minor misdeeds — are all said to be pacified by reciting the Sutra of the Three Heaps.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Having recited the Sutra of the Three Heaps three times by day and by night, one must pacify the remaining downfalls by relying on the victors and bodhicitta. If a downfall of affliction occurs to a beginner bodhisattva, one must confess it with the four powers complete.

**Key points.**

- Three times by day and three times by night one must recite the Sutra of the Three Heaps — confessing sins, rejoicing, and dedicating roots of virtue.
- Relying on the victors, the thirty-five Blissful Ones, bodhisattvas of the tenth ground, and so on, is going for refuge and generating bodhicitta — the power of reliance.
- If a root downfall occurs, the methods of repair for persons of the highest, middling, and lowest faculties, and the repair of a defeat through refuge and bodhicitta, and so on, are taught in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*.
- It pacifies the root downfalls of those dependent on kings, ministers, and ordinary people, and the remaining downfalls — the major and minor misdeeds.
