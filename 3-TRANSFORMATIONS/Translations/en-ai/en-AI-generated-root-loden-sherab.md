# Chapter One: The Benefits of the Mind of Enlightenment (ལེའུ་དང་པོ། བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ཀྱི་ཕན་ཡོན་བཤད་པ།)

I pay respectful homage to the Blissful Ones who possess the Truth Body, and to their heirs. I also bow to everyone who is worthy of honor. I shall now briefly describe how to enter the commitments of the Buddha's heirs, in accordance with the scriptures. ^1-1

I have nothing new to say here, and I do not possess any skill in writing poetry. Therefore, I have no expectation of benefiting others. I wrote this solely to train and cultivate my own mind. ^1-2

By writing this, the strength of my faith to cultivate virtue will temporarily increase. If anyone of equal fortune should see these words, perhaps they will also find them useful. ^1-3

This precious human life of leisure and opportunity is extremely difficult to find. Now I have this chance to accomplish my goals. If I do not use it to do good, how will I ever find such an opportunity again? ^1-4

A flash of lightning on a dark, cloudy night reveals everything for a single instant. Similarly, the power of the Buddha occasionally inspires a brief moment of virtue in this world. ^1-5

Therefore, ordinary virtue is always extremely weak. Negativity is immensely powerful and unbearable. Except for the perfect mind of enlightenment, what other virtue can overcome such great evil? ^1-6

The Wise Sages contemplated this for many eons and saw that this mind alone is beneficial. Through it, countless multitudes of beings easily attain the highest happiness. ^1-7

Do you wish to overcome the hundreds of sufferings of worldly existence? Do you wish to remove the unhappiness of sentient beings and enjoy hundreds of joys? If so, you must never abandon the mind of enlightenment. ^1-8

The moment this mind is born, wretched beings bound in the prison of cyclic existence become heirs of the Blissful Ones. They also become worthy of respect by gods and humans alike. ^1-9

Like a supreme alchemical elixir, this mind takes our impure body and transforms it into the priceless body of a Buddha. Therefore, hold firmly to this precious mind of enlightenment. ^1-10

The sole guide of wandering beings thoroughly examined this mind with immeasurable wisdom. He saw its immense value. Therefore, those who wish to escape worldly suffering must hold firmly to this precious jewel of enlightenment. ^1-11

All other virtues are like a banana tree that dies after bearing fruit once. In contrast, the tree of the mind of enlightenment continuously bears fruit and always grows. ^1-12

Even if someone has committed terrible misdeeds, relying on this mind will swiftly liberate them. This is like relying on a brave companion to escape great danger. Why then do careful people not rely on it? ^1-13

In a single instant, this mind consumes even the greatest sins, like the fire at the end of an age. Its immeasurable benefits were explained by Maitreya, the Loving Protector, to Sudhana. ^1-14

Briefly, the mind of enlightenment has two aspects: the aspiring mind of enlightenment, and the engaging mind of enlightenment. ^1-15

Think of the difference between wishing to travel and actually traveling. In the same way, the wise should understand the difference between these two minds. ^1-16

Great fruits arise in the cycle of life from the aspiring mind. However, it does not produce a continuous flow of merit in the way the engaging mind does. ^1-17

From the moment one formally adopts the engaging mind with an irreversible resolve to free infinite sentient beings, ^1-18

from then on, even while asleep or careless, a vast stream of merit arises continuously, as vast as the sky. ^1-19

The Buddha explained this clearly with sound reasoning in the teaching requested by Subāhu. He did this for the sake of those with lesser aspirations. ^1-20

Even if someone merely wishes to cure a headache for other beings, that kind intention yields immeasurable merit. ^1-21

What need is there to mention wishing to remove the immeasurable pain of every being, and wishing to bring them immeasurable qualities? ^1-22

Who among our fathers or mothers has such a beneficial wish? Do the gods or ancient sages possess it? Does even the creator god Brahma have it? ^1-23

In the past, those beings never dreamed of such a wish for their own sake, even in their dreams. How could it ever arise for the sake of others? ^1-24

This precious jewel of the mind arises in no other being even for their own sake. It is a wondrous birth that is truly unprecedented. ^1-25

This jewel of the mind is the cause of joy for all wandering beings, and the medicine that cures their suffering. How can its immeasurable merit ever be measured? ^1-26

Merely wishing to benefit others is far superior to making offerings to the Buddhas. What need is there to speak of striving for the happiness of all sentient beings without exception? ^1-27

Although beings wish to abandon suffering, they run directly into suffering itself. Although they desire happiness, out of ignorance they destroy their own happiness like an enemy. ^1-28

This mind satisfies those deprived of happiness with every joy. It also cuts away all their suffering. ^1-29

It also dispels their ignorance. Where is there a virtue equal to this? Where is there such a spiritual friend, and where is there such merit? ^1-30

We praise those who merely repay a favor. What need is there to speak of the Hero of Enlightenment, who does good unsolicited? ^1-31

People honor a person as a virtuous benefactor for giving a single meal to a few beings. They do this even when the food is given with disrespect, satisfying them for only half a day. ^1-32

What need is there to speak of the Heroes of Enlightenment? They give the supreme bliss of the Blissful Ones to countless beings, fulfilling every wish in their minds. ^1-33

If anyone harbors a negative mind toward such a child of the Buddha, they will dwell in hell for eons. The Sage said they will remain there for as many eons as there were moments of that negative mind. ^1-34

However, if one generates a faithful mind toward them, its fruit will increase even further. Even when great harm befalls these children of the Buddha, no negativity arises, and their virtue naturally increases. ^1-35

I pay homage to the body of anyone in whom this precious jewel of the mind has arisen. I take refuge in those sources of happiness, who connect even their harmers with joy. ^1-36
