# Day 2 — The virtue in you is not your own doing

## Opening

Yesterday Shantideva stated his purpose. Today's verses build the argument behind it — three verses that assess the situation honestly before pointing to the only remedy that actually fits. They read as a single unit.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be parted from joy free of suffering.
> May all beings dwell in equanimity, free from attachment and aversion.

> Until I reach the heart of awakening,
> I take refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.

> Just as the buddhas of the past aroused bodhicitta
> and progressively trained in the bodhisattva conduct,
> so I too, for the benefit of all beings,
> will arouse bodhicitta and progressively train in those precepts.

## From the Tradition

The lightning metaphor in verse 5 carries a detail easy to miss. The momentary arising of a virtuous mind in a worldly person is not, according to all three commentators, something the person generates through their own effort. It arises exclusively through the power of the Buddha — what Sazang Mati Panchen calls the light rays of compassion of the Buddhas, which momentarily illuminate beings trapped in the darkness of samsara. The darkness is not neutral: the thick cloud of a stormy night represents, in the commentators' reading, the force of accumulated negativity — powerful enough, Sazang Mati Panchen writes, to produce the fierce and unendurable sufferings of the lower realms. Against that, a flash is brief. The commentators do not say: try harder. They say: when the light appears, act on it immediately, because it is not yours to summon again at will.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,
> where it has not arisen, arise;
> where it has arisen, may it not decline,
> but grow ever further.

> By whatever merit arises from this practice,
> may all wandering beings without exception
> enter the bodhisattva path.

## Today's Practice Challenge

When you notice the impulse to do something good today — text someone you've been putting off, say something kind you've been editing down to silence — Sazang Mati Panchen says that impulse was not generated by you alone. It is the light of the Buddhas' compassion landing briefly in the darkness of your ordinary day. Act on it before the cloud closes again.
