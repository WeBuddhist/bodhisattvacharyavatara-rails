## 9. Chapter Nine: The Perfection of Wisdom ^9-0

All these limbs of practice, ^9-1
The Buddha taught for the sake of wisdom.
Therefore, whoever wishes to calm ^9-1
Suffering should generate wisdom. ^9-1


Conventional reality and ultimate, ^9-2
These are considered the two truths.
Ultimate reality is not an object of the mind; ^9-2
The mind is said to be conventional. ^9-2


In this regard, two kinds of people are seen: ^9-3
Practitioners and ordinary people.
Among these, ordinary people ^9-3
Are refuted by practitioners. ^9-3


Practitioners, too, by differences in their minds, ^9-4
Are refuted by those of higher stages.
Both, however, rely on examples ^9-4
And do not analyze for the sake of results. ^9-4


Ordinary people perceive things ^9-5
And conceive them as truly existent.
Since they are not like illusions, ^9-5
Practitioners and ordinary people dispute here. ^9-5


Direct perception of forms and so forth ^9-6
Is by convention, not by valid cognition.
It is false, like the convention of purity ^9-6
In impure things and so forth. ^9-6


For the purpose of guiding ordinary people, ^9-7
The Buddha taught that things exist,
But they are not momentary in ultimate reality. ^9-7
If you say this contradicts conventional reality: ^9-7


The conventional reality of practitioners is faultless; ^9-8
Compared to ordinary people, they perceive ultimate reality.
Otherwise, if a woman is conceived as impure, ^9-8
This would be refuted by ordinary people. ^9-8


How can merit from an illusory Buddha ^9-9
Be like that from a truly existing one?
If beings are illusory, ^9-9
How can they be reborn after death? ^9-9


As long as conditions are assembled, ^9-10
So long will an illusion also arise.
How can beings be truly existent ^9-10
Because they last for a long time? ^9-10


Killing an illusory person and so forth ^9-11
Does not create misdeeds, because there is no mind.
But for an illusory being endowed with a mind, ^9-11
Merit and misdeeds arise. ^9-11


Because mantras and so forth lack power, ^9-12
An illusory mind does not arise.
The illusion that arises from various conditions ^9-12
Is also of various kinds. ^9-12


A single condition is not capable of everything; ^9-13
Such a thing does not exist anywhere.
If ultimate reality is nirvana, ^9-13
And cyclic existence is conventional, then ^9-13


Even a buddha would return to cyclic existence, ^9-14
So what would bodhisattva conduct accomplish?
If the stream of conditions is unbroken, ^9-14
An illusion would also not cease. ^9-14


But when the stream of conditions ceases, ^9-15
It does not arise even conventionally.
When delusion itself does not exist, ^9-15
By what would an illusion be perceived? ^9-15


When you have no illusion, ^9-16
What then is perceived?
If something else truly exists, ^9-16
That form is the mind itself. ^9-16


When the mind itself is an illusion, ^9-17
What sees what, and by what?
Even the Buddha, the protector of the world, ^9-17
Taught that the mind does not see the mind. ^9-17


Just as the blade of a sword ^9-18
Cannot cut itself, so too the mind.
Just as a lamp truly ^9-18
Illuminates its own nature, ^9-18


A lamp does not illuminate itself, ^9-19
Because it is not obscured by darkness.
Like a crystal that is blue ^9-19
Does not depend on anything else for its blueness. ^9-19


Likewise, some things are seen ^9-20
To depend on others, and some do not.
It is not blue, but it is blue; ^9-20
It does not make itself blue. ^9-20


If one says that a lamp illuminates itself, ^9-21
Knowing it by knowledge,
By what knowledge is it known ^9-21
That the mind is inherently clear? ^9-21


When nothing is seen by anyone, ^9-22
Whether clear or unclear,
Like the gait of a barren woman's daughter, ^9-22
Speaking of it is meaningless. ^9-22


If there is no self-awareness, ^9-23
How can consciousness be remembered?
When something else is experienced, it is remembered ^9-23
Through connection, like the poison of a mouse. ^9-23


Since it is seen to be endowed with other conditions, ^9-24
If it is self-illuminating,
From applying magical eye-salve, ^9-24
Seeing a pot does not make it the eye-salve itself. ^9-24


Seeing, hearing, and knowing ^9-25
Are not to be refuted here.
Here, what is to be averted ^9-25
Is the true conception that causes suffering. ^9-25


An illusion is not other than the mind, ^9-26
And if it is not other, it is impermanent.
How can it not be other if it truly exists? ^9-26
If it is not other, it does not truly exist. ^9-26


Just as an illusion is not true, ^9-27
So too the mind that perceives it.
If cyclic existence has a truly existing basis, ^9-27
Then it would be like space elsewhere. ^9-27


Since non-existence relies on existence, ^9-28
How can it be an agent and be endowed with it?
Your mind, without a companion, ^9-28
Would be utterly alone. ^9-28


When the mind is free from grasping, ^9-29
Then all are buddhas.
Even so, what benefit is there ^9-29
In conceiving of mere mind? ^9-29


Even if one knows something to be like an illusion, ^9-30
How can defilements cease?
When it comes to an illusory woman, ^9-30
Even the creator himself generates attachment. ^9-30


The creator has not abandoned ^9-31
The latent patterns of defilements regarding the object of knowledge.
Therefore, when he sees it, ^9-31
The latent patterns of emptiness are weak. ^9-31


Through cultivating the latent patterns of emptiness, ^9-32
The latent patterns of truly existing things will be abandoned.
And through cultivating "nothing exists," ^9-32
That too will later be abandoned. ^9-32


When the object to be investigated ^9-33
Is not perceived as "nothing exists,"
How can non-existence, devoid of a basis, ^9-33
Remain before the mind? ^9-33


When both existence and non-existence ^9-34
Do not remain before the mind,
Then, with no other aspect, ^9-34
It utterly calms into non-perception. ^9-34


Just as a wish-granting jewel and wish-granting tree ^9-35
Completely fulfill hopes,
So too, by the power of aspirations, ^9-35
Buddhas appear in forms to tame beings. ^9-35


For example, a shrine to a garuda, ^9-36
Once built, even after it has passed away ^9-36
For a long time, still pacifies ^9-36
Poisons and so forth. ^9-36


Likewise, a buddha shrine built ^9-37
In accordance with bodhisattva conduct,
Even after the bodhisattva has passed into nirvana, ^9-37
Accomplishes all purposes. ^9-37


How can making offerings to something inanimate ^9-38
Be fruitful?
Because it is taught that ^9-38
Those who remain and those in nirvana are equally worthy. ^9-38


Whether conventionally or ultimately, ^9-39
It is appropriate, and the result is from scripture.
For example, just as ^9-39
There is a result from an existing Buddha. ^9-39


One is liberated by seeing the truth; ^9-40
What is accomplished by seeing emptiness?
Because scripture teaches that ^9-40
Without this path, there is no awakening. ^9-40


If the Mahayana is not established, ^9-41
How is your scripture established?
Because this is established for both, ^9-41
But for you, it is not established at first. ^9-41


The condition by which one believes in it ^9-42
Is the same for the Mahayana.
If it is true because others desire it, ^9-42
Then the Vedas and so forth would also be true. ^9-42


If you say the Mahayana is disputed, ^9-43
Then your scriptures, like those of non-Buddhists,
And other scriptures, being disputed ^9-43
By yourself and others, should be abandoned. ^9-43


If the root of the teaching is the state of being a monk, ^9-44
Then the state of being a monk is also difficult to maintain.
And for those whose minds are focused on objects, ^9-44
Nirvana is also difficult to attain. ^9-44


If one is liberated by abandoning defilements, ^9-45
Then one would immediately attain it.
Even without defilements, ^9-45
The power of karma is seen in them. ^9-45


If you say that grasping craving ^9-46
Does not exist, as is certainly the case,
Why would craving not exist ^9-46
Even if it is not a defilement, like general delusion? ^9-46


Craving arises from feelings, ^9-47
And feelings exist even in them.
A mind focused on objects ^9-47
Will remain in some state. ^9-47


A mind devoid of emptiness, ^9-48
Having ceased, will arise again,
Like the absorption of non-perception. ^9-48
Therefore, one should cultivate emptiness. ^9-48


If you consider any statement ^9-49
That enters the sutras to be taught by the Buddha,
Why do you not consider most of the Mahayana ^9-49
To be similar to your sutras? ^9-49


If, by the exception of one, ^9-50
Everything becomes flawed,
Why would not everything ^9-50
Be taught by the Buddha by the exception of one sutra? ^9-50


Any statement that ^9-51
Great Kashyapa and others could not fathom,
Who would make it unacceptable ^9-51
Because you have not understood it? ^9-51


For the sake of those suffering from ignorance, ^9-52
Liberated from the extremes of attachment and fear,
Remaining in cyclic existence is accomplished. ^9-52
This is the fruit of emptiness. ^9-52


Therefore, refuting ^9-53
The aspect of emptiness is inappropriate.
Therefore, without doubt, ^9-53
One should cultivate emptiness. ^9-53


Emptiness is the antidote to the darkness ^9-54
Of the veils of defilements and objects of knowledge.
Whoever wishes to quickly attain omniscience, ^9-54
How can they not cultivate it? ^9-54


If something that causes suffering ^9-55
Gives rise to fear,
How can fear arise ^9-55
From emptiness, which pacifies suffering? ^9-55


If there were some self, ^9-56
Then fear would arise from anything.
But since no self exists, ^9-56
Who is it that would be afraid? ^9-56


Teeth, hair, and nails are not the self. ^9-57
Bones and blood are not the self.
Mucus is not the self, phlegm is not the self, ^9-57
And lymph and pus are also not the self. ^9-57


Fat and sweat are not the self. ^9-58
Lungs and liver are also not the self.
Other internal organs are also not the self. ^9-58
Feces and urine are not the self. ^9-58


Flesh and skin are not the self. ^9-59
Heat and wind are also not the self.
Pores are not the self. In every respect, ^9-59
The six consciousnesses are also not the self. ^9-59


If the cognition of sound were permanent, ^9-60
Then one would always perceive sound.
If there is no object of knowledge, what does it know? ^9-60
By what is it called knowledge? ^9-60


If non-knowing is knowledge, ^9-61
Then wood would also be knowledge.
Therefore, it is certain that ^9-61
Without an object of knowledge present, there is no knowledge. ^9-61


If it knows form by itself, ^9-62
Why then is it not also hearing?
If it is because sound is not close, ^9-62
Then there is no knowledge of it either. ^9-62


How can the nature of the perceiver of sound ^9-63
Be the perceiver of form?
One is conceived as father and son, ^9-63
But it is not truly so. ^9-63


Thus, clarity, activity, and ^9-64
Inertia are not sons, nor are they fathers.
It is not seen to be ^9-64
Of the nature of the perceiver of sound. ^9-64


If it is seen in another aspect, like an actor, ^9-65
Then it is not permanent.
If that other aspect is itself, ^9-65
Then it is one, and that one did not exist before. ^9-65


If that other aspect is not true, ^9-66
Then tell me its own nature.
If you say it is knowledge itself, then ^9-66
All beings would be one. ^9-66


Sentient and insentient things ^9-67
Would also be one, because their existence is similar.
When the distinction is contradictory, ^9-67
What is the basis of similarity? ^9-67


Insentient things are also not the self, ^9-68
Because they are insentient, like pots and so forth.
If you know it is sentient, ^9-68
Then non-knowing would be destroyed. ^9-68


If the self is unchanging, ^9-69
What has the mind done to it?
Thus, non-knowing and actionless, ^9-69
Space would be made the self. ^9-69


If there is no self, ^9-70
The connection of karma and its results is illogical.
Since karma is done and then ceases, ^9-70
Whose karma would it be? ^9-70


The agent and experiencer are established for both ^9-71
As having distinct actions and bases for results,
And also as having no self. ^9-71
Is it not meaningless to dispute this here? ^9-71


This seeing that a cause ^9-72
Is endowed with a result is not possible.
It is taught that the agent and experiencer ^9-72
Are based on a single continuum. ^9-72


Past and future minds ^9-73
Are not the self, because they do not exist.
If the present mind is the self, ^9-73
Then when it ceases, the self also ceases. ^9-73


For example, if a plantain tree trunk ^9-74
Is separated into parts, nothing is found.
Likewise, when analyzed ^9-74
By thorough analysis, the self is also not truly existent. ^9-74


If there are no beings, ^9-75
For whom should compassion be cultivated?
It is what is conceived by ignorance, ^9-75
Accepted for the sake of results. ^9-75


If there are no beings, whose is the result? ^9-76
True, but it is desired from ignorance.
For the sake of pacifying suffering, ^9-76
The ignorance regarding results should not be averted. ^9-76


The pride that is the cause of suffering ^9-77
Increases through the ignorance of a self.
If you say that it is not averted even from that, ^9-77
Then cultivating selflessness is supreme. ^9-77


The body is not the feet or calves. ^9-78
The thighs and waist are also not the body.
The belly and back are also not the body, ^9-78
And the chest and shoulders are also not the body. ^9-78


The ribs and hands are also not the body. ^9-79
The armpits and shoulders are also not the body.
The internal organs are also not it, ^9-79
And the head and throat are also not the body.
What then is this body? ^9-79


If this body exists ^9-80
In all its individual parts,
Then the parts would exist in the parts, ^9-80
But where does it itself exist? ^9-80


If the self, which is the body of all, ^9-81
Exists in the hands and so forth,
Then there would be as many bodies ^9-81
As there are hands and so forth. ^9-81


If the body does not exist externally or internally, ^9-82
How can the body exist in the hands and so forth?
If there is nothing other than the hands and so forth, ^9-82
How can it exist? ^9-82


Therefore, the mind conceives a body ^9-83
In the hands and so forth, which are bodiless, through ignorance.
Like the conception of a person in a scarecrow post ^9-83
Due to a particular configuration of shape. ^9-83


As long as conditions are assembled, ^9-84
So long does the body appear like a person.
Likewise, as long as it exists in the hands and so forth, ^9-84
So long does the body appear there. ^9-84


Likewise, since it is a collection of fingers, ^9-85
What then is the hand?
And since it is a collection of joints, ^9-85
The joints are also separated into their own parts. ^9-85


The parts are also separated into particles, ^9-86
And the particle is also divided by its directional parts.
Since directional parts are free from parts, ^9-86
Like space, therefore, even particles do not exist. ^9-86


Thus, who with discernment ^9-87
Would be attached to a form like a dream?
When the body is thus non-existent, ^9-87
What person is there, and what woman? ^9-87


If suffering truly exists, ^9-88
Why does it not harm intense gladness?
If there is happiness, why does it not ^9-88
Cause gladness in grief and torment, like delicious tastes? ^9-88


If it is not experienced because it is overwhelmed ^9-89
By something stronger,
Then what is not the experiencer itself? ^9-89
How can that be feeling? ^9-89


Subtle suffering truly exists. ^9-90
Is not its gross aspect removed?
If you say that is merely other happiness, ^9-90
Then that subtle aspect is also its. ^9-90


If, due to the arising of conflicting conditions, ^9-91
Suffering does not arise,
Is it not established that the conception of feeling ^9-91
Is clinging to an object? ^9-91


Therefore, this analysis, ^9-92
The antidote to this, should be cultivated.
The food of practitioners in meditation ^9-92
Arises from the field of analysis. ^9-92


If there is space between the sense faculties and their objects, ^9-93
Where do they meet?
If there is no space, they are one, ^9-93
So what meets what? ^9-93


Particles do not enter particles; ^9-94
There is no gap, they are equal.
There is no mixing in what has not entered, ^9-94
And no meeting in what is not mixed. ^9-94


How can meeting ^9-95
Be appropriate for something without parts?
If you see meeting ^9-95
And something without parts, then demonstrate it. ^9-95


The meeting of consciousness with a bodiless thing ^9-96
Is not appropriate.
Since a collection is non-existent, ^9-96
As was analyzed before. ^9-96


Thus, if contact does not exist, ^9-97
From what does feeling arise?
What is the purpose of this toil? ^9-97
What harms what? ^9-97


When no experiencer exists, ^9-98
And feeling also does not exist,
Then, seeing this situation, ^9-98
Why would craving not cease? ^9-98


Seeing or touching ^9-99
Is of the nature of a dream or illusion.
Since it arises simultaneously with the mind, ^9-99
Feeling is not seen by it. ^9-99


What arises before and after ^9-100
Is remembered, not experienced.
One does not experience oneself, ^9-100
Nor do others experience it. ^9-100


No experiencer exists; ^9-101
Therefore, feeling is not itself.
Thus, in this collection of selflessness, ^9-101
Why would this harm it? ^9-101


The mind does not dwell in the sense faculties. ^9-102
It is not in forms and so forth, nor in between.
It is not the mind internally or externally, ^9-102
Nor is it found elsewhere. ^9-102


What is not the body, nor elsewhere, ^9-103
Nor mixed, nor separate, nor anywhere,
That is nothing at all. Therefore, ^9-103
The nature of beings is nirvana. ^9-103


If knowledge exists before the object of knowledge, ^9-104
On what does it focus to arise?
If knowledge and the object of knowledge are simultaneous, ^9-104
On what does it focus to arise? ^9-104


If it exists after the object of knowledge, ^9-105
Then from what does knowledge arise?
Thus, the arising of all phenomena ^9-105
Will not be understood. ^9-105


If conventional reality does not exist in that way, ^9-106
Where are the two truths?
And if it is by another conventional reality, ^9-106
How can beings pass beyond sorrow? ^9-106


This is the notion of another's mind; ^9-107
It is not one's own conventional reality.
If it is later ascertained, then it exists; ^9-107
Otherwise, conventional reality itself is non-existent. ^9-107


Conception and the object to be conceived ^9-108
Are mutually dependent.
Just as all analysis ^9-108
Is spoken of based on convention. ^9-108


When analysis ^9-109
Analyzes by thorough analysis,
Then that thorough analysis ^9-109
Is also without end, because it is analysis. ^9-109


When the object of analysis is analyzed, ^9-110
There is no basis for thorough analysis.
Since there is no basis, it does not arise, ^9-110
And that too is said to be nirvana. ^9-110


According to whom these two are true, ^9-111
That is very difficult to maintain.
If things are established by the power of knowledge, ^9-111
What is the basis for knowledge existing? ^9-111


If knowledge is established from the object of knowledge, ^9-112
What is the basis for the object of knowledge existing?
If they exist by mutual dependence, ^9-112
Then both would be non-existent. ^9-112


If there is no son, there is no father, ^9-113
From what does sonship arise?
Without a son, there is no father; ^9-113
Likewise, both are non-existent. ^9-113


Just as a sprout arises from a seed, ^9-114
And the seed is understood by it,
Why is it not understood that ^9-114
Knowledge, born from the object of knowledge, exists? ^9-114


If a seed is understood by knowledge ^9-115
Other than the sprout,
From what is the existence of knowledge ^9-115
That understands that object of knowledge understood? ^9-115


For the time being, all causes ^9-116
Are seen by direct perception in the world.
The distinctions of lotus stems and so forth ^9-116
Are produced by the distinctions of causes. ^9-116


If you ask what made the distinctions of causes, ^9-117
It is from the distinctions of previous causes.
Why are causes able to produce results? ^9-117
It is from the power of previous causes. ^9-117


If Ishvara is the cause of beings, ^9-118
First, tell me who Ishvara is.
If you say it is the elements, that is true, ^9-118
But what is the toil in merely naming? ^9-118


However, earth and other elements are many, ^9-119
Impermanent, unmoving, not divine,
Trampled, and impure. ^9-119
Therefore, that is not Ishvara. ^9-119


Ishvara is not space, because it is unmoving. ^9-120
It is not the self, because it was refuted before.
And what is the use of speaking ^9-120
Of an inconceivable creator by saying it is inconceivable? ^9-120


What is it that is desired to be created by it? ^9-121
Is not the self, and earth and so forth,
And the essence of Ishvara also permanent? ^9-121
Knowledge arises from the object of knowledge, and ^9-121


Beginningless pleasure and pain are from karma. ^9-122
Tell me what is created by that.
If the cause has no beginning, ^9-122
Where is the beginning of the result? ^9-122


Why does it not always create? ^9-123
It does not depend on anything else.
If it is not created by it, and nothing else exists, ^9-123
On what does it depend? ^9-123


If it depends, then a collection ^9-124
Would be the cause, not Ishvara.
If there is a collection, it does not arise, because it is powerless, ^9-124
And without it, there is no power to arise. ^9-124


If Ishvara creates without desiring, ^9-125
Then it would be dependent on others.
If it desires, it would depend on desire, ^9-125
So how can it be Ishvara if it creates? ^9-125


Those who assert that particles are permanent ^9-126
Were also refuted before.
The Samkhyas assert that the permanent Primal Substance ^9-126
Is the cause of beings. ^9-126


The equilibrium of the qualities called ^9-127
Clarity, activity, and inertia
Is called the Primal Substance. ^9-127
Disequilibrium is said to be beings. ^9-127


It is illogical for one to have three natures; ^9-128
Therefore, it does not exist.
Likewise, qualities do not exist, ^9-128
Because they are also threefold individually. ^9-128


If qualities do not exist, then sounds and so forth ^9-129
Would also be very far from existing.
And happiness and so forth cannot exist ^9-129
In insentient things like cloth. ^9-129


If phenomena are of the nature of their causes, ^9-130
Are not phenomena already analyzed?
Your cause is also happiness and so forth, ^9-130
And cloth and so forth do not arise from that. ^9-130


Happiness and so forth are from cloth and so forth. ^9-131
Since that does not exist, happiness and so forth do not exist.
And happiness and so forth, as permanent, ^9-131
Are never perceived. ^9-131


If clear happiness and so forth exist, ^9-132
Why is experience not grasped?
If it is subtle, ^9-132
How can it be gross and subtle? ^9-132


By abandoning the gross and becoming subtle, ^9-133
Those subtle and gross things are impermanent.
Likewise, why do you not assert ^9-133
That all phenomena are impermanent? ^9-133


If the gross is not other than happiness, ^9-134
Then clear happiness is impermanent.
If you assert that nothing ^9-134
Arises because it does not exist, ^9-134


You do not desire it to arise without being clear, ^9-135
But it exists.
If the result exists in the cause, ^9-135
Then eating food would be eating impurity. ^9-135


Buy cotton seeds with the price of cloth ^9-136
And wear them.
If the world does not see it through ignorance, ^9-136
Then know that it is established by itself. ^9-136


Since that knowledge also exists in the world, ^9-137
Why is it not seen?
If the world is not valid cognition, ^9-137
Then seeing what is clear is also not true. ^9-137


If valid cognition is not valid, ^9-138
Will not what is measured by it be false?
Therefore, in ultimate reality, emptiness ^9-138
Would be inappropriate to cultivate. ^9-138


Without touching a conceived thing, ^9-139
Its non-existence is not grasped.
Therefore, whatever is a false thing, ^9-139
Its non-existence is clearly false. ^9-139


Therefore, the notion that ^9-140
A child in a dream has died, and that it does not exist,
Is an obstacle to the notion ^9-140
That it exists, and that too is false. ^9-140


Thus, by such analysis, ^9-141
Nothing exists without a cause.
It does not exist in conditions ^9-141
That are individual or collected. ^9-141


It does not come from elsewhere, ^9-142
It does not remain, it does not go.
What difference is there between this ^9-142
That ignorance makes true, and an illusion? ^9-142


Whatever is created by an illusion, ^9-143
And whatever is created by causes,
Investigate from where it comes ^9-143
And where it goes. ^9-143


Whatever is seen ^9-144
By being close, and is not absent if it is not,
Is like a fabricated reflection. ^9-144
How can it have true existence? ^9-144


If a thing exists, ^9-145
What need is there for a cause?
And if it does not exist, ^9-145
What need is there for a cause? ^9-145


Even hundreds of millions of causes ^9-146
Cannot transform a non-existent thing.
How can that state be a thing? ^9-146
And what else would be a thing? ^9-146


If existence is not possible when non-existent, ^9-147
When will a thing ever exist?
Until a thing arises, ^9-147
Its non-existence will not cease. ^9-147


If it is not free from non-existence, ^9-148
There is no possibility of a thing existing.
And a thing will also not be non-existent, ^9-148
Because it would entail two natures. ^9-148


Thus, cessation does not exist, ^9-149
And things also do not exist. Therefore,
All these beings ^9-149
Are always unborn and unceasing. ^9-149


Beings are like a dream; ^9-150
When analyzed, they are like a plantain tree.
Nirvana and non-nirvana ^9-150
Are ultimately no different. ^9-150


Thus, in empty things, ^9-151
What is gained, what is lost?
What is honored ^9-151
Or completely disdained? ^9-151


From what is happiness or suffering? ^9-152
What is there to dislike, what is there to like?
When investigated in ultimate reality, ^9-152
Who craves, and for what? ^9-152


When analyzed, in this living world, ^9-153
Who dies here?
What will arise, what has arisen? ^9-153
And who are relatives and friends? ^9-153


Let me embrace all ^9-154
As being like space.
Those who desire their own happiness ^9-154
Are driven by causes of conflict and eagerness. ^9-154


They are greatly agitated and rejoice, ^9-155
Grieve, strive, dispute,
Mutually cut and pierce, ^9-155
And live with great difficulty through misdeeds. ^9-155


Repeatedly coming to happy realms, ^9-156
And repeatedly enjoying many pleasures,
After death, they fall into ^9-156
Long, unbearable sufferings in the lower realms. ^9-156


There are many chasms in cyclic existence. ^9-157
There is nothing like this that is not itself.
There, too, by mutual contradiction, ^9-157
There is nothing like this truly existing. ^9-157


That ocean of unbearable suffering, ^9-158
Without example, is boundless.
There, one is so weak, ^9-158
And life there is also short. ^9-158


There, too, by being alive and free from illness, ^9-159
By acting, by the toil of hunger and thirst,
By sleep, by harm, and likewise, ^9-159
By childishness and meaningless association, ^9-159


Life passes quickly and meaninglessly, ^9-160
And discernment is very hard to find.
There, too, where is the means ^9-160
To avert the habit of distraction? ^9-160


There, too, Mara strives ^9-161
To make one fall into the great lower realms.
There are many wrong paths, ^9-161
And it is hard to overcome doubt. ^9-161


Again, leisure is hard to find, ^9-162
And buddhas are very hard to find.
The river of defilements is hard to abandon. ^9-162
Alas, suffering will persist! ^9-162


Thus, though greatly suffering, ^9-163
Those who do not see their own suffering,
These who dwell in the river of suffering, ^9-163
Alas, they are worthy of lamentation! ^9-163


For example, someone repeatedly bathes, ^9-164
And repeatedly enters fire.
Likewise, though dwelling in great suffering, ^9-164
They imagine themselves to be happy. ^9-164


Thus, acting as if ^9-165
There is no aging and death,
Having first killed themselves, ^9-165
Unbearable downfalls in the lower realms will come. ^9-165


When will I pacify ^9-166
Those tormented by the fire of suffering,
With showers of my own happiness, ^9-166
Born from the clouds of merit? ^9-166


When will I accumulate ^9-167
Merit with reverence in a non-objectifying way,
And teach emptiness ^9-167
To those who are ruined by objects? ^9-167


From Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, ^9-a
