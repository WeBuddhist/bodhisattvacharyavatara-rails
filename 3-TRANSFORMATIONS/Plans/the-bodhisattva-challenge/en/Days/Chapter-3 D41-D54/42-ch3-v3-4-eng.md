## Today's Verse

> I rejoice with joy
> in the ocean of good deeds—the Mind of Awakening—
> that brings happiness to all beings,
> and in the acts that benefit beings. ^3-3

> I bow with palms joined to the buddhas in all directions
> and pray:
> “Please light the lamp of the teachings
> for beings bewildered by the darkness of suffering.” ^3-4

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 3 and 4 from the third chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Two things can be felt in them. One is the joy that rises in the heart at the good others have done. The other is the warmth and the light of lighting the lamp of the Dharma for beings left in the dark.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses tell us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago King Prasenajit invited the Buddha and his retinue and served them with great honour. At that time a poor woman rejoiced from deep in her heart at the king's good deed. The Buddha dedicated the good not to the king but to that poor woman. Why? Because the good she gained by rejoicing with a pure mind turned out to be greater than the king's offering.

What does this teach us? When we see others doing good, we may have nothing of our own. But if we rejoice with a pure mind, we can gain a great deal of good. Rejoicing needs no wealth at all, which makes it a special kind of good that anyone can do.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when I see someone doing good out of kindness, I will rejoice from my heart.

**Explanation:** _(Doing good)_ When others do good that helps beings, and we cultivate heartfelt joy without jealousy, we gain a great deal of good ourselves. It makes our own mind peaceful and clear. As the verse says, rejoicing in what others do is a practice that is easy and brings great benefit.
