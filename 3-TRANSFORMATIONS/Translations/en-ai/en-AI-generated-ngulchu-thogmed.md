# Explanation of the Benefits of Bodhicitta

### 1.1

Regarding the first of the three parts of the second section, namely, the intrinsic nature of each: ^1-1-1

The subject matter refers to "entering the vows of the Sons of the Sugatas," which represents the completeness of the Mahayana lineage, path, and fruit. ^1-1-2

Mainly, it refers to the trainings of the Bodhisattvas. ^1-1-3

To realize these subject matters by relying on this treatise is the purpose. ^1-1-4

The dependence of realization on the treatise is the connection. ^1-1-5

Having put the meaning of realization into practice, the attainment of its fruit is the ultimate purpose. ^1-1-6

Second, the purpose of teaching is that those who possess realization, understanding that the treatise has a purpose and so forth, will enter into it. ^1-1-7

Third, regarding how it is taught: the *Difficult Points Commentary* of Kṛṣṇapāda (*Nagpopa*) states that the purpose and so forth of this work are shown by the name of the treatise itself. Śāntideva (*Dgelha*) holds that "entering the vows of the Sons of the Sugatas" indicates the subject matter, the welfare of oneself and others is the purpose, and the treatise and the purpose are connected as the means and that which arises from the means. ^1-1-7

A Buddha having appeared in the world; his teaching of the Dharma; the teachings remaining because there are individuals who have seen the truth; following the teachings by having instructions taught in accordance with the realization of the Aryas; and having benefactors who provide necessities, showing compassion for others—these are the five circumstantial fortunes. Thus, this body endowed with these eighteen qualities is extremely difficult to find. ^1-3-6

As stated in the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra* regarding liberation through the sensory spheres of the warmth of the Conqueror: "Being free from the eight non-leisures is difficult to find. Obtaining the perfectly pure leisure is also difficult to find." ^1-3-7

Since this body of leisure and fortune has the capacity to accomplish the high status and definite goodness of a person, one must make effort to accomplish virtue at this time of having obtained it. If at this time one does not accomplish virtue that benefits oneself and others, how could one obtain such leisure and fortune again in the future? ^1-3-9

Since it will not be obtained, the instruction is "do not make this meaningless." ^1-3-10

As stated in the Sūtras: "Therefore, do not make this leisure and fortune meaningless." ### 1.7

Regarding the first of the five parts of the common benefits, namely, abandoning negativity: Since the support and the attitude are difficult to find and of short duration, the opposing virtue is extremely weak and always remains of small power, ^1-7-1

While the causes of non-virtue are easily gathered, so extremely fierce and unbearable negativity continuously arises. Therefore, one must now generate virtue that can overcome it. ^1-7-1

That great negativity cannot be overcome by any other virtue other than the precious bodhicitta, which is of the nature of wisdom and compassion. Therefore, one must generate the mind. ^1-7-2

As stated in the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*: "Just as mice are overcome and cannot bear the sight of a cat's eyes, likewise, the mice of karma and afflictions are overcome and cannot bear the precious eye of the mind of all-knowingness." ^1-7-3

Thus it is said. ^1-7-4

Second, accomplishing happiness and bliss: Having contemplated and examined the means to benefit sentient beings for many aeons—that is, for three countless aeons—the Lords of Sages saw this very bodhicitta as beneficial for sentient beings. ^1-7-5

By this, the boundless assemblies of beings will easily obtain the supreme bliss of buddhahood. Third, fulfilling desires and hopes: Those who wish to overcome the hundreds of sufferings of their own existence, those who wish to clear all the unhappiness of other sentient beings, and those who wish to enjoy hundreds of happinesses for themselves and others, must never abandon this bodhicitta. ^1-7-6

For it is certain to accomplish those purposes. Fourth, the transference of name and meaning: If those who are impoverished by suffering under the force of being bound by the chains of karma and afflictions in the prison of cyclic existence generate bodhicitta, from that instant onward, they will be called "Sons of the Sugatas." ^1-7-7

In meaning, they will become objects of homage for the world, including gods and humans. Fifth, praising the benefits through analogies, there are six parts: First, showing how the low is transformed into the supreme through the analogy of the gold-making elixir: Since adopting this body, which is of an impure nature, transforms it into the priceless, precious body of a Conqueror, ^1-7-8

Firmly seize this which is called bodhicitta, which possesses such capacity. ^1-7-9

As stated in the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*, using the analogy of a single ounce of gold-making elixir being able to transform a thousand ounces of iron into gold: "Likewise, the element of the gold-making elixir of generating the mind of all-knowingness, held by the wisdom of dedicating roots of virtue, completely exhausts all the iron of the obscurations of karma and afflictions, ^1-7-10

Making all phenomena of the color of all-knowingness. Yet, this single element of generating the mind of all-knowingness cannot be defiled or exhausted by all the iron of karma and afflictions." ^1-7-11

And so forth. Second, showing that it is difficult to find and of great value through the analogy of a jewel: The peerless leader who guides all wandering beings to the city of liberation and connects them with the wealth of qualities is the Buddha. Since his all-knowing wisdom pervades all objects of knowledge and cannot be measured by others, if one examines well with an immeasurable mind, this bodhicitta is seen to be rare and of great benefit, hence of great value. ^1-7-13

As stated in the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*, using the analogy of the king of jewels being of great value among all wealth: "Likewise, as long as the wisdom of all-knowingness in the three times makes the sphere of phenomena appear as its object, all the contaminated and uncontaminated roots of virtue of all gods, humans, Hearers, and Solitary Realizers cannot match the value of the wish-fulfilling king of jewels of generating bodhicitta." ^1-7-14

Thus it is said. ^1-7-15

Therefore, those who wish to be free from the state of suffering of wandering beings should firmly seize the precious bodhicitta. Third, showing that the fruit is inexhaustible and increases through the analogy of a tree: All other virtues not embraced by bodhicitta are like a plantain tree, which perishes after bearing fruit once. ^1-7-16

The tree of bodhicitta, however, always bears fruit, is never exhausted, and increases more and more. ^1-7-17

As stated in the *Ratnakara Sūtra*: "Mañjuśrī, it is like this: ^1-7-18

Various trees grow and increase when supported by the four elements. ^1-7-19

Mañjuśrī, likewise, if the roots of virtue are held by bodhicitta and dedicated to all-knowingness, they will grow and increase." ^1-7-20

Thus it is said. ^1-7-21

Fourth, showing that it protects from the fruits of negativity through the analogy of an escort: Even if one has committed extremely fierce negativity such as harming the Three Jewels, by relying on a brave escort, one is freed from the great fear of enemies. As stated in the *Akṣayamatiparipṛcchā Sūtra*: "Son of good family, it is like this: ^1-7-23

For example, a person who relies on a brave man does not fear any enemies. ^1-7-24

Fifth, destroying negativity from the root through the analogy of fire: Just as the fire at the end of an aeon completely burns the vessels below the first dhyāna without exception, likewise, this bodhicitta completely burns great negativities such as the five immediate actions in a single instant, destroying them from the root. ^1-7-27

From that Sūtra: "If I gird my loins for the sake of benefiting and bringing happiness to the infinite assemblies of sentient beings, the boundless roots of virtue that focus on the benefit and happiness of all sentient beings, even if I am unmindful or asleep, will increase, grow, and become completely perfected day and night in every instant of mind." ^1-8-15
