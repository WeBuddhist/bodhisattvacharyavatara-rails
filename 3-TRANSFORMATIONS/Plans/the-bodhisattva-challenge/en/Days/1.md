# Day 1 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: The Benefits of the Mind of Enlightenment — Verses 1-1 to 1-3

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Releasing Pride (Humility):** The great teacher Śāntideva said that he did not have any special skill or new ideas. This shows us that if we let go of being too proud of our knowledge, it is easier to learn. When we are humble, we stop being arrogant. This is important because arrogance can stop us from making progress on the spiritual path.
*   **Strengthening Faith (Inner Growth):** When we study and reflect on these teachings, our faith in the path naturally grows stronger. This faith is not blind belief — it is a clear confidence in good actions and their results. A stronger faith leads to more effort, better focus, and deeper wisdom over time.
*   **Finding Shared Purpose (Connection):** These teachings were written not just for one person, but for anyone with the wish to grow. Recognizing that others share the same spiritual goals helps reduce feelings of loneliness or isolation. Knowing we walk the same path as others gives us encouragement and strength.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-1**

> བདེ་གཤེགས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་མངའ་སྲས་བཅས་དང་། །
> ཕྱག་འོས་ཀུན་ལའང་གུས་པར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏེ། །
> བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་ནི། །
> ལུང་བཞིན་མདོར་བསྡུས་ནས་ནི་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ། །
>
> *I pay respectful homage to the Blissful Ones who possess the Truth Body, and to their heirs. I also bow to everyone who is worthy of honor. I shall now briefly describe how to enter the commitments of the Buddha's heirs, in accordance with the scriptures.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-1**

The opening verse pays homage to the Three Jewels — the Buddhas, their teachings, and the Heroes of Enlightenment — as well as to all others worthy of respect. This act of paying homage is not just a formal gesture. It clears away obstacles before the text begins, creates great spiritual merit, and shows that the great teacher Śāntideva is a trustworthy guide. The verse then states the purpose of the whole text: to briefly explain how to take up the conduct of a Hero of Enlightenment, following faithfully what the scriptures teach.

---

#### **Verse 1-2**

> སྔོན་ཆད་མ་བྱུང་བ་ཡང་འདིར་བརྗོད་མེད། །
> སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་མཁས་པའང་བདག་ལ་ཡོད་མིན་ཏེ། །
> དེ་ཕྱིར་གཞན་དོན་བསམ་པ་བདག་ལ་མེད། །
> རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་ངས་འདི་བརྩམས། །
>
> *I have nothing new to say here, and I do not possess any skill in writing poetry. Therefore, I have no expectation of benefiting others. I wrote this solely to train and cultivate my own mind.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-2**

The great teacher Śāntideva makes two humble statements: he has no original teachings to offer, and he lacks any special skill in writing beautiful poetry. By saying this, he removes all pride from his work. Since those with sharp minds can understand the scriptures directly, this text was not written for their sake. Instead, it was written as a personal tool to train the mind, to keep the wish for enlightenment alive, and to prevent spiritual qualities from fading away.

---

#### **Verse 1-3**

> དགེ་བ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་བདག་གི་དད་པའི་ཤུགས། །
> འདི་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་རེ་ཞིག་འཕེལ་འགྱུར་ལ། །
> བདག་དང་སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཀྱང་། །
> ཅི་སྟེ་འདི་དག་མཐོང་ན་དོན་ཡོད་འགྱུར། །
>
> *By writing this, the strength of my faith to cultivate virtue will temporarily increase. If anyone of equal fortune should see these words, perhaps they will also find them useful.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-3**

Writing down these teachings is not only useful for study — it also causes the author's own faith and spiritual qualities to grow. Three kinds of faith grow through this process: clear confidence in the results of actions, deep admiration for the qualities of the Buddhas, and a personal wish to practice the path. While self-training is the immediate purpose, the verse gently opens a door for others. Those with a similar wish for enlightenment — who are sincere, open-minded, and ready to practice — may also benefit greatly from seeing these words.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Practising Humility in Daily Conversations:** Today, I will notice moments when I want to show off my knowledge or correct others. In those moments, I will pause and ask myself whether saying something will truly help. Following the example of the great teacher Śāntideva, I will try to speak less and listen more.
*   **2. Writing to Strengthen My Practice:** Today, I will take a few minutes to write down one teaching from today's verses in my own words. By putting it on paper, I am training my own mind — just as the great teacher Śāntideva did. This small act will help my understanding and faith grow stronger over time.
*   **3. Recognising Shared Aspiration in Others:** Today, I will look at the people around me and remember that some of them also carry a quiet wish to be better and to help others. When I see this, I will feel less alone in my practice. I will remind myself that I am not walking this path by myself.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Blissful One (བདེ་གཤེགས་):** This is one of the most important names for a fully enlightened Buddha. It means one who has "gone to bliss" — someone who has completely removed all faults and perfectly developed all good qualities. The name shows three things: the Buddha left behind all problems without return, left completely with nothing remaining, and arrived at a state of perfect peace and wisdom.

*   **Truth Body (ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་):** This refers to the deepest nature of a Buddha's mind — a state of pure, open awareness that is completely free from all stains and confusion. It is not a physical body. It is the state of full awakening itself, which is naturally pure and inseparable from wisdom.

*   **Heroes of Enlightenment (བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ / བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་):** These are the noble practitioners who have deeply committed themselves to achieving full awakening for the sake of all living beings. They are called "sons and daughters" of the Buddhas because they follow in the Buddhas' footsteps. They are worthy of great respect from gods, humans, and all beings.

*   **Commitments of the Buddha's Heirs (བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་):** This refers to the ethical guidelines and practices that Heroes of Enlightenment take on — the full training of body, speech, and mind on the path to awakening. Entering these commitments means agreeing to live in a way that supports the welfare of all beings, not just oneself.

*   **Cultivation / Mind Training (བསྒོམ་པ་):** This means the repeated practice of keeping the mind in a good state — again and again, until it becomes natural and stable. It is like building a muscle through daily exercise. In this text, it refers especially to keeping the wish for enlightenment alive and growing through consistent effort.
