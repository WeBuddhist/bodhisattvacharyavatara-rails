## Today's Verse

> Even without intending to be angry,
> people become angry aimlessly.
> Even without intending to generate it,
> anger likewise arises. ^6-24

> All faults
> and various misdeeds
> arise from the power of conditions.
> They are not independent. ^6-25

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the twenty-fourth and twenty-fifth verses of the sixth chapter. A mirror and a face do not plan it, yet a reflection appears. In the same way, we do not think about it first. So who really chose that anger?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable story, which is easy to understand and encouraging.

In the land of Varanasi, a monk went to a householder's house to ask for alms. A red light fell on a jewel on a stand. It looked like red meat, so a tame peacock swallowed it. The householder saw that the jewel was gone and put the blame on the monk. The monk said, "I did not take it myself." But he did not say that the peacock had eaten it. The householder became angry and tied a knot in his own clothes. Then the monk also tied a knot in his own clothes. Later, the householder killed the peacock and found the jewel. He understood that the monk had stayed silent because he feared the peacock would be killed. The householder gained faith. He offered the jewel to the monk and generated bodhicitta.

This story shows that anger does not arise freely on its own. It arises depending on the coming together of three conditions: the clothes, the jewel and the peacock.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, even if others shout bad words at me, I will think that it came from conditions, and I will not get angry.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ All faults and all wrongdoing arise from the power of conditions. The people who harm us also depend on conditions, so they are not free. If we think about this reason and let compassion arise, we are freed from anger.
