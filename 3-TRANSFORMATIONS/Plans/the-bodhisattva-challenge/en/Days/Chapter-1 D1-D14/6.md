# Day 6 — Wishing to travel is not the same as traveling

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Still just meaning to do it?
> **Detail:** Wishing isn't doing. Notice one thing you keep meaning to do but never start.

## Introduction

Today the chapter shifts. Shantideva has spent the first fourteen verses making the case for bodhicitta — its rarity, its power, its inexhaustible fruit. Now he defines it more precisely. There are two kinds: the aspiring mind, which wishes for awakening, and the engaging mind, which actually steps onto the path. They are not the same thing.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

*MIND TRAINING (recite 3 times)*

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in immeasurable equanimity, free from attachment and aversion.

*REFUGE (recite 3 times)*

> Until I reach the heart of awakening, I take refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.

*TAKING THE BODHISATTVA VOW (recite 3 times)*

> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And trained step by step in the bodhisattva conduct,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And train step by step in that same conduct.

## From the Tradition

Ngulchu Thokme and Gyaltsab Je both cite Maitreya's image from the Gandavyuha Sutra: even a cracked diamond still outshines every golden ornament and doesn't lose the name "diamond." The aspiring mind, they explain, is like that cracked diamond. Even when not yet joined with actual practice, it already surpasses all the virtues of practitioners on lesser paths. It doesn't lose the name Bodhisattva. It still dispels the poverty of samsara. The aspiring mind is not a consolation prize for people who haven't yet committed fully. It's already something extraordinary — just not the same as the engaging mind that sets out on the road.

## Aspiration

> May the precious and supreme bodhicitta —
> Where it has not yet arisen, may it arise;
> Where it has arisen, may it not decline,
> But grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of this practice,
> May all wandering beings, without exception,
> Enter the path of the bodhisattva.

## Today's Practice Challenge

Maitreya says in the sutra: those who wish for awakening are rare, but those who actually enter the conduct are even rarer. Pick one thing in your practice or your life where you've been in the wishing phase for a long time — intending to, meaning to, planning to. Notice what keeps you there. You don't have to act on it today. But be honest about the gap.
