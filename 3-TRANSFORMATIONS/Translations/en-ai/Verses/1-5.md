## Combined Commentary Summaries

All three commentators agree on the core meaning of the lightning flash metaphor (*vidyut* / *གློག་འགྱུ*), using it to illustrate the extreme rarity, brevity, and weakness of ordinary virtuous thoughts in comparison to the overwhelming force of negative actions in cyclic existence. They also share the consensus that any virtuous impulse arising in worldly beings is not due to their own inherent strength, but is inspired solely by the blessing and power of the Buddhas (*buddhānubhāva* / *སངས་རྒྱས་མཐུ*).

### 1. The Metaphor of the Lightning Flash
- **Consensus on Rarity and Brevity:** All commentators explain that a flash of lightning on a pitch-black, cloudy night represents the momentary and non-continuous arising of a virtuous mind (*dge ba'i blo gros* / *puṇyeṣu matiḥ*). 
- **The Dark, Cloudy Night:** The darkness of the night represents the pervasive suffering and powerful negativity that traps worldly beings. Gyaltsab Je notes that the dark night represents an era where virtue is weak and negativity is powerful, creating an extremely fierce situation of great fear. Ngulchu Thokme similarly describes the night as the darkness of suffering due to negativity. Sazang Mati Panchen adds that the thick darkness of a cloudy night possesses great power to produce the unendurable sufferings of hell.
- **The Flash of Light:** The lightning flash represents a brief moment of virtuous intelligence. For Sazang Mati Panchen, this is specifically the thought, "I must engage in the holy Dharma." All commentators emphasize that this light is extremely transient and does not remain for long.

### 2. The Power of the Buddha
- **Inspiration and Blessings:** The commentators agree that worldly beings, left to their own devices, are incapable of generating virtue. The arising of a virtuous mind is entirely due to the power of the Buddha.
- **Sazang Mati Panchen's Insight:** Sazang explicitly attributes this to "the light rays of compassion of the Buddhas," which illuminate worldly beings dwelling in the darkness of cyclic existence.
- **Gyaltsab Je and Ngulchu Thokme:** Both reiterate that the virtuous mind arises only occasionally by the power of the Buddha, serving as a reminder that we must make active efforts to cultivate and increase this virtue when it does appear.

### 3. The Need for Bodhicitta as the Ultimate Remedy
- **Inadequacy of Ordinary Virtue:** Sazang Mati Panchen provides a key doctrinal distinction: ordinary virtue (which is weak and transient like lightning) is utterly insufficient to overcome the powerful, dark force of negativity. Only the perfect mind of enlightenment (Bodhicitta), which is endowed with the essence of emptiness and compassion, possesses the strength to outshine and overcome such great evil.
- **Call to Action:** Because ordinary virtue is weak and easily exhausted, both Ngulchu Thokme and Gyaltsab Je emphasize that we must make great efforts to cultivate and firmly maintain the remedy—the precious mind of enlightenment—and never let it decline.
