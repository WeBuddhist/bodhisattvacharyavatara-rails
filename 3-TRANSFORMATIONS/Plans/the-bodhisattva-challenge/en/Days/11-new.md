# Day 11 — Kindness that asks for nothing, not even good karma

## Opening

Chapter 1 has spent several days praising bodhicitta in broad terms. Verses 29 to 31 make the praise specific. They describe three things the awakening mind does for beings, then ask: if we already honor someone for repaying a kindness, what should we say about one who helps without being asked and expects nothing back?

## From the Tradition

When you hold a door for someone who walks through without a word, there is often a faint sting. You did not do it for thanks, and yet you notice their absence. That quiet ledger is something most of us keep without deciding to. Sazang Mati Panchen, a 14th-century Sakya master, draws out what makes the bodhisattva's help structurally different. The verse says the bodhisattva acts without being asked. Sazang adds one further detail: they act without expecting any return or maturation of action. This covers the quiet hope that being good will pay off in future lives. The bodhisattva's generosity carries no such expectation. The ledger is not merely invisible. It is absent.

## Today's Practice Challenge

The next time you do something for someone, notice whether you are waiting to find out if it landed. Did they seem grateful? Did you feel good about yourself afterward? That waiting is what Sazang Mati Panchen calls expecting maturation of action. You do not need to erase that instinct today. Just notice it when it appears, and see whether the act itself can stand without the confirmation.
