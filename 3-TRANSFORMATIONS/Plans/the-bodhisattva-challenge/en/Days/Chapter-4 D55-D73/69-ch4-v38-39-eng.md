## Today's Verse

> then, for me, who strives to conquer
> this enemy, which is the cause of all suffering,
> and is naturally an enemy,
> needless to say, I will not become disheartened or despondent by any of the hundreds of causes of suffering! ^4-38

> If a meaningless enemy inflicts wounds,
> and one cherishes them as if they were ornaments on the body,
> then for me, who strives to accomplish a great purpose,
> why should suffering be a harm? ^4-39

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 38 and 39 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Just as a soldier takes pride in the scars of a pointless battle and wears them like ornaments, we too must hold this same outlook toward the hardships of working for awakening.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

For example, fire is hot by nature. We do not need to ask whether a particular fire is hot, since heat is the very nature of fire. In the same way, whoever's mind the afflictions arise in, they cause harm by their very nature. We do not need to look for another reason why the afflictions cause harm. They always give rise to suffering.

So, since the very nature of the afflictions is to cause harm, we work without hesitation to conquer them. Even if we meet a hundred hardships, we face them without losing heart. If an ordinary soldier wears the scars of a pointless battle like ornaments, then we should count the hardships met for the sake of great awakening as the finest ornament of all, and welcome them with joy.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will not resent the hardship of my practice. I will welcome it with joy.

**Explanation:** _(Welcoming hardship)_ A soldier counts even the scars of a pointless battle as ornaments and is glad of them. So we should be even more glad of the hardships met while working for the great purpose of awakening. If we welcome hardship without resentment, our practice will grow greatly.
