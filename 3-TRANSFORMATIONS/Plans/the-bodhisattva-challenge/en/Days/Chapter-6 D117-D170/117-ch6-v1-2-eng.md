## Today's Verse

> Good deeds accumulated over a thousand eons,
> such as generosity and offerings to buddhas—
> all such good conduct
> is destroyed by a single moment of anger. ^6-1

> There is no misdeed like hatred,
> and no austerity like patience.
> Therefore, cultivate patience diligently
> in various ways. ^6-2

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the first and second verses of the sixth chapter. A single flame of anger burns up all the good actions we have gathered. So today, we think about the value of patience.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. For example, a small spark of fire burns a big heap of grass. In the same way, a single instance of strong anger, not held back by antidotes such as regret, completely destroys the many roots of good gathered from generosity, offerings, and ethics. This example clearly shows how anger destroys the roots of good.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, even if other people say unkind words to me, I will not get angry. I will meditate on patience.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ Hatred is the greatest wrongdoing, so it harms compassion. Patience clears away the heat of the afflictions and becomes the best austerity. Therefore, we must not fall under the power of anger, and we must meditate on patience in many ways.
