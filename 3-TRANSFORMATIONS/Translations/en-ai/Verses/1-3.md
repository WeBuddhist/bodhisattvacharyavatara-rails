## Combined Commentary Summaries
The commentators provide a unified and deeply psychological explanation of Verse 1-3, which serves as the final part of Śāntideva's introduction of humility. They analyze the verse along three main thematic dimensions: the primary focus on self-cultivation with complete freedom from pride, the mechanism of how writing down the teachings increases personal virtue, and the secondary, altruistic benefit for other practitioners of equal fortune.

### 1. Primary Motivation: Self-Cultivation and Humility
All three commentators agree that Śāntideva's primary motivation for composing the treatise is self-benefit and mental training. By declaring a complete lack of poetic skill and stating that his work contains no new doctrines, Śāntideva systematically abandons any prideful claim of writing for the benefit of others. 
- **Gyaltsab Je** and **Ngulchu Thokme** point out a logical boundary: since the vast meaning of the Mahayana scriptures is already fully present in the sūtras, those who possess sharp intelligence and can easily comprehend them directly do not need this text. Thus, Śāntideva does not write for their sake.
- **Sazang Mati Panchen** and **Ngulchu Thokme** note that the treatise is written specifically as a tool to familiarize, habituate, and practice the aspiring mind of enlightenment and the engagement of the six perfections directly in his own heart to prevent spiritual decline.

### 2. The Mechanism of Increasing Personal Virtue and Faith
The commentators explain how the physical act of writing down his realizations acts as a powerful cause for the growth of Śāntideva's own spiritual qualities.
- **Sazang Mati Panchen's Classification of Faith:** Sazang Mati Panchen provides a unique, highly detailed scholastic breakdown of the "power of faith" (*dad pa'i shugs*) mentioned in the verse. He distinguishes three specific types of faith that will increase upon seeing these words written down:
  1. *Faith of conviction* in the law of karma and its fruits.
  2. *Faith of clarity* (or admiration) toward those who possess noble qualities (such as the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas).
  3. *Faith of aspiration* toward one's own ability to practice.
  Furthermore, Sazang Mati Panchen links this increase of faith to the gradual, progressive development of other essential path qualities: diligence, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom, which eventually lead to ultimate liberation.
- **Gyaltsab Je** adds that by expressing these realizations in writing, Śāntideva's faith, wisdom, and compassion will all temporarily increase.
- **Ngulchu Thokme** explains that by writing down these exceptional realizations for the purpose of meditation, the author's faith and other virtues will temporarily develop and grow.

### 3. Secondary Motivation: Benefiting Others of "Equal Fortune"
While self-cultivation remains the immediate goal, the commentators agree that the treatise holds a secondary, altruistic purpose of benefiting other Mahayana practitioners.
- **The Meaning of "Temporarily" (*re zhig*):** **Ngulchu Thokme** cites classical Indian commentators Kṣemadeva and Vairocana to explain that the word "temporarily" (*re zhig*) in the verse indicates that while self-cultivation is the immediate, immediate focus, there is a secondary, long-term purpose of benefiting others.
- **Defining "Equal Fortune" (*skal ba mnyam pa*):** The commentators define who these fortunate readers are:
  - **Sazang Mati Panchen** defines them as those who share the same spiritual family, qualities, and aspiration.
  - **Gyaltsab Je** defines them as fellow Mahayana followers with a similar capacity and disposition.
  - **Ngulchu Thokme** defines them as unbiased practitioners seeking to practice the Bodhisattva conduct.
- **Inspiring Trainees:** **Gyaltsab Je** notes that demonstrating this potential for mutual benefit serves as a "branch of entry" (*'jug sgo*) that encourages other fortunate individuals to study and engage with the treatise. **Sazang Mati Panchen** similarly concludes that because of this temporary and ultimate benefit, trainees should approach and study the text with joy.
- **Seeking Forgiveness:** **Ngulchu Thokme** highlights that expressing humility in this manner ensures the author is free from pride, and if any composition faults arise, they will be easily forgiven by his peers.
