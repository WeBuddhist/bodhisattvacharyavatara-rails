## Today's Verse

> In this and in my other lives Throughout beginningless samsara, Deluded, I have done misdeeds. I’ve instigated them, and also— ^2-28

> Compelled by ignorant confusion— I have rejoiced in them. I see They were mistakes, and I confess To the protectors from my depths. ^2-29

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on verses 28 and 29 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Throughout our lives, knowingly and unknowingly, we gather faults in three ways: wrongdoing we commit ourselves, wrongdoing we get others to do, and wrongdoing we are quietly pleased about when others do it. These verses do not let us make excuses for any of the three. They give us the courage to name our mistakes honestly and to confess them from the heart rather than with the mouth alone.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

Shantideva has been confessing what he did. Here he widens it. Across beginningless samsara he did wrong himself, he got other people to do wrong, and when others did wrong he was quietly pleased about it.

Khenpo Kunzang Palden, an early commentator on this text, makes the point most of us would never reach on our own. There is no difference in weight between the three. Being glad about someone else's wrongdoing carries the same fault as doing it yourself. Rejoicing costs nothing, nobody sees it, and so nobody ever counts it against themselves.

The commentaries also say why we do it, and the answer is not cruelty. We do not see a misdeed as a misdeed, and we are confused about how actions lead to results. On that question, they say, we are no wiser than an animal: we follow whatever pleases us now and never look at where it leads.

This is why verse 29 puts seeing before speaking. Shantideva recognizes the mistake as a mistake first, and confesses after. Words on their own would not have been a confession.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when I see someone being criticized or harmed, I will not let myself feel pleased. I will recognize what is happening as a mistake.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ Most of us do not commit serious harm ourselves. But when someone else does something bad, or when a person we dislike meets defeat or trouble, a quiet satisfaction rises in us that we never admit to. Today's verses say that being glad about another person's wrongdoing carries the same fault as doing it ourselves. So today I will watch for that quiet satisfaction and set it down. When I hear that someone has failed or been caught out, I will name it inwardly as a mistake rather than as good news. Giving up this one habit closes a door through which we gather harm without ever noticing.
