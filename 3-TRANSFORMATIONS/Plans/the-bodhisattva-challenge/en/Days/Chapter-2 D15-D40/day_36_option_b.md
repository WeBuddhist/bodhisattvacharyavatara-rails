# Day 36 — Looking for the cure in the wrong place

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Notice one thing you reach for that quietly makes it worse
> **Detail:** Today's verse warns: one affliction does great harm, and the real cure isn't where we look.

## Opening

Shantideva makes two sharp points today. First, do not underestimate a single affliction; even one, left alone, can do enormous damage. Second, the cure for it cannot be found just anywhere. We tend to treat anger or craving as small and to reach for relief in the very things that feed them. The verse says the real medicine is found in one place, the path the buddhas teach, and nowhere else.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, refuses to treat the afflictions as minor. He gives a stark example: a single burst of anger can do harm out of all proportion to its size, ruining what took years to build. So these are not small habits to manage; they are powerful, and they deserve real treatment. But here is the catch he draws out. The cure is not found just anywhere, and certainly not in the affliction's own logic. We try to cure restlessness with more distraction, loneliness with more scrolling, anger with winning the argument, and each "remedy" quietly feeds the disease. Those are false medicines. The real one, he says, is found only in actually applying the path. The first move is honest: to notice the thing we keep reaching for to feel better, and to see that it has been making us more unwell, not less.

## Today's Practice

Pick one thing you habitually reach for to soothe a hard feeling that, honestly, tends to feed it instead, scrolling when restless, snapping back when angry, a snack when low. Just once today, when the urge comes, don't reach for it. You do not have to replace it with anything yet. Simply notice what is underneath, now that you are not covering it with the false medicine.
