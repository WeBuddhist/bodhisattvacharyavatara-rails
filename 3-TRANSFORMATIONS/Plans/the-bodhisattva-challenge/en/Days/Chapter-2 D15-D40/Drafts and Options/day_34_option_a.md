# Day 34 — Not just asking, but giving yourself

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Give your whole day over this morning
> **Detail:** Today refuge goes further than asking for help. Shantideva hands himself over.

## Opening

Yesterday refuge was a decision: from this day, I rely on you. Today it goes one step deeper and gets personal. Still shaken by fear, Shantideva does not just ask the bodhisattvas for help; he gives himself to them. He offers his very body to Samantabhadra, the bodhisattva of boundless good intentions, and to Manjughosha, the bodhisattva of wisdom. Refuge becomes a handing-over.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, notices two things in this offering. First, it is bigger than a request. Shantideva does not say "lend me a hand"; he gives his whole self. When you truly trust someone with your safety, you stop holding back and simply put yourself in their hands, and that is what refuge becomes here. Second, Gyaltsab underlines that it is done freely. The offering to Manjughosha is made, in his words, of one's own accord, not because anyone urged it, with a devoted mind. No one is twisting Shantideva's arm. Having seen clearly that he cannot save himself, he chooses, gladly, to belong to those who can. The fear that opened these verses has quietly turned into something warmer: not a cornered animal grasping for rescue, but a person willingly entrusting their whole life to hands that will hold it well.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

This morning, before the day takes off, try offering the whole of it: your body, your hours, whatever you have, given over to the buddhas and bodhisattvas. A simple line is enough: "I am yours today; please use this life well." Then go about your day as someone who belongs to something larger, and see if a few of your usual knots loosen.
