## Today's Verse

> I do not want suffering, contempt,
> harsh words, or unpleasant speech,
> for myself or my friends.
> For my enemies, it is the opposite. ^6-11

> Causes of happiness sometimes arise,
> but causes of suffering are very many.
> There is no liberation without suffering.
> Therefore, mind, maintain your steadfastness. ^6-12

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the eleventh and twelfth verses of the sixth chapter. Even if others say harsh words to us and our friends, we keep our own mind steady and undisturbed.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable quotation, which is easy to understand and encouraging. The _Smrityupasthana_ says, "In samsara, there is never happiness, not even as much as the tip of a needle." It is the nature of samsara that suffering arises. So we must give up unhappiness and accept it as the result of causes we ourselves made. Suffering is a guide on the path to liberation, an encouragement to do good, and a broom that sweeps away wrongdoing. If we never experienced suffering, the wish to be free from samsara would not arise.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when suffering and problems come to me, I will keep my mind steady and undisturbed, and accept them.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ In samsara, the causes of suffering are very many. But if we accept suffering, it becomes smaller. Suffering urges us toward liberation and doing good, and it purifies wrongdoing. If we did not experience suffering, the wish for liberation would not arise. So we must stay steady, with a mind that is not disturbed.
