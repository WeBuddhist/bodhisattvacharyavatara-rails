## Chapter One: Explaining the Benefits of Bodhicitta ^1-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-1]]

To the Sugatas who possess the Dharmakāya, along with their sons,
And to all who are worthy of homage, I respectfully bow down;
The engagement in the vows of the sons of the Sugata
I shall state concisely, in accordance with scripture. ^1-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-2]]

Nothing that has not appeared before is stated here,
Nor do I possess any skill in composition;
Therefore, I have no thought of benefiting others;
I composed this in order to cultivate my own mind. ^1-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-3]]

In order to cultivate virtue, the force of my faith
Will, for a time, increase through these;
And if others, equal in fortune to myself,
Should see these, they will become meaningful. ^1-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-4]]

This leisure and endowment is extremely difficult to find;
Since that which accomplishes human purpose has been attained,
If one does not accomplish what is beneficial with this,
How will this be perfectly found again later? ^1-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-5]]

Just as on a dark night, within the womb of clouds,
A flash of lightning shows brilliant illumination for an instant,
Likewise, by the power of the Buddha, occasionally
Intelligence for merit arises for a moment in the world. ^1-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-6]]

Therefore, virtue is always feeble,
While misdeeds are extremely powerful and unbearable;
Except for the bodhicitta for perfect awakening,
What other virtue could overcome them? ^1-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-7]]

The Lords of Sages, having thoroughly contemplated for many eons,
Have seen that this alone is beneficial.
By this, limitless multitudes of beings
Will easily attain supreme happiness. ^1-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-8]]

Those who wish to overcome the hundreds of sufferings of existence,
Who wish to dispel the unhappiness of sentient beings,
And who wish to enjoy many hundreds of joys,
Should never forsake bodhicitta. ^1-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-9]]

When bodhicitta is generated, in an instant,
The wretched bound in the prison of saṃsāra
Will be called the children of the Sugatas,
And will be paid homage by the world, including its gods and humans. ^1-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-10]]

Like the supreme form of a gold-making elixir,
It takes this impure body and transforms it
Into the priceless jewel of a Victor's body;
Therefore, firmly hold to what is called bodhicitta. ^1-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-11]]

Since the sole caravan leader of wandering beings, with limitless intellect,
Has thoroughly examined it and found it to be highly precious,
Those who wish to be separated from the realms of wandering beings
Should firmly hold to precious bodhicitta. ^1-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-12]]

All other virtues are like the plantain tree:
Having produced a result, they are exhausted;
But the tree of bodhicitta perpetually
Yields a result, is not exhausted, and increases. ^1-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-13]]

Even if one has committed extremely unbearable misdeeds,
Just as relying on a hero frees one from great fear,
By relying on which one will be freed in an instant,
Why do the conscientious not rely on it? ^1-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-14]]

Like the fire at the end of time, it
Certainly burns up great misdeeds in a single instant.
Its immeasurable benefits
Were explained to Sudhana by the wise Protector Maitreya. ^1-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-15]]

In brief, that bodhicitta
Should be understood to be of two types:
Aspiring bodhicitta and
Engaging bodhicitta itself. ^1-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-16]]

Between wishing to go and going,
Just as one understands the difference,
Likewise, a wise person should understand
The difference between these two in their respective order. ^1-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-17]]

From aspiring bodhicitta,
Although a great result arises while in saṃsāra,
Unlike from engaging bodhicitta,
Merit does not arise continuously. ^1-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-18]]

From the moment that, to thoroughly liberate
The infinite realm of sentient beings,
With an irreversible mind,
One correctly adopts that mind, ^1-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-19]]

From this moment onward, whether asleep,
Or falling into heedlessness, a force of merit,
Uninterrupted and manifold,
Continually arises, equal to space. ^1-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-20]]

Together with the reasoning for this,
In the Question of Subāhu,
For the sake of sentient beings with lesser inclinations,
The Tathāgata himself proclaimed this. ^1-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-21]]

If even by thinking, "I shall dispel
The mere headaches of sentient beings,"
One possesses a beneficial intention
And becomes endowed with immeasurable merit, ^1-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-22]]

Wishing to dispel the immeasurable discomforts
Of each and every sentient being,
And wishing to accomplish immeasurable good qualities
For each one—what need is there to mention this? ^1-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-23]]

Whether it be a father or even a mother,
Who has such a beneficial intention?
Whether gods or seers,
Does even Brahmā have this? ^1-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-24]]

If, for those very sentient beings previously,
Such a mind for their own sake
Was never dreamed of even in a dream,
How could it arise for the sake of others? ^1-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-25]]

That intention for the welfare of sentient beings,
Which does not arise in others even for their own sake—
This extraordinary jewel of a mind
Is born as an unprecedented wonder. ^1-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-26]]

The cause of joy for all wandering beings,
The medicine for the suffering of sentient beings—
The merit of such a precious mind,
How could it ever be measured? ^1-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-27]]

If even the mere intention to be beneficial
Is more exalted than making offerings to the Buddhas,
Then for all sentient beings without exception,
What need is there to mention striving for their happiness? ^1-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-28]]

Although they have the wish to discard suffering,
They rush headlong into suffering itself;
Although they desire happiness, through ignorance
They destroy their own happiness as if it were an enemy. ^1-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-29]]

For those who are destitute of happiness
And who have many sufferings,
That which satisfies them with all happiness
And cuts through all suffering, ^1-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-30]]

And it even dispels their ignorance.
Where is there a virtue equal to that?
Where is there a friend like that?
Where is there even merit like that? ^1-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-31]]

If whoever repays a benefit done to them
Is indeed worthy of some praise,
What need is there to mention the bodhisattva
Who does good without being asked? ^1-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-32]]

One who provides regular food to a few wandering beings,
Giving merely a meal for a brief moment,
And satisfying them for half a day with contempt,
Is honored by people as a doer of virtue. ^1-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-33]]

To limitless numbers of sentient beings for a long time,
The unsurpassed happiness of the Sugatas,
Given constantly so as to fulfill all the wishes in their minds—
What need is there to mention one who gives thus? ^1-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-34]]

If anyone toward such a benefactor, a child of the Victors,
Generates a malicious thought, that one,
For eons equal to the number of malicious thoughts generated,
Will abide in hell, so the Sage has taught. ^1-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-35]]

But if anyone develops a deeply devoted mind,
Its result increases even more than that.
Even in great adversity for the children of the Victors,
Misdeeds do not arise, and virtue naturally increases. ^1-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-36]]

In whomever that supreme, precious mind
Has arisen, I bow to their body;
And those to whom even doing harm connects one to happiness—
I go for refuge to those sources of happiness. ^1-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, this is the first chapter, called Explaining the Benefits of Bodhicitta. ^1-a

## Chapter Two: Confession of Misdeeds ^2-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-1]]

In order to take hold of that precious mind,
To the Tathāgatas and the sublime Dharma,
The stainless Jewel, and the children of the Buddhas,
Who are oceans of good qualities, I make excellent offerings. ^2-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-2]]

Whatever flowers and fruits there are,
And whatever kinds of medicine there are,
Whatever jewels there are in the world,
And whatever pure and pleasing waters there are, ^2-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-3]]

Jewel mountains, and likewise
Forest regions, secluded and delightful,
Trees adorned and embellished with flowers,
And trees whose branches bow with excellent fruit, ^2-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-4]]

Even in the worlds of gods and others, fragrances,
Incense, wish-fulfilling trees, and jewel trees,
Various crops that grow without being ploughed,
And other ornaments worthy of being offered, ^2-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-5]]

Lakes and ponds adorned with lotuses,
Possessing swans with highly sweet and pleasing voices,
Reaching the limits of the infinite realm of space,
All those things that are unpossessed, ^2-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-6]]

Taking them up with my mind, to the Sages, the supreme among persons,
Together with their children, I excellently offer them;
May those supreme worthy recipients of offerings, those of great compassion,
Think of me with love and accept these things of mine. ^2-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-7]]

I am without merit and extremely destitute,
And I have no other wealth at all for making offerings.
Therefore, may the Protectors who consider the welfare of others
Accept this for my sake by their own power. ^2-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-8]]

To the Victors and their children,
I shall offer my entire body forever.
O supreme beings, please completely accept me;
With devotion I will become your subject. ^2-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-9]]

Because I am completely accepted by you,
I will be fearless in existence and act for the benefit of sentient beings.
I will perfectly transcend my previous misdeeds,
And will commit no other misdeeds henceforth. ^2-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-10]]

In exceedingly fragrant bathhouses,
Arranged with clear and shining crystal floors,
Endowed with pleasing pillars of blazing jewels,
And with canopies of radiant pearls spread above, ^2-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-11]]

To the Tathāgatas and their children,
With many precious vases of scented water,
Well filled with what is pleasing,
Accompanied by manifold songs and music, I offer a bath. ^2-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-12]]

Their bodies, with matchless garments,
Clean and thoroughly perfumed, I dry.
Then, to them, I offer excellently dyed,
Exceedingly fragrant, supreme robes. ^2-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-13]]

With various fine garments, thin and soft,
And hundreds of those supreme ornaments,
The noble Samantabhadra and Mañjuśrī,
Avalokiteśvara and others, I shall also adorn. ^2-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-14]]

With supreme perfumes, whose fragrance wafts
Throughout the three-thousandfold world system,
I shall anoint the bodies of all the Lords of Sages,
Radiant and blazing like polished, refined gold. ^2-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-15]]

To the Lords of Sages, the supreme objects of offering,
With pleasing mandāra flowers and lotuses,
Blue lotuses, and all other fragrant flowers,
And beautifully strung, pleasing garlands, I make offerings. ^2-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-16]]

From supreme incense, captivating and pervasive in fragrance,
Cloud-masses of smoke I also offer to them.
With meals comprising various foods and drinks,
Divine feasts I shall also offer to them. ^2-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-17]]

Within golden lotuses arranged in rows,
I shall also offer precious lamps.
Upon the smoothed ground anointed with perfume,
I shall scatter pleasing loose flowers. ^2-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-18]]

Immeasurable palaces endowed with pleasing songs of praise,
Beautifully blazing with hanging ornaments of pearls and jewels,
Which have become ornaments of boundless space:
These too I shall offer to those whose nature is compassion. ^2-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-19]]

Beautiful jeweled parasols with golden handles,
Endowed with pleasing forms of ornaments all around,
Well-shaped, lovely to behold, and raised aloft:
These too I shall always offer to the Lords of Sages. ^2-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-20]]

Besides these, multitudes of offerings,
Endowed with music and pleasing, sweet melodies:
May clouds that soothe the suffering of sentient beings
Remain in their respective places. ^2-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-21]]

Upon all the jewels of the sublime Dharma,
Upon the stūpas and the images,
May a rain of jewels, flowers, and the like
Fall without interruption. ^2-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-22]]

Just as Mañjuśrī and the others
Make offerings to the Victors,
Likewise, I make offerings to the Tathāgatas,
The Protectors, along with their children. ^2-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-23]]

I praise those who are oceans of good qualities
With oceans of branches of melodious praise.
May those sweet clouds of melodious praise
Certainly arise everywhere for them. ^2-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-24]]

To all the Buddhas who have gone in the three times,
Along with the Dharma and the Supreme Assembly,
With bodies as numerous as all the atoms in the fields,
Bowing down, I pay homage. ^2-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-25]]

To the bases of bodhicitta
And to the stūpas, I pay homage.
To the preceptors and likewise the masters,
And to the supreme ascetics, I pay homage. ^2-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-26]]

Until I reach the essence of awakening,
I take refuge in the Buddhas.
In the Dharma and the bodhisattvas'
Assembly, likewise, I take refuge. ^2-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-27]]

To those who reside in all directions,
The fully awakened Buddhas and those with bodhicitta,
Who possess great compassion,
Joining my palms, I pray: ^2-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-28]]

From beginningless saṃsāra,
In this life or in others,
Whatever misdeeds I have unwittingly committed
Or caused to be committed, ^2-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-29]]

Overwhelmed by the confusion of ignorance,
Whatever I have rejoiced in,
Having seen those mistakes,
I confess them to the Protectors from the depths of my heart. ^2-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-30]]

To the Three Jewels,
To parents, or to other teachers,
Through the gateway of the afflictions, with body, speech,
And mind, whatever harm I have done, ^2-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-31]]

Stained by many faults,
Whatever misdeeds I, one burdened with misdeeds,
Have committed that are extremely unbearable,
I confess them all to the Guides. ^2-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-32]]

Without the purification of my misdeeds,
I may meet my death beforehand.
So that there is definite liberation from this,
Please protect me swiftly. ^2-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-33]]

This Lord of Death cannot be trusted;
He does not wait for what is done or not done.
Whether sick or healthy,
No one can trust this life, which can end suddenly. ^2-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-34]]

That I must depart, leaving everything behind,
Not having understood this,
For the sake of friends and enemies,
I have committed various kinds of misdeeds. ^2-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-35]]

Enemies will cease to be,
And friends will cease to be;
I too will cease to be,
And likewise everything will cease to be. ^2-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-36]]

Just like the experiences of a dream,
Whatever things I have enjoyed
Will become objects of memory;
All that has passed will not be seen. ^2-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-37]]

Even during this very life,
Many friends and enemies have passed away;
The unbearable misdeeds committed for their sake
Are what remain before me. ^2-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-38]]

Because I did not realize
That I am transient in this way,
Through ignorance, attachment, and hatred,
I have committed many kinds of misdeeds. ^2-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-39]]

Without pausing by day or night,
This life is constantly diminishing;
Since no addition comes from elsewhere,
Why would someone like me not die? ^2-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-40]]

While I lie upon my bed,
Even though surrounded by all my relatives and friends,
The sensations of my life being severed
Will be experienced by me alone. ^2-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-41]]

When seized by the messengers of Yama,
What help are kinsmen, and what help are friends?
If at that time merit alone is a refuge,
I have not relied upon even that. ^2-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-42]]

O Protectors, I, through heedlessness,
Unaware of such terrors as these,
For the sake of this impermanent life,
Have committed many misdeeds. ^2-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-43]]

Even a person being led today
To a place where their limbs will be severed is terrified;
With a parched mouth, distressed eyes, and the like,
If they appear transformed from how they were before, ^2-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-44]]

Seized by the messengers of Yama
With their terrifying physical forms,
And stricken by the disease of great terror,
What need to mention their utter misery? ^2-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-45]]

"Who will perfectly protect me
From this great terror?"
With eyes wide open in panic,
I will search the four directions for a refuge. ^2-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-46]]

Seeing no refuge in the four directions,
I will then fall into utter despair.
If there is no refuge in that place,
What shall I do at that time? ^2-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-47]]

Therefore, in the Victors, Protectors of wandering beings,
Who strive for the purpose of protecting wandering beings,
The mighty ones who dispel all fears,
From this very day, I take refuge. ^2-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-48]]

In the Dharma they have realized,
Which dispels the fears of saṃsāra,
And in the assembly of bodhisattvas,
Likewise, I perfectly take refuge. ^2-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-49]]

Terrified by fear, I
Offer myself to Samantabhadra;
To Mañjuśrī, I myself
Shall offer my body. ^2-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-50]]

Whose conduct through compassion is unerring,
To the protector Avalokiteśvara as well,
I cry out with an anguished voice:
"Please protect me, one possessing misdeeds!" ^2-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-51]]

To noble Ākāśagarbha,
And to Kṣitigarbha,
And to all the Protectors with great compassion,
Seeking refuge, I cry out from my heart. ^2-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-52]]

Seeing whom, the messengers of Yama
And other malicious beings
Flee in terror to the four directions,
I take refuge in the Vajra-wielder. ^2-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-53]]

Previously, I transgressed your commands;
Now, having seen great fear,
I take refuge in you.
Please swiftly dispel my fears! ^2-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-54]]

Even when frightened by an ordinary illness,
If one must act according to the physician's words,
When one is constantly stricken by the disease
Of a hundred faults such as desire, what need to mention? ^2-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-55]]

If even just one of them, in Jambudvīpa,
Destroys all the people dwelling there,
And no other medicine to cure them
Can be found anywhere in all directions— ^2-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-56]]

In that case, of the Omniscient Physician
Who draws out all pain,
The intention not to act according to his commands
Is extreme ignorance and an object of reproach. ^2-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-57]]

Even at a small, ordinary precipice,
If one must remain careful,
Of falling a thousand yojanas
Into a long-lasting precipice, what need to mention? ^2-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-58]]

Thinking, "I will not die this very day,"
It is not right to remain at ease.
The time when I will cease to be
Will undoubtedly arrive. ^2-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-59]]

Who has given me fearlessness?
How will I definitely escape from this?
If I will undoubtedly cease to exist,
How can my mind remain at ease? ^2-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-60]]

From what was previously experienced and has perished,
What remains for me?
By being deeply attached to that,
I have transgressed the teacher's commands. ^2-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-61]]

Leaving behind this lifetime, and likewise
Relatives and friends,
If I must go somewhere alone,
What is the use of all friends and non-friends? ^2-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-62]]

From non-virtue, suffering arises.
'How will I definitely escape from that?'
Constantly, day and night, it is right for me
To ponder only this. ^2-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-63]]

Because of my unknowing and ignorance,
Natural misdeeds or
Whatever proscribed misdeeds there are—
Whichever of these I have committed, ^2-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-64]]

Directly in the presence of the Protectors,
With joined palms and a mind terrified of suffering,
I pay homage again and again,
And I confess all of those. ^2-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-65]]

I pray that the Guides regard my misdeeds
As faults.
Since this is not good,
Henceforth I will never do it. ^2-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the second chapter, Confession of Misdeeds. ^2-a

## Chapter Three: Completely Adopting Bodhicitta ^3-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-1]]

In the virtue of all sentient beings
That brings rest from the suffering of the lower realms,
And in suffering beings abiding in happiness,
With joy I rejoice. ^3-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-2]]

In the accumulated virtue that becomes the cause for awakening,
In that I rejoice.
For embodied beings, from the suffering of saṃsāra,
In definite liberation I rejoice.
In the awakening of the Saviours, and
In the levels of the children of the Victors too, I rejoice. ^3-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-3]]

Bringing happiness to all sentient beings,
In the ocean of virtue of the generation of bodhicitta, and
In the deeds that benefit sentient beings,
With joy I rejoice. ^3-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-4]]

To the Buddhas of all directions,
I join my palms and pray:
For sentient beings suffering and bewildered in darkness,
Please light the lamp of the Dharma. ^3-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-5]]

To the Victors who wish to pass into nirvāṇa,
I join my palms and pray:
Do not leave these wandering beings in blindness,
But please remain for countless eons. ^3-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-6]]

Having done all this in this way,
Whatever virtue I have accumulated,
By that, may all the suffering
Of all sentient beings be dispelled. ^3-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-7]]

For as long as wandering beings are sick,
Until they are cured of their illness,
May I become the medicine and the doctor,
And act as their nurse. ^3-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-8]]

By raining down showers of food and drink,
May I dispel the suffering of hunger and thirst.
During the intermediate eon of famine,
May I myself become food and drink. ^3-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-9]]

For sentient beings who are destitute and poor,
May I become an inexhaustible treasure;
As all the various needed provisions,
May I remain closely present before them. ^3-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-10]]

My body and likewise my possessions,
And all virtues of the three times,
In order to accomplish the welfare of all sentient beings,
I shall give away without a sense of loss. ^3-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-11]]

By giving up everything, nirvāṇa is attained,
And my mind seeks to accomplish nirvāṇa;
Since I must give up everything all the same,
It is best given to sentient beings. ^3-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-12]]

Since, to all embodied beings, I
Have already given this body for their pleasure,
Whether they constantly kill and revile it,
Or strike it and so forth, let them do as they please. ^3-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-13]]

Whether they play with my body,
Or make it a source of ridicule and jest,
Since I have already given this body away,
What is the use of caring for it? ^3-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-14]]

Any action that does not bring harm to them,
Whatever it may be, let them do it;
Focusing upon me, at any time,
May it never be meaningless for anyone. ^3-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-15]]

Focusing upon me, for those in whom
A mind of anger or faith arises,
May that very mind always become for them
The cause for accomplishing all their goals. ^3-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-16]]

Those who speak ill of me,
Or others who cause me harm,
Or likewise those who mock me,
May they all have the fortune of awakening. ^3-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-17]]

May I become a Protector for those without a Protector,
A guide for those who travel on the path,
And for those who wish to cross over,
May I become a boat, a ship, and a bridge. ^3-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-18]]

May I be an island for those who seek an island,
A resting place for those who desire a resting place,
And for embodied beings who desire a servant,
May I become a servant to them all. ^3-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-19]]

May I become a wish-fulfilling jewel and an excellent vase,
An accomplished knowledge-mantra and a great medicine,
Wish-granting trees,
And a cow of plenty for embodied beings. ^3-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-20]]

Just as earth and the other great elements,
And like space, always,
For immeasurable sentient beings,
May I also be the ground of manifold sustenance. ^3-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-21]]

Likewise, extending to the limits of space,
For the realm of sentient beings, in all ways,
Until they all attain nirvāṇa,
May I also be the cause of their sustenance. ^3-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-22]]

Just as the Sugatas of former times
Generated bodhicitta
And in the trainings of the bodhisattvas
Progressively abided, ^3-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-23]]

Likewise, for the benefit of wandering beings,
I shall generate bodhicitta,
And likewise in the trainings too
I shall progressively train. ^3-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-24]]

In this way, the intelligent one,
Having taken hold of bodhicitta with serene faith,
In order to make it flourish further,
Should uplift the mind as follows: ^3-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-25]]

Today my life is fruitful;
I have well obtained a human existence.
Today I am born into the family of the Buddhas;
Today I have become a child of the Buddhas. ^3-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-26]]

Now I must, by whatever means,
Undertake actions in accord with the family,
So that this flawless, noble family
Is not defiled—thus shall I act. ^3-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-27]]

Just as a blind person from a heap of refuse
Might find a precious jewel,
Likewise, by some sheer chance,
This bodhicitta has been born in me. ^3-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-28]]

It is the supreme nectar
That destroys the Lord of Death for wandering beings.
It is the inexhaustible treasure
That dispels the poverty of wandering beings. ^3-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-29]]

It is the supreme medicine
That thoroughly pacifies the illnesses of wandering beings.
It is the tree of rest for wandering beings
Who are weary from wandering the path of existence. ^3-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-30]]

It is the universal bridge that liberates
All wandering beings from the lower realms.
It is the rising moon of the mind
That dispels the torment of the afflictions of wandering beings. ^3-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-31]]

It is the great sun that uproots
The dimness of the unknowing of wandering beings.
It is the essence of butter that has emerged
From churning the milk of the sublime Dharma. ^3-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-32]]

For wandering beings, the guests who travel the path of existence
And wish to experience the enjoyment of happiness,
This is stationed nearby as the supreme happiness,
Satisfying the great guests, the sentient beings. ^3-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-33]]

Today, in the presence of all the Saviours,
I have invited wandering beings as guests to Sugatahood itself,
And to happiness in the meantime.
Let gods, asuras, and others rejoice! ^3-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the third chapter, called “Fully Taking Hold of Bodhicitta.” ^3-a

## Chapter Four: Teaching Heedfulness ^4-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-1]]

A child of the Victors, in that way,
Having firmly taken hold of bodhicitta,
Always and without lapse,
Should strive not to transgress the trainings. ^4-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-2]]

Whatever is undertaken rashly,
Or whatever is not well examined,
Even if a promise has been made regarding it,
It is appropriate to examine whether to do it or abandon it. ^4-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-3]]

By the Buddhas and their children,
Whatever has been examined with great wisdom,
And which I myself have examined again and again,
What reason is there to delay it? ^4-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-4]]

If, having promised in that way,
I do not accomplish it through action,
By deceiving all those sentient beings,
What sort of wandering being will I become? ^4-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-5]]

Even regarding a slight, ordinary thing,
Having intended in mind to give it,
Any person who does not give it—
If it is taught that they will become a hungry ghost, ^4-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-6]]

To unsurpassed happiness,
Having sincerely invited them,
If I deceive all wandering beings,
How shall I go to a happy realm? ^4-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-7]]

Even if someone abandons bodhicitta,
That they attain liberation
Is an inconceivable way of karma,
Which only the Omniscient One knows. ^4-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-8]]

For a bodhisattva, this
Is the heaviest among downfalls,
Because should it occur,
One falls short in accomplishing the welfare of all sentient beings. ^4-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-9]]

If anyone else, even for a single moment,
Creates an obstacle to this bodhisattva's merit,
Since he is left falling short in the welfare of sentient beings,
Their lower realms will be endless. ^4-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-10]]

Even the happiness of a single sentient being,
If destroying it leads to one's own ruin,
Limitless as the entirety of space,
What need to speak of destroying the happiness of embodied beings? ^4-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-11]]

Thus, if powerful downfalls
And the powerful bodhicitta
Alternate in saṃsāra,
It will take a long time to attain the grounds. ^4-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-12]]

Therefore, just as I have vowed,
I must respectfully accomplish it.
If from today I do not apply diligence,
I will go lower and lower. ^4-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-13]]

Benefiting all sentient beings,
Countless Buddhas have passed away, yet
Due to my own faults, I
Have not become an object of their healing. ^4-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-14]]

If I still act in this way,
Again and again it will be the same:
In the lower realms, illness and bondage,
Cutting, cleaving, and so forth, I will experience. ^4-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-15]]

The appearance of a Tathāgata,
Faith, and obtaining a human body,
And such fitness to cultivate virtue—
Since they are rare, when will they be obtained? ^4-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-16]]

A day without illness like this,
With food and free from harm—even so,
Life deceives in an instant,
And the body is like a brief loan. ^4-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-17]]

With conduct such as mine,
I will not attain even a human body.
If a human body is not attained,
There are only misdeeds and no virtue. ^4-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-18]]

Even when I have the fortune to practice virtue,
If I do not practice virtue,
Completely bewildered by the suffering of the lower realms,
What will I be able to do at that time? ^4-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-19]]

If I have not practiced virtues,
And have accumulated misdeeds,
For even a hundred ten-million eons,
I will not hear even the sound of the happy realms. ^4-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-20]]

For that very reason, the Blessed One
Declared that, like a turtle's neck entering the hole
Of a yoke drifting on the great ocean,
The human state is extremely difficult to attain. ^4-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-21]]

If even a misdeed committed in a single instant
Leads to dwelling in the Avīci hell for an eon,
Due to the misdeeds accumulated in beginningless saṃsāra,
What need is there to mention that one will not go to a happy realm? ^4-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-22]]

Having experienced just that much,
One will not attain liberation,
Because while experiencing it in this way,
Other misdeeds are fully generated. ^4-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-23]]

Having found such leisure as this,
If I do not habituate myself to virtue,
There is no other deception than this,
And there is no other bewilderment than this. ^4-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-24]]

If, having understood this,
I am still lazy later out of bewilderment,
At the time of death,
Great sorrow will arise. ^4-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-25]]

If the unbearable fire of hell
Burns my body for a long time,
Due to the blazing fire of unbearable regret,
My mind will doubtless be tormented. ^4-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-26]]

This state of benefit that is extremely difficult to find,
Having found it by some chance,
If, while I myself possess understanding,
I am led back again to that hell, ^4-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-27]]

As if bewildered by a mantra,
I am reduced to having no mind here.
Even I do not know what bewilders me;
What is there inside me? ^4-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-28]]

The enemies, hatred, craving, and so forth,
Have no hands, feet, and so forth,
And are neither brave nor wise; yet how is it
That they have made me like a slave? ^4-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-29]]

While dwelling within my mind,
they do harm to me at their pleasure;
to not be angry even at that and to practice patience
is misplaced patience, an object of reproach. ^4-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-30]]

Even if the gods and asuras
all rose up against me as enemies,
even they would be unable to lead
and cast me into the fire of the Avīci hell. ^4-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-31]]

This powerful enemy, the afflictions,
casts me in a single instant there
where, upon contact, even Mount Meru
does not remain even as ashes. ^4-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-32]]

These enemies of mine, the afflictions,
are long-lasting, as if without beginning or end;
all other enemies are incapable
of lasting for such a long time. ^4-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-33]]

If amiably served and honored,
all others bring benefit and happiness;
but if the afflictions are served,
they bring further suffering and harm. ^4-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-34]]

Thus, if these long-standing, continuous enemies,
the sole cause for the great increase of a multitude of harms,
truly take up residence within my heart,
how could I be fearless and joyful in saṃsāra? ^4-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-35]]

If these guards of the prison of saṃsāra,
Who become executioners in hell and so forth,
Remain within the net of attachment dwelling in the mind,
How could there be happiness for me? ^4-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-36]]

Thus, as long as I do not manifestly
And certainly destroy this enemy, I shall not forsake my effort here.
For instance, angered even by a slight wrongdoer,
Those swollen with pride do not sleep until they have destroyed that enemy. ^4-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-37]]

Those wretched enemies made to suffer by natural death—
If even those who desire to forcefully destroy them when arrayed in battle,
Having disregarded the suffering of being struck by arrows, spears, and weapons,
Do not turn back and flee without accomplishing their goal, ^4-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-38]]

The constant cause of all suffering—
Today, as I strive to destroy these definite natural enemies,
Whatever becomes the cause of hundreds of sufferings,
Needless to say, will not make me despair and lose heart. ^4-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-39]]

If even the scars that enemies inflict for no meaningful purpose
Are worn like ornaments upon the body,
For me, who perfectly strives to accomplish the great purpose,
Why should suffering be a harm? ^4-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-40]]

If fishermen, outcastes, farmers, and so forth,
Thinking only of their own mere livelihood,
Endure the harms of cold, heat, and so forth,
Why should someone like me not endure them for the happiness of wandering beings? ^4-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-41]]

To liberate from afflictions the wandering beings
Who reach to the ends of space in the ten directions,
When I promised this, I myself
Was not freed from afflictions. ^4-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-42]]

Without even knowing my own measure,
How am I not a madman to speak thus?
Therefore, in destroying afflictions,
I shall never turn back. ^4-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-43]]

I shall be attached to this,
And, holding a grudge, engage in battle;
Afflictions of such a kind
Are excepted from what is to be abandoned, since they destroy afflictions. ^4-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-44]]

Even if I am burned and killed,
Or even if my head is severed, so be it;
I shall never, in any circumstance,
Bow down to the enemies, the afflictions. ^4-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-45]]

Even when ordinary enemies are expelled from a country,
They reside in other lands and take possession,
And having gathered strength, return from there;
But the way of the enemies, the afflictions, is not like that. ^4-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-46]]

Afflictions, wretched afflictions, are to be abandoned by the eye of wisdom;
Expelled from my mind, where will they go?
Where will they dwell, to return and harm me?
It is only that I, being weak-minded, lack effort. ^4-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-47]]

Afflictions do not reside in sense objects, nor in the collection of faculties, nor in between.
If not elsewhere either, where do they reside and harm all wandering beings?
They are like an illusion; therefore, abandon fear in the heart and rely on effort in order to know them.
Why do I pointlessly harm myself in hells and so forth? ^4-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-48]]

Having reflected in this way, I shall make effort
To accomplish the training just as it has been explained.
If one does not listen to the physician's words,
How can a patient who needs to be treated by medicines be cured? ^4-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-a]]

The fourth chapter from Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, called Heedfulness. ^4-a

## Chapter Five: Guarding Introspection ^5-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-1]]

Those who wish to guard the training
Must strictly guard the mind;
If this mind is not guarded,
One will be completely unable to guard the training. ^5-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-2]]

Letting the elephant of the mind run loose
Inflicts harm in the Avīci hell;
An untamed, maddened elephant
Does not inflict such harm here. ^5-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-3]]

If, with the rope of mindfulness on all sides,
The elephant of the mind is firmly bound,
All fears will cease to be,
And all virtues will come into one's hands. ^5-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-4]]

Tigers, lions, elephants, bears,
Snakes, and all enemies,
Hell guardians, and
Witches, and likewise rākṣasas— ^5-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-5]]

By binding this mind alone,
All of them will be bound;
By subduing this mind alone,
All of them will be subdued. ^5-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-6]]

Thus, all fears and
Immeasurable sufferings too
Arise from the mind,
As the Speaker of Truth Himself has taught. ^5-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-7]]

The weapons of the hell of sentient beings,
Who intentionally created them?
Who created the burning iron ground?
From what did those groups of women arise? ^5-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-8]]

All such things as these
Were taught by the Sage to arise from the mind of misdeed.
Thus, in the three worlds,
There is no fear other than the mind. ^5-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-9]]

If removing the poverty of wandering beings
Is the perfection of generosity,
Since destitute wandering beings still exist,
How did the past Saviours reach perfection? ^5-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-10]]

All possessions together with their results,
Through the mind of relinquishing them to all people,
The perfection of generosity is taught;
Therefore, it is the mind itself. ^5-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-11]]

Where could fish and so forth
Be banished so that they are not killed?
From attaining the mind of renunciation,
The perfection of ethical discipline is explained. ^5-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-12]]

Unruly sentient beings are like space;
They can never be fully overcome.
If this single mind of anger is overcome,
It is as if all those enemies were overcome. ^5-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-13]]

To cover this surface of the earth with leather,
How could there be enough leather?
With just the leather for the soles of one's shoes,
It is as if the entire surface of the earth were covered. ^5-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-14]]

Likewise, external entities too
Cannot be completely turned back by me;
I shall turn back this mind of mine;
What need is there to turn back others? ^5-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-15]]

Whatever the result of generating a single clear mind,
Such as Brahmā and so forth,
The result, even when accompanied by body and speech,
Because of weak conduct, is not like that. ^5-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-16]]

All recitations and austerities,
Even if practiced for a long time,
If performed with a mind distracted elsewhere,
Are said by the Knower of Reality to be meaningless. ^5-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-17]]

Whoever does not know the supreme principal of the Dharma,
This secret of the mind,
Even though they wish to attain happiness and overcome suffering,
They will wander meaninglessly and in vain. ^5-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-18]]

Therefore, this mind of mine
I shall hold well and properly guard.
Except for the ascetic discipline of guarding the mind,
What is the use of many ascetic disciplines? ^5-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-19]]

When dwelling in the midst of an unruly crowd,
Just as one carefully protects a wound,
So too, when dwelling among bad people,
One should always guard this wound of the mind. ^5-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-20]]

If even one afraid of the minor suffering of a wound
Carefully protects a wound,
Why do those who fear the Crushing Hell and Destroying hell
Not guard the wound of the mind? ^5-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-21]]

If one abides in such conduct,
Whether dwelling among wicked people
Or even dwelling among women,
The steadfast practitioner of restraint will not decline. ^5-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-22]]

Better that my gain and honor
And my body and livelihood perish,
And better that other virtues decline,
But I must never let my mind decline. ^5-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-23]]

To those who wish to guard the mind,
"Both mindfulness and introspection,
Guard them with all your effort!"—saying this,
I thus join my palms together. ^5-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-24]]

People who are disturbed by illness
Are powerless in all activities;
Likewise, the mind disturbed by delusion
Is powerless in all activities. ^5-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-25]]

For one who has a mind lacking introspection,
Even what is heard, pondered, and meditated upon,
Like water in a leaking vase,
Does not remain in mindfulness. ^5-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-26]]

Even many who possess learning and faith,
And who are dedicated to diligence,
By incurring the fault of lacking introspection,
Will become tainted by the stain of downfalls. ^5-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-27]]

Because the thieves of a lack of introspection
Follow upon the decline of mindfulness,
Even when merits have been accumulated,
One goes to the lower realms, as if robbed by thieves. ^5-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-28]]

This band of affliction thieves
Searches for an opportunity;
Having found an opportunity, they steal virtue,
And destroy even the life of a happy realm. ^5-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-29]]

Therefore, one should never let mindfulness go
From the gateway of the mind.
Even if it departs, recalling the harms of the lower realms,
One should closely establish it. ^5-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-30]]

From associating with a teacher,
And the instruction of a preceptor,
For fortunate ones who act with respect out of fear,
Mindfulness is easily generated. ^5-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-31]]

The Buddhas and bodhisattvas
Possess unobstructed vision everywhere.
"In the presence of them all,
I always dwell." ^5-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-32]]

Having thought in this way, with shame,
Respect, and fear, one should bear it in mind.
Therefore, the recollection of the Buddha
Will also arise in one again and again. ^5-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-33]]

When mindfulness at the gateway of the mind
Remains for the sake of guarding,
At that time introspection will come,
And even that which has gone will return. ^5-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-34]]

First, having recognized a mind such as this
To be faulty,
At that time I, like a log of wood,
Should remain steadfast. ^5-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-35]]

Looking around distractedly without purpose
I should never do;
With a resolute mind, I should always
Look with eyes cast down. ^5-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-36]]

For the sake of resting the gaze,
Occasionally I should look in the directions;
If someone appears in my field of vision,
I should look at them and say, "Welcome." ^5-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-37]]

To check for dangers on the path and so forth,
I should look repeatedly in the four directions;
Having rested, turning to look back,
I should look toward the area behind. ^5-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-38]]

Having examined in front and behind,
I should proceed or else return;
Likewise, in all situations,
Having understood what is needed, I should act. ^5-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-39]]

Having set about an action with the thought,
"My body shall remain like this,"
One should then occasionally look,
Asking, "How is this body remaining?" ^5-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-40]]

So that the mad elephant of the mind,
Once tied to the great pillar of reflecting on the Dharma,
Does not escape,
One should thus examine with all effort. ^5-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-41]]

So that striving in concentration
Is not lost for even a single moment,
One should thus individually examine the mind,
Asking, "Where is this mind of mine engaged?" ^5-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-42]]

When involved in dangers, festivities, and the like,
If one is unable, one should act at ease;
For it is taught that at times of generosity,
Ethical discipline is to be treated with equanimity. ^5-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-43]]

Whatever one has intended and begun to do,
One should not think of anything other than that;
With one's thought directed toward it,
That very thing should be accomplished first. ^5-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-44]]

In that way, everything is well done;
Otherwise, neither will be accomplished.
In that way, the secondary affliction
Of lacking introspection will not increase. ^5-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-45]]

Various kinds of idle chatter,
And many sorts of wondrous spectacles—
When engaging in all of them,
One should abandon attachment to them. ^5-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-46]]

If one digs the earth or cuts grass without purpose,
Or draws lines on the ground and so forth,
Having remembered the training of the Sugata,
One should, out of fear, instantly abandon them. ^5-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-47]]

Whenever one desires to move,
Or even when one desires to speak,
Having first examined one's own mind,
With steadfastness, one should act with propriety. ^5-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-48]]

Whenever one's own mind has attachment,
Or desires anger at that time,
One should not act and should not speak;
One should remain like a log of wood. ^5-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-49]]

If there is agitation or mockery,
Or if one possesses pride and arrogance,
And the intention of exposing others' faults,
Or if there is duplicity or a deceptive mind, ^5-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-50]]

Whenever one is intent on self-praise,
Or the very disparagement of others,
Or becomes abusive and full of regret,
At that time, one should remain like a log of wood. ^5-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-51]]

Desiring gain, honor, or fame,
Or desiring to seek attendants and retinue,
Or if my mind desires to be served,
At that time, I should remain like a log of wood. ^5-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-52]]

If a mind arises that forsakes the welfare of others,
Desires to pursue my own welfare,
Or desires to speak,
Then I should remain like a log of wood. ^5-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-53]]

Impatience, laziness, and fear,
And likewise impudence and idle chatter,
Or if a mind attached to my own side arises,
At that time, I should remain like a log of wood. ^5-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-54]]

Having thus examined the mind for complete affliction,
And for its striving for meaningless things,
At that time, the hero, by means of an antidote,
Should firmly hold it. ^5-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-55]]

With utmost certainty and excellent faith,
Steadfast, respectful, and courteous,
With a sense of shame and with apprehension,
Be peaceful and strive to make others happy. ^5-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-56]]

Do not be disheartened by the mutually discordant
Desires of childish ones,
But thinking, "Because afflictions have arisen in them,
This mind of theirs has arisen," be a compassionate one. ^5-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-57]]

Regarding blameless things,
Governed by myself and sentient beings,
Like an emanation, without "I",
I should always hold this mind. ^5-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-58]]

After a long time, the supreme leisure,
Reflecting again and again on having obtained it,
Such a mind, like Mount Meru,
I should hold completely unshakable. ^5-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-59]]

By vultures craving flesh,
Even when the body is dragged back and forth everywhere,
If you, O mind, are not displeased,
Why do you now care for it? ^5-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-60]]

Having grasped this body as "mine,"
O mind, why do you guard it?
If you and this are separate,
What can it do for you? ^5-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-61]]

Mind of delusion, for what reason
Do you not grasp a clean wooden form?
This machine of a collection of impurities,
What is the use of guarding this rotten thing? ^5-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-62]]

First, this layer of skin,
Separate it with your own intellect;
And the flesh from the lattice of bones,
Separate it off with the weapon of wisdom. ^5-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-63]]

Having separated even the bones,
Look down to the marrow,
And examine for yourself,
"What essence is there in this?" ^5-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-64]]

If, having searched there with such effort,
You see no essence,
Why do you still, with attachment,
Guard this body? ^5-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-65]]

Impurity is not fit for you to eat,
Blood, too, is not fit to drink,
And entrails are not fit to suck, so
What will the body do for you? ^5-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-66]]

At best, it is fit to be guarded
As food for foxes and vultures.
Every human body
Is merely to be put to use. ^5-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-67]]

Even if you guard it in this way,
If the merciless Lord of Death
Snatches it and gives it to birds and dogs,
What can you do at that time? ^5-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-68]]

If one does not give clothes and such
Even to a servant who cannot be put to work,
And if this body departs even when nourished,
Why do you exhaust yourself nourishing it? ^5-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-69]]

Having given wages to this,
Now make it serve your own purpose.
Without any benefit, to this
Do not give everything. ^5-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-70]]

As a mere support for going and coming,
Conceive of the body as a boat, and
In order to accomplish the welfare of sentient beings,
Transform it into a wish-fulfilling body. ^5-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-71]]

Thus, assume self-mastery, and
Always maintain a smiling face.
Completely abandon frowning and scowling;
Be a friend to wandering beings and speak sincerely. ^5-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-72]]

Chairs and so forth, recklessly,
Do not throw down with noise.
Also, do not open doors forcefully;
Always delight in acting quietly. ^5-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-73]]

Herons, cats, and thieves,
By going silently and stealthily,
Accomplish their desired goals;
The Sage should always act in that manner. ^5-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-74]]

The words of those skilled in advising others,
And words that act beneficially unasked,
Accept them with respect upon your crown;
Always become the disciple of everyone. ^5-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-75]]

To all that is well spoken,
One should say, "Excellently spoken!"
If one sees someone creating merit,
One should generate joy well by praising them. ^5-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-76]]

One should speak of good qualities in their absence,
And if good qualities are spoken of, join in.
If one's own good qualities are spoken of,
One should understand that as them knowing good qualities. ^5-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-77]]

All undertakings are for the sake of joy,
Which is rare even if bought with a price.
Therefore, through the good qualities achieved by others,
I should enjoy the happiness of joy. ^5-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-78]]

In this life there is no loss for me,
And in the next life there is great happiness.
Through faults, there is unhappiness and suffering,
And in the next life there is great suffering. ^5-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-79]]

When speaking, one's words should be comforting and coherent,
Clear in meaning and pleasing;
Having abandoned attachment and hatred,
One should speak gently and in moderation. ^5-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-80]]

Even when looking at sentient beings with one's eyes,
Thinking, "By relying upon these very beings,
I will become a Buddha,"
One should look in a direct and loving manner. ^5-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-81]]

Whether motivated by constant, intense, fixed aspiration,
Or motivated by an antidote,
Toward the fields of good qualities and benefactors,
And those who are suffering, virtue becomes great. ^5-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-82]]

Having become skilled and endowed with faith,
I shall always perform actions myself.
In all actions, upon anyone at all,
I should not rely. ^5-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-83]]

One should practice the perfection of generosity and the rest
In a progressively more exalted manner.
One should not forsake the greater for the sake of the lesser;
Principally, one should consider the welfare of others. ^5-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-84]]

Having understood this, one should always remain
Diligent in the welfare of others.
Because the Compassionate One sees far ahead,
He permits even prohibited acts for that one. ^5-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-85]]

One should share with those fallen into wretched states, the unprotected,
And those established in ascetic discipline;
One should eat only a moderate amount,
And give away everything except the three Dharma robes. ^5-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-86]]

This body, which practices the sublime Dharma,
Should not be harmed for the sake of something trivial.
If one acts in this way, the wishes
Of sentient beings will be swiftly fulfilled. ^5-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-87]]

While the intention of compassion is impure,
One should not give away this body;
Rather, only when in this and other lives
It becomes a cause for accomplishing a great purpose, should it be given. ^5-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-88]]

One should not teach the Dharma to the disrespectful,
Nor to those who wear head-wraps while not ill,
Nor to those holding parasols, staffs, or weapons,
Nor to those whose heads are covered. ^5-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-89]]

Do not teach the profound and vast to those of lesser capacity,
Nor to a woman without a man present;
Toward the lesser and supreme Dharmas,
One should always act with equal respect. ^5-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-90]]

Those who have become vessels for the vast Dharma
Should not be engaged in the lesser Dharma;
Conduct should not be completely forsaken,
Nor should they be deceived with sūtras and mantras. ^5-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-91]]

Tooth-sticks and saliva,
When discarded, should be covered;
Also, discharging urine and the like
Into usable water or land is deprecated. ^5-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-92]]

With a full mouth, making noise,
Or with the mouth wide open, one should not eat;
One should not sit with legs outstretched,
Nor rub the hands together. ^5-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-93]]

In vehicles, on beds, seats, and in dwellings,
One should not be alone with another woman.
Having observed and inquired, one should avoid
Everything that causes the world to lose faith. ^5-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-94]]

One should not indicate with a finger;
But respectfully, with the right
Hand in its entirety,
Even the path should be shown in this way. ^5-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-95]]

One should not move the hands excessively;
Moving them slightly, one should make a sound
And signal by snapping the fingers and so forth;
Otherwise, one will lack restraint. ^5-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-96]]

Just as the Protector lay when passing into nirvāṇa,
One should lie down in the desired direction;
With introspection, to the thought of rising quickly,
One should firmly direct the mind from the very beginning. ^5-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-97]]

Although the conduct of bodhisattvas
Is taught to be measureless,
One should certainly practice
The conduct of purifying the mind until one is able to practice it all. ^5-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-98]]

Three times by day and three times by night,
One should recite the Three Heaps Sūtra;
By relying on the Victors and bodhicitta,
One should thereby pacify the remaining downfalls. ^5-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-99]]

Whether under one's own or another's power,
In whatever situation, whatever one is doing,
Whatever has been taught as a training,
In that situation, one should exert oneself and train. ^5-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-100]]

That which is not a training for the children of the Victors
Does not exist at all;
For the wise who abide in this way,
There is nothing that does not become merit. ^5-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-101]]

Whether directly or indirectly,
Do nothing other than the benefit of sentient beings;
For the sake of sentient beings alone,
Make a dedication of everything for awakening. ^5-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-102]]

Always, a spiritual friend
Who is learned in the meaning of the Mahāyāna
And who keeps the supreme ascetic discipline of the bodhisattvas,
Should not be given up even for the sake of one's life. ^5-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-103]]

From the liberation story of Śrīsaṃbhava,
One should train in the manner of relying on the teacher;
This and other words spoken by the Buddha
Should be known by reading the sūtras. ^5-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-104]]

The trainings appear in the sūtras,
Therefore, one should read the sūtras;
The Ākāśagarbha Sūtra
Should be looked at first of all. ^5-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-105]]

Because what must be constantly practiced
Is extensively demonstrated within it,
The Śikṣāsamuccaya too
Should definitely be examined again and again. ^5-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-106]]

Alternatively, for the time being, the concise
Compendium of Sūtras should be examined;
And those composed by the noble Nāgārjuna,
Both of them, should also be examined with effort. ^5-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-107]]

Whatever is not prohibited therein,
That itself is what should be practiced;
In order to guard the minds of the world,
Having seen the training, one should practice perfectly. ^5-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-108]]

Regarding the states of body and mind,
Examining them again and again—
That alone, in brief,
Is the definition of guarding introspection. ^5-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-109]]

These should be practiced with the body;
What will be accomplished by merely uttering words?
By merely reading a medical treatise,
Will the sick be benefited? ^5-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the fifth chapter: Guarding Introspection. ^5-a

## Chapter Six: Teaching Patience ^6-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-1]]

Accumulated over a thousand eons,
Generosity, offerings to the Sugatas, and so forth—
Whatever good conduct there is, all of that
A single instance of anger destroys. ^6-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-2]]

There is no misdeed like hatred;
There is no austerity like patience.
Therefore, earnestly regarding patience,
One should practice meditation in various ways. ^6-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-3]]

If one harbors a mind with the dart of hatred,
The mind does not experience peace,
One obtains neither joy nor happiness,
Sleep does not come, and one becomes unsteady. ^6-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-4]]

Those who, through wealth and honor,
Have been favored and depend upon him,
Even they will attack to kill
That master possessed by hatred. ^6-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-5]]

Because of it, friends become disheartened;
Though gathered by generosity, they do not attend him.
In brief, someone with anger who dwells in happiness—
There is no such person at all. ^6-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-6]]

The enemy, anger, produces
These and other such sufferings.
Whoever strives to destroy anger
Is happy in this life and the next. ^6-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-7]]

Arising from the doing of what is undesired
And the hindering of what is desired—
Having found the food of mental unhappiness,
Hatred flourishes and destroys me. ^6-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-8]]

Therefore, I shall completely destroy
The food of that enemy;
For, other than harming me,
This enemy has no other task. ^6-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-9]]

Whatever happens, I
Shall not disturb my joyful mind;
Even if I am unhappy, what is desired is not accomplished,
And virtues will decline. ^6-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-10]]

If it can be remedied,
Why be unhappy about it?
If it cannot be remedied,
What is the use of being unhappy about it? ^6-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-11]]

For myself or for my friends,
Suffering, contempt, harsh words,
And disrepute are not desired,
Because for an enemy it is the reverse. ^6-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-12]]

The causes of happiness occur occasionally;
The causes of suffering are extremely numerous.
Without suffering, there is no renunciation;
Therefore, O mind, resolve to be steadfast. ^6-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-13]]

Devotees of Durgā and the Karṇapas
Endure the feeling of burning, cutting, and so forth
Pointlessly; for the sake of liberation,
Why then am I cowardly? ^6-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-14]]

That which does not become easy through habituation—
There is no such thing whatsoever.
Therefore, by habituating to minor harms,
Endure great harms. ^6-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-15]]

Snakes and gadflies,
Feelings of hunger, thirst, and so forth,
And afflictions such as rashes—
Why do you not see their pointless suffering? ^6-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-16]]

Towards heat, cold, rain, wind, and so forth,
Sickness, bondage, beatings, and so forth,
I shall not be overly sensitive;
If I am, the harm increases. ^6-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-17]]

Some, when they see their own blood,
Become exceptionally brave and steadfast;
Some, when they see the blood of others,
Become dazed and faint. ^6-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-18]]

This comes from the steadfastness of the mind
And from the nature of a coward.
Therefore, disregard harm
And make yourself unaffected by sufferings. ^6-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-19]]

Even when suffering arises, the wise
Should not cloud the clarity of the mind,
For in waging war against the afflictions,
Harms are many during the battle. ^6-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-20]]

Having disregarded all suffering,
Those who conquer enemies such as hatred—
They are the victorious heroes;
The rest slay corpses. ^6-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-21]]

Furthermore, the good quality of suffering is:
Through disillusionment, haughtiness is dispelled,
Compassion arises for those in saṃsāra,
One shuns misdeeds and delights in virtue. ^6-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-22]]

Without being angry at bile and the like,
Which are great sources of suffering,
Why be angry at beings with a mind?
They too are all incited by conditions. ^6-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-23]]

For example, even without wishing it,
Just as this sickness arises,
Likewise, even without wishing it,
The afflictions forcefully arise. ^6-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-24]]

Even without thinking, "I shall become angry,"
People become angry at the slightest condition;
Even without thinking, "I shall produce it,"
Anger likewise arises. ^6-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-25]]

All faults, however many there are,
And various kinds of misdeeds,
All these arise through the power of conditions;
They do not possess independence. ^6-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-26]]

Even those assembled conditions
Have no intention, "I shall produce,"
And that which is produced by them
Has no intention, "I am produced." ^6-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-27]]

Whatever is asserted as "Pradhāna,"
And whatever is imputed as the "self,"
Do not arise by purposefully thinking,
"I shall come into being." ^6-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-28]]

If it does not exist when unarisen,
At that time, what wishes to arise?
Since it would constantly be distracted by sense objects,
It would also never cease. ^6-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-29]]

If that self is permanent,
It is evidently inactive, like space.
Even if it encounters other conditions,
What can be done to the unchanging? ^6-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-30]]

If, even at the time of acting, it remains as before,
What has the action done to it?
What connection could there be
By which one could say, "This is its action"? ^6-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-31]]

Thus, everything is under the power of others,
And through that power, it is powerless.
Knowing this, one will not become angry
At any entities, which are like emanations. ^6-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-32]]

If you object, "By what is what to be prevented?
Even preventing is unreasonable!"
In dependence upon that, it is not unreasonable
To wish for sufferings to be severed. ^6-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-33]]

Therefore, whether it is an enemy or a friend,
If you see them doing what is improper,
Thinking, "This has arisen from such conditions,"
Reflect in this way and remain at ease. ^6-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-34]]

If things were accomplished by their own will,
Since no one desires suffering,
For all embodied beings,
Suffering would not arise for anyone. ^6-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-35]]

Through heedlessness, they even harm themselves
With thorns and so forth;
In order to obtain women and the like,
They become obsessed and engage in fasting and so forth. ^6-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-36]]

Some hang themselves or leap from cliffs,
Eat poison and unwholesome food,
And through non-meritorious conduct,
Bring harm upon themselves. ^6-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-37]]

When, under the power of afflictions,
They even kill their beloved selves,
At that time, how could they not cause harm
To the bodies of others? ^6-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-38]]

For those who, through the arising of afflictions,
Engage in such acts as killing themselves,
If compassion does not even arise,
What sense is there in anger? ^6-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-39]]

If causing harm to others
Is the nature of childish ones,
It is inappropriate to feel anger toward them,
Like resenting fire for its nature to burn. ^6-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-40]]

Alternatively, if this fault is adventitious,
And the nature of sentient beings is gentle,
Even then it is inappropriate to feel anger,
Like resenting the sky when smoke rises within it. ^6-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-41]]

Having employed sticks and the like as the actual instruments,
if one feels anger at the one who throws them,
since he too is driven by hatred,
then, in the second place, it is fitting to feel anger at hatred. ^6-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-42]]

In the past, to sentient beings,
I inflicted harm such as this;
Therefore, upon me, a harmer of sentient beings,
It is fitting that this harm should arise. ^6-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-43]]

His weapon and my body,
both are causes of suffering;
since he has drawn the weapon and I have taken up the body,
at whom should I direct my anger? ^6-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-44]]

Like a human-shaped abscess,
Full of suffering, unable to bear being touched,
Since I, blinded by craving, have grasped it,
When it is harmed, at whom should I direct my anger? ^6-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-45]]

Childish ones do not desire suffering,
Yet they crave the causes of suffering;
When harmed by their own faults,
Why should they resent others? ^6-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-46]]

For example, like hell guardians,
And the forest of sword-leaves,
Since this is produced by my own karma,
At whom should I direct my anger? ^6-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-47]]

Impelled by my own karma,
Those who harm me appear;
If because of this those beings go to hell,
Have I not ruined them? ^6-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-48]]

By relying upon them,
Through patience my many misdeeds are purified;
But by relying upon me, they
Go to the hells of long-lasting suffering. ^6-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-49]]

I am the one who harms them,
While they are the ones who benefit me;
Why, out of perversity,
O wicked mind, do you give rise to anger? ^6-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-50]]

If I possess the intention's
Good quality, I will not go to hell;
If I protect myself,
What will happen to them here? ^6-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-51]]

But if I cause harm in return,
They will not be protected,
And my conduct will also decline;
Thus my austerity will be destroyed. ^6-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-52]]

Because the mind is not an embodied being,
No one can destroy it anywhere;
Due to fixation on the body,
The body is harmed by sufferings. ^6-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-53]]

Contempt, speaking harsh speech,
And those unpleasant words—
Since they do not harm the body,
O mind, why do you react with intense anger? ^6-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-54]]

If others' displeasure with me
Will not consume me
In this life or some other life,
Why am I averse to it? ^6-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-55]]

If I am averse to this
Because it creates an obstacle to gain,
My gain will be discarded here,
But my misdeeds will remain steadfast. ^6-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-56]]

It is better that I die this very day
Than to live a long time through wrong livelihood;
Even if I were to remain for a long time,
The suffering of death is exactly the same. ^6-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-57]]

Whoever awakens
After experiencing happiness in a dream for a hundred years,
And another who awakens
After experiencing happiness for just an instant— ^6-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-58]]

For both of those who awaken,
That happiness does not return;
For both a long life and a short life,
At the time of death, it is exactly the same. ^6-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-59]]

Even if I acquire abundant gain,
And enjoy happiness for a long time,
Just like someone robbed by a thief,
I will depart naked and empty-handed. ^6-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-60]]

If I say, "By surviving on gain,
I will exhaust misdeeds and create merit,"
If I feel anger for the sake of gain,
Will merit not be exhausted and misdeeds arise? ^6-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-61]]

The very purpose for which I live—
If it is itself destroyed,
What is the use of that life
That commits only misdeeds? ^6-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-62]]

If you say, "Because it ruins sentient beings,
I feel anger at those who speak unpleasantly,"
Even regarding those who speak unpleasantly about others,
Why do you not likewise feel anger? ^6-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-63]]

Since a lack of faith depends on others,
If you have patience for those without faith,
Since it depends on the arising of afflictions,
Why do you not have patience for those who speak unpleasantly? ^6-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-64]]

Toward images, stūpas, and the sublime Dharma,
Even for those who disparage and destroy them,
It is inappropriate for me to feel hatred,
For the Buddhas and so forth suffer no harm. ^6-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-65]]

Toward those who harm teachers, relatives, and so forth,
And even those who harm friends,
In the previous manner, from conditions,
Seeing that this arises, I should turn back anger. ^6-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-66]]

If both beings with a mind
and the insentient cause harm to embodied beings,
why single out beings with a mind to blame?
Therefore, practice patience toward harm. ^6-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-67]]

Some commit misdeeds out of delusion;
If others, out of delusion, react with anger,
Who among them is faultless,
And who is the one at fault? ^6-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-68]]

That karma by which others cause harm,
Why did I previously commit it?
If everything depends upon karma,
Why should I harbor resentment toward this? ^6-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-69]]

Having seen this, by all means,
So that all will have a loving mind for one another,
So that this may come to be, I
Shall exert myself in merit. ^6-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-70]]

For example, when the fire from a burning house
Moves to another house,
Whatever straw and such it catches on,
That is pulled out and discarded. ^6-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-71]]

Likewise, whatever the mind has attachment to,
In which the fire of hatred has spread,
Out of fear that merit will be burned,
That must be discarded instantly. ^6-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-72]]

If a person to be killed has a hand cut off
And is freed, why is it not good?
If through human suffering
One is freed from hell, why is it not good? ^6-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-73]]

If even this much present suffering
Cannot be endured by me,
Then the cause of suffering in hell,
Anger, why do I not avert it? ^6-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-74]]

For the sake of sensual desire, burning and so forth
I have experienced in hell thousands of times;
Yet I have accomplished neither my own welfare
Nor the welfare of others. ^6-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-75]]

This is not so harmful,
And great purposes will also be accomplished;
In the suffering that dispels the harm of wandering beings,
It is appropriate here only to rejoice. ^6-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-76]]

If others, by praising those possessing good qualities,
Attain the happiness of joy,
O mind, you too having praised them,
Why do you not rejoice in that way? ^6-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-77]]

This happiness of your rejoicing
Is a source of happiness without blame;
It is permitted by ones endowed with good qualities,
And is also the supreme way of gathering others. ^6-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-78]]

Moreover, thinking, "In that way, the other will be happy,"
If you do not desire this happiness,
Through forsaking the giving of wages and the like,
The seen and unseen will be ruined. ^6-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-79]]

When your own good qualities are spoken of,
You want others to have happiness too;
When the good qualities of others are spoken of,
You do not want happiness even for yourself. ^6-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-80]]

Desiring happiness for all sentient beings,
Having undertaken the generation of bodhicitta for awakening,
If sentient beings find happiness themselves,
Why do you become angry at that? ^6-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-81]]

Awakening as a Buddha worshipped by the three worlds,
If you desire this for sentient beings,
Upon seeing them receive mere meager honor and respect,
Why do you become tormented by that? ^6-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-82]]

Whoever is a dependent you nurture,
To whom you yourself must give,
If those relatives find a livelihood,
Instead of being glad, do you become angry in return? ^6-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-83]]

If you do not desire this for wandering beings,
Who is it that desires awakening for them?
In whoever becomes angry at another's prosperity,
Where is bodhicitta? ^6-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-84]]

Whether he receives it from him,
Or it remains in the benefactor's house,
Since in any case you do not have it,
What does it matter whether it is given or not? ^6-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-85]]

Merit and faith,
Or your own good qualities, why do you cast them away?
Not taking hold of the gains that occur,
Tell me, why do you not become angry? ^6-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-86]]

For the misdeeds you yourself have committed,
Not only are you without sorrow,
But with others who have performed merit,
Do you wish to compete?

The Second Fascicle. ^6-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-87]]

Even if an enemy is unhappy,
What is there for you to rejoice in?
Your mere wishing
Will not become a cause of harm to him. ^6-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-88]]

Even if, through your desire, that suffering
Is accomplished, what is there for you to rejoice in?
If you say, "I will be satisfied,"
What is more ruinous than that? ^6-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-89]]

Cast by the fisherman of the afflictions,
This hook is an unbearable barb;
Caught by it, in the cauldrons of hell,
The hell guardians will certainly boil me. ^6-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-90]]

The honors of praise and fame
Do not become merit, nor do they become long life;
They do not become my strength, nor are they health;
And they do not even make the body comfortable. ^6-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-91]]

If I were to know my own welfare,
What welfare of mine is there in that?
If I desire only mental happiness,
I must also rely on gambling, alcohol, and the like. ^6-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-92]]

If, for the sake of fame, I squander my wealth,
And even get myself killed,
What is the use of mere syllables?
Once dead, who will be made happy by them? ^6-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-93]]

When a sandcastle collapses,
Children weep in despair;
Likewise, when praise and fame decline,
My own mind is like a child's. ^6-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-94]]

First of all, since sound is mindless,
It cannot possibly have the intention to praise me.
As for the fact that it is said, "Others are pleased with me,"
Perhaps that is the cause of joy? ^6-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-95]]

Whether it is directed at another or even at myself,
How does the joy of another benefit me?
That joy and happiness belong to them alone;
I do not obtain a share of it. ^6-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-96]]

If I am happy through their happiness,
Then I must do the same toward everyone.
How is it that when they become happy
Through rejoicing in others, I am not happy? ^6-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-97]]

Therefore, the joy that arises in me
Thinking, "I am praised!"—
Since that too is thus unjustified,
It is nothing but the behavior of a child. ^6-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-98]]

Praise and so forth distract me,
And they destroy my disillusionment too;
They cause envy toward those with good qualities,
And they also destroy excellence. ^6-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-99]]

Therefore, those who are present
In order to destroy my praise and so forth—
Are they not engaged in protecting me
From falling into the lower realms? ^6-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-100]]

Since I seek release,
I do not need the bonds of gain and honor.
Those who release me from being bound—
How could I be angry with them? ^6-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-101]]

When I wish to enter into suffering,
Like a blessing of the Buddha,
They become the very door that does not let me enter.
How could I be angry with them? ^6-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-102]]

Thinking, "This is an obstacle to merit,"
It is not right to be angry even at them.
If there is no austerity equal to patience,
Shall I not abide in it? ^6-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-103]]

If, due to my own faults,
I do not practice patience toward this,
When the cause of merit is present,
I am merely creating an obstacle to it. ^6-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-104]]

If something does not occur without another,
And comes to be when it is present,
If that very thing is its cause,
How can it be called an obstacle? ^6-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-105]]

By a beggar who arrives at the right time,
No obstacle to generosity is made;
One who grants ordination
Cannot be called an obstacle to ordination. ^6-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-106]]

In the world, beggars are plentiful,
But those who cause harm are rare;
For if I do not cause harm to others,
No one will cause harm to me. ^6-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-107]]

Therefore, like a treasure appearing in the house,
Not acquired through wearisome toil,
Since he becomes a helper in the conduct of awakening,
I should delight in my enemy. ^6-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-108]]

Since it is accomplished by them and myself,
Therefore, the result of patience
Should first be offered to them,
For they are thus the cause of patience. ^6-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-109]]

If you say that because he lacks the intention to accomplish patience,
This enemy is not to be venerated,
Why do you venerate even the sublime Dharma,
Which is a fit cause for accomplishment? ^6-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-110]]

If you say that because this enemy has the intention
To harm, they are not to be venerated,
If they strove to benefit me like a physician,
How could my patience be accomplished? ^6-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-111]]

Therefore, since patience arises
In dependence on a very hateful mind,
Since they are the very cause of patience,
They are worthy of veneration like the sublime Dharma. ^6-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-112]]

Therefore, the field of sentient beings and
The field of the Victors, the Sage has proclaimed.
By pleasing them, many
Have thus reached the perfection of excellence. ^6-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-113]]

From sentient beings and the Victors,
The accomplishment of the qualities of a Buddha is alike;
Just as one respects the Victors,
Why not do so for sentient beings? ^6-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-114]]

It is not by the good quality of intention itself,
But it is from the result that they are similar.
Sentient beings too possess good qualities,
And therefore they are equal. ^6-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-115]]

Whatever offering is made to one who possesses a loving mind,
That is the very greatness of sentient beings.
Whatever merit there is from faith in the Buddhas,
That too is the very greatness of the Buddhas. ^6-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-116]]

Because they have a share in accomplishing the qualities of a Buddha,
They are therefore asserted to be equal.
Yet to those with an infinite ocean of good qualities,
To the Buddhas, no one is equal. ^6-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-117]]

Of the unique collection of supreme good qualities,
If merely a fraction of those good qualities
Appears in someone, in order to make offerings to them,
Even offering the three realms would be too little. ^6-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-118]]

Since a fraction of the arising of the supreme qualities of a Buddha
Is present in sentient beings,
In accordance with just this fraction,
It is fitting that sentient beings be objects of offering. ^6-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-119]]

Furthermore, to those who become friends without deceit
And who perform immeasurable benefit,
Other than pleasing sentient beings,
By what else could they be repaid? ^6-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-120]]

For those for whose sake they give up their bodies and enter the Avīci hell,
Benefiting them becomes a repayment;
Therefore, even if these beings cause great harm,
I should act toward all of them with complete goodness. ^6-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-121]]

Even my own lords
Do not regard even their own bodies for their sake;
How could a fool like me
Act with pride toward them and not act as a servant? ^6-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-122]]

Those by whose happiness the Sages are pleased,
And by harming whom displeasure arises;
By bringing joy to them, all the Sages are pleased,
And by harming them, harm is done to the Sages. ^6-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-123]]

Just as when one's body is completely ablaze,
No desirable things can make the mind happy,
Likewise, when harm is done to sentient beings,
There is no way for those with great compassion to be pleased. ^6-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-124]]

Therefore, whatever harm I have done to wandering beings,
Which has caused all those with great compassion to be displeased,
I individually confess that misdeed today;
I pray the Sages forgive whatever displeasure I have caused. ^6-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-125]]

In order to please the Tathāgatas,
From today onward I shall be a servant to the world.
Even if many wandering beings strike my head with their feet,
Or kill me, I will not retaliate; may the Protectors of the World be pleased! ^6-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-126]]

Those possessing the nature of compassion
Have made all these wandering beings into themselves; of this there is no doubt.
These very ones seen in the form of sentient beings
Are those Protectors themselves; why do I not respect them? ^6-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-127]]

This alone is what pleases the Tathāgatas.
This alone is also the perfect accomplishment of one's own welfare.
This alone is also what dispels the suffering of the world.
Therefore, I shall always practice this alone. ^6-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-128]]

For example, even if one of a king's men
Should cause harm to many people,
Farsighted people,
Even if they are capable, do not harm him in return. ^6-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-129]]

Because he is not alone;
The might of the king is his might.
Likewise, a weak harm-doer
Should not be despised at all. ^6-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-130]]

Because the hell guardians
And the compassionate ones are his forces.
Therefore, just as subjects please a fierce king,
I should please sentient beings. ^6-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-131]]

Even if a king were to become angry,
Whatever will be experienced
By displeasing sentient beings—
Could he inflict that harm of hell? ^6-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-132]]

Even if a king were to be pleased,
He could not possibly bestow Buddhahood itself,
Which is what is attained
By pleasing sentient beings. ^6-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-133]]

Let alone the future accomplishment of a Buddha
Arising from pleasing sentient beings,
Why do you not see that in this very life
Great glory, fame, and happiness will come? ^6-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-134]]

While in saṃsāra, through patience, one attains beauty and the like,
Health, and renown,
An extremely long life,
And the vast happiness of a universal monarch. ^6-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, this is the sixth chapter, entitled "Patience." ^6-a

## Chapter Seven: Teaching Diligence ^7-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-1]]

Thus, having patience, one should initiate diligence,
For awakening abides in such striving.
Just as there is no movement without wind,
Merit does not arise without diligence. ^7-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-2]]

What is diligence? It is delight in virtue.
Its opposing factors shall be explained:
Laziness, attachment to what is base, and
Despondency, which is self-contempt. ^7-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-3]]

Through tasting the pleasure of idleness, and
Through the craving that relies on sleep,
From feeling no weariness toward the suffering of saṃsāra,
Laziness comes to arise. ^7-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-4]]

Having been snared by the fowler of the afflictions,
Having been caught in the net of birth,
And having gone into the mouth of the Lord of Death,
How is it that you still do not know? ^7-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-5]]

That your own group is being killed one by one,
Have you not seen this?
Yet you who rely on sleep
Are like a buffalo beside its outcaste butcher. ^7-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-6]]

With the paths completely blocked,
And the Lord of Death watching you,
How can you take joy in eating,
And how can you take joy in sleeping like this? ^7-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-7]]

Because you will die very quickly,
You should gather the accumulations until then.
Even if you abandon laziness at that time,
What can you do when it is not the right time? ^7-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-8]]

"This is not done, this is just begun,
And this remains only half-done"—
When the Lord of Death suddenly arrives,
I will think, "Alas, I am destroyed!" ^7-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-9]]

With eyes red and swollen by the force of grief,
Tears streaming down their faces,
My relatives having lost all hope,
I will gaze upon the faces of the messengers of Yama. ^7-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-10]]

Tormented by the memory of my own misdeeds,
And hearing the sounds of hell,
I will soil my body with filth in terror;
When I become delirious, what will I do? ^7-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-11]]

If you, like a live fish writhing about,
Are possessed by fear in this life,
Having committed misdeeds, what need is there to mention
The unbearable sufferings of hell? ^7-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-12]]

When touched by hot water,
You of tender flesh are intensely scorched;
Having created the karma of hell,
Why do you remain at ease like this? ^7-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-13]]

Desiring the result without exertion,
Being fragile and subject to many harms,
Seized by death, yet acting like a god—
Alas, you are destroyed by sufferings! ^7-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-14]]

Relying on the boat of a human body,
Cross over the great river of suffering!
Since this boat will be hard to find later,
O fool, do not sleep at this time! ^7-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-15]]

Having abandoned the supreme joy of the sublime Dharma,
Which is a boundless cause of joy,
Why do you delight in distraction by the causes of suffering,
And in agitation and the like? ^7-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-16]]

Without despondency, with a host of forces,
With earnest application and self-mastery,
Practice the equality of self and others,
And the exchange of self and others! ^7-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-17]]

Thinking, "How could I attain awakening?"
Do not fall into despondency;
For in this way the Tathāgata,
The Speaker of Truth, has spoken this truth: ^7-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-18]]

Flies, gadflies, and bees,
And likewise whatever has become a worm—
Even they, if they generate the power of diligence,
Will attain unsurpassable awakening, which is hard to attain. ^7-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-19]]

Since someone like me has been born as a human by birth,
And recognizes what is beneficial and harmful,
If I do not abandon the conduct of awakening,
Why should I not attain awakening? ^7-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-20]]

"But my arms, legs, and so forth,
Must be given away, so I am afraid," if I think—
Without analyzing what is heavy and what is light,
I am merely afraid due to delusion. ^7-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-21]]

For countless tens of millions of eons,
I will be cut many times over,
Pierced, burned, and split apart,
Yet I will not attain awakening. ^7-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-22]]

The suffering of my accomplishing awakening
Has a limit;
It is just like the suffering of cutting open the body
In order to remove the harm of a splinter destroying it from within. ^7-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-23]]

Even all physicians, through medical treatments,
Cure illness by means of discomfort;
Therefore, to destroy many sufferings,
I should endure a minor discomfort. ^7-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-24]]

Such ordinary medical treatments
The Supreme Physician does not perform;
Through an extremely gentle method,
He cures immeasurable great diseases. ^7-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-25]]

Even in generosity with vegetables and so forth,
The Guide initially engages one;
Having become accustomed to that, later on,
One gradually gives away even one's own flesh. ^7-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-26]]

When, regarding one's own body,
An attitude arises that it is like vegetables and so forth,
At that time, in giving away flesh and so forth,
What difficulty is there in that? ^7-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-27]]

Because of having abandoned misdeeds, there is no suffering;
Because of being a wise person, there is no unhappiness.
In this way, wrong conceptions
And misdeeds harm the mind and body. ^7-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-28]]

If the body is at ease through merit,
And the mind is at ease through being a wise person,
Even while remaining in saṃsāra for the welfare of others,
Why would the compassionate ones become weary? ^7-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-29]]

Because this bodhisattva, through the power of bodhicitta,
Exhausts previous misdeeds
And gathers an ocean of merit,
He is explained to be superior to the Śrāvakas. ^7-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-30]]

Therefore, having mounted the horse of bodhicitta,
Which dispels all weariness and fatigue,
While proceeding from happiness to happiness,
What intelligent person would fall into despondency? ^7-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-31]]

To accomplish the welfare of sentient beings, the forces
Are aspiration, steadfastness, joy, and relinquishment.
Aspiration is generated through the fear of suffering
And by contemplating the benefits of that aspiration. ^7-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-32]]

Thus, having abandoned the opposing factors,
Through aspiration, pride, joy, and relinquishment,
And the powers of earnest application and mastery,
I should strive to increase diligence. ^7-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-33]]

The faults of myself and others,
Which are immeasurable, I must destroy;
Wherein for each and every fault
An ocean of eons would be exhausted. ^7-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-34]]

If of the effort to exhaust those faults,
I do not see even a fraction in myself,
Being an abode of immeasurable suffering,
Why does my heart not burst? ^7-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-35]]

The good qualities of myself and others,
Which are many, I must accomplish;
Wherein if for each and every good quality
I must familiarize myself for an ocean of eons, ^7-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-36]]

I, for even a fraction of a good quality,
Have never generated familiarization;
Having somehow found this birth,
It is astonishing that I have made it meaningless. ^7-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-37]]

I have not made offerings to the Blessed One,
Nor provided the happiness of great festivals;
I have not performed service for the teaching,
Nor fulfilled the wishes of the poor. ^7-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-38]]

If I have not given fearlessness to the frightened,
And have not given happiness to the distressed,
I have merely produced the pangs
And suffering of my mother's womb. ^7-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-39]]

Because I have been devoid of aspiration for the Dharma,
Previously and even now,
Such destitution has come about;
Who would forsake aspiration for the Dharma? ^7-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-40]]

The Sage has stated that aspiration
Is the root of all virtuous factors,
And its root constantly is
Meditating on the ripened result. ^7-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-41]]

Suffering and mental unhappiness,
Various kinds of fears,
And separation from what is desired
Will arise from engaging in misdeeds. ^7-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-42]]

By performing virtue intended in the mind,
Wherever one may go,
There, through that merit,
One will be manifestly honored with the offerings of its results. ^7-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-43]]

Although those who commit misdeeds desire happiness,
Wherever they may go,
There, through those misdeeds,
They are completely destroyed by the weapons of suffering. ^7-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-44]]

Dwelling in the core of a vast, fragrant, and cool lotus,
With splendor nourished by the food of the Victor's sweet speech,
Possessing a supreme body born from a lotus blossomed by the Sage's light,
Through virtue one becomes a son of the Sugata dwelling before the Victor. ^7-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-45]]

Utterly tormented by Yama's henchmen completely flaying one's skin,
Molten copper melted by intensely hot fire poured upon one's body,
Pierced by blazing swords and short spears, one's flesh scattered into a hundred pieces,
Through many non-virtues one falls onto the intensely blazing iron ground. ^7-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-46]]

Therefore, one should aspire to virtue,
And cultivate it with reverence.
By the method of the Vajradhvaja,
Having begun, one should cultivate pride. ^7-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-47]]

First, having examined one's resources,
One should either begin or not begin.
It is best not to begin at all,
But having begun, one should not turn back. ^7-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-48]]

Habituated to that in another life,
Misdeeds and suffering will increase.
Another deed, and the time of the result,
Will become inferior, and that other deed too will not be accomplished. ^7-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-49]]

Karma, afflictions, and capacity:
One should cultivate pride in these three.
Thinking, "I shall do it alone,"
This is pride in action. ^7-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-50]]

Since this world, powerless through afflictions,
Is unable to accomplish its own welfare,
Wandering beings are not capable like me;
Therefore I shall do this. ^7-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-51]]

If others perform inferior actions,
How can I myself remain idle?
I do not do this out of pride;
For me, to be without pride is supreme. ^7-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-52]]

When encountering a dead snake,
Even a crow acts like a garuḍa.
If I myself am weak,
Even a minor downfall will cause harm. ^7-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-53]]

For one who is despondent and abandons effort,
Is there liberation from destitution?
By generating pride and effort,
One is hard to overcome even by great downfalls. ^7-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-54]]

Therefore, with a steadfast mind,
Downfalls must be conquered;
If I am defeated by a downfall,
My desire to conquer the three realms becomes a laughingstock. ^7-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-55]]

I shall conquer all,
And no one shall conquer me.
I, a child of the Victor Lion,
Shall abide in this pride. ^7-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-56]]

Any being who is overcome by pride
Is afflicted, not one who possesses pride.
One who possesses pride does not fall under the power of the enemy;
They fall under the power of the enemy that is pride. ^7-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-57]]

Those inflated by the affliction of pride
Are led by pride to the lower realms;
Their human joy is destroyed,
And they become slaves who eat the food of others, ^7-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-58]]

Foolish, ugly, and weak,
They are despised everywhere.
If ascetics inflated with pride
Are also included among the proud,
Tell me, what are the lowly like? ^7-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-59]]

Whoever holds pride in order to conquer the enemy that is pride
Is the proud one and the victorious hero.
Whoever definitively destroys even the thriving enemy that is pride
Perfects the result of victory for wandering beings as they wish. ^7-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-60]]

If I dwell in the midst of the faction of afflictions,
I shall endure in a thousand ways;
Like a lion among foxes and the like,
I shall not be harmed by the host of afflictions. ^7-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-61]]

Even if great peril arises,
Just as a person protects their eyes,
Likewise, even if peril arises,
I shall not fall under the power of the afflictions. ^7-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-62]]

Just as one desires the happiness that is the result of play,
Whatever action is to be done by this one,
One should be attached to that action,
And, unsated by that action, find joy in it. ^7-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-63]]

Even if one acts for the sake of happiness,
It is uncertain whether or not happiness will result;
But for one whose very action becomes happiness,
How can there be happiness without doing that action? ^7-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-64]]

If one is not satiated by sensual desires,
Which are like honey smeared on a razor's edge,
By merit, whose ripening is happiness and which is peaceful,
How could one ever be satiated? ^7-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-65]]

Therefore, in order to complete the action,
Tormented by the midday sun,
Just as an elephant encountering a lake plunges into it,
One should also engage in that action. ^7-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-66]]

If it entails a decline in strength,
One should set it aside in order to resume it later;
When it is well completed, desiring the next
And the next, one should leave it behind. ^7-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-67]]

Just as a veteran warrior, together with enemies,
Clashes swords on the battlefield,
One should dodge the weapons of the afflictions
And strike to destroy the enemy afflictions. ^7-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-68]]

Just as a sword dropped in battle
Is swiftly picked up out of fear,
So too, if the weapon of mindfulness is lost,
One should quickly take it up, remembering the fears of hell. ^7-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-69]]

Just as poison, by relying on the blood,
Spreads to pervade the body,
So too, if an opportunity is found,
A fault will pervade the mind. ^7-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-70]]

Just as one carrying a vessel filled with mustard oil,
With a sword-bearer standing nearby,
Fears the threat of death if it spills,
An ascetic should likewise be focused. ^7-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-71]]

Therefore, just as one swiftly springs up
If a snake crawls into one's lap,
Likewise, if sleep and lethargy arrive,
One should quickly repel them. ^7-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-72]]

For each and every fault that occurs,
I shall reproach myself and ponder for a long time:
"By all means, I will act in such a way
That this never happens to me again." ^7-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-73]]

Thinking, "How, in these situations,
Will mindfulness become a habit?"
For this reason, one should seek out association
Or appropriate actions. ^7-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-74]]

So that before performing an action,
I have the capability for everything,
Thus, recalling the discourse on heedfulness,
I should make myself rise lightly. ^7-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-75]]

Just as the wind's going and
Coming control a tuft of cotton,
Likewise, I should be controlled by enthusiasm;
In that way, success will be achieved. ^7-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, this is the seventh chapter, entitled the Exposition of Diligence. ^7-a

## Chapter Eight: The Exposition of Meditative Concentration ^8-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-1]]

Having thus generated diligence,
I should place my mind in concentration.
A person whose mind is completely distracted
Dwells between the fangs of the afflictions. ^8-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-2]]

Through the solitude of body and mind,
Complete distraction will not arise.
Therefore, I should renounce the world
And completely discard discursive thought. ^8-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-3]]

Because of attachment and through craving
For gain and so forth, one does not abandon the world.
Therefore, completely abandoning these,
A wise person should analyze in this way. ^8-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-4]]

By special insight fully endowed with calm abiding
The afflictions are completely destroyed; having known this,
One should first seek calm abiding; and that
Is accomplished by joyful delight in non-attachment to the world. ^8-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-5]]

What impermanent being, toward the impermanent,
Would form a true attachment?
Through that, for thousands of lifetimes,
They will not see their beloved. ^8-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-6]]

If they are not seen, one feels no joy,
And the mind does not rest in meditative equipoise;
Even when they are seen, one is not satisfied,
And is tormented by craving just as before. ^8-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-7]]

If one becomes attached to sentient beings,
True reality is completely obscured,
The mind of disillusionment is also destroyed,
And in the end, one will be tormented by grief. ^8-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-8]]

By merely thinking about them,
This life will pass away meaninglessly;
Because of impermanent friends and relatives,
Even the eternal Dharma will be destroyed. ^8-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-9]]

If one shares the same fortune as a childish one,
One will certainly go to the lower realm;
If they lead one to a fortune unlike that of the noble ones,
What is the use of relying on a childish one? ^8-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-10]]

In a single instant they become friends,
And in a mere moment they even become enemies;
Since they become angry at occasions for joy,
Ordinary beings are difficult to please. ^8-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-11]]

If I speak beneficially, they become angry,
And they also turn me away from what is beneficial;
If I do not listen to their words,
Out of anger they will go to the lower realm. ^8-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-12]]

They have envy for superiors and compete with equals,
They have pride toward inferiors and are conceited when praised;
If spoken to unpleasantly, anger arises;
When is benefit ever obtained from a childish person? ^8-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-13]]

If one associates with a childish one, from that childish one,
Self-praise, the disparagement of others,
And talk that delights in saṃsāra and so forth—
Non-virtue will certainly arise in every way. ^8-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-14]]

Thus, when oneself and another rely on each other,
By that, one will merely come to ruin;
Since they do not accomplish my welfare,
And I too will not accomplish their welfare, ^8-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-15]]

I shall flee far away from a childish person.
If encountered, I shall please them with joy;
Without becoming highly intimate,
I shall behave well as a mere ordinary person. ^8-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-16]]

Just as a bee takes nectar from a flower,
Having taken only what serves the purpose of the Dharma,
As if I had never seen them before,
I shall remain without familiarity toward everyone. ^8-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-17]]

"I have much gain and honor,
And many are fond of me"—
If I harbor such conceit,
Fear will arise after death. ^8-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-18]]

Therefore, whatever the thoroughly deluded mind
Becomes attached to,
Will all combine and, multiplied a thousandfold,
Become and arise as suffering. ^8-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-19]]

Therefore, a wise person should not form attachment,
For fear arises from attachment.
Since these things will naturally be left behind,
Remain steadfast and thoroughly understand this. ^8-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-20]]

Even if much gain has been acquired,
And fame and a good reputation have arisen,
Along with the collection of gain and fame,
There is no knowing where one will go. ^8-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-21]]

If there are others who disparage me,
what reason is there for me to be pleased by praise?
If there are others who praise me,
what reason is there for me to be displeased by disparagement? ^8-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-22]]

Since sentient beings have diverse aspirations,
if even the Victors cannot please them,
what need is there to mention an inferior one like me?
Therefore, I shall abandon thoughts of the world. ^8-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-23]]

Sentient beings disparage those without gains,
and speak unpleasantly of those with gains.
How will joy arise from those
who are naturally difficult to associate with? ^8-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-24]]

Because joy does not arise for childish ones
without their own benefit,
the Tathāgatas have said
that no childish one is a friend. ^8-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-25]]

In the forest, deer, birds,
and trees do not speak unpleasantly.
When will I dwell together
with those whose company is pleasant? ^8-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-26]]

Making my dwelling in a cave, an empty temple,
or at the foot of a tree,
when will I never look back
and become free from attachment? ^8-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-27]]

In places not held as "mine,"
Which are naturally vast,
Acting freely and without attachment,
When will I come to dwell? ^8-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-28]]

Possessing merely an alms bowl and the like,
Wearing garments wanted by no one,
And not even concealing this body,
When will I dwell without fear? ^8-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-29]]

Having gone to a charnel ground,
The skeletons of others and my body,
As having the nature of perishing,
When will I treat them as equal? ^8-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-30]]

Even this very body of mine
Will come to be such that,
By its stench, even foxes
Will not come near. ^8-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-31]]

Although this body arose as one,
The flesh and bones that were born together
Will disintegrate and separate;
What need to mention other loved ones? ^8-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-32]]

When born, one is born alone,
And when dying, one dies alone;
Since others do not take a share of the suffering,
What is the use of loved ones who create obstacles? ^8-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-33]]

Just as those entered upon a road
Take up a place to stay,
So too do those entered on the path of existence
Fully take up a place of birth. ^8-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-34]]

While the world grieves all around,
As long as four men
Have not yet carried that body away from there,
Until then, go to the forest! ^8-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-35]]

Having made no friends and no enemies,
This body dwells alone in solitude;
Being reckoned as already dead,
Even when dying, no one feels grief. ^8-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-36]]

Since there is no one staying nearby
To cause the harm of grief,
No one at all will distract me from
The recollection of the Buddha and so forth. ^8-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-37]]

Therefore, in exceptionally delightful
Forests, free of hardship and blissful,
Which pacify all distractions,
I myself shall dwell alone. ^8-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-38]]

Having let go of all other thoughts,
With a mind intent solely on my own mind,
In order to settle the mind in meditative equipoise
And to subdue it, I shall strive. ^8-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-39]]

In this world and in the next,
sensual desires produce ruin;
here, they lead to being killed, bound, and mutilated,
and in the next, they bring about the hells and so forth. ^8-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-40]]

Previously, through male and female messengers,
for whose sake you made many requests,
and regarding even misdeeds or infamy,
for whose sake you did not hesitate; ^8-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-41]]

casting yourself even into danger
and exhausting your wealth,
by fully embracing whom
you would be supremely delighted—those very ones ^8-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-42]]

are nothing but skeletons, nothing else.
Since they have neither independence nor a self,
desiring and clinging to them intensely,
why do you not go to nirvāṇa? ^8-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-43]]

That which you first lifted with effort,
and though drawn up, looked down out of modesty;
whether seen before or unseen,
that face was covered by a cloth. ^8-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-44]]

That face of your affliction,
just as it has now become manifest,
seeing it unveiled by vultures,
why do you flee from it now? ^8-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-45]]

Even when others merely looked with their eyes,
You kept it completely guarded;
While it is being eaten by them,
Why, O miser, do you not guard it? ^8-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-46]]

Seeing this heap of flesh,
When vultures and others eat it,
Why do you honor the food of others
With flower garlands, sandalwood, and ornaments? ^8-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-47]]

Seeing it as a mere skeleton,
If you are terrified even when it is motionless,
Why are you not afraid when it is moved
By something, like a reanimated corpse? ^8-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-48]]

Since from a single food alone
Excrement and saliva are produced,
Out of these you dislike the excrement,
So why do you delight in the saliva? ^8-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-49]]

Not taking delight in a pillow
Of cotton that is soft to the touch,
Saying, "It does not leak a foul odor,"
Lustful persons are deluded about the impure. ^8-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-50]]

If you have attachment to this even when covered,
Why do you not desire it when uncovered?
If you have no need for that,
Why do you embrace it when covered? ^8-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-51]]

If you have no attachment to impurity,
Why do you embrace in your lap another—
A cage of bones bound by sinews,
Plastered with the mud of flesh? ^8-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-52]]

You yourself contain much impurity,
Which you constantly make use of;
And yet for other bags of filth as well,
You desire them out of greed for impurity. ^8-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-53]]

Saying, "I delight in this flesh,"
If you desire to touch and look at it,
How can you desire the flesh
Which is of an insentient nature? ^8-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-54]]

That mind which you desire
Cannot be touched or seen,
And that which can be is not consciousness;
What is the use of that meaningless embrace? ^8-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-55]]

Another's body is of the nature of impurity—
Not realizing this is not a great wonder;
But that you yourself are impurity itself—
Not realizing this is a great wonder! ^8-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-56]]

Having abandoned a fresh lotus
Opened by the rays of a cloudless sun,
With a mind attached to impurity,
Why do you delight in a cage of filth? ^8-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-57]]

If you do not wish to touch a patch of ground
Smeared with impurity,
How can you wish to touch the body
From which that arose? ^8-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-58]]

If you have no attachment to impurity,
Why do you embrace another in your lap
Who arose from an impure field
And was produced by that seed? ^8-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-59]]

You do not desire even a tiny filth-worm
That has arisen from impurity,
Yet you desire a body whose nature is much impurity
And which was born from impurity. ^8-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-60]]

Not only do you not disparage
Your own impurity,
But out of greed for impurity,
You also desire another bag of filth. ^8-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-61]]

Even pleasant things like camphor,
Or cooked rice and vegetables,
Once put into the mouth and spat out,
Make even the ground impure and foul. ^8-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-62]]

If you doubt such impurity as this,
Even though it is plainly evident,
The bodies discarded in the charnel grounds—
Look at those other impurities as well. ^8-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-63]]

If the skin is peeled away from it,
Great terror will arise;
Even knowing this, how can joy arise
In that very same thing once again? ^8-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-64]]

That scent smeared upon the body, too,
Is sandalwood and the like, not anything else.
Because of that scent of another,
Why do you have attachment to another? ^8-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-65]]

If it is naturally foul-smelling,
Is it not better to have no attachment to it?
Those in the world who pointlessly crave,
Why do they smear sweet scents upon it? ^8-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-66]]

But if the sweet scent is sandalwood,
What originates from the body here?
Because of the scent of another,
Why do you have attachment to another? ^8-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-67]]

If, with long hair and nails,
Yellowish teeth with filth, and imbued with the smell of mud,
The nature of the body
Is terrifying indeed when naked, ^8-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-68]]

Like a weapon that harms oneself,
Why do you make efforts to polish it?
Through the efforts of those deluded regarding the self,
This earth is completely agitated by madmen. ^8-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-69]]

Having seen nothing but skeletons,
If you feel revulsion in the charnel ground,
Do you delight in the charnel ground of the village,
Pervaded by moving skeletons? ^8-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-70]]

Even that which has thus become impure
Cannot be obtained without a price.
From accomplishing that purpose comes exhaustion,
And harm in hell and so forth. ^8-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-71]]

Since a child cannot amass wealth,
When coming of age, what happiness is there in this?
If life is exhausted by amassing wealth,
Once old, what can one do with sensual desires? ^8-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-72]]

Some wretched lustful people,
Utterly exhausted by a full day's work,
Return home, and their exhausted bodies
Sleep like corpses. ^8-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-73]]

Some, afflicted by traveling abroad
And suffering from going far away,
Though longing for their wives and children,
Do not see them even for years. ^8-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-74]]

Utterly deluded, though desiring their own benefit,
For whatever purpose they even sell themselves,
Without obtaining that, they are driven
By the wind of others' meaningless work. ^8-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-75]]

Some sell their own bodies,
And, powerless, are put to work by others;
Even when their wives give birth,
It happens just anywhere, at the foot of a tree or in the wilderness. ^8-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-76]]

Fools deceived by sensual desire,
Wishing to survive and thinking, "I will make a living,"
Enter into battle despite fearing the loss of their lives,
And go into servitude for the sake of profit. ^8-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-77]]

Some lustful persons even have their bodies mutilated;
Some are impaled on the points of stakes;
Some are pierced by short spears;
And some are even seen being burned. ^8-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-78]]

Through the torments of accumulating, guarding, and losing,
Wealth should be known as endless ruin.
Those who are distracted by attachment to wealth
Have no opportunity to be freed from the suffering of existence. ^8-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-79]]

For lustful persons, these and so forth
Are the many disadvantages, and the enjoyment is slight,
Just as beasts that pull a cart
Eat only a few mouthfuls of grass. ^8-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-80]]

For the sake of that small benefit,
Which is not rare even for beasts to accomplish,
This perfect leisure and endowment, so difficult to find,
Is destroyed by those tormented by karma. ^8-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-81]]

Sensual desires will certainly perish
And cause one to fall into hell and so forth.
For the sake of what is not great, at all times,
Whatever wearisome hardship has occurred— ^8-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-82]]

If, by merely a ten-millionth fraction of that
Hardship, Buddhahood is accomplished,
For a lustful person, compared to the conduct of awakening,
The suffering is greater, yet there is no awakening. ^8-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-83]]

If one considers the suffering of hell and so forth,
For sensual desires, weapons,
Poison, fire, precipices,
And enemies cannot even serve as examples. ^8-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-84]]

Having thus become weary of desire,
One should generate joy for solitude
Within peaceful forests
Empty of disputes and afflictions. ^8-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-85]]

The fortunate ones, in delightful fine dwellings of broad stone slabs
Cooled by moonlight and sandalwood,
Fanned by the silent, peaceful forest breeze,
Pace while contemplating the benefit of others. ^8-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-86]]

In empty houses, at the foot of trees, and in caves,
Staying wherever and for as long as one desires,
Having abandoned the suffering of possessing and guarding,
One lives carefree, without dependence. ^8-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-87]]

Acting independently and without attachment,
Unconnected to anyone at all,
Whatever happiness of contentment one enjoys,
That is difficult even for Indra to find. ^8-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-88]]

Through these and other such aspects,
Having reflected on the good qualities of solitude,
With conceptual thought fully pacified,
One should meditate on bodhicitta. ^8-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-89]]

The equality of self and others
One should first strive to meditate upon;
Since they are equal in happiness and suffering,
All should be protected like oneself. ^8-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-90]]

Although the divisions of hands and so forth are many,
Just as they are one as a body to be fully protected,
Likewise, diverse wandering beings, with their happiness and suffering,
Are all one in equally desiring happiness, like oneself. ^8-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-91]]

If my suffering
Does not harm the bodies of others,
Even so, that suffering is mine,
Unbearable due to clinging to self. ^8-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-92]]

Likewise, although the sufferings of others
Do not fall upon me,
Even so, that suffering is mine,
Difficult to bear due to clinging to self. ^8-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-93]]

I should dispel the suffering of others
Because it is suffering, like my own suffering.
I should benefit others
Because they are sentient beings, like my own body. ^8-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-94]]

When both myself and others
Are equal in desiring happiness,
What difference is there with myself,
That I strive for my own happiness alone? ^8-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-95]]

When both myself and others
Are equal in not desiring suffering,
What difference is there with myself,
That I protect myself and not others? ^8-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-96]]

If, because their suffering
Does not harm me, I do not protect them,
If future suffering also
Does not harm me, why protect against it? ^8-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-97]]

Thinking "I will experience it"—
That conceptual thought is false;
For the one who dies is indeed another,
And the one who is born is also another. ^8-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-98]]

If whenever anyone's suffering
Must be protected by that person alone,
The suffering of the foot is not the hand's;
Why should it be protected by it? ^8-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-99]]

If you say, "Even if it is illogical, here
One engages through grasping at a self,"
Whatever is illogical regarding self and other
Is precisely what should be abandoned to the best of one's ability. ^8-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-100]]

What are called a continuum and a collection
Are false, like a garland, an army, and so forth;
Since there is no one who possesses suffering,
Therefore, who will have control over this? ^8-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-101]]

Since suffering has no owner,
All are entirely without distinction;
Because it is suffering, it is to be dispelled—
What is the point of making restrictions there? ^8-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-102]]

"Why should the suffering of all
Be averted?"—there is no room for debate;
If it is to be averted, all is to be averted;
If not, I too am like sentient beings. ^8-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-103]]

If you ask, "Since compassion results in much suffering,
Why forcibly generate it?"
When one contemplates the suffering of wandering beings,
How is the suffering of compassion great? ^8-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-104]]

If by means of a single suffering
Many sufferings are eliminated,
By the compassionate one, that suffering
Should certainly be generated for oneself and others. ^8-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-105]]

Therefore, Supuṣpacandra,
Even knowing the harm from the king,
Did not dispel his own suffering,
So that the suffering of many would be exhausted. ^8-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-106]]

Thus, one whose continuum is habituated,
Because of delighting in pacifying others' suffering,
Like a swan into a lotus pool,
Will plunge even into the Avīci hell. ^8-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-107]]

When sentient beings are liberated,
The ocean of joy that exists—
Is that itself not sufficient?
What is the use of desiring liberation? ^8-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-108]]

Therefore, even when acting for the welfare of others,
There is no conceit or amazement.
Because of delighting solely in the welfare of others,
There is no hope for a ripened result. ^8-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-109]]

Therefore, just as I protect myself
From even the slightest unpleasant word,
Likewise, a protective mind toward others
And a mind of compassion should be cultivated in this way. ^8-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-110]]

Through habituation, regarding others'
Drops of semen and blood,
Even though there is an absence of an entity,
Just as one knows them as "I", ^8-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-111]]

Likewise, regarding the bodies of others too,
Why are they not apprehended as "I"?
Positing my own body as other
Is in that way not difficult. ^8-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-112]]

Having known that oneself has faults, and that others too
Are oceans of good qualities,
One should meditate on completely discarding self-grasping
And taking up others. ^8-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-113]]

Just as hands and so forth
Are accepted as limbs of the body,
Likewise, as limbs of wandering beings,
Why are embodied beings not accepted? ^8-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-114]]

Just as toward this body of selflessness,
Through habituation, the intellect of self has arisen,
Likewise, toward other sentient beings as well,
Through habituation, why would the intellect of self not arise? ^8-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-115]]

If it is like that, even having accomplished
The welfare of others, amazement and conceit do not arise,
Just as when one has eaten food oneself,
Hope for a return does not arise. ^8-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-116]]

Therefore, just as I protect myself
From even minor unpleasantness,
Likewise, a protective mind toward wandering beings
And a mind of compassion should be habituated. ^8-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-117]]

Therefore, the Protector Avalokiteśvara,
Out of great compassion,
In order to dispel even wandering beings' fear in assemblies,
Bestowed a blessing upon even his own name. ^8-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-118]]

One should not turn back from hardship;
For through the power of habituation in this way,
Even someone whose name caused fear to hear,
Without them, one becomes joyless. ^8-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-119]]

Whoever wishes to quickly protect
Themselves and others,
Should practice the exchange of self and others,
The supreme secret. ^8-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-120]]

Through attachment to this body of mine,
Fear arises toward small sources of fear;
That body which gives rise to fear,
Who would not hate as an enemy? ^8-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-121]]

Whoever, wishing to perform the treatment to cure
The body's illnesses of hunger, thirst, and the like,
Kills birds, fish, and wild animals,
And lies in ambush along the roads; ^8-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-122]]

If one, for the sake of profit and respect,
Kills even their father and mother,
And, having stolen the wealth of the Three Jewels,
Is thereby burned in the Avīci hell, ^8-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-123]]

What wise person would desire,
Protect, and venerate that body?
Who would not view this
As an enemy, and despise it? ^8-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-124]]

"If I give, what will I consume?"
Thinking of one's own welfare is the way of a demon.
"If I consume, what will I give?"
Thinking of the welfare of others is the way of a god. ^8-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-125]]

If one harms others for one's own sake,
One will be tormented in hell and so forth.
If one harms oneself for the sake of others,
One will obtain all excellence. ^8-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-126]]

Through wishing for oneself to be exalted,
One will be reborn in a lower realm, base, and stupid.
If that very wish is shifted to others,
One will obtain a happy realm and respect. ^8-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-127]]

If one employs others for one's own welfare,
One will experience servitude and the like.
If one employs oneself for the welfare of others,
One will experience lordship and the like. ^8-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-128]]

Whatever happiness there is in the world,
It all arises from wishing for the happiness of others.
Whatever suffering there is in the world,
It all arises from wishing for one's own happiness. ^8-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-129]]

What need is there to say much?
The childish one who acts for their own welfare, and
The Sage who acts for the welfare of others—
Look at the difference between these two! ^8-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-130]]

If one does not truly exchange
One's own happiness and the suffering of others,
Buddhahood will not be achieved,
And even in saṃsāra there is no happiness. ^8-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-131]]

Setting aside the next world,
When a servant does not do their work
And a master does not pay wages,
Even the purpose of this life will not be achieved. ^8-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-132]]

Completely abandoning the perfect joy
That achieves seen and unseen happiness,
Through the cause of inflicting suffering on others,
One takes on unbearable suffering through delusion. ^8-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-133]]

Whatever harm there is in the world, and
However much fear and suffering there may be,
If all of that arises from grasping at a self,
What use is that great demon to me? ^8-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-134]]

If one does not completely let go of the very self,
One cannot abandon suffering,
Just as if one does not let go of fire,
One cannot avoid being burned. ^8-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-135]]

Therefore, to pacify harm to myself
And to pacify the suffering of others,
I should give myself to others
And hold others as myself. ^8-135

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-136]]

"I am under the power of others"—
O mind, you must know this with certainty.
Apart from the welfare of all sentient beings,
You should now think of nothing else. ^8-136

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-137]]

With eyes and so forth that are under the power of others,
It is not appropriate to accomplish one's own welfare.
With eyes and so forth that are for their sake,
It is not appropriate to act wrongly toward them. ^8-137

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-138]]

Therefore, make sentient beings foremost.
Whatever you see upon my body,
Having snatched it away,
Act for the benefit of others. ^8-138

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-139]]

Having made the inferior and so forth into oneself,
And having made oneself into others,
With a mind free of conceptual thought,
Meditate on envy, rivalry, and pride. ^8-139

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-140]]

This one is honored, but I am not;
I have not obtained as this one has.
This one is praised, but I am disparaged;
This one is happy, while I am in suffering. ^8-140

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-141]]

I perform the labors, and
This one remains in happiness itself.
This one is great in the world, and
I am known as inferior and without good qualities. ^8-141

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-142]]

What is the use of being without good qualities?
All of us are indeed endowed with good qualities.
There are those compared to whom this one is inferior, and
there are those compared to whom I am superior. ^8-142

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-143]]

The decline of ethical discipline, view, and so forth
is due to the power of afflictions; it is not under my control.
I must be healed as much as possible,
and I will accept even harm. ^8-143

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-144]]

However, if I am not to be healed by this one,
why does he despise me?
What use are his good qualities to me?
This one himself possesses good qualities! ^8-144

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-145]]

Dwelling in the jaws of vicious lower realms,
this one lacks compassion for wandering beings.
Moreover, conceited about his good qualities,
he wishes to challenge wise persons. ^8-145

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-146]]

Looking at one equal to me,
in order to make myself superior,
my gain and honor
I shall certainly secure, even through dispute. ^8-146

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-147]]

I shall ensure that my good qualities
Are made clear to the entire world,
And furthermore, whatever good qualities this one has,
I shall ensure that no one hears of them. ^8-147

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-148]]

I shall also conceal my faults,
I shall be venerated, but not this one.
Today I shall acquire excellent gains,
I shall be respected, but not this one. ^8-148

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-149]]

When this one is brought to ruin,
I shall look on with joy for a long time.
I shall make him the laughingstock of all wandering beings,
And the object of their mutual derision. ^8-149

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-150]]

It is said that even this miserable one
Is competing together with me.
But is this one equal in learning or wisdom,
Or in form, family, or wealth? ^8-150

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-151]]

Having heard of my good qualities
Proclaimed to everyone in this way,
I shall fully enjoy the happiness
Of the thrilling joy that arises. ^8-151

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-152]]

Even if this one acquires some gain,
If he does my work,
I shall give him just enough to live on,
And I shall take it by my power. ^8-152

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-153]]

This one shall be made to fall from happiness,
And be constantly subjected to my harm;
For by this one, across all hundreds of lifetimes,
Harm was done to me in saṃsāra. ^8-153

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-154]]

O mind, because you wished to accomplish your own welfare,
Even though countless eons have passed,
Through such great weariness
You have accomplished only suffering. ^8-154

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-155]]

Thus, you must certainly apply yourself
Thoroughly to the welfare of others;
Because the word of the Sage is infallible,
You will later see its good qualities. ^8-155

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-156]]

If, in former times,
You had performed this action,
A situation like this, other than the perfect happiness
Of a Buddha, could not possibly occur. ^8-156

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-157]]

Therefore, just as upon drops
Of the semen and blood of others
You have engaged in grasping as "I",
Likewise habituate yourself toward others too. ^8-157

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-158]]

Having acted as a spy for others,
Whatever appears upon my body,
Having snatched those very things away,
You must practice what is beneficial for others. ^8-158

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-159]]

"I am happy and others are unhappy,
I am high and others are low,
I do what is beneficial and others do not"—
Why do you not feel envy toward yourself? ^8-159

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-160]]

Separate yourself from happiness,
And apply the suffering of others to yourself.
Asking, "What is this one doing at any time?"
Examine your own faults. ^8-160

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-161]]

Even when a fault is committed by others,
Transform it into your own fault;
And even if a fault committed by yourself is small,
Confess it fully to many people. ^8-161

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-162]]

By speaking highly of the reputation of others,
Suppress your own reputation.
Like the lowest of servants,
Employ yourself for all purposes. ^8-162

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-163]]

This one is of a faulty nature,
Do not praise it for a fraction of an adventitious good quality.
Regarding its good qualities, by all means,
Act so that no one at all will know them. ^8-163

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-164]]

In short, for your own sake,
Whatever harm you have done to others,
For the sake of sentient beings,
Let that harm descend upon yourself. ^8-164

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-165]]

Lest it become overbearing,
Do not foster its strength;
Just like a new bride,
Keep it restrained with shame and fear. ^8-165

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-166]]

"Do that! Remain like that!
You must not act in that way!"
In this manner, bring this under control;
If it transgresses that, it must be subdued. ^8-166

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-167]]

Yet, even when instructed in that way,
if you, my mind, do not act like that,
since all faults rely upon you,
there is nothing left but to subdue you yourself. ^8-167

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-168]]

That former time when you ruined me
Was another time;
Now I see you—where will you go?
I shall destroy all your haughtiness. ^8-168

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-169]]

"I still have my own welfare"—
Discard that thought!
Since I have sold you to others,
Do not be discouraged, offer your service. ^8-169

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-170]]

If, falling into heedlessness,
I do not give you to sentient beings,
You will certainly hand me over
To the guardians of hell. ^8-170

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-171]]

In that way, you have handed me over time and again,
And I have suffered for a long time;
I, remembering those grudges,
Will destroy your thinking of your own welfare. ^8-171

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-172]]

If I wish to be happy,
I should not take delight in myself.
If I wish to protect myself,
I should always protect others. ^8-172

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-173]]

To whatever extent this body
Is completely cared for,
To that same extent it falls
Into becoming extremely fragile. ^8-173

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-174]]

Even when it has fallen in that way,
If its desires cannot be fulfilled
By this entire earth,
Who can fulfill its desires? ^8-174

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-175]]

From desiring the impossible, afflictions
and a deteriorated intention will also arise.
Whoever does not depend on anything,
their excellence is inexhaustible. ^8-175

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-176]]

Therefore, one should not give an opportunity
For the desires of the body to increase.
Whatever attractive thing one does not grasp,
That is an excellent thing. ^8-176

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-177]]

Ultimately reaching its end in ashes,
Motionless, it is moved by another;
An impure and unbearable form—
Why do I direct grasping at self toward this? ^8-177

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-178]]

Whether it is alive or whether it is dead,
What use to me is this machine?
If it is no different from a clod of earth and the like,
Alas, why do I not dispel my pride? ^8-178

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-179]]

By tending to the body,
Having accumulated meaningless suffering,
Attachment and anger—
What use are they toward this, which is similar to wood? ^8-179

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-180]]

Whether it is nurtured by me in this way,
Or eaten by vultures and the like,
Since it has no attachment and no hatred,
Why do I have attachment toward it? ^8-180

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-181]]

That which is angered by blame,
And that which is delighted by praise—
If it itself knows nothing,
For whose sake have I toiled? ^8-181

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-182]]

Whoever desires this body,
If I say, "They and I are friends,"
Since everyone desires their own body,
Why do I not delight in them? ^8-182

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-183]]

Therefore, without attachment, I
Shall give up the body to benefit beings.
Thus, although it has many faults,
I shall retain it like an instrument for action. ^8-183

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-184]]

Therefore, enough of childish behavior!
I shall follow in the footsteps of the wise,
And having recalled the discourse on heedfulness,
I shall dispel sleep and mental dullness. ^8-184

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-185]]

Therefore, in order to clear away obscurations,
Having withdrawn my mind from mistaken paths,
Constantly upon the perfect object of focus,
I shall rest in meditative equipoise. ^8-185

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the eighth chapter, called Meditative Concentration. ^8-a

## Chapter Nine: The Perfection of Wisdom ^9-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-1]]

All these branches
The Sage taught for the sake of wisdom.
Therefore, those who wish to pacify suffering
Should generate wisdom. ^9-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-2]]

Conventional truth and ultimate truth:
These are held to be the two truths.
Ultimate truth is not the domain of the intellect;
The intellect is said to be conventional truth. ^9-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-3]]

In this regard, two kinds of world are seen:
Yogis and ordinary people.
In this regard, the ordinary world
Is invalidated by the world of yogis; ^9-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-4]]

Even yogis, due to differences in intellect,
are invalidated by those successively higher,
through examples accepted by both,
because, for the sake of the result, they are not analyzed. ^9-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-5]]

Worldly persons see entities,
And conceptualize them as true reality,
Not as being like illusions; therefore, here,
Yogis and the world dispute. ^9-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-6]]

Even the direct perception of forms and so forth
Is through convention, not through valid cognition.
That is false, just as the convention of purity and so forth
Regarding impurity and so forth is false. ^9-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-7]]

For the sake of guiding the world,
The Protector taught entities; in reality,
They are not momentary.
If one objects, "This contradicts even conventional truth," ^9-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-8]]

There is no fault in the conventional truth of yogis;
In relation to the world, they see reality.
Otherwise, regarding women as impure,
That realization would be invalidated by the world. ^9-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-9]]

"How is there merit from a Victor like an illusion?"
Just as it is for one who is truly existent.
"If sentient beings are like illusions,
How are they born after dying?" ^9-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-10]]

As long as conditions are assembled,
For that long even an illusion will arise.
Merely because of a long continuum, how
Can a sentient being be truly existent? ^9-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-11]]

In killing an illusory person and the like,
Because there is no mind, there is no misdeed;
But toward one possessing an illusory mind,
Merit and misdeed arise. ^9-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-12]]

Because mantras and so forth lack the capacity,
An illusory mind does not arise.
Arising from various conditions,
That illusion itself is of various kinds. ^9-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-13]]

Nowhere is there a single condition
That is capable of everything.
"If ultimate truth is nirvāṇa,
And saṃsāra is conventional truth, then ^9-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-14]]

"Since even a Buddha would cycle [in saṃsāra],
What is the use of the conduct of awakening?"
If the continuum of conditions is not severed,
Even an illusion does not cease; ^9-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-15]]

But because the continuum of conditions is severed,
It does not arise even in conventional truth.
"When even error does not exist,
By what will an illusion be perceived?" ^9-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-16]]

When for you the illusion itself
Does not exist, then what is perceived?
"If in reality another exists,
That aspect is the mind itself." ^9-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-17]]

When the mind itself is an illusion,
Then what is seen by what?
Even the Protector of the world
Stated that the mind does not see the mind. ^9-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-18]]

Just as the blade of a sword
Does not cut itself, so too the mind.
"If [you say], just as a lamp
Perfectly illuminates its own entity," ^9-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-19]]

A lamp is not something to be illuminated,
Because it is not obscured by darkness.
"Unlike a crystal, a blue thing
Does not depend on another for its blueness." ^9-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-20]]

"Likewise, some things are seen to depend
On others, and some to not depend."
When it is not blue itself,
That is not made blue by itself. ^9-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-21]]

If it is known by consciousness and thus expressed,
"The lamp makes things luminous,"
Having been known by what is it thus expressed,
"The intellect itself is luminous"? ^9-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-22]]

When it is not seen by anyone at all,
Whether it is luminous or not luminous,
Like the alluring pose of a barren woman's daughter,
Even to express it is meaningless. ^9-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-23]]

"If self-awareness does not exist,
How does the memory of consciousness occur?"
From its connection to experiencing another,
Memory arises, like the poison of a rat. ^9-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-24]]

"If, because one endowed with other conditions sees,
it is luminous to itself,"
through the application of accomplished eye ointment,
the vase is seen, but not the eye ointment itself. ^9-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-25]]

Whatever is seen, heard, and known
Is not what is to be negated here;
Here, the conceptualization of true existence,
Which is the cause of suffering, is to be overturned. ^9-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-26]]

If an illusion is not other than the mind,
And it is not conceived as non-other either,
If it is an entity, how is it not other?
If it is not other, it does not exist as an entity. ^9-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-27]]

Just as an illusion, although not true,
Is something to be seen, so too the mind is the one who sees.
"If saṃsāra is based on an entity,
Otherwise it would be like space." ^9-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-28]]

Since a non-entity depends on an entity,
How could it be endowed with a function?
Your mind, without a companion,
Would become entirely solitary. ^9-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-29]]

When the mind is free of an apprehended object,
At that time, all are Tathāgatas.
Even if that is so, what benefit is there
In conceptually constructing Cittamātra? ^9-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-30]]

"Even if one knows things to be like an illusion,
How will the afflictions be averted?
Since, toward an illusory woman,
Even her creator generates attachment." ^9-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-31]]

Her creator, toward objects of knowledge,
Has not abandoned the latent tendencies of the afflictions.
Therefore, when he sees her,
His latent tendency of emptiness is weak. ^9-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-32]]

Through habituation to the latent tendency of emptiness,
The latent tendency of entities will be abandoned;
And through habituation to "there is nothing at all,"
That too will later be abandoned. ^9-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-33]]

When one asserts, "Such and such does not exist,"
And the entity to be analyzed is not apprehended,
At that time, how can a non-entity lacking a basis
Remain before the intellect? ^9-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-34]]

When entities and non-entities
Do not remain before the intellect,
At that time, since there is no other aspect,
There is thorough pacification in non-apprehension. ^9-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-35]]

Just as a wish-fulfilling jewel and a wish-fulfilling tree
Completely fulfill hopes,
Likewise, to the ones to be tamed, through the power of aspiration prayers,
The form of the Victor appears. ^9-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-36]]

For example, just as when a garuḍa shrine
Has been accomplished and its creator has passed away,
Even though a long time has elapsed since he passed,
It pacifies poison and so forth. ^9-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-37]]

Likewise, in accordance with the conduct of awakening,
The shrine of the Victor, too, has been accomplished;
Even when the bodhisattva has passed
Into nirvāṇa, it accomplishes all aims. ^9-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-38]]

"If offerings are made to one without a mind,
How will they be endowed with a result?"
Because whether he remains or has passed into nirvāṇa,
It is taught that it is exactly the same. ^9-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-39]]

Whether in conventional truth or in reality,
It is acceptable, for that result is from scripture,
Just as, for example, toward a true Buddha,
It is endowed with a result. ^9-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-40]]

"Liberation comes from seeing the truths;
What is the use of seeing emptiness?"
Because scripture states that without this path,
There is no awakening. ^9-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-41]]

"If the Mahāyāna is not established,
How is your scripture established?"
"Because this is established for both of us."
Initially, this was not established for you. ^9-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-42]]

By whatever conditions you believe in that,
That is the same for the Mahāyāna as well.
If it is true because any two other parties accept it,
The Vedas and so forth would also be true. ^9-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-43]]

"If you say it is because the Mahāyāna is subject to dispute,"
Regarding scripture, there is dispute with non-Buddhists,
And regarding other scriptures, with your own and other schools;
Since they are subject to dispute, they would be discarded. ^9-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-44]]

If being a monk is the root of the teaching,
Even being a monk is difficult to establish.
For those whose minds have apprehension,
Even nirvāṇa is difficult to establish. ^9-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-45]]

If one is liberated by abandoning afflictions,
It would occur immediately after that.
Even without afflictions, in them
The power of karma is seen. ^9-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-46]]

If you say it is certain that, for the time being,
The craving that is the cause of grasping does not exist,
Even if that craving is not afflicted,
Why would it not exist, just like delusion? ^9-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-47]]

Craving occurs due to the condition of feeling,
And feeling also exists in them.
A mind that has apprehension
Will remain fixed upon something. ^9-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-48]]

A mind that is devoid of emptiness,
Though ceased, will arise again,
Like the meditative absorption of non-perception;
Therefore, one should meditate on emptiness. ^9-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-49]]

If any speech that accords with the sūtras
Is asserted to be spoken by the Buddha,
Is it not asserted that most of the Mahāyāna
Is equal to your sūtras? ^9-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-50]]

If, due to a single unincluded one,
All become faulty,
Then due to a single one similar to a sūtra,
Why are they not all spoken by the Victor? ^9-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-51]]

Any speech whose depth was unfathomed
By Mahākāśyapa and the rest,
Because you do not understand it,
Who would make it unacceptable? ^9-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-52]]

For the sake of those suffering through delusion,
Being freed from the extremes of attachment and fear,
Remaining in saṃsāra is accomplished—
This is the result of emptiness. ^9-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-53]]

Thus, toward the position of emptiness,
Refutation is not tenable.
Therefore, without any doubt,
One should meditate on emptiness. ^9-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-54]]

Of the obscuration of afflictions and of objects of knowledge,
Emptiness is the antidote to the darkness.
By one who wishes to quickly become omniscient,
How could it not be meditated upon? ^9-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-55]]

Whatever thing produces suffering,
If fear arises from that,
Emptiness pacifies suffering;
How could fear arise toward it? ^9-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-56]]

If any self existed,
one might become afraid of anything;
but since no self exists at all,
who is there to be afraid? ^9-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-57]]

Teeth, hair, and nails are not the self,
and the self is not bones or blood;
it is not mucus, nor is it phlegm,
and it is neither lymph nor pus. ^9-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-58]]

The self is not fat or sweat,
and the lungs and liver are not the self;
other internal organs are also not the self,
and the self is not feces or urine. ^9-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-59]]

Flesh and skin are not the self,
and warmth and wind are not the self;
the orifices are not the self, and in all ways,
the six consciousnesses are not the self either. ^9-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-60]]

If the consciousness of sound were permanent,
it would apprehend sound at all times;
if there is no object of knowledge, what does it cognize,
and for what reason is it called a consciousness? ^9-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-61]]

If that which does not cognize were consciousness,
it would follow that a piece of wood is consciousness too;
therefore, it is certain that without the presence
of an object of knowledge, there is no consciousness. ^9-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-62]]

If that very thing knows form,
Why does it not also hear at that time?
If it is because sound is not nearby,
Then the consciousness of that is also absent. ^9-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-63]]

Whatever has the nature of apprehending sound,
How could it become the apprehender of form?
One person is designated as a father and a son,
But this is not true reality. ^9-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-64]]

Thus, sattva, rajas, and
Tamas are neither a son nor a father,
And that apprehender of form is not seen to have a nature
That possesses the apprehension of sound. ^9-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-65]]

If, like an actor, in another mode
That very thing is seen, it is not permanent.
If that other mode is that very thing,
That oneness is an unprecedented one. ^9-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-66]]

If that other mode is not true,
Then state its own nature.
If you say it is consciousness itself, then in that case,
It would follow that all persons are one. ^9-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-67]]

The conscious and the non-conscious, too,
Would become one, because their existence is the same.
When specific differences are false,
What then is the basis for similarity? ^9-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-68]]

That which is mindless is also not the self,
Because it is mindless, like a vase and so forth.
If, however, it cognizes because it is endowed with a mind,
It follows that the non-cognizer perishes. ^9-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-69]]

But if there is no change in the self,
What has consciousness done to it?
Thus, lacking cognition and devoid of activity,
Space would be made into the self. ^9-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-70]]

If you say: "If the self does not exist,
The connection between karma and result is illogical;
Because one perishes after performing a karma,
Whose karma will it be?" ^9-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-71]]

That the action and the result have distinct bases,
And that the self has no activity therein,
Is established for both of us,
So is this debate here not pointless? ^9-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-72]]

That the one possessing the cause is endowed with the result—
To see this is impossible.
It is by relying on a single continuum
That an agent and an experiencer are taught. ^9-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-73]]

The mind of the past and future
Is not the self, because it does not exist.
If, however, the arisen mind is the self,
When that perishes, there is no self either. ^9-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-74]]

Just as the trunk of a plantain tree
Is nothing when divided into parts,
Likewise, when sought through analysis,
The self is also not real. ^9-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-75]]

"If sentient beings do not exist,
Toward whom should one practice compassion?"
Toward whatever is imputed by delusion,
Which is accepted for the sake of the result. ^9-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-76]]

"If there are no sentient beings, whose is the result?"
That is true, yet it is asserted through delusion.
For the sake of pacifying suffering,
The delusion regarding the result should not be reversed. ^9-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-77]]

The pride that is the cause of suffering
Increases through the delusion of a self.
If you say that even that cannot be reversed,
Meditating on selflessness is supreme. ^9-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-78]]

The body is not the feet or the calves,
The thighs and the waist are also not the body.
The belly and the back are not the body,
And the chest and the arms are also not the body. ^9-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-79]]

The ribs and the hands are also not the body,
The armpits and the shoulders are also not the body.
The internal organs are also not it,
And if the head and the neck are also not the body,
Then what in this is the body? ^9-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-80]]

If this body were to abide in all of these
Through its individual portions,
The parts would indeed abide in the parts,
But where would it itself abide? ^9-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-81]]

If the body in its entirety
Were to abide in the hands and so forth,
However many hands and so forth there are,
There would be that many bodies. ^9-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-82]]

If the body does not exist outside or inside,
How does the body exist in the hands and so forth?
If it does not exist as other than the hands and so forth,
How does it exist? ^9-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-83]]

Therefore, there is no body; with regard to the hands and so forth,
Due to delusion, the notion of a body arises,
Because of a specific configuration of shape,
Just like the notion of a person with regard to a stump. ^9-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-84]]

As long as the collection of conditions lasts,
The body appears like a person;
Likewise, as long as that is present in the hands and so forth,
For that long, the body appears there. ^9-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-85]]

Likewise, because it is a collection of fingers,
What would the hand be?
Because that too is a collection of joints,
And the joint, too, by its division into its own parts, ^9-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-86]]

Even the part is divided into atoms,
And that atom too, by division into directional parts;
And the directional division too, because it is devoid of parts,
Is like space; therefore, even atoms do not exist. ^9-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-87]]

Thus, to form, which is like a dream,
What discerning person would be attached?
When the body thus does not exist,
Then who is a man and who is a woman? ^9-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-88]]

If suffering exists in reality,
Why does it not afflict those who are exceedingly joyful?
If happiness exists, for those tormented by grief and the like,
Why do delicious things and so forth not cause joy? ^9-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-89]]

If you say it is not experienced
Because it is overwhelmed by what is powerful,
How can that which is not of the nature of experience
Be a feeling? ^9-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-90]]

"Suffering exists in a subtle state."
Has not its coarse state been removed?
If you say, "It is merely a joy distinct from that,"
Then that subtle state too belongs to that. ^9-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-91]]

If, because an opposing condition has arisen,
Suffering does not arise,
Is it not established that what is called feeling
Is nothing but conceptual clinging? ^9-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-92]]

For this very reason, one should meditate
On this analysis as its antidote;
The meditative concentration arising from the field of investigation
Is the food of the yogi. ^9-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-93]]

If there is a gap between the sense faculty and the object,
Where would they meet?
Even if there is no gap, they are just one;
What would meet with what? ^9-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-94]]

An atom does not enter an atom;
It lacks space and they are equal.
Without entering, there is no mixing,
And without mixing, there is no meeting. ^9-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-95]]

How could it be logical to say
That what is partless can meet?
If meeting and being partless
Have been seen together, demonstrate it! ^9-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-96]]

For consciousness, which is without a body,
Meeting is simply not reasonable.
Because a collection, too, lacks an entity,
Just as was analyzed before. ^9-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-97]]

Thus, if contact does not exist,
From what would feeling arise?
For what purpose is this toil?
What would harm what? ^9-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-98]]

When there is no feeler at all,
And feeling also does not exist,
Then, seeing this situation,
Why does craving not turn away? ^9-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-99]]

Seeing or even touching
Is of a nature like a dream and an illusion;
Because feeling arises simultaneously with the mind,
It is not seen by that mind. ^9-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-100]]

Even by what arises previously or later,
It is remembered but not experienced.
It does not experience itself,
Nor is it experienced by others. ^9-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-101]]

There is no feeler at all,
Therefore, feeling does not exist in reality.
Thus, how could this collection of selflessness
Be harmed by this? ^9-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-102]]

The mind does not abide in the sense faculties,
Nor in forms and so forth, nor in between.
The mind is not inside, nor is it outside,
And it is not found anywhere else. ^9-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-103]]

That which is not the body, nor anywhere else,
Not mixed, nor anywhere separate,
Is nothing whatsoever; therefore,
Sentient beings are by nature in nirvāṇa. ^9-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-104]]

If consciousness exists before the object of knowledge,
Directed toward what does it arise?
If consciousness and the object of knowledge are simultaneous,
Directed toward what does it arise? ^9-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-105]]

But if it exists after the object of knowledge,
Then from what does consciousness arise?
In this way, of all phenomena,
Arising cannot be realized. ^9-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-106]]

"If, in this way, conventional truth does not exist,
How could there be the two truths?
And if that is through another conventional truth,
How could sentient beings pass into nirvāṇa?" ^9-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-107]]

This is the conceptual thought of another's mind;
It is not one's own conventional truth.
If that is ascertained later, it exists;
If not, conventional truth simply does not exist. ^9-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-108]]

Conceptual thought and what is to be conceptualized,
The two are mutually dependent.
Relying on what is commonly known,
All analyses are expressed. ^9-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-109]]

"When one analyzes with an analysis
That is itself analyzed,
Then that analysis too,
Because of analysis, has no end." ^9-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-110]]

When the object of analysis has been analyzed,
There is no basis for the analysis.
Because there is no basis, it does not arise,
And that too is expressed as nirvāṇa. ^9-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-111]]

For those who hold that these two are true,
That very position is extremely difficult to maintain.
If the object is established through the power of consciousness,
What basis is there for the existence of consciousness itself? ^9-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-112]]

If, however, consciousness is established through the object of knowledge,
What basis is there for the existence of the object of knowledge?
If they exist by the power of one another,
Both of them would not exist. ^9-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-113]]

If without a son there is no father,
From what does the son himself arise?
Without a son there is no father;
Likewise, those two do not exist. ^9-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-114]]

"A sprout arises from a seed, and
Just as the seed is known by that very sprout,
By the consciousness arisen from the object of knowledge,
Why is its existence not known?" ^9-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-115]]

If, by a consciousness other than the sprout,
It is known that the seed exists,
As for that by which the object of knowledge is known,
From what is the existence of consciousness itself known? ^9-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-116]]

For now, by worldly direct perception,
All causes are seen.
The distinctions of the lotus stalk and so forth
Are produced by the distinctions of causes. ^9-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-117]]

If you ask what made the distinctions of causes,
It is from the very distinctions of preceding causes.
Why is a cause able to produce a result?
From the very power of the preceding cause. ^9-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-118]]

If Īśvara is the cause of wandering beings,
For a start, state what Īśvara is!
If you say it is the elements, so be it,
But why weary yourself over a mere name? ^9-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-119]]

However, earth and so forth are multiple,
Impermanent, inert, and not divine;
Since they are to be trodden upon and are impure,
They are not Īśvara himself. ^9-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-120]]

Īśvara is not space, because it is inert;
He is not the self, because it was already refuted;
And as for a creator who is inconceivable,
What is the point of speaking of the inconceivable? ^9-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-121]]

And what is it that he is asserted to produce?
Are not that self, earth and so forth,
And the essence of Īśvara permanent?
Consciousness arises from the object of knowledge, and ^9-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-122]]

Beginningless happiness and suffering are from karma.
Tell me, what was generated by him?
If the cause has no beginning,
How can there be a beginning to the result? ^9-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-123]]

Why does he not create constantly?
For he does not depend on another.
If there is nothing else that was not made by him,
Upon what would he depend? ^9-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-124]]

If he depends, then the collection itself
Would be the cause, and not Īśvara.
When assembled, there is no power not to produce,
And without it, there is no power to produce. ^9-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-125]]

If Īśvara acts without desiring to,
It follows that he is under the control of another.
Even if he desires, he is dependent upon desire.
If he acts, how is he Īśvara? ^9-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-126]]

Those who assert permanent atoms
Have also already been refuted earlier.
The Sāṃkhyas assert that the permanent Pradhāna
Is the cause of wandering beings. ^9-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-127]]

The equilibrium of the good qualities
Known as sattva, rajas, and tamas,
Is proclaimed to be the Pradhāna;
Their disequilibrium is said to be wandering beings. ^9-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-128]]

For one thing to have three natures
Is illogical; therefore, it does not exist.
Likewise, the good qualities do not exist,
Because they too each have three aspects. ^9-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-129]]

If the good qualities do not exist, the existence
Of sound and so forth also becomes extremely remote.
In insentient things such as clothing,
It is even impossible for happiness and so forth to exist. ^9-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-130]]

If entities have the nature of their cause,
Have entities not already been analyzed?
Your cause, moreover, is just happiness and so forth,
And from that, cloth and so forth do not arise. ^9-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-131]]

Happiness and so forth arise from cloth and so forth;
Since those do not exist, happiness and so forth do not exist.
Furthermore, happiness and so forth as permanent
Are never observed. ^9-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-132]]

If the manifestation of happiness and so forth exists,
Why is the experience not apprehended?
If that very thing becomes subtle,
How can it be both coarse and subtle? ^9-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-133]]

Since it abandons the coarse and becomes subtle,
The subtle and coarse are impermanence.
Likewise, why do you not accept
The impermanence of all entities? ^9-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-134]]

If the coarse is not other than happiness,
Manifest happiness is impermanence itself.
If you assert that nothing non-existent whatsoever
Arises, because it is non-existent, ^9-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-135]]

The arising of the manifest that was non-existent
Remains the case, even if you do not assert it.
If the result abides in the cause,
One who eats food would eat filth. ^9-135

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-136]]

With the price of a cotton cloth,
Buy cotton seeds and wear them!
If the world does not see this through delusion,
That very thing is posited by the knowers of reality. ^9-136

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-137]]

That knowledge exists even in the world;
Since it exists, why is it not seen?
If the world is not valid cognition,
Seeing the manifest is also not true. ^9-137

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-138]]

If valid cognition is not valid,
Is what is measured by it not false?
In reality, emptiness—
Meditation on it therefore becomes untenable. ^9-138

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-139]]

Without contacting the imputed entity,
Its non-entity is not apprehended.
Therefore, whatever entity is false,
Its non-entity is clearly false. ^9-139

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-140]]

Thus, when a son dies in a dream,
The conceptual thought that he does not exist
Is an obstruction to the conceptual thought that he exists,
And that is also false. ^9-140

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-141]]

Therefore, by analyzing in this way,
Nothing at all exists without a cause,
Nor does it abide within conditions,
Whether individual or assembled. ^9-141

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-142]]

It does not come from elsewhere,
Nor does it abide, nor does it go.
This which is made true through delusion,
What difference is there from an illusion? ^9-142

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-143]]

Whatever is emanated by an illusion,
And whatever is emanated by causes—
Where has it come from,
And where does it go? Examine this! ^9-143

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-144]]

Whatever is seen through the proximity of something,
And is not seen when that is absent,
Is fabricated, similar to a reflection;
How could there be true existence in that? ^9-144

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-145]]

For an entity that exists,
What need is there for a cause?
Yet even if it does not exist,
What need is there for a cause? ^9-145

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-146]]

Even by hundreds of millions of causes,
A non-entity cannot be transformed.
How could that state be an entity?
And what else could become an entity? ^9-146

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-147]]

If an entity cannot exist at the time of non-existence,
When will it become an existent entity?
Without an entity having been produced,
It will not separate from that non-entity. ^9-147

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-148]]

If it does not separate from non-entity,
There is no opportunity for an entity to exist.
An entity also does not become non-existent,
Because it would follow that it has two natures. ^9-148

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-149]]

Thus, there is no cessation,
And there is also no entity; therefore,
All these wandering beings
Are always unborn and unceasing. ^9-149

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-150]]

Wandering beings are like a dream;
When analyzed, they are like a plantain tree.
Even between nirvāṇa and not passing into nirvāṇa,
In reality, there is no distinction. ^9-150

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-151]]

Thus, regarding entities that are empty,
What is there to gain and what is there to lose?
In being honored by anyone,
Or being utterly despised, what is there? ^9-151

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-152]]

From what do happiness and suffering arise?
What is there to be displeased about, what is there to be pleased about?
When searched for in reality,
Who is craving, and for what are they craving? ^9-152

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-153]]

When analyzed, in this world of the living,
Who is it that will die here?
What will come to be, and what has been?
And who is a relative, and who is a friend? ^9-153

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-154]]

May those like me completely grasp
That everything is like space.
Those who desire happiness for themselves,
Through causes of conflict and excitement, ^9-154

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-155]]

Become greatly agitated and delighted.
With grief, exertion, and dispute,
Mutually cutting and piercing one another,
And through misdeeds, they live in great hardship. ^9-155

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-156]]

Having come again and again to happy realms,
And having enjoyed much happiness,
After dying, they fall into the sufferings
Of the lower realms, which are long-lasting and unbearable. ^9-156

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-157]]

In existence, there are many precipices,
And there, what is not reality is like this.
Because there is also mutual contradiction there,
In existence, there is no such reality. ^9-157

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-158]]

There too, the incomparable, unbearable
Ocean of suffering is boundless.
There, one thus has little strength,
And there, the lifespan is also short. ^9-158

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-159]]

Even there, through activities for survival
And health, the weariness of hunger,
Sleep, harm, and likewise
Meaningless association with childish persons, ^9-159

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-160]]

Life passes quickly and meaninglessly,
And analysis is extremely hard to find.
Even there, as for habituation to distraction,
Where is the means to reverse it? ^9-160

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-161]]

Even there, into the great lower realm,
Māra exerts himself to make one fall.
There, wrong paths are many,
And it is hard to overcome even doubt. ^9-161

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-162]]

Once again, leisure is hard to find,
And a Buddha's appearance is extremely hard to find.
The river of afflictions is hard to abandon;
Alas, suffering will continue in succession! ^9-162

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-163]]

Even though they are thus in extreme suffering,
Those who do not see their own suffering—
These beings dwelling in the river of suffering,
Alas, are worthy of lamentation! ^9-163

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-164]]

For example, just as some bathe again and again
And then repeatedly enter a fire,
Likewise, although dwelling in extreme suffering,
They pride themselves on being happy. ^9-164

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-165]]

Thus, for those who abide acting
As if there were no aging and death,
First of all, they will be killed,
And then the unbearable fall into the lower realms will come. ^9-165

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-166]]

Thus, for those tormented by the fire of suffering,
Well-arisen from the clouds of merit—
With a rain of my own accumulation of happiness—
When will I pacify them? ^9-166

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-167]]

When, in the manner of non-apprehension,
Having respectfully gathered the accumulation of merit,
To those who are ruined by apprehension,
Will I teach emptiness? ^9-167

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the ninth chapter, the Perfection of Wisdom. ^9-a

## Chapter Ten: Dedication ^10-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-1]]

In my having composed
Engaging in the Conduct of Awakening,
Through whatever virtue there is, may all wandering beings
Engage in the conduct of awakening. ^10-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-2]]

In all directions, however many there are
Sick with the suffering of body and mind,
May they, through my merit,
Attain an ocean of happiness and joy. ^10-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-3]]

For as long as they remain in saṃsāra,
May they never decline from happiness.
May wandering beings attain a bodhisattva's
Uninterrupted happiness. ^10-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-4]]

In the world systems, the hells,
However many there are,
In them, may embodied beings
Rejoice with the happiness of Sukhavati. ^10-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-5]]

May those tormented by cold attain warmth.
From the great clouds of the bodhisattvas,
Through the boundless rivers that have arisen,
May those tormented by heat be cooled. ^10-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-6]]

May the forest of sword-leaves also
Become a dense sandalwood forest for them.
May the trunks of the śālmali trees also
Grow into wish-fulfilling trees. ^10-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-7]]

May they be beautified by the sweet calls
Of waterfowl, ruddy geese, swans, cranes, and so forth,
And may the regions of hell become delightful
With lakes possessing greatly fragrant lotuses. ^10-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-8]]

May those heaps of coals become heaps of jewels,
May the scorched earth become a leveled crystal floor,
And may the mountains of the Crushing Hell also become
Celestial palaces of offerings, filled with Sugatas. ^10-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-9]]

May the rain of embers, burning stones, and weapons
From now on become a rain of flowers,
And may that striking of one another with weapons
From now on become a tossing of flowers in play. ^10-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-10]]

May those submerged in the unfordable river that is like a fire pit,
Their flesh all destroyed and their bones the color of kunda flowers,
By the power of my virtue attain the bodies of gods,
And abide together with goddesses in the gently flowing river. ^10-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-11]]

"Why are the men of Yama, and the unbearable crows and vultures terrified here?
Whose noble power is this that dispels darkness everywhere and generates bliss and joy?"
Thinking thus, looking upward, and seeing the blazing Vajrapāṇi residing in the midst of the sky,
Freed from misdeeds by the force of intense joy, may they accompany him. ^10-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-12]]

By the falling of a rain of flowers mixed with scented water,
Seeing the fires and embers of hell extinguished with a hiss,
And thinking, "What is this sudden satisfaction with bliss?"
May the hell beings behold Padmapāṇi. ^10-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-13]]

"Friends, cast away fear and come quickly here to us!
By whose power we are freed from all suffering and the force of joy arrives,
He who thoroughly protects all wandering beings, who has given rise to bodhicitta and affection—
Who is this blazing youth with a topknot who arrives to make us fearless?" ^10-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-14]]

"You, look at this one whose lotus feet the crowns of a hundred gods worship,
Whose eyes are moistened with compassion, and upon whose head a rain of multitudes of flowers falls,
Endowed with delightful tiered palaces and thousands of goddesses proclaiming songs of praise!"
Upon seeing Mañjuśrī like this, may the hell beings now cry out in joy. ^10-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-15]]

Likewise, by my roots of virtue, upon seeing Samantabhadra and other
Bodhisattvas' unobstructed clouds, and the blissful,
Cool, fragrant rain they let fall,
May those sentient beings of hell become manifestly joyful. ^10-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-16]]

May animals be freed
From the fear of eating one another,
And just like the humans of Uttarakuru,
May the hungry ghosts become happy. ^10-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-17]]

Of the noble Avalokiteśvara,
By the stream of milk flowing from his hands,
May the hungry ghosts be satisfied,
And having bathed, may they always be cooled. ^10-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-18]]

May the blind see with their eyes,
And may the deaf always hear sounds.
Just like the goddess Māyādevī,
May pregnant women also give birth without harm. ^10-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-19]]

May the naked obtain clothing,
And the hungry obtain food;
May the thirsty obtain water
And delicious drinks. ^10-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-20]]

May the destitute obtain wealth;
May those who are wretched with sorrow obtain joy;
May the despondent be restored in mind
And attain perfect steadfastness. ^10-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-21]]

However many sick sentient beings there are,
May they swiftly be freed from illness;
May the illnesses of wandering beings, without exception,
Never arise again. ^10-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-22]]

May the terrified be fearless;
May the bound be freed;
May the powerless be endowed with power,
And may their minds be tender toward one another. ^10-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-23]]

For all travelers,
May all directions be auspicious;
Whatever purpose they travel for,
May it be accomplished without effort. ^10-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-24]]

May those who board boats and large ships
Accomplish what they have in mind,
And having safely reached the water's shore,
May they rejoice together with their relatives. ^10-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-25]]

May those who wander lost on desolate paths
Meet with fellow travellers,
And without fear of bandits, tigers, and the like,
May they proceed easily, without fatigue. ^10-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-26]]

In fearful, pathless wildernesses and the like,
Children, the elderly, the unprotected,
And those asleep, intoxicated, or utterly intoxicated—
May the gods protect them. ^10-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-27]]

Freed from all states of non-leisure,
Endowed with faith, wisdom, and compassion,
And perfect in sustenance and conduct,
May they always remember their lifetimes. ^10-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-28]]

Just like the treasury of space,
May all have inexhaustible enjoyments;
Without dispute and without harm,
May they act independently. ^10-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-29]]

Whatever sentient beings have little splendor,
May they have great splendor;
Whatever ascetics have poor forms,
May they attain perfect, beautiful forms. ^10-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-30]]

However many women there are in the world,
May they become men;
May the lowly attain high status,
And may their pride be subdued. ^10-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-31]]

By this merit of mine,
may all sentient beings without exception,
having abandoned all misdeeds,
always perform virtue. ^10-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-32]]

May they never part from bodhicitta,
and devote themselves to the conduct of awakening;
may they be completely embraced by the Buddhas,
and abandon the actions of Māra. ^10-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-33]]

May all those sentient beings
have immeasurably long lives;
may they always live happily,
and may even the sound of death not be heard. ^10-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-34]]

With groves of wish-fulfilling trees,
filled with Buddhas and children of the Buddhas
proclaiming the sweet Dharma,
may all directions be filled! ^10-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-35]]

Everywhere may the ground
be free of pebbles and the like,
as level as the palm of a hand,
and remain of the soft nature of lapis lazuli. ^10-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-36]]

In all the circles of retinues that exist,
may many bodhisattvas,
with their own excellence,
adorn the surface of the earth and remain there. ^10-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-37]]

By all embodied beings, from birds,
From trees and all rays of light,
And even from the sky, may the sound of the Dharma
Be heard unceasingly. ^10-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-38]]

May they always meet with the Buddhas
And the children of the Buddhas.
With boundless clouds of offerings,
May they make offerings to the teacher of wandering beings. ^10-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-39]]

May the gods also send rain at the proper time,
And may the harvests be abundant.
May kings act in accordance with the Dharma,
And may the world also flourish. ^10-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-40]]

May medicines be endowed with potency,
And may the recitation of secret mantras be accomplished.
May ḍākinīs, rākṣasas, and the like
Be endowed with minds of compassion. ^10-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-41]]

May no sentient being suffer,
May they not be afraid or despised,
And may no one ever be unhappy. ^10-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-42]]

May monasteries, adorned with reading
And recitation, be well established.
May the Saṅgha always be in harmony,
And may the purposes of the Saṅgha be accomplished. ^10-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-43]]

May monks who desire to train
Also attain solitude.
Having abandoned all distractions,
May they meditate with workable minds. ^10-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-44]]

May nuns be endowed with requisites
And abandon quarreling and harm.
Likewise, may all who have taken ordination
Never let their ethical discipline decline. ^10-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-45]]

May those whose ethical discipline has been broken become weary of it,
And always exhaust their misdeeds.
Having also attained a happy realm,
May their ascetic discipline not decline even there. ^10-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-46]]

May wise persons be honored
And also receive alms.
May their continuums be completely pure,
And may they be renowned in all directions. ^10-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-47]]

Not experiencing the suffering of the lower realms,
And even without hardship,
With bodies superior to those of the gods,
May they swiftly become Buddhas. ^10-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-48]]

May all sentient beings repeatedly
Make offerings to all the Buddhas,
And through the inconceivable happiness of the Buddhas,
May they always be endowed with happiness. ^10-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-49]]

May the bodhisattvas
Accomplish what they intend in their minds for the welfare of others.
Whatever the Protectors intend,
May that be attained by sentient beings. ^10-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-50]]

Likewise, may the Pratyekabuddhas
And the Śrāvakas also be happy. ^10-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-51]]

May I too, through the kindness of Mañjuśrī,
Until I attain the Ground of Perfect Joy,
Always remember my past lifetimes
And attain ordination. ^10-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-52]]

Even with simple food,
May I live endowed with vitality.
In all my lifetimes,
May I attain the perfect circumstances to dwell in a solitary place. ^10-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-53]]

Whenever I wish to see him,
Or even wish to ask a slight question,
May I behold the Protector Mañjuśrī himself
Without any hindrance. ^10-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-54]]

In order to accomplish the welfare of all sentient beings
As far as the limits of space in the ten directions,
Just as Mañjuśrī acts,
May my conduct also be like that. ^10-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-55]]

As long as space remains,
And as long as wandering beings remain,
So long may I too remain,
To dispel the suffering of wandering beings. ^10-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-56]]

Whatever the suffering of wandering beings may be,
May it all ripen upon me.
Through the Saṅgha of bodhisattvas,
May wandering beings enjoy happiness. ^10-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-57]]

The sole medicine for the suffering of wandering beings,
The source from which all happiness arises,
May the teaching, together with gain and honor,
Remain for a long time. ^10-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-58]]

Through whose kindness a virtuous intellect arises,
I prostrate to Mañjuśrī.
Through whose kindness I have flourished,
I also prostrate to the spiritual friend. ^10-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-a]]

The tenth chapter, called Dedication, from Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva. ^10-a
