## 1. Chapter One: Explaining the Benefits of the Mind of Awakening ^1-0

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-1]]

I bow respectfully to the buddhas, their spiritual children,
who embody the truth, and all worthy ones.
I will briefly explain, in accordance with the scriptures,
how to engage in the vows of the spiritual children of the buddhas. ^1-1

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-2]]

I will not state anything here that has not been said before.
Nor do I possess skill in composition.
Therefore, I have no intention of benefiting others.
I composed this for the sake of cultivating my own mind. ^1-2

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-3]]

Through these words, the strength of my faith
will temporarily increase for the sake of cultivating good deeds.
And if others, who share my fortune,
see these words, may they be meaningful. ^1-3

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-4]]

This opportunity is very hard to find.
Having attained the ability to accomplish human purpose,
if one does not accomplish what is beneficial in this life,
how will this opportunity arise again later? ^1-4

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-5]]

Just as a flash of lightning briefly illuminates
a dark, cloudy night,
likewise, rarely, through the power of the buddhas,
does a virtuous state of mind arise. ^1-5

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-6]]

Therefore, consider that good deeds are weak,
while misdeeds are powerful and unbearable.
What other virtue, besides the perfect Mind of Awakening,
could overcome them? ^1-6

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-7]]

The mighty Sages, who reflected deeply for many eons,
reflected that this alone is beneficial.
By this, countless beings
easily attain supreme happiness. ^1-7

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-8]]

Wishing to overcome hundreds of cyclic existence’s sufferings,
wishing to remove beings’ unhappiness,
and wishing to experience hundreds of joys,
one should always hold fast to the Mind of Awakening. ^1-8

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-9]]

When the Mind of Awakening arises, in a single moment,
those wretched ones bound in the prison of cyclic existence
are called spiritual children of the buddhas,
and become worthy of homage by gods and humans alike. ^1-9

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-10]]

Like the supreme elixir that transforms base metals into gold,
having taken this impure body, it transforms it into the immeasurable,
precious body of a buddha.
Therefore, hold fast to what is called the Mind of Awakening. ^1-10

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-11]]

The sole guide of beings, with immeasurable wisdom,
upon proper examination, finds it precious.
Therefore, those who wish to be free from the states of suffering
should firmly hold fast to the precious Mind of Awakening. ^1-11

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-12]]

All other good deeds, like a plantain plant,
produce fruit and then perish.
But the tree of the Mind of Awakening always
produces fruit and grows inexhaustibly. ^1-12

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-13]]

Even if one has committed extremely unbearable misdeeds,
relying on a hero, like relying on one who dispels great fear,
why would the wise not rely on that
which quickly liberates one? ^1-13

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-14]]

Therefore, it certainly burns away great misdeeds
in a single moment, like the fire at the end of an eon.
Its immeasurable benefits
were explained by Maitreya, the wise and excellent one. ^1-14

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-15]]

To summarize, the Mind of Awakening
should be understood in two aspects:
the aspiring mind of awakening
and the engaging mind of awakening. ^1-15

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-16]]

Just as one knows the difference
between wishing to go and actually going,
likewise, the wise should understand
the difference between these two in order. ^1-16

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-17]]

From the aspiring mind of awakening,
great fruit arises in cyclic existence,
but it does not continuously produce merit
in the same way as the engaging mind. ^1-17

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-18]]

From the moment one correctly takes
that resolve,
the irreversible resolve,
to liberate all countless beings, ^1-18

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-19]]

from that moment on, even when asleep
or careless, the strength of merit
continuously arises in abundance,
equal to space. ^1-19

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-20]]

This was taught by the buddhas themselves
for the benefit of humble beings,
in response to a reasonable request
from the bodhisattva Bhadrapāla. ^1-20

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-21]]

If one merely thinks,
"May I relieve the headache of a single being,"
that person, endowed with the intention to help,
possesses immeasurable merit. ^1-21

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-22]]

So what need is there to speak
of one who wishes to remove the immeasurable unhappiness
of every single being,
and to accomplish immeasurable good qualities for each? ^1-22

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-23]]

Whether it be a father or a mother,
who possesses such a benevolent mind?
Whether it be gods or sages,
does even Brahma possess this? ^1-23

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-24]]

If, in the past,
for one's own sake,
one has not even dreamed of such a thought,
how could it arise for the sake of others? ^1-24

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-25]]

That intention for the benefit of beings,
which does not arise even for one's own sake in others,
this precious and exceptional mind,
is an unprecedented wonder that has arisen. ^1-25

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-26]]

What measure can there be
for the merit of this precious mind,
which is the cause of joy for all beings,
and the elixir for the suffering of beings? ^1-26

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-27]]

If merely intending to benefit
is superior to worshipping the buddhas,
what need is there to speak of the effort
to bring about the happiness of all beings without exception? ^1-27

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-28]]

Though beings wish to abandon suffering,
they rush directly into suffering itself.
Though they wish for happiness, through ignorance,
they destroy their own happiness like an enemy. ^1-28

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-29]]

Whoever wishes to satisfy with all happiness
those who are deprived of happiness
and filled with much suffering,
and to cut off all suffering, ^1-29

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-30]]

and also removes delusion—
where is there a virtue equal to that?
Where is there such a friend?
Where is there such merit? ^1-30

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-31]]

If even one who repays kindness
is temporarily worthy of praise,
what need is there to speak of a bodhisattva
who acts kindly without being asked? ^1-31

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-32]]

People honor as virtuous one who offers
a small amount of ordinary food to beings,
giving it only briefly each time, though regularly,
or satisfying them for half a day with disdain. ^1-32

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-33]]

What need is there to speak of the continuous generosity
that fulfills all the wishes
of countless beings for a long time,
leading to the unsurpassed happiness of the buddhas? ^1-33

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-34]]

The Sage taught that if anyone
generates an evil thought towards such a spiritual child,
that person will remain in hell for as many eons
as the number of evil thoughts generated. ^1-34

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-35]]

However, if one generates a pure mind,
its fruit will increase even more.
Even with great effort, no misdeeds arise
for the spiritual children, and good deeds naturally increase. ^1-35

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-36]]

I bow to the body of one in whom
that precious and supreme mind has arisen.
I take refuge in that source of happiness,
who, even when harmed, brings about happiness. ^1-36

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-a]]

This concludes the first chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "Explaining the Benefits of the Mind of Awakening." ^1-a

## 2. Chapter Two: Confession of Misdeeds ^2-0

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-1]]

To take hold of the precious Mind of Awakening,
I offer excellent homage to the buddhas, the sacred Dharma,
the pure jewels, and the spiritual children,
who are oceans of good qualities. ^2-1

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-2]]

Whatever flowers and fruits exist,
whatever kinds of medicine exist,
whatever worldly jewels exist,
and whatever pure, delightful water exists, ^2-2

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-3]]

precious mountains, and likewise,
forests and places that are secluded and delightful,
trees adorned with blossoms,
and trees whose branches bend with fine fruits, ^2-3

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-4]]

and in the realms of gods and others, fragrances,
incense, wish-granting trees, and jewel trees,
various kinds of uncultivated crops,
and other ornaments worthy of offering, ^2-4

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-5]]

lakes and ponds adorned with lotuses,
with swans uttering very sweet and delightful sounds,
all those unowned things,
reaching to the ends of the boundless expanse of space, ^2-5

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-6]]

I take them in my mind and offer them well to the Supreme Sage,
together with his spiritual children.
May the supreme recipients, the Great Compassionate Ones,
consider me with kindness and accept these offerings of mine. ^2-6

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-7]]

I am devoid of merit and greatly poor;
I have no other wealth for offerings.
Therefore, may the Protectors, who intend the benefit of others,
accept these for my sake, by their own power. ^2-7

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-8]]

To the buddhas and their spiritual children, I
permanently offer my entire body.
May the Supreme Beings completely accept me.
With reverence, I will become your servant. ^2-8

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-9]]

Since I am completely accepted by you,
I will be fearless in cyclic existence and benefit beings.
I will truly turn away from former misdeeds,
and I will never commit other misdeeds again. ^2-9

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-10]]

In a very fragrant bathing chamber,
with a crystal floor, clear and gleaming, spread out,
with delightful pillars blazing with jewels,
and canopies shimmering with pearls, ^2-10

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-11]]

I offer ritual bathing to the buddhas and their spiritual children,
with many jewel vases filled with delightful, pure water,
accompanied by various songs
and musical instruments. ^2-11

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-12]]

Then, I dry their bodies with unparalleled garments,
pure and imbued with sweet fragrance.
After that, I offer them excellent, dyed,
very fragrant, supreme robes. ^2-12

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-13]]

With various fine, delicate, and soft garments,
and hundreds of supreme ornaments,
I adorn Noble Samantabhadra, Mañjuśrī,
Avalokiteśvara, and others. ^2-13

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-14]]

With supreme fragrances, whose sweet scent
fills the triple world, I anoint the bodies of all the mighty Sages,
who are radiant and blazing,
like refined, polished gold. ^2-14

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-15]]

To the mighty Sages, the supreme recipients of offerings,
I offer delightful Mandarava flowers, lotuses,
utpalas, and all other fragrant flowers,
and well-arranged, delightful garlands. ^2-15

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-16]]

I also offer to them clouds of incense,
with supreme, captivating, pervasive fragrance.
I also offer to them various divine foods,
consisting of edibles and drinks. ^2-16

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-17]]

I also offer jewel lamps,
with golden lotuses arranged in rows.
On the ground, spread and anointed with incense,
I scatter delightful loose flowers. ^2-17

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-18]]

I also offer immeasurable palaces, filled with delightful praises,
adorned with beautiful, blazing pearl and jewel hangings,
which are boundless ornaments of space,
to those whose nature is compassion. ^2-18

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-19]]

Beautiful jewel parasols with golden handles,
whose fringes are adorned with delightful ornaments,
well-shaped and lovely to behold, hoisted aloft,
I always offer to all the mighty Sages. ^2-19

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-20]]

Furthermore, may clouds of offerings,
with delightful, sweet musical sounds,
arise everywhere, individually,
satisfying the sufferings of beings. ^2-20

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-21]]

May showers of jewels, flowers, and so forth,
continuously fall
upon all the sacred Dharma jewels,
stupas, and statues. ^2-21

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-22]]

Just as Mañjuśrī and others
made offerings to the buddhas,
likewise, I offer homage to the buddhas,
the Protectors, and their spiritual children. ^2-22

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-23]]

To the oceans of good qualities, I
offer praise with oceans of verses of praise.
May clouds of sweet praises
definitely arise everywhere. ^2-23

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-24]]

To all the buddhas who passed in the three times,
together with the Dharma and the supreme assembly,
I bow with bodies
as numerous as all the atoms of the universe. ^2-24

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-25]]

I bow to the foundations of the Mind of Awakening,
and to the stupas.
Likewise, I bow to the masters, teachers,
and the supreme practitioners. ^2-25

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-26]]

Until I attain awakening,
I go for refuge to the buddhas.
Likewise, I go for refuge to the Dharma
and the assembly of bodhisattvas. ^2-26

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-27]]

To the fully enlightened Buddhas and bodhisattvas,
who reside in all directions,
and who embody great compassion,
I join my palms and pray: ^2-27

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-28]]

From beginningless cyclic existence,
in this life or in others,
whatever misdeeds I unknowingly committed,
or caused to be committed, ^2-28

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-29]]

overwhelmed by delusion and confusion,
and whatever I rejoiced in,
having seen those faults,
I confess them to the Protectors from the heart. ^2-29

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-30]]

Whatever harm I committed with body, speech,
and mind, driven by troubling emotions,
towards the Three Jewels,
my parents, or other suffering beings, ^2-30

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-31]]

whatever extremely unbearable wrongdoings
I, a wrongdoer tainted by many faults,
have committed,
I confess all of them to the guides. ^2-31

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-32]]

I will die before
my misdeeds are purified.
Please protect me swiftly,
so that I may definitely be freed from this. ^2-32

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-33]]

This unreliable Lord of Death
does not wait for what is done or undone.
Therefore, everyone, whether sick or not,
cannot rely on fleeting life. ^2-33

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-34]]

I did not understand that I must
abandon everything and depart.
For the sake of friends and enemies,
I committed various misdeeds. ^2-34

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-35]]

My enemies will cease to exist,
and my friends will also cease to exist.
I too will cease to exist,
and likewise, everything will cease to exist. ^2-35

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-36]]

Just like experiences in a dream,
whatever things were enjoyed,
those will become objects of memory.
All that has passed will not be seen. ^2-36

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-37]]

For the time being, in this very life,
many friends and enemies have passed away.
The unbearable misdeeds committed for their sake
remain before me. ^2-37

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-38]]

I did not realize
that I am fleeting.
Therefore, through delusion, attachment, and hatred,
I committed many kinds of misdeeds. ^2-38

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-39]]

This life continuously diminishes,
never staying for a day or night.
If there is no increase from elsewhere,
why would I not die? ^2-39

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-40]]

While I sit on my bed,
surrounded by all my relatives and friends,
I will experience the sensations of departing life
all by myself. ^2-40

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-41]]

When seized by the Lord of Death's messengers,
what good are relatives, what good are friends?
If merit alone can protect me at that time,
I did not rely on it. ^2-41

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-42]]

Protectors, I, being careless,
did not realize this great fear.
For the sake of this impermanent life,
I accumulated many misdeeds. ^2-42

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-43]]

Even someone led to a place of execution today
becomes terrified.
If their face appears dry, with bad eyes, and so forth,
and seems different from before, ^2-43

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-44]]

what need is there to speak of one
seized by terrifying messengers of the Lord of Death,
afflicted by a great illness,
and utterly wretched? ^2-44

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-45]]

"Who will protect me well from this great fear?"
With wide, terrified eyes,
they search for refuge
in all four directions. ^2-45

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-46]]

Having seen that there is no refuge in any direction,
they then become utterly despondent.
If there is no refuge in that situation,
what will I do then? ^2-46

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-47]]

Therefore, to the buddhas, the Protectors of beings,
who strive for the sake of protecting beings,
and who are powerful and dispel all fears,
I go for refuge from this very day. ^2-47

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-48]]

Likewise, I truly go for refuge to the Dharma,
which is realized by them
and dispels the fears of cyclic existence,
and to the assembly of bodhisattvas. ^2-48

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-49]]

I, terrified by fear,
offer myself to Samantabhadra.
I offer my body
to Mañjuśrī. ^2-49

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-50]]

To Protector Avalokiteśvara,
whose compassionate conduct is unfailing,
I cry out with a wretched voice.
Please protect me, a wrongdoer! ^2-50

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-51]]

To Noble Ākāśagarbha,
Kṣitigarbha,
and all the greatly compassionate Protectors,
I cry out from my heart, seeking refuge. ^2-51

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-52]]

I go for refuge to Vajrapāṇi,
who, upon seeing them, causes the messengers of the Lord of Death
and other enemies to flee in terror
in all four directions. ^2-52

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-53]]

In the past, I transgressed your words.
Now, having seen great fear,
I go for refuge to you.
Please quickly dispel my fear. ^2-53

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-54]]

If one must act according to a doctor's words
even when afraid of an ordinary illness,
what need is there to speak of one
constantly afflicted by the illness of hundreds of faults, such as attachment? ^2-54

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-55]]

That single fault can destroy
all people dwelling on earth.
If no other medicine to cure them
can be found anywhere, ^2-55

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-56]]

then to disregard the words of the all-knowing doctor,
who extracts all piercing pains,
is an act of extreme delusion
and a cause for scorn. ^2-56

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-57]]

If one must be careful
even on a small, ordinary precipice,
what need is there to speak of
a long precipice, falling for thousands of miles? ^2-57

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-58]]

It is not right to sit happily,
thinking, "I will not die today."
The time when I will cease to exist
will certainly come. ^2-58

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-59]]

Who will grant me fearlessness?
How will I definitely be freed from this?
If I will certainly cease to exist,
how can my mind be at ease? ^2-59

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-60]]

What remains of me
from what I experienced and perished in the past?
I clung to that,
and transgressed the words of my guru. ^2-60

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-61]]

If I must abandon this life,
and likewise, my relatives and friends,
and go somewhere alone,
what good are all my friends and enemies? ^2-61

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-62]]

Suffering arises from unwholesome deeds.
"How will I definitely be freed from that?"
It is right for me to think
only of this, day and night. ^2-62

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-63]]

Whatever misdeeds I committed,
whether inherently harmful or prohibited,
due to my ignorance and delusion,
I confess all of them. ^2-63

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-64]]

Before the Protectors, directly,
with palms joined, and with fear of suffering,
I repeatedly prostrate,
and confess all those misdeeds. ^2-64

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-65]]

May the guides consider my misdeeds
as faults.
Since this is not good,
I will never commit them again. ^2-65

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^2-a]]

This concludes the second chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "Confession of Misdeeds." ^2-a

## 3. Chapter Three: Taking Hold of the Mind of Awakening ^3-0

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-1]]

I rejoice in the good deeds
that bring rest from the suffering of all beings
in the lower realms,
and in the happiness of those who suffer. ^3-1

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-2]]

I rejoice in the good deeds accumulated
that are the cause of awakening.
I rejoice in the definite liberation
of embodied beings from the suffering of cyclic existence.
I also rejoice in the awakening of the protectors
and the bodhisattvas on the paths. ^3-2

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-3]]

I rejoice with joy
in the ocean of good deeds—the Mind of Awakening—
that brings happiness to all beings,
and in the acts that benefit beings. ^3-3

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-4]]

I bow with palms joined to the buddhas in all directions
and pray:
“Please light the lamp of the teachings
for beings bewildered by the darkness of suffering.” ^3-4

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-5]]

I bow with palms joined to the buddhas who wish to pass beyond sorrow
and pray:
“Please do not abandon these blind beings,
but remain for countless eons.” ^3-5

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-6]]

Having done all these things,
whatever good deeds I have accumulated,
may all the suffering of all beings
be removed by them. ^3-6

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-7]]

For as long as there are sick beings,
until their illnesses are cured,
may I be medicine, a doctor,
and their nurse. ^3-7

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-8]]

May I rain down food and drink,
removing the suffering of hunger and thirst.
During the eons of famine,
may I become food and drink. ^3-8

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-9]]

For beings who are destitute and poor,
may I become an inexhaustible treasure.
May all kinds of necessities
appear before them. ^3-9

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-10]]

My body, as well as my possessions,
and all my good deeds of the three times,
I give away without reservation
to accomplish the benefit of all beings. ^3-10

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-11]]

By giving up everything, one passes beyond sorrow.
My mind accomplishes passing beyond sorrow.
Giving up everything is the same for all,
but giving to beings is supreme. ^3-11

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-12]]

I have given this body to all embodied beings,
to do with it as they please.
Therefore, they are free to always kill, insult,
beat, or do whatever else they wish. ^3-12

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-13]]

Even if they make sport of my body,
or make it the object of their mockery and derision,
I have already given this body away,
so what is the use of fussing over it? ^3-13

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-14]]

Let them do whatever actions
will not harm them.
May no one ever act pointlessly
by focusing on me. ^3-14

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-15]]

If anyone becomes angry or faithful
by focusing on me,
may that itself always
be the cause for accomplishing all their aims. ^3-15

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-16]]

Whoever speaks ill of me,
or harms others,
or slanders them,
may all of them attain awakening. ^3-16

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-17]]

May I be a protector for the unprotected,
a guide for those on the path,
a boat,
a raft, and a bridge for those who wish to cross. ^3-17

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-18]]

May I be an island for those desiring an island,
a resting place for those desiring a resting place.
May I become a servant for all
beings who wish for a servant. ^3-18

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-19]]

May I become a wish-fulfilling jewel, an excellent vase,
an accomplished spell, a great medicine,
a wish-granting tree,
and a wish-granting cow for embodied beings. ^3-19

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-20]]

Like the great elements of earth and so forth,
and like space, may I always
be the basis of various kinds of sustenance
for countless beings. ^3-20

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-21]]

Likewise, for all beings
reaching to the ends of space,
until all pass beyond sorrow,
may I be the cause of their sustenance. ^3-21

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-22]]

Just as the buddhas of the past
generated the Mind of Awakening,
and remained in the bodhisattva training
in that sequence, ^3-22

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-23]]

likewise, for the benefit of beings,
I will generate the Mind of Awakening.
And likewise, I will train
in the training in that sequence. ^3-23

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-24]]

In this way, the wise one,
having taken up the excellent Mind of Awakening,
strengthens the mind as follows
to also cause it to flourish: ^3-24

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-25]]

Today my life is fruitful.
I have perfectly obtained human existence.
Today I am born into the family of the buddhas.
Today I have become a spiritual child of the buddhas. ^3-25

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-26]]

Now, by whatever means, I will
undertake actions befitting my family.
I will act so that this pure, noble family
is not defiled. ^3-26

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-27]]

Just as a blind person
might find a jewel in a heap of rubbish,
likewise, by some chance,
this Mind of Awakening has arisen in me. ^3-27

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-28]]

This is the supreme nectar
that conquers the Lord of Death for beings.
This is also the inexhaustible treasure
that removes the poverty of beings. ^3-28

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-29]]

This is also the supreme medicine
that perfectly cures the illnesses of beings.
It is a resting tree for beings
who wander and are weary on the road of existence. ^3-29

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-30]]

It is a bridge that delivers
all beings from the lower realms.
It is the moon that has risen
to clear away the torment of the disturbing emotions of beings. ^3-30

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-31]]

It is the great sun that entirely removes
the confusion and blurred vision of beings.
It is the essence of butter that emerged
from churning the milk of the sacred teachings. ^3-31

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-32]]

For the great guests—beings traveling on the road of existence
who wish to enjoy the delights of happiness—
this is the supreme source of happiness,
and it satisfies them. ^3-32

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-33]]

Today, before all the protectors, I have
invited all beings to buddhahood
and to happiness in the interim.
Therefore, gods, demigods, and others, rejoice! ^3-33

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^3-a]]

This concludes the third chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "Taking Hold of the Mind of Awakening." ^3-a

## 4. Chapter Four: Teaching on Carefulness ^4-0

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-1]]

Having firmly taken hold of the Mind of Awakening,
the spiritual children of the buddhas
should always strive without straying
from the training. ^4-1

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-2]]

Whatever has been undertaken rashly,
or whatever has not been properly examined,
even if a vow has been made,
it is right to examine whether to do it or abandon it. ^4-2

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-3]]

What the buddhas and their spiritual children
have examined with great wisdom,
and what I myself have examined and re-examined—
what reason is there to turn away from that? ^4-3

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-4]]

If, having made such a vow,
I do not accomplish the task,
having deceived all those beings,
what will my destiny be like? ^4-4

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-5]]

It is said that if a person
merely thinks of giving even a small, ordinary thing,
but does not give it,
that person will become a hungry ghost. ^4-5

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-6]]

If, having sincerely invited
all beings to unsurpassed happiness,
I then deceive them all,
how can I go to happy realms? ^4-6

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-7]]

Even if a person abandons the Mind of Awakening,
the way they are set free
is inconceivable karma,
known only by the all-knowing. ^4-7

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-8]]

For a bodhisattva, this
is the heaviest of downfalls.
If this occurs,
it diminishes the benefit for all beings. ^4-8

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-9]]

If anyone obstructs this merit
even for a moment,
having diminished the benefit for beings,
their lower realms will be endless. ^4-9

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-10]]

If the happiness of a single being
is destroyed, I myself will be harmed.
Needless to say, if the happiness of countless
beings, filling all of space, is destroyed! ^4-10

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-11]]

Thus, if powerful downfalls
and the powerful Mind of Awakening
alternate in cyclic existence,
it will take a long time to attain the stages. ^4-11

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-12]]

Therefore, just as I have vowed,
I will respectfully accomplish it.
If I do not strive from now on,
I will go lower and lower. ^4-12

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-13]]

Countless buddhas, who benefit all beings,
have passed.
But I, due to my own faults,
was not within the sphere of their healing. ^4-13

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-14]]

If I continue to act in the same way,
it will be the same again and again.
In the lower realms, I will experience
illness, bondage, cutting, piercing, and so forth. ^4-14

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-15]]

When will I obtain
such a rare human body,
the appearance of a buddha,
faith, and the ability to practice virtue? ^4-15

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-16]]

This life, free from illness,
with food and without harm,
is fleeting and deceptive;
the body is like a temporary loan. ^4-16

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-17]]

With such conduct,
I will not obtain a human body.
If I do not obtain a human body,
there will be only misdeeds and no good deeds. ^4-17

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-18]]

If, when I have the fortune to practice virtue,
I do not perform good deeds,
and am bewildered by all the suffering of the lower realms,
what will I be able to do then? ^4-18

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-19]]

If I have not performed good deeds,
and have accumulated misdeeds,
even for a hundred million eons,
I will not even hear the word "happy realms." ^4-19

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-20]]

Therefore, the Blessed One taught
that a human body is extremely hard to obtain,
like a turtle putting its neck
through the hole of a yoke in a vast ocean. ^4-20

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-21]]

If even a single moment of misdeed
can cause one to dwell in the lowest hell for an eon,
needless to say, if misdeeds accumulated in beginningless cyclic existence
will prevent one from going to happy realms! ^4-21

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-22]]

Even after experiencing that much,
one is not set free.
For while experiencing it,
other misdeeds arise. ^4-22

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-23]]

If, having found such an opportunity,
I do not cultivate virtue,
there is no greater deception than this,
and no greater foolishness than this. ^4-23

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-24]]

If, having understood this,
I later become lazy due to delusion,
at the time of death,
great sorrow will arise. ^4-24

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-25]]

If the unbearable fires of hell
burn my body for a long time,
my mind will surely be tormented
by the blazing fire of unbearable regret. ^4-25

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-26]]

If, having somehow found
this extremely rare and beneficial ground,
I, being endowed with knowledge,
am again led to hell, ^4-26

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-27]]

it is as if I have been deluded by a spell.
I simply have no mind here.
I do not know what deludes me;
what is within me? ^4-27

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-28]]

Enemies such as hatred and craving
do not possess hands or feet.
Yet, in some way, though not brave or wise,
they have made me their slave. ^4-28

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-29]]

While dwelling in my mind,
they harm me with delight.
Not to be angry at them, but to endure—
this is inappropriate patience, a cause for scorn. ^4-29

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-30]]

Even if all gods and demigods
rise up as my enemies,
they cannot lead me
into the fire of the lowest hell. ^4-30

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-31]]

But this powerful enemy, defilement,
when it encounters anyone, even Mount Meru
will be reduced to ashes, leaving nothing.
It casts me into that in a single instant. ^4-31

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-32]]

My enemy, defilement,
has been an enemy continuously for a beginningless time.
No other enemy
can last for such a long time. ^4-32

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-33]]

If I serve my friends respectfully,
they all bring benefit and happiness.
But if I rely on defilements,
they again inflict suffering and harm. ^4-33

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-34]]

How can I be fearless and joyful in cyclic existence
if this enemy, which has been a continuous foe for so long,
the sole cause for the increase of all harm,
is truly established in my heart? ^4-34

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-35]]

How can I have happiness
if these enemies, who are the guardians of the prison of cyclic existence,
and have become executioners in hell and so forth,
dwell in the net of attachment in my mind? ^4-35

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-36]]

As long as I have not truly conquered this enemy,
I will not abandon my striving here.
If those puffed up with pride
become angry even at a minor hindrance, they cannot sleep until it is conquered. ^4-36

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-37]]

If, when defilements, which are naturally mortal and cause suffering,
are arrayed on the battlefield, one wishes to conquer them forcefully,
and disregards the suffering of being struck by arrows, spears, and weapons,
and does not retreat without accomplishing the purpose, ^4-37

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-38]]

then, for me, who strives to conquer
this enemy, which is the cause of all suffering,
and is naturally an enemy,
needless to say, I will not become disheartened or despondent by any of the hundreds of causes of suffering! ^4-38

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-39]]

If a meaningless enemy inflicts wounds,
and one cherishes them as if they were ornaments on the body,
then for me, who strives to accomplish a great purpose,
why should suffering be a harm? ^4-39

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-40]]

If fishermen, hunters, farmers, and so forth,
who only think of their own livelihood,
endure the harm of cold, heat, and so forth,
why should I not endure it for the happiness of beings? ^4-40

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-41]]

When I vowed to liberate
all beings, filling the ten directions of space,
from defilements,
I myself was not yet free from defilements. ^4-41

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-42]]

How is it not madness
to speak without knowing my own measure?
Therefore, I must always
be unwavering in conquering defilements. ^4-42

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-43]]

I must cling to this,
hold a grudge, and wage battle.
Such defilements
are nothing but the destroyers of defilements. ^4-43

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-44]]

Even if I am burned and killed,
or my head is cut off,
I will never surrender
to the enemy of defilements. ^4-44

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-45]]

An ordinary enemy, even if driven from one land,
will dwell in another land,
gather strength, and return from there.
The enemy of defilement is not like that. ^4-45

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-46]]

Defilements are abandoned by the eye of wisdom.
Once removed from my mind, where will they go?
Where will they dwell and return to harm me?
Clinging to the mind is merely striving for myself. ^4-46

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-47]]

Defilements do not dwell in objects, nor in the sense faculties, nor in between.
Nor are they elsewhere. So where do these things dwell that harm all beings?
This is like an illusion. Therefore, abandon fear in your heart and strive to know.
Why do they needlessly inflict harm on me in hell and so forth? ^4-47

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-48]]

Having reflected in this way,
I must strive to accomplish the training as explained.
If a patient does not listen to the doctor's words,
how can that patient, needing treatment, be cured by medicines? ^4-48

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^4-a]]

This concludes the fourth chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "Teaching on Carefulness." ^4-a

## 5. Chapter Five: Guarding Awareness ^5-0

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-1]]

Those who wish to uphold the training
must guard their minds with great effort.
If this mind is not guarded,
the training cannot be upheld. ^5-1

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-2]]

Just as an untamed, wild elephant
inflicts immeasurable harm,
a wild, untamed mind-elephant
does not inflict such harm in this world. ^5-2

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-3]]

If the mind-elephant is firmly bound
with the rope of mindfulness,
all fears will cease,
and all good deeds will come to hand. ^5-3

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-4]]

Tigers, lions, elephants, bears,
snakes, and all enemies,
the guardians of the hell realms,
and likewise sorcerers and demons— ^5-4

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-5]]

By binding this single mind,
all those are bound.
By taming this single mind,
all those are tamed. ^5-5

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-6]]

The Buddha taught that all these fears
and immeasurable suffering
arise from the mind.
This is truly what he taught. ^5-6

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-7]]

Who intentionally created
the weapons of the hell beings?
Who made the burning iron ground?
From what did those heaps of fire arise? ^5-7

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-8]]

The Sage taught that all such things
are the result of harmful thoughts.
Thus, in the three realms,
there is no other fear than the mind. ^5-8

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-9]]

If giving to the poor
is the perfection of generosity,
and if there are still poor beings, then how
did the protectors perfect generosity before? ^5-9

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-10]]

The perfection of generosity is taught
as the mind of relinquishment,
giving away all possessions with fruitful intent
to all beings. Therefore, it is the mind itself. ^5-10

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-11]]

Wherever fish and other creatures
are driven away so they are not killed,
when the intention to desist is attained,
it is taught as the perfection of discipline. ^5-11

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-12]]

Unruly beings are like space;
they cannot be overcome.
If this single angry mind is conquered,
it is like conquering all those enemies. ^5-12

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-13]]

How could there be enough leather
to cover all the surface of the earth?
It is like covering the entire earth
with just a piece of leather for a shoe sole. ^5-13

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-14]]

Likewise, I cannot turn away
external things.
I should turn away this mind of mine;
what need is there to turn away others? ^5-14

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-15]]

What fruit arises from generating a single clear mind,
like that of Brahmā and other gods?
The fruit of actions of body and speech
is not like that due to weaker conduct. ^5-15

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-16]]

Even if one performs all recitations
and ascetic practices for a long time,
the wise teach that what is done with a distracted mind
is meaningless. ^5-16

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-17]]

Whoever does not know this secret of the mind,
the supreme essence of the teachings,
even if they wish to attain happiness and conquer suffering,
they wander aimlessly without purpose. ^5-17

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-18]]

Therefore, this mind of mine
must be well-held and well-guarded.
What use are many disciplines
except for the discipline of guarding the mind? ^5-18

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-19]]

If one lives amidst wild, untamed people,
one must carefully guard the wound,
just as one guards a wound with care.
Likewise, one must always guard this wound of the mind. ^5-19

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-20]]

If one is afraid of a minor wound's suffering
and guards the wound with care,
why would one not guard the mind's wound
from the fears of crushing and dismemberment? ^5-20

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-21]]

If one lives with such conduct,
whether living among bad people
or among women,
the steadfast discipline will not be harmed. ^5-21

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-22]]

My gains, respect,
body, and livelihood may be lost,
and other good deeds may be lost,
but the mind must never be lost. ^5-22

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-23]]

To those who wish to guard their minds,
I implore you with joined palms:
"Guard mindfulness and awareness
with all your effort!" ^5-23

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-24]]

Just as people afflicted by illness
are powerless in all tasks,
likewise, those whose minds are disturbed by delusion
are powerless in all tasks. ^5-24

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-25]]

Even hearing, contemplating, and cultivating
with a mind that lacks awareness,
like water in a leaky pot,
it does not remain in memory. ^5-25

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-26]]

Many learned, faithful,
and diligent individuals,
marred by the fault of lacking awareness,
fall into downfalls. ^5-26

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-27]]

Robbers of unawareness,
following the loss of mindfulness,
even if merit is accumulated,
they go to lower realms as if stripped by thieves. ^5-27

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-28]]

This multitude of troubling emotions, like robbers,
seeks an opening.
Once an opening is found, they steal good deeds
and destroy the life of happy realms. ^5-28

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-29]]

Therefore, never let mindfulness
depart from the gate of the mind.
Even if it departs, recall the harms of lower realms
and bring it back close. ^5-29

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-30]]

From associating with a teacher,
from the instructions of a master,
and from fear, mindfulness easily arises
in those who are fortunate and respectful. ^5-30

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-31]]

The Buddhas and bodhisattvas
possess unobstructed vision everywhere.
"I always dwell in the presence
of all of them," ^5-31

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-32]]

Thinking thus, one feels shame,
respect, and fear, and likewise caution.
Therefore, the recollection of the Buddhas
also arises repeatedly in that person. ^5-32

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-33]]

When mindfulness remains at the gate of the mind
for the sake of guarding,
then awareness will arise,
and what was lost will also return. ^5-33

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-34]]

First, knowing that this mind
is flawed in this way,
at that time, I must remain
as inert as a piece of wood. ^5-34

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-35]]

I must never look
aimlessly at distractions.
With focused attention, I must always
lower my gaze and look. ^5-35

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-36]]

For the sake of resting my gaze,
I may occasionally look in a direction.
If someone appears in my sight,
I should look and offer a welcome. ^5-36

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-37]]

To check for dangers on the road and so forth,
I should repeatedly look in the four directions.
After resting, I should turn my head and
look behind me. ^5-37

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-38]]

Having checked ahead and behind,
I should go or come.
In all situations,
I should act with appropriate knowledge. ^5-38

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-39]]

"I will remain in this way with my body,"
Having made this resolve, then
"How is this body
positioned?" I should examine it. ^5-39

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-40]]

The wild elephant of the mind
must be examined with all effort,
so that it does not slip from the great pillar
of focusing on the teachings, just as it was bound. ^5-40

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-41]]

"Where is this mind of mine engaged,
so that diligence in meditation
does not slip away for even a single moment?"
Thus, I should individually examine my mind. ^5-41

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-42]]

If one is unable to do so due to fear,
celebrations, or similar circumstances, one may do as one pleases.
For example, at times of giving,
it is taught that one should be indifferent to discipline. ^5-42

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-43]]

Whatever one has decided to do,
one should not think of anything else.
With a mind focused on that,
one should accomplish that very thing for the time being. ^5-43

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-44]]

If so, everything will be well-accomplished.
Otherwise, neither will be achieved.
Then, minor afflictions
will not increase due to unawareness. ^5-44

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-45]]

When engaging in various idle chatter
and many amazing spectacles,
one should abandon
attachment to them. ^5-45

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-46]]

If one digs aimlessly in the ground, cuts grass,
or draws in the dirt, and so forth,
recalling the Buddha's training,
one should abandon it immediately with fear. ^5-46

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-47]]

Whenever one wishes to act
or wishes to speak,
one should first examine one's own mind
and remain with steady awareness. ^5-47

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-48]]

Whenever one's mind desires attachment
or wishes to be angry,
one should not act or speak,
but remain like a piece of wood. ^5-48

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-49]]

Whether prone to laughter or mockery,
or if arrogant and puffed up,
or with the intention of exposing faults,
or if flattering and deceitful, ^5-49

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-50]]

Whenever one is eager to praise oneself,
or to criticize others,
or is prone to scolding or regret,
at that time, one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-50

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-51]]

If one desires gain, respect, or fame,
or wishes to seek attendants,
or if one's mind desires service,
at that time, one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-51

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-52]]

If the mind arises wishing to neglect others' benefit,
or wishing to pursue one's own benefit,
or wishing to speak,
then one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-52

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-53]]

If impatience, laziness, fear,
recklessness, idle talk,
or a mind clinging to one's own side arises,
at that time, one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-53

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-54]]

Thus, having examined the mind
that strives for all troubling emotions and meaningless things,
the hero should firmly grasp
the antidote at that time. ^5-54

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-55]]

With full certainty and deep faith,
steadfastness, respect, and humility,
shame, and fear,
calmly and striving to please others. ^5-55

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-56]]

Not wearied by the childish
desires that conflict with each other,
and with compassion, thinking, "These troubling emotions
have arisen in this mind." ^5-56

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-57]]

Regarding blameless matters,
having exerted control over oneself and beings,
I must always hold this mind
as if I were an illusion, without a self. ^5-57

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-58]]

Having repeatedly reflected on the supreme opportunity
obtained after a long time,
I must hold this mind, like Mount Meru,
completely unmoving. ^5-58

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-59]]

Vultures, attached to flesh,
drag it back and forth everywhere.
If your mind is not displeased,
why fuss now? ^5-59

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-60]]

Having taken this body as "mine,"
why do you guard it, mind?
If you and this body are separate,
what can it do for you? ^5-60

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-61]]

Foolish mind, why do you not
take a clean wooden image?
What good is it to guard this machine
of impure aggregates, which is decaying? ^5-61

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-62]]

First, with your own wisdom,
separate this layer of skin.
Then, with the weapon of wisdom,
separate the flesh from the network of bones. ^5-62

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-63]]

Having separated the bones,
look into the marrow.
"What essence is there in this?"
You yourself should examine. ^5-63

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-64]]

If, after striving and searching,
you find no essence there,
then why, with attachment,
do you guard this body? ^5-64

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-65]]

You cannot eat what is impure,
nor can you drink blood.
You cannot lick the intestines;
what can the body do for you? ^5-65

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-66]]

Therefore, it is right for foxes and vultures
to guard this for food.
This human body
is merely something to be used. ^5-66

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-67]]

Even if you guard it,
the merciless Lord of Death
will seize it and give it to birds and dogs.
What can you do then? ^5-67

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-68]]

If one does not give clothes and so forth
to a servant who cannot be employed,
if this body goes to others even after being fed,
why do you lavishly feed it? ^5-68

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-69]]

Having given wages to this body,
now let it do its own work.
One should not give everything
to this body without benefit. ^5-69

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-70]]

Regarding the body as merely a boat
for coming and going,
transform it into a wish-fulfilling body
to accomplish the benefit of beings. ^5-70

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-71]]

Since you are independent,
always keep a smiling face.
Completely abandon frowning and scowling,
and be gentle with beings, like a friend. ^5-71

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-72]]

Do not carelessly throw down
chairs and other things with noise.
Do not forcefully open doors;
always be quiet and joyful. ^5-72

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-73]]

Herons, cats, and robbers,
who move stealthily and silently,
accomplish their desired aims.
The Sage always practiced likewise. ^5-73

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-74]]

Skillfully encouraging others,
and speaking unsolicited beneficial words,
one should respectfully accept them with bowed head,
and always be a student to everyone. ^5-74

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-75]]

To all well-spoken words,
one should say, "Well said!"
If one sees someone performing good deeds,
one should generate gladness with praise. ^5-75

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-76]]

One should praise good qualities in secret,
and if good qualities are praised, one should echo them.
If one's own good qualities are praised,
one should recognize them as good qualities. ^5-76

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-77]]

All endeavors are for joy,
which is hard to obtain even for a price.
Therefore, one should experience the joy
of others' good qualities. ^5-77

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-78]]

Here, no harm comes to me,
and great happiness comes to others.
Displeasure brings unhappiness and suffering,
and great suffering comes to others. ^5-78

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-79]]

When speaking, one should be confident, connected,
clear, and pleasing.
One should speak gently and appropriately,
having abandoned attachment and hatred. ^5-79

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-80]]

When looking at beings with one's eyes,
one should look with sincerity and loving-kindness,
thinking, "Through relying on these very beings,
I will attain buddhahood." ^5-80

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-81]]

Always inspired by aspiration,
or inspired by the antidote,
virtue and benefit arise in those who suffer,
and great good deeds are generated. ^5-81

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-82]]

Being skilled and energetic,
I must always perform tasks.
I must not rely on anyone
for any task. ^5-82

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-83]]

The perfections of generosity and so forth
should be practiced, advancing higher and higher.
One should not give up the greater for the lesser.
Primarily, one should consider the benefit of others. ^5-83

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-84]]

Having understood this, one should always
strive for the benefit of others.
The compassionate ones, who see far,
permit even what is forbidden in such cases. ^5-84

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-85]]

One should share with those who have fallen,
the helpless, and those upholding discipline.
One should eat a moderate amount,
and give away everything except the three robes. ^5-85

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-86]]

One should not harm this body,
which is the practice of the sacred teachings, for trivial reasons.
If one acts in this way, the aspirations of beings
will quickly be fulfilled. ^5-86

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-87]]

One should not give up this body
without pure compassion.
By all means, in this and future lives,
it should be used as a cause for accomplishing great aims. ^5-87

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-88]]

Do not teach the teachings to the disrespectful,
to those with covered heads who are not ill,
to those with umbrellas, staffs, or weapons,
or to those with covered heads. ^5-88

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-89]]

To the lowly, do not teach the profound and vast teachings,
nor to women without men present.
One should always act with equal respect
towards the teachings of the lesser and the supreme. ^5-89

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-90]]

One should not apply the vast teachings,
which are a vessel for the Dharma, to lesser teachings.
One should not completely abandon conduct.
One should not be misled by sutras and mantras. ^5-90

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-91]]

When discarding tooth-sticks and saliva,
one should cover them.
It is improper to discard urine and other waste
in water or on the ground. ^5-91

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-92]]

One should not eat with a full mouth,
with noise, or with an open mouth.
One should not sit with outstretched legs,
nor rub one's hands together. ^5-92

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-93]]

In conveyances, beds, and dwelling places,
one should not be alone with other women.
All those who lack faith in the world
should be avoided by seeing and inquiring. ^5-93

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-94]]

One should not indicate with a finger;
with respect, one should use the right hand.
Everyone should likewise
show the way in that manner. ^5-94

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-95]]

One should not move the hand excessively,
but move it slightly and make a sound.
By signaling with snapping fingers and so forth,
otherwise, one will be unrestrained. ^5-95

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-96]]

One should lie down facing the desired direction,
like the Protector resting in nirvana.
One should immediately apply awareness
to the resolve to rise quickly. ^5-96

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-97]]

The practices of bodhisattvas
are taught as immeasurable.
Therefore, one must definitely practice
the conduct of purifying the mind for that long. ^5-97

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-98]]

Three times a day and night,
one should recite the three aggregates.
By relying on the Buddhas and the Mind of Awakening,
one pacifies the remaining downfalls. ^5-98

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-99]]

Whether one is independent or dependent,
whatever one practices in any situation,
one should diligently train in that situation
which is taught in the training. ^5-99

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-100]]

There is nothing
that the spiritual children do not train in.
To a wise person who dwells in that way,
there is no merit that does not arise. ^5-100

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-101]]

Whether directly or indirectly,
one should not act for anything other than the benefit of beings.
For the sake of beings alone,
I dedicate everything for awakening. ^5-101

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-102]]

One should never abandon a virtuous friend,
who is skilled in the meaning of the Mahayana,
and the supreme discipline of a bodhisattva,
even for the sake of one's life. ^5-102

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-103]]

One should train according to the way of relying on a teacher
as described in the biography of Paljung.
This and other teachings taught by the Buddha
should be understood by reading the sutras. ^5-103

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-104]]

The training appears in the sutras.
Therefore, one should read the sutras.
One should first examine
the Akasagarbha Sutra. ^5-104

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-105]]

Because it extensively teaches
the practice that is always to be done,
one must definitely and repeatedly examine
the Sikṣāsamuccaya. ^5-105

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-106]]

Alternatively, for the time being, one may examine
the concise compendium of all sutras.
One should also diligently examine both works
composed by the noble Nagarjuna. ^5-106

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-107]]

Whatever is not forbidden
should be practiced.
For the sake of guarding the worldly mind,
one should truly practice what is seen in the training. ^5-107

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-108]]

Repeatedly examining the states
of body and mind—
that alone, in brief,
is the definition of guarding awareness. ^5-108

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-109]]

These things should be practiced with the body.
What is accomplished by merely speaking words?
Does merely reading medical texts
benefit sick people? ^5-109

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^5-a]]

This concludes the fifth chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "Guarding Awareness." ^5-a

## 6. Chapter Six: Teaching on Patience ^6-0

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-1]]

Good deeds accumulated over a thousand eons,
such as generosity and offerings to buddhas—
all such good conduct
is destroyed by a single moment of anger. ^6-1

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-2]]

There is no misdeed like hatred,
and no austerity like patience.
Therefore, cultivate patience diligently
in various ways. ^6-2

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-3]]

If you harbor the dart of hatred in your mind,
your mind will never experience peace.
You will not attain joy or happiness,
and you will not sleep; your mind will be unstable. ^6-3

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-4]]

Those who have been helped
by a master who gave them wealth and respect,
even they will set upon that master
if he is filled with hatred. ^6-4

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-5]]

Because of this, friends become disheartened,
and even if won over by generosity, they will not stay.
In short, no one who is angry
can ever dwell in peace. ^6-5

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-6]]

The enemy of anger
will cause such sufferings.
Whoever strives to conquer anger
will be happy in this life and the next. ^6-6

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-7]]

When you find the food of unhappiness
because something undesirable happened
or something desirable was hindered,
hatred grows strong and destroys you. ^6-7

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-8]]

Therefore, I will completely destroy
the food of that enemy.
For this enemy has no other function
than to harm me. ^6-8

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-9]]

Whatever happens, I will not
disturb my joyful mind.
Even if I am unhappy, my wishes will not be accomplished,
and my good deeds will be ruined. ^6-9

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-10]]

If something can be remedied,
what is the point of being unhappy about it?
If something cannot be remedied,
what benefit is there in being unhappy? ^6-10

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-11]]

I do not want suffering, contempt,
harsh words, or unpleasant speech,
for myself or my friends.
For my enemies, it is the opposite. ^6-11

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-12]]

Causes of happiness sometimes arise,
but causes of suffering are very many.
There is no liberation without suffering.
Therefore, mind, maintain your steadfastness. ^6-12

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-13]]

Why did I not previously
bear the meaningless suffering of hard ascetic practices,
faith, and the feelings of being burned or torn apart,
for the sake of liberation? ^6-13

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-14]]

There is nothing
that cannot be made easy through habit.
Therefore, by becoming accustomed to minor harm,
endure great harm. ^6-14

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-15]]

Have you not seen
the meaningless suffering
of snakes, flesh-flies,
hunger, thirst, and itching? ^6-15

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-16]]

I should not be delicate
about heat, cold, rain, wind,
illness, bondage, or beatings.
If I am, the harm will increase. ^6-16

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-17]]

Some people, seeing their own blood,
become even more courageous.
Some people, seeing the blood of others,
collapse and faint. ^6-17

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-18]]

This comes from the mind's steadfastness
and from past habits.
Therefore, disregard harm
and be untouched by sufferings. ^6-18

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-19]]

Even if suffering arises, the wise
should not let their minds be agitated.
When waging battle with defilements,
there is much harm in battle. ^6-19

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-20]]

Those who disregard all suffering
and conquer enemies such as hatred
are true heroes.
The rest are merely killing corpses. ^6-20

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-21]]

Furthermore, the benefits of suffering are
that weariness removes arrogance.
Compassion arises for those in cyclic existence.
One avoids misdeeds and delights in virtue. ^6-21

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-22]]

Why be angry at the great causes
of suffering such as bile,
but be angry at sentient beings?
All of them are driven by conditions. ^6-22

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-23]]

Just as this illness
arises even though one does not want it,
likewise, defilements arise forcefully
even though one does not want them. ^6-23

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-24]]

Even without intending to be angry,
people become angry aimlessly.
Even without intending to generate it,
anger likewise arises. ^6-24

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-25]]

All faults
and various misdeeds
arise from the power of conditions.
They are not independent. ^6-25

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-26]]

Those assembled conditions
also do not intend to generate anything.
Nor does that which is generated by them
intend to generate itself. ^6-26

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-27]]

That which is considered the Primal Substance
or the self that is conceived
does not arise with the specific intention
"I will arise." ^6-27

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-28]]

If it does not exist before it arises,
what is that which desires to arise at that time?
That which is always distracted by objects
also does not cease. ^6-28

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-29]]

If that self is permanent,
it is clearly inactive, like space.
Even if it meets other conditions,
it does not change, so what can be done to it? ^6-29

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-30]]

If it remains as before when acting,
what has the agent done to it?
What connection is there
to say that this is its action? ^6-30

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-31]]

Thus, everything is dependent on others.
Because of that dependence, it is not independent.
Knowing this, you will not be angry
at any phenomena, like a snake. ^6-31

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-32]]

If you say, "What is to be averted by what?
Averting is also illogical,"
it is not illogical to desire that sufferings
cease by relying on that. ^6-32

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-33]]

Therefore, if you see someone, whether enemy or friend,
doing something inappropriate,
reflect, "This arose from such conditions,"
and maintain your peace. ^6-33

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-34]]

If things happened by their own will,
since no one desires suffering,
no suffering would ever arise
for any sentient being. ^6-34

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-35]]

Through carelessness, I myself
inflict harm on myself with thorns and such.
To obtain women and such,
I become greedy and fast. ^6-35

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-36]]

Some hang themselves and jump into chasms.
They eat poison and incompatible foods.
By unwholesome actions,
they inflict harm on themselves. ^6-36

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-37]]

When defilements overpower them,
they even kill themselves.
How can they then
avoid harming others? ^6-37

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-38]]

If compassion does not arise for those
who, driven by defilements,
engage in self-harm and such,
what is the point of being angry? ^6-38

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-39]]

If harming others
is the nature of foolish people,
it is inappropriate to be angry at them,
just as it is to blame fire for its burning nature. ^6-39

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-40]]

But if that fault is temporary,
and sentient beings are naturally pure,
then it is also inappropriate to be angry,
like smoke rarely appearing in the sky. ^6-40

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-41]]

If you are angry at someone
who wields a stick or other object and strikes,
that person is also driven by hatred.
Therefore, it is appropriate to be angry at hatred itself. ^6-41

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-42]]

In the past, I inflicted
similar harm on sentient beings.
Therefore, it is appropriate that this harm
comes to me from those who harm sentient beings. ^6-42

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-43]]

The weapon and my body
are both causes of suffering.
If I remove my body from the weapon,
at whom should I be angry? ^6-43

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-44]]

If I grasp a boil
that resembles a human form,
is unbearable and painful,
at whom should I be angry for the harm? ^6-44

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-45]]

Foolish people do not desire suffering,
yet they crave the causes of suffering.
Therefore, when harm comes from their own faults,
what is there to blame others for? ^6-45

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-46]]

Just like the guardians of hell
and the sword-leaf forest,
at whom should I be angry
when this is created by my own actions? ^6-46

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-47]]

Driven by my own actions,
those who harm me arise.
If they go to hell because of this,
have I not ruined them? ^6-47

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-48]]

By relying on these,
my misdeeds greatly increase through patience.
By relying on me, they
will go to hell for a long time, suffering. ^6-48

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-49]]

I inflict harm on them,
and they benefit me.
Why then, perverse mind,
do you become angry? ^6-49

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-50]]

If I have good intentions,
I will not go to hell.
If I protect myself,
what harm has come to them here? ^6-50

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-51]]

But if I harm them in return,
they are not protected,
and my conduct is ruined.
Thus, my ascetic practice will be destroyed. ^6-51

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-52]]

The mind is not physical,
so no one can destroy it anywhere.
Because of clinging to the body,
the body is harmed by sufferings. ^6-52

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-53]]

If contempt, harsh words,
and unpleasant speech
do not harm the body,
why, mind, are you so angry? ^6-53

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-54]]

If others' dislike of me
does not consume me in this life
or the next,
why do I dislike it? ^6-54

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-55]]

If I dislike this
because it hinders my gains,
I will abandon my gains here,
but my misdeeds will remain firm. ^6-55

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-56]]

It is better for me to die right now
than to live long by wrong livelihood.
Even if I live long,
the suffering of death is the same. ^6-56

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-57]]

Someone who experiences happiness for a hundred years in a dream
and then awakens,
and someone else who experiences happiness for a moment
and then awakens— ^6-57

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-58]]

for both of them, upon awakening,
that happiness is gone.
Both a long life and a short life
are merely like that at the time of death. ^6-58

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-59]]

Even if one obtains many gains
and enjoys happiness for a long time,
like someone whose possessions are stripped by robbers,
one goes away naked and empty-handed. ^6-59

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-60]]

If you say, "I live by gains,
so I will exhaust my misdeeds and perform good deeds,"
if you become angry for the sake of gains,
will your good deeds not be exhausted and misdeeds arise? ^6-60

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-61]]

If that for which I live
is ruined,
what is the point of a life
that only commits misdeeds? ^6-61

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-62]]

If you are angry at someone who harms sentient beings
and speaks unpleasant speech,
why are you not angry in the same way
at someone who speaks unpleasant speech to others? ^6-62

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-63]]

If you tolerate lack of faith
in others who are dependent on it,
why do you not tolerate unpleasant speech
that is dependent on the arising of defilements? ^6-63

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-64]]

I should not be angry
at those who mock and destroy statues, stupas,
or the sacred teachings,
because the Buddhas and such are not harmed. ^6-64

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-65]]

Even towards those who harm
gurus, relatives, and friends,
seeing that it arises from conditions in the same way as before,
turn away from anger. ^6-65

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-66]]

If both sentient and inanimate things
cause harm,
why single out sentient beings for blame?
Therefore, endure harm. ^6-66

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-67]]

Some commit faults out of ignorance.
If some become angry out of ignorance,
who is faultless in that?
Who is it that is flawed? ^6-67

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-68]]

Why did one previously commit
the actions that cause others to be harmed?
If everything depends on karma,
why do I blame this? ^6-68

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-69]]

Seeing this, I will certainly
strive to perform good deeds
so that all beings
mutually develop loving-kindness. ^6-69

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-70]]

For example, if a house catches fire

whatever grass and such it spreads to

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-71]]

Likewise, if the fire of hatred spreads
to whatever one is attached to,
fearing that one's good deeds will be consumed,

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-72]]

If a condemned person has a limb cut off

frees one from hell, why is that not good? ^6-72

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-73]]

If I cannot endure
even this much present suffering,
why do I not avert the anger
that is the cause of hellish suffering? ^6-73

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-74]]

For the sake of desires, I have experienced
thousands of burnings and such in hell,

nor the benefit of others. ^6-74

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-75]]

This is not so harmful,
and great purposes will be accomplished.
Therefore, it is appropriate to only rejoice
in the suffering that removes the harm of beings. ^6-75

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-76]]

If, when others praise those with good qualities,
you obtain the happiness of joy,
why do you not rejoice in the same way
by praising them, mind? ^6-76

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-77]]

This happiness of your joy
is blameless and a source of happiness.
It is permitted by those with good qualities,
and it is also the supreme way to attract others. ^6-77

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-78]]

Furthermore, if you do not desire
this happiness, thinking, "They will also be happy,"

both visible and invisible good deeds will be ruined. ^6-78

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-79]]

When speaking of one's own good qualities,
one desires happiness for others.
When speaking of others' good qualities,
one does not desire happiness for oneself. ^6-79

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-80]]

Having generated the Mind of Awakening
because all beings desire happiness,
if beings find happiness themselves,
why then should one be angry? ^6-80

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-81]]

If you desire that beings
worship the three worlds and attain buddhahood,
why do you grieve
when you see even a small amount of respect? ^6-81

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-82]]

Whatever sick person you heal,

will you not be angry again instead of rejoicing? ^6-82

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-83]]

If you do not desire this for beings,
what is that awakening you desire?
If you are angry at others' prosperity,
how can you have the Mind of Awakening? ^6-83

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-84]]

If they receive it from that,
or if the patron remains in the house,
if it is not for you at all,
what does it matter whether it is given or not? ^6-84

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-85]]

Why do you abandon your good deeds and faith,
or your own good qualities?
Tell me, who would not be angry
at not holding onto what has been gained? ^6-85

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-86]]

You yourself have committed misdeeds

End of Section Two. ^6-86

If an enemy is unhappy,

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-88]]

This hook cast by the fisherman of defilements

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-90]]

Nor does my body become happy. ^6-90

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-91]]

If I understand my own purpose,
what purpose is there in that?
If I only desire mental ease,
I should also rely on ornaments and alcohol. ^6-91

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-92]]

If one loses wealth for the sake of fame
and even kills oneself,
what is the point of mere words?
To whom is that flesh good? ^6-92

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-93]]

If a sandcastle collapses,
children cry out in distress.
Likewise, if praise and fame are lost,
my mind is like a child's. ^6-93

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-94]]

For the time being, sound is inanimate,
so there cannot be a mind that praises me.
That it is known that others are glad for me—
is that a cause for gladness? ^6-94

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-95]]

Whether for others or for myself,
what benefit is there for me in others' happiness?
That joy and happiness belong to them.
I do not obtain a fraction of it. ^6-95

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-96]]

If I am happy because of their happiness,
then I should do the same for everyone.
How can I be unhappy
if I am happy for others? ^6-96

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-97]]

Therefore, that my own joy arises
because I am praised
is also inappropriate,
and is merely childish behavior. ^6-97

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-98]]

Praise and such distract me.
They also destroy weariness.
They cause envy towards those with good qualities,
and they destroy prosperity. ^6-98

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-99]]

Therefore, those who are present
to destroy my praise and such—
do I not engage with them to protect myself
from falling into the lower realms? ^6-99

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-100]]

I seek liberation,
so I do not need to be bound by gains and respect.
How can I be angry at those
who free me from my bondage? ^6-100

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-101]]

I desire to enter suffering,
and like the Buddhas' blessings,
they are the very gateway that I do not abandon.
How can I be angry at them? ^6-101

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-102]]

It is inappropriate to be angry at them,
thinking, "This hinders my good deeds."
If there is no austerity equal to patience,
do I not rely on them? ^6-102

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-103]]

If I, due to my own faults,
do not practice patience towards them,
then I merely hinder
this cause of good deeds. ^6-103

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-104]]

That without which something does not arise,
and when it exists, that something exists—
if that is its cause,
how can it be called a hindrance? ^6-104

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-105]]

A beggar who arrives on time
is not a hindrance to generosity.
One who causes renunciation
is not inappropriately called a hindrance to renunciation. ^6-105

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-106]]

There are many beggars in the world,
but those who cause harm are rare.
If one does not harm in return,
no one will cause harm. ^6-106

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-107]]

Therefore, like a treasure
found in a house without effort,
I should rejoice in my enemies
who have become companions on the bodhisattva path. ^6-107

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-108]]

Because I have accomplished this with them,
the fruit of patience
should first be offered to them.
For they are the cause of patience. ^6-108

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-109]]

If you say, "This enemy, without intending to cultivate patience,
is not worthy of offering,"
why then do you offer to the sacred teachings
that are worthy of being a cause of accomplishment? ^6-109

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-110]]

If you say, "This enemy, with the intention to harm,
is not worthy of offering,"
if I strive to benefit them like a doctor,
how can my patience be accomplished? ^6-110

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-111]]

Therefore, because patience arises
by relying on intense hatred,
that very hatred is the cause of patience,
and is therefore worthy of offering, like the sacred teachings. ^6-111

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-112]]

Therefore, the Sage declared that sentient beings
are a field of merit, and the Buddhas are a field of merit.
By pleasing these, many
have thus perfected transcendent qualities. ^6-112

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-113]]

Sentient beings and the Buddhas
are similar in accomplishing buddhahood.
Why then should one show respect to the Buddhas
but not to sentient beings? ^6-113

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-114]]

The good qualities of intention are not inherent;
they come from the result. Therefore, similarly,
sentient beings also have good qualities.
Therefore, they are equal. ^6-114

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-115]]

Whatever offering is made to those with loving-kindness
is the greatness of sentient beings.
Whatever merit is generated by faith in the Buddhas
is also the greatness of the Buddhas. ^6-115

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-116]]

Because there is a part in the accomplishment of buddhahood,
they are considered equal.
No one is equal to the Buddhas,
whose ocean of good qualities is boundless. ^6-116

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-117]]

Even if a fraction of the good qualities
of the supreme collection of good qualities
appears in someone, to offer to them,
even offering the three realms would be small. ^6-117

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-118]]

Because sentient beings have a part
in the arising of the supreme Dharma of the Buddhas,
by aligning with this much,
sentient beings become worthy of offering. ^6-118

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-119]]

Furthermore, what other way is there to repay
those unwavering friends
who perform immeasurable benefits,
except by pleasing sentient beings? ^6-119

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-120]]

Since one repays the benefit done to them
by abandoning one's body and entering the lowest hell,
therefore, even if these inflict great harm,
one should use all goodness towards them. ^6-120

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-121]]

Since my master himself
does not care for his own body,
how can I, foolish as I am,
be arrogant and not act as a servant? ^6-121

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-122]]

The Buddhas rejoice in happiness,
and are displeased when harm is done.
They are all pleased by their joy,
and harming them harms the Buddhas. ^6-122

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-123]]

Just as a body ablaze with fire
cannot be at ease with any desire,
likewise, when sentient beings are harmed,
the Great Compassionate Ones have no way to rejoice. ^6-123

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-124]]

Therefore, whatever displeasure
the Great Compassionate Ones feel because I have harmed beings,
I confess that misdeed today.
May the Buddhas forgive whatever displeasure there was. ^6-124

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-125]]

To please the Buddhas,
from now on, I will be a servant to the world.
May the Protectors of the world be pleased,
even if the multitude of beings step on my head or kill me, I will not resist. ^6-125

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-126]]

There is no doubt that these beings,
whose nature is compassion, are considered my self.
These very beings, seen as my self—
why should I not respectfully serve that Protector? ^6-126

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-127]]

This pleases the Buddhas.
This also truly accomplishes my own purpose.
This also removes the suffering of the world.
Therefore, I will always do this. ^6-127

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-128]]

For example, if a king's attendant
harms many people,
people with foresight
do not harm him in return, even if they could. ^6-128

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-129]]

For he is not alone;
the king's power is his power.
Likewise, one should not despise
any weak harm-doer. ^6-129

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-130]]

For the guardians of hell
and the compassionate ones are their allies.
Therefore, just as common people please a cruel king,
one should please sentient beings. ^6-130

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-131]]

Even if a king is angry,
does the harm of hell
that one experiences by displeasing sentient beings
cause that? ^6-131

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-132]]

Even if a king is pleased,
the buddhahood that one obtains
by pleasing sentient beings
is impossible to be granted. ^6-132

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-133]]

Let alone the future buddhahood
that arises from pleasing sentient beings,
have you not seen great glory,
fame, and happiness in this very life? ^6-133

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-134]]

In cyclic existence, through patience, one obtains
beauty and such, freedom from illness, and fame.
One lives for a very long time,
and attains the great happiness of a universal monarch. ^6-134

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^6-a]]

This concludes the sixth chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "Teaching on Patience." ^6-a

## 7. Chapter Seven: Practice of Diligence ^7-0

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-1]]

Having cultivated patience in that way, cultivate diligence.
Awakening dwells in diligence like this:
Like an unmoving place free of wind,
Merit does not arise without diligence. ^7-1

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-2]]

Diligence is enthusiasm for good deeds.
Its opposite will be explained:
Indolence, attachment to unwholesome things,
Despondency, and self-contempt. ^7-2

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-3]]

From tasting the flavor of ease and indolence,
And from craving that depends on sleep,
Weariness with the suffering of cyclic existence does not arise,
And indolence comes about. ^7-3

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-4]]

Once caught in the net of troubling emotions,
And fallen into the net of rebirth,
Having gone into the mouth of the Lord of Death,
Why do you still not know? ^7-4

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-5]]

Did you not see your own kind being gradually killed?
Yet, what is this dependence
on sleep,
Like an outcaste and a buffalo? ^7-5

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-6]]

With all paths blocked,
And the Lord of Death watching,
How can you enjoy eating?
How can you enjoy sleeping like this? ^7-6

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-7]]

You will die soon,
So accumulate good deeds for as long as you can.
At that time, even if you abandon indolence,
What will you do when it is too late? ^7-7

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-8]]

"This was not done, this was begun,
This was left half-finished."
When the Lord of Death suddenly arrives,
You will think, "Alas, I am ruined!" ^7-8

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-9]]

With eyes red and tears streaming down your swollen face
From intense grief,
And your relatives having lost hope,
You will look at the face of Yama's messengers. ^7-9

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-10]]

When you are tormented by the memory of your misdeeds,
And hear the sounds of hell,
And your body is soiled with impurity from fear,
What will you do when you are overwhelmed? ^7-10

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-11]]

If you are afraid in this life,
Like a living fish writhing,
Then needless to speak of the unbearable suffering
Of hell, which arises from misdeeds. ^7-11

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-12]]

When touched by hot water,
A young person feels intense heat.
Having committed the actions that lead to hell,
Why do you remain so happily? ^7-12

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-13]]

One who desires results without diligence,
And is delicate, experiences much harm.
Seized by death, yet like a god,
Alas, overcome by sufferings! ^7-13

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-14]]

Relying on the human boat,
Cross the great river of suffering.
This boat is hard to find again,
So, foolish person, do not sleep at this time. ^7-14

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-15]]

Having abandoned the supreme joy of the sacred Dharma,
Which is the endless cause of joy,
Why do you delight in distraction,
Frivolity, and the like, which are causes of suffering? ^7-15

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-16]]

Non-despondency, strength,
Taking up, self-mastery,
Equality of self and others,
And exchanging self and others. ^7-16

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-17]]

Do not be despondent,
thinking, "How could I attain awakening?"
For the buddhas, who speak the truth,
Declared this truth: ^7-17

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-18]]

A fly, a flesh-fly, a bee,
Or a worm, whatever kind of being,
If it cultivates the strength of diligence,
Will attain unsurpassed, hard-to-reach awakening. ^7-18

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-19]]

I, born as a human of good family,
Knowing what is beneficial and harmful,
If I do not abandon bodhisattva conduct,
Why would I not attain awakening? ^7-19

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-20]]

But if you are afraid that you must give up
Your limbs and so forth,
You are merely afraid out of ignorance,
Without weighing what is heavy and what is light. ^7-20

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-21]]

For countless eons,
You have been cut,
Pierced, burned, and torn many times,
But you will not attain awakening. ^7-21

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-22]]

The suffering I endure to accomplish awakening
Is limited,
Like the pain of lancing a wound
To remove a deeply embedded thorn. ^7-22

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-23]]

All doctors, through their treatments,
Eliminate the discomfort of illness.
Therefore, to destroy many sufferings,
Endure minor discomfort. ^7-23

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-24]]

Such ordinary medical treatments
Were not performed by the Supreme Sage.
With extremely gentle methods,
He performed countless great cures. ^7-24

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-25]]

The guide first taught giving
Vegetables and the like.
Having grown accustomed to that, later on,
One also gives one's own flesh. ^7-25

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-26]]

When the thought arises in one's body
That it is like vegetables and the like,
What difficulty is there then
In giving one's flesh and so forth? ^7-26

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-27]]

There is no suffering because misdeeds are abandoned.
There is no unhappiness because of wisdom.
For wrong thoughts
And misdeeds harm the mind and body. ^7-27

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-28]]

If the body is happy through good deeds,
And the mind is happy through wisdom,
Then even dwelling in cyclic existence for the benefit of others,
Why would compassionate beings be weary? ^7-28

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-29]]

This is said to be supreme by the hearers
Because the power of the Mind of Awakening
Exhausts former misdeeds
And gathers an ocean of merit. ^7-29

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-30]]

Therefore, mounting the horse of the Mind of Awakening,
Which dispels all weariness,
Who, being wise, would be despondent
When going from happiness to happiness? ^7-30

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-31]]

To accomplish the benefit of beings, strength,
Aspiration, confidence, joy, and relinquishment are needed.
Aspiration is generated by contemplating
The fear of suffering and its benefits. ^7-31

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-32]]

Having abandoned the opposing factors in that way,
One should strive to increase diligence
Through aspiration, confidence, joy, relinquishment,
Taking up, self-mastery, and strength. ^7-32

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-33]]

I must destroy
my own and others' faults,
And for each fault,
An ocean of eons will be exhausted. ^7-33

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-34]]

If I have not seen even a fraction
Of the effort to exhaust faults,
Then I am the source of immeasurable suffering.
Why does my heart not break? ^7-34

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-35]]

I must accomplish
my own and others' many good qualities.
If each good quality
Is cultivated for an ocean of eons, ^7-35

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-36]]

Yet I have never cultivated
Even a fraction of good qualities,
Then it is astonishing how I have wasted
This life, which is somehow obtained. ^7-36

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-37]]

I have not honored the buddhas,
Nor given the joy of great celebrations.
I have not performed actions for the teachings,
Nor fulfilled the wishes of the poor. ^7-37

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-38]]

I have not given fearlessness to the fearful,
Nor happiness to the wretched.
I have merely caused the pain of my mother's womb
And nothing but suffering. ^7-38

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-39]]

Because I lacked aspiration for the Dharma
In the past and now,
Such destitution has arisen.
Who would abandon aspiration for the Dharma? ^7-39

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-40]]

The Sage taught that aspiration
Is the root of all wholesome factors.
Its root is always
Cultivated by contemplating karmic results. ^7-40

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-41]]

Suffering, unhappiness,
Various kinds of fear,
And separation from desired things
Arise from committing misdeeds. ^7-41

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-42]]

By performing good deeds with the mind,
Wherever one goes,
There, by that merit,
One is honored with the fruit of good qualities. ^7-42

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-43]]

Misdeed-doers, though wanting happiness,
Wherever they go,
There, by those misdeeds,
They are utterly destroyed by the weapon of suffering. ^7-43

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-44]]

Dwelling in the heart of vast, fragrant, cool lotuses,
Nourished by the sweet speech of the victorious ones,
With supreme bodies born from lotuses, expanded by the light of the Sage,
One becomes a spiritual child before the victorious ones through virtue. ^7-44

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-45]]

By Yama's henchmen, utterly tormented, with all skin flayed,
Molten copper poured onto the body by intense fire,
Torn into a hundred pieces of flesh by blazing swords and spears,
And falling onto an intensely burning iron ground—this happens through many non-virtues. ^7-45

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-46]]

Therefore, aspire to virtue,
And cultivate it with reverence.
Having begun with the ritual of the Vajra Victory Banner,
One should cultivate confidence. ^7-46

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-47]]

First, having assessed one's capacity,
One should either begin or not.
Not beginning is best,
But once begun, one should not turn back. ^7-47

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-48]]

Otherwise, in other births, one becomes accustomed to it,
And misdeeds and suffering will persist.
At the time of the result, one will be inferior,
And it will not be accomplished. ^7-48

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-49]]

Confidence should be cultivated in three things:
Actions, troubling emotions, and capacity.
"I alone will do it!"
This is the confidence of action. ^7-49

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-50]]

This world, powerless over troubling emotions,
Cannot accomplish its own purpose.
Beings cannot do it as I can,
Therefore, I will do this. ^7-50

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-51]]

If others perform inferior actions,
How can I remain idle?
I will not act out of arrogance;
It is best for me to be free of arrogance. ^7-51

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-52]]

If a crow encounters a dead snake,
It acts like a garuda.
If I am weak,
Even a minor downfall will inflict harm. ^7-52

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-53]]

If one abandons effort with despondency,
Is there any escape from destitution?
By cultivating the effort of confidence,
Even a great one is hard to conquer. ^7-53

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-54]]

Therefore, with steadfast mind,
One must destroy downfalls.
If I am defeated by downfalls,
I will be a laughingstock to those who wish to conquer the three realms. ^7-54

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-55]]

I will conquer everyone,
And no one will conquer me.
I, a lion's child,
Will abide in this confidence. ^7-55

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-56]]

Those beings conquered by arrogance
Are not arrogant due to troubling emotions.
The arrogant do not fall under the sway of enemies;
Those others fall under the sway of the enemy of arrogance. ^7-56

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-57]]

One filled with the arrogance of troubling emotions
Is led to the lower realms by arrogance,
Deprived of human joys,
And becomes a servant who eats others' food. ^7-57

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-58]]

Foolish, unattractive, weak,
And always scorned.
If an ascetic filled with arrogance
Is counted among the arrogant,
Tell me, what kind of inferior person is that? ^7-58

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-59]]

Whoever cultivates confidence to conquer the enemy of arrogance,
That one is truly confident and a victorious hero.
Whoever utterly destroys the rising enemy of arrogance
Fulfills the desired results for beings, like a victorious one. ^7-59

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-60]]

Dwelling amidst the factors of troubling emotions,
One must stand firm in a thousand ways.
Like lions among jackals and the like,
One must not be overcome by the collection of troubling emotions. ^7-60

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-61]]

Even if great danger arises,
Just as one guards one's eyes,
Likewise, even if danger arises,
One must not fall under the sway of troubling emotions. ^7-61

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-62]]

Whatever action one undertakes,
Like desiring the delight of play,
One should be devoted to that action,
And rejoice, never sated with it. ^7-62

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-63]]

Even if one acts for the sake of happiness,
Whether happiness arises or not is uncertain.
If one does not perform the action itself, which becomes happiness,
How can one be happy? ^7-63

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-64]]

If one is not sated with desires,
Which are like honey on the edge of a razor,
Then why would one be sated with the peaceful
Merit that is the result of happiness? ^7-64

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-65]]

Therefore, to complete the action,
Like an elephant tormented by the midday sun,
Entering a lake when it finds one,
One should also engage in that action. ^7-65

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-66]]

If one is exhausted and connected with weariness,
One should abandon it for the sake of resuming it later.
If it is well-completed, one should give it up,
Desiring further and further. ^7-66

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-67]]

Like an old enemy encountered in battle,
One should avoid the weapon of troubling emotions,
And move to destroy
the enemies of troubling emotions. ^7-67

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-68]]

If one's sword falls in battle,
One quickly picks it up out of fear.
Likewise, if the weapon of mindfulness is lost,
One should quickly recall the fear of hell. ^7-68

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-69]]

Just as poison, relying on blood,
Spreads through the body,
Likewise, if an opportunity is found,
Faults spread through the mind. ^7-69

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-70]]

Like someone carrying a pot filled with mustard oil,
With a sword-wielder standing nearby,
Fearing death if it spills,
A practitioner should likewise be vigilant. ^7-70

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-71]]

Therefore, if a snake comes into one's lap,
Just as one quickly stands up,
Likewise, if sleep and indolence come,
One should quickly ward them off. ^7-71

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-72]]

For each fault that arises,
Reproaching oneself, one should resolve
That this will never happen to me again,
And reflect on it for a long time. ^7-72

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-73]]

"How will mindfulness become a habit
In these circumstances?"
For this reason, one should desire
Appropriate actions, whether encountered or not. ^7-73

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-74]]

Before performing any action,
With the power that is in everything,
Remembering the teaching on carefulness,
One should make oneself light and ready to rise. ^7-74

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-75]]

Just as the wind controls cotton,
Making it go and come,
Likewise, controlling oneself with enthusiasm,
One will succeed in that way. ^7-75

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^7-a]]

This concludes the seventh chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "Practice of Diligence." ^7-a

## 8. Chapter Eight: Teaching on Meditation ^8-0

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-1]]

When diligence has thus been generated,
the mind should be settled in meditation.
A person whose mind is distracted
remains in the grip of troubling emotions. ^8-1

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-2]]

By being in solitude, in body and mind,
distraction will not arise.
Therefore, abandon worldly things
and completely give up discursive thoughts. ^8-2

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-3]]

Due to attachment and craving for gain and so forth,
one does not abandon worldly things.
Therefore, completely give up these things,
and the wise should examine them in this way. ^8-3

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-4]]

Knowing that insight, perfectly endowed with calm,
completely destroys troubling emotions,
one should first seek calm, and that too
is accomplished by delight in non-attachment to worldly things. ^8-4

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-5]]

Who among the impermanent
would truly become attached to the impermanent?
For a thousand eons,
one will never see what is pleasant. ^8-5

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-6]]

If one does not see it, one will not rejoice.
The mind will not be settled in equipoise.
Even if one sees it, one will not be satisfied,
and one will be tormented by craving as before. ^8-6

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-7]]

If one becomes attached to beings,
one is completely obscured from the truth.
One also destroys the mind of weariness,
and ultimately one will be tormented by sorrow. ^8-7

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-8]]

Merely by thinking of them,
this life will pass meaninglessly.
By impermanent friends,
the eternal Dharma will also be destroyed. ^8-8

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-9]]

If one associates with foolish people,
one will certainly go to the lower realms.
If one leads them to what is not equal,
what is the use of relying on foolish people? ^8-9

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-10]]

In a single instant, one becomes a friend,
and in a moment, one also becomes an enemy.
Becoming angry at the object of joy,
it is hard to please ordinary people. ^8-10

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-11]]

If one speaks beneficently, they become angry.
One also turns away from benefit.
If one does not listen to their words,
one will go to the lower realms due to anger. ^8-11

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-12]]

They envy the high, compete with equals,
are arrogant when praised by the low,
and become angry when unpleasant things are said.
When will one receive benefit from foolish people? ^8-12

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-13]]

If one associates with foolish people,
one will certainly generate unwholesome things,
such as praising oneself and criticizing others,
and talk that delights in cyclic existence. ^8-13

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-14]]

Thus, relying on oneself and others
will only lead to ruin.
One's own purpose has not been accomplished by them,
and one will not accomplish their purpose. ^8-14

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-15]]

One should keep far away from foolish people.
If one meets them, one should please them with joy.
One should act well, merely as a stranger,
without becoming too intimate. ^8-15

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-16]]

Like a bee taking honey from a flower,
one should take only the essence of the Dharma.
One should dwell without intimacy with everyone,
as if one had never seen them before. ^8-16

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-17]]

If one harbors such pride as,
"I have much gain and respect,
and many people are fond of me,"
fear will arise after death. ^8-17

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-18]]

Therefore, whatever the deluded mind
is attached to,
that, multiplied a thousandfold,
will become suffering and arise. ^8-18

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-19]]

Therefore, the wise should not be attached.
Fear arises from attachment.
Since these things are by nature impermanent,
be steadfast and truly understand. ^8-19

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-20]]

Even if much gain arises,
and fame and renown arise,
where will one go
with the collection of gain and fame, which is uncertain? ^8-20

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-21]]

If others criticize me,
what joy is there in praise for me?
If others praise me,
what unhappiness is there in criticism? ^8-21

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-22]]

If the buddhas themselves
cannot satisfy the various inclinations of beings,
what need is there to speak of a lowly person like me?
Therefore, abandon worldly concerns. ^8-22

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-23]]

Beings criticize those who have no gain.
They speak unpleasant things to those who have gain.
How can joy arise
from those whose nature is difficult-to-please? ^8-23

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-24]]

Because foolish people's own purposes
do not arise without joy,
the buddhas taught
that no foolish person is a friend. ^8-24

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-25]]

The deer and birds in the forest
do not speak unpleasant things.
When will I dwell together
with those with whom association is pleasant? ^8-25

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-26]]

When will I dwell in a cave,
a deserted temple, or at the foot of a tree,
without looking back,
and without attachment? ^8-26

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-27]]

When will I dwell freely
in naturally spacious places,
without claiming any place as my own,
and without attachment? ^8-27

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-28]]

When will I dwell fearlessly,
holding only the bare necessities, such as an alms bowl,
and wearing clothes that no one wants,
even if this body is not hidden? ^8-28

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-29]]

When will I go to a charnel ground
and treat the bones of others
and my own body
as equally subject to decay? ^8-29

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-30]]

When will this very body of mine
become such that even foxes
will not approach it
because of its smell? ^8-30

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-31]]

Even this body, which arose alone,
and the flesh and bones born with it,
will decay and separate.
What need is there to speak of other friends? ^8-31

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-32]]

One is born alone,
and one dies alone.
If one does not take on the suffering of others,
what is the use of friends who cause hindrance? ^8-32

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-33]]

Just as travelers
take up lodging,
likewise, those who have set out on the path of cyclic existence
take up a place of rebirth. ^8-33

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-34]]

As long as this body
is not carried away by four men,
while all the world is tormented,
one should go to the forest. ^8-34

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-35]]

Having no friends and no enemies,
one should dwell alone in solitude.
Having considered oneself as already dead,
there will be no grief even if one dies. ^8-35

For the sake of those

one has also entered into danger

Those very ones,
by embracing whom one experiences supreme joy, ^8-41

are merely skeletons, nothing else.

Why, then, does one not go to nirvana,

its face is covered with clothes. ^8-43

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-44]]

having seen it removed by vultures? ^8-44

That which one completely protects

why do you, miserly one, not protect it? ^8-45

sandalwood, and ornaments to the food of others? ^8-46

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-47]]

Having seen it as merely bones,
if you are afraid even when it is unmoving,
why would you not be afraid
if it were moved by someone like a zombie? ^8-47

If feces and saliva
arise from the same food,
why do you dislike feces
but like saliva? ^8-48

Not delighting in a soft pillow
made of cotton,
the lustful, deluded by impurity,
say it is not oozing with foul smell. ^8-49

why embrace what is covered? ^8-50

If there is no attachment to impurity,

plastered with the mud of flesh? ^8-51

Having abandoned the young lotus

why does one delight in a cage of impurity
with a mind attached to impurity? ^8-56

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-57]]

If you do not want to touch
a place soiled with impurity,
how can you desire to touch
the body from which it came? ^8-57

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-58]]

If there is no attachment to impurity,
and it arose from a field of impurity,
and its seed was generated by it,
why do you embrace another in your lap? ^8-58

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-59]]

You do not desire even a tiny worm
that arose from impurity,
yet you desire a body that is the nature of much impurity,
and you desire an impure person. ^8-59

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-60]]

Not only do you not criticize
your own impurity,
but you also desire others
with craving for the impure bag of impurity. ^8-60

Even delightful camphor and so forth,

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-62]]

If there is doubt even when such impurity

in bodies discarded in charnel grounds. ^8-62

Even knowing that great fear arises
when the skin is peeled off,
how can joy arise again
for that very thing? ^8-63

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-64]]

The fragrance applied to the body

Why does one become attached to others
by that external fragrance? ^8-64

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-65]]

If it were not attached to this

apply sweet fragrance to it? ^8-65

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-66]]

What has happened to this body
with sweet-smelling sandalwood?
Why does one become attached to others
by the fragrance of others? ^8-66

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-67]]

If the body's nature is terrifying
when naked, with long hair and nails,
yellow teeth, and a foul smell,
and smeared with the smell of mud, ^8-67

this whole world is disturbed by madness. ^8-68

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-69]]

If one is disgusted in a charnel ground
merely by seeing bones,

filled with moving bones? ^8-69

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-70]]

That which has become impure
is not found without a price.
By striving for it, one experiences lavish
and hellish harm. ^8-70

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-71]]

Foolish people, unable to increase wealth,

If one's life is spent on wealth and so forth,

Some base people, full of craving,

Some are tormented by the hardships of travel

Deluded by wanting self-benefit,

Some sell their own bodies

Some lustful people have their bodies cut.

Desires will certainly be destroyed

In this and similar ways,

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-89]]

similarly, why should the notion of "I" not arise
for other beings through habit? ^8-114

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-115]]

If that is the case, then when one acts for the benefit of others,
no wonder or pride will arise,
just as one does not hope for a reward
after eating one's own food. ^8-115

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-116]]

Therefore, just as one protects oneself
from the slightest unpleasantness,
likewise, one should cultivate the mind of protection
and compassion for beings. ^8-116

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-117]]

Therefore, Protector Avalokiteshvara,
with great compassion,
blessed his own name
to remove the fear of cyclic existence for beings. ^8-117

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-118]]

One should not turn back from hardship,
for by the power of habit,
even the fear of hearing a name
will become displeasure if it is not there. ^8-118

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-119]]

Whoever wishes to quickly protect
themselves and others,
should practice the supreme secret
of exchanging self and others. ^8-119

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-120]]

Whoever is attached to this body,
fear arises from minor dangers.
Who would not hate
that body, like an enemy, which causes fear? ^8-120

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-121]]

Whoever wishes to perform the ritual
of healing this body, which is afflicted by hunger, thirst, and other illnesses,
kills birds, fish, and wild animals,
and waits on the road. ^8-121

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-122]]

Whoever kills their own father and mother
for profit and respect,
and steals the property of the Three Jewels,
will be burned in the lowest hell. ^8-122

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-123]]

What wise person would desire,
protect, and honor that body?
Who would not look at it like an enemy
and despise it? ^8-123

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-124]]

If one asks, "What is used if given?"
the self-interested mind is demonic.
If one asks, "What is given if used?"
the other-interested mind is divine. ^8-124

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-125]]

If one harms others for oneself,
one will be tormented in hell and so forth.
If one harms oneself for others,
one attains all excellent qualities. ^8-125

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-126]]

Whoever desires to be exalted
will go to the lower realms, become bad, and foolish.
If one transfers that to others,
one will attain happy realms and respect. ^8-126

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-127]]

If one uses others for oneself,
one will experience being a servant and so forth.
If one uses oneself for others,
one will experience being a lord and so forth. ^8-127

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-128]]

All the happiness in the world
arises from the desire for others' happiness.
All the suffering in the world
arises from the desire for one's own happiness. ^8-128

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-129]]

What need is there to speak much?
Look at the difference
between a foolish person acting for self
and a buddha acting for the benefit of others. ^8-129

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-130]]

If one does not truly exchange
one's own happiness for the suffering of others,
one will not attain buddhahood,
and there will be no happiness in cyclic existence. ^8-130

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-131]]

Let alone the next world,
even in this life, the purpose of a servant not working
and a lord not giving rewards
will not be accomplished. ^8-131

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-132]]

Abandoning all excellent happiness,
both visible and invisible,
and due to causing suffering to others,
the deluded take on unbearable suffering. ^8-132

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-133]]

Whatever harm,
fear, and suffering exist in the world,
all of that arises from self-clinging.
What will that great demon do to me? ^8-133

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-134]]

If one does not abandon oneself,
one cannot abandon suffering,
just as if one does not abandon fire,
one cannot abandon burning. ^8-134

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-135]]

Therefore, to pacify one's own harm
and to pacify the suffering of others,
one should give oneself to others
and take on others as oneself. ^8-135

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-136]]

"I have become dependent on others,"
you, mind, should certainly know.
Now, you should not think of anything else
except for the benefit of all beings. ^8-136

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-137]]

It is not appropriate for the dependent eyes and so forth
to accomplish their own purpose.
It is not appropriate for the eyes and so forth
to act wrongly towards their purpose. ^8-137

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-138]]

Therefore, prioritize beings.
Whatever one sees in one's own body,
one should take that
and use it to benefit others. ^8-138

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-139]]

Having made the lowly and so forth into oneself,
and having made oneself into others,
one should cultivate envy, competition, and pride
with a mind free of discursive thoughts. ^8-139

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-140]]

"This one is honored, but I am not.
I have not found this, but this one has.
This one is praised, but I am criticized.
This one is happy, but I am suffering." ^8-140

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-141]]

"I am doing the work,
but this one dwells in happiness.
This one is great in the world,
but I am known as lowly and without good qualities." ^8-141

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-142]]

"What is the use of being without good qualities?
I am completely endowed with good qualities.
There are those who are inferior to this one,
and there are also those who are superior to me." ^8-142

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-143]]

Ethical discipline, views, and other things
are not under my control due to the power of troubling emotions.
I must heal myself as much as possible,
and I must accept harm. ^8-143

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-144]]

If I am not to be healed,
why do I despise myself?
What is the use of their good qualities to me?
I am endowed with good qualities. ^8-144

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-145]]

This one, who dwells in the mouth of the cruel lower realms,
without compassion for beings,
wishes to overcome the wise
with pride in external good qualities. ^8-145

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-146]]

Looking at those who are equal to me,
I will certainly accomplish, even through conflict,
my gain and respect,
so that I may be superior to them. ^8-146

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-147]]

My good qualities
should be evident to all the world,
and no one should hear
of their good qualities. ^8-147

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-148]]

My faults should be hidden,
and I should be honored, not this one.
I have found good gain now,
and I should be respected, not this one. ^8-148

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-149]]

I will look with delight for a long time
at the wrongdoings committed by this one.
I will make all beings laugh
and criticize each other. ^8-149

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-150]]

"This troubling emotion also
is said to compete with me.
Are they equal in hearing, wisdom,
form, lineage, or wealth?" ^8-150

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-151]]

Having heard of my good qualities,
thus proclaimed to all,
one should fully enjoy the happiness
of joy that makes the hair stand on end. ^8-151

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-152]]

If this one has gain,
and if they work for me,
I will give them only enough to live,
and I will take it by my own strength. ^8-152

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-153]]

I will deprive this one of happiness
and constantly inflict harm upon them.
This one has harmed me
in cyclic existence through all hundreds of lifetimes. ^8-153

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-154]]

You, mind, desiring your own purpose,
even though countless eons have passed,
you have only accumulated suffering
through such great toil. ^8-154

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-155]]

Therefore, you should certainly
engage in the benefit of others.
The Buddha's words are unfailing,
and its benefits will be seen later. ^8-155

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-156]]

If you had done this work
in earlier times,
it would not be possible for you to be in such a state
of not being the perfect Buddha. ^8-156

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-157]]

Therefore, just as you clung to "I"
in the semen and drops of blood
of others,
similarly, cultivate that for others. ^8-157

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-158]]

Having greatly considered others,
whatever appears in one's own body,
one should take that
and use it to benefit others. ^8-158

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-159]]

"I am happy, but others are not.
I am high, but others are low.
I am beneficial, but others are not."
Why do you not envy yourself? ^8-159

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-160]]

"I will be free of happiness,
and the suffering of others will be inflicted upon me."
When this happens, "What is this doing?"
one should examine one's own faults. ^8-160

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-161]]

Even the wrongdoings committed by others
should be transformed into one's own faults.
Even if one's own wrongdoing is small,
it should be proclaimed to many people. ^8-161

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-162]]

By speaking of the superior fame of others,
eclipse one's own fame.
I should be used for all purposes,
like the lowest servant. ^8-162

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-163]]

"This one is by nature flawed,
so do not praise them for temporary good qualities.
Ensure that no one
ever knows their good qualities." ^8-163

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-164]]

In short, whatever harm you have done to others
for your own sake,
let that harm
fall upon yourself for the sake of beings. ^8-164

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-165]]

This one should not be strengthened
in a stubborn way.
It should be established with shame and fear,
like a new bride, restrained. ^8-165

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-166]]

"Do this, dwell in this way,
and do not do that."
This one should be controlled in this way.
If it transgresses, it should be punished. ^8-166

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-167]]

If, even though thus advised,
you, mind, do not act in that way,
then, by relying on all faults,
you yourself will certainly be punished. ^8-167

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-168]]

The former time when you ruined me
was different.
I see you now; where will you go?
All your arrogance will be destroyed. ^8-168

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-169]]

Now, abandon the thought
that "I still have my own purpose."
I have sold you to others.
Do not be sad; offer your milk and flesh. ^8-169

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-170]]

If, having become careless, you
do not give yourself to beings,
you will certainly be given by me
to the guardians of hell. ^8-170

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-171]]

Thus, for a while,
you will suffer for a long time because I have given you.
I will remember my grievances
and destroy your self-centered thoughts. ^8-171

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-172]]

If I desire happiness,
I should not delight in myself.
If I desire to protect myself,
I should always protect others. ^8-172

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-173]]

The more this body is
Protected and cared for,
The more it falls into the trap
Of being demanding. ^8-173

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-174]]

If the desires of such a demanding body
Cannot be fulfilled
by all the things on this earth,
Then who can fulfill its desires? ^8-174

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-175]]

Unfulfilled desires cause troubling emotions
And disappointment.
But for one who is free of all dependence,
Their happiness is endless. ^8-175

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-176]]

Therefore, do not give an opening
For the body's desires to grow.
That which does not cling to pleasant things
Is truly good. ^8-176

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-177]]

This unbearable, impure form
Ends up as ashes in the end,
Unmoving and moved by others.
Why do I cling to it as a self? ^8-177

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-178]]

Whether I live or die, it is better.
What use is this machine to me?
What difference is there between this and a log?
Alas, it does not remove pride! ^8-178

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-179]]

By caring for the body,
I accumulate needless suffering.
What use is this body, which is like a piece of wood,
To attachment and anger? ^8-179

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-180]]

If I protect this body,
Or if vultures and the like eat it,
If there is no attachment or hatred,
Why should I be attached to it? ^8-180

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-181]]

If I become angry when insulted,
And pleased when praised,
If I do not understand that very thing,
Then what effort have I made? ^8-181

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-182]]

If I consider that this body is what I desire,
And that I am its friend,
Since everyone desires their own body,
Why should I not rejoice in that? ^8-182

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-183]]

Therefore, I will abandon this body without attachment
For the benefit of beings.
Although it has many faults,
I will accept it as a tool for actions. ^8-183

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-184]]

Therefore, childish behavior is enough.
I will follow the wise,
And remembering the teaching on carefulness,
I will ward off sleep and dullness. ^8-184

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-185]]

Therefore, to remove obscurations,
I will turn my mind from wrong paths,
And always, with proper focus,
I will settle myself in meditation. ^8-185

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^8-a]]

This concludes the eighth chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "Teaching on Meditation." ^8-a

## 9. Chapter Nine: The Perfection of Wisdom ^9-0

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-1]]

All these limbs of practice,
The Buddha taught for the sake of wisdom.
Therefore, whoever wishes to calm
Suffering should generate wisdom. ^9-1

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-2]]

Conventional reality and ultimate,
These are considered the two truths.
Ultimate reality is not an object of the mind;
The mind is said to be conventional. ^9-2

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-3]]

In this regard, two kinds of people are seen:
Practitioners and ordinary people.
Among these, ordinary people
Are refuted by practitioners. ^9-3

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-4]]

Practitioners, too, by differences in their minds,
Are refuted by those of higher stages.
Both, however, rely on examples
And do not analyze for the sake of results. ^9-4

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-5]]

Ordinary people perceive things
And conceive them as truly existent.
Since they are not like illusions,
Practitioners and ordinary people dispute here. ^9-5

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-6]]

Direct perception of forms and so forth
Is by convention, not by valid cognition.
It is false, like the convention of purity
In impure things and so forth. ^9-6

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-7]]

For the purpose of guiding ordinary people,
The Buddha taught that things exist,
But they are not momentary in ultimate reality.
If you say this contradicts conventional reality: ^9-7

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-8]]

The conventional reality of practitioners is faultless;
Compared to ordinary people, they perceive ultimate reality.
Otherwise, if a woman is conceived as impure,
This would be refuted by ordinary people. ^9-8

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-9]]

How can merit from an illusory Buddha
Be like that from a truly existing one?
If beings are illusory,
How can they be reborn after death? ^9-9

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-10]]

As long as conditions are assembled,
So long will an illusion also arise.
How can beings be truly existent
Because they last for a long time? ^9-10

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-11]]

Killing an illusory person and so forth
Does not create misdeeds, because there is no mind.
But for an illusory being endowed with a mind,
Merit and misdeeds arise. ^9-11

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-12]]

Because mantras and so forth lack power,
An illusory mind does not arise.
The illusion that arises from various conditions
Is also of various kinds. ^9-12

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-13]]

A single condition is not capable of everything;
Such a thing does not exist anywhere.
If ultimate reality is nirvana,
And cyclic existence is conventional, then ^9-13

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-14]]

Even a buddha would return to cyclic existence,
So what would bodhisattva conduct accomplish?
If the stream of conditions is unbroken,
An illusion would also not cease. ^9-14

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-15]]

But when the stream of conditions ceases,
It does not arise even conventionally.
When delusion itself does not exist,
By what would an illusion be perceived? ^9-15

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-16]]

When you have no illusion,
What then is perceived?
If something else truly exists,
That form is the mind itself. ^9-16

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-17]]

When the mind itself is an illusion,
What sees what, and by what?
Even the Buddha, the protector of the world,
Taught that the mind does not see the mind. ^9-17

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-18]]

Just as the blade of a sword
Cannot cut itself, so too the mind.
Just as a lamp truly
Illuminates its own nature, ^9-18

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-19]]

A lamp does not illuminate itself,
Because it is not obscured by darkness.
Like a crystal that is blue
Does not depend on anything else for its blueness. ^9-19

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-20]]

Likewise, some things are seen
To depend on others, and some do not.
It is not blue, but it is blue;
It does not make itself blue. ^9-20

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-21]]

If one says that a lamp illuminates itself,
Knowing it by knowledge,
By what knowledge is it known
That the mind is inherently clear? ^9-21

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-22]]

When nothing is seen by anyone,
Whether clear or unclear,
Like the gait of a barren woman's daughter,
Speaking of it is meaningless. ^9-22

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-23]]

If there is no self-awareness,
How can consciousness be remembered?
When something else is experienced, it is remembered
Through connection, like the poison of a mouse. ^9-23

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-24]]

Since it is seen to be endowed with other conditions,
If it is self-illuminating,
From applying magical eye-salve,
Seeing a pot does not make it the eye-salve itself. ^9-24

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-25]]

Seeing, hearing, and knowing
Are not to be refuted here.
Here, what is to be averted
Is the true conception that causes suffering. ^9-25

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-26]]

An illusion is not other than the mind,
And if it is not other, it is impermanent.
How can it not be other if it truly exists?
If it is not other, it does not truly exist. ^9-26

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-27]]

Just as an illusion is not true,
So too the mind that perceives it.
If cyclic existence has a truly existing basis,
Then it would be like space elsewhere. ^9-27

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-28]]

Since non-existence relies on existence,
How can it be an agent and be endowed with it?
Your mind, without a companion,
Would be utterly alone. ^9-28

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-29]]

When the mind is free from grasping,
Then all are buddhas.
Even so, what benefit is there
In conceiving of mere mind? ^9-29

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-30]]

Even if one knows something to be like an illusion,
How can defilements cease?
When it comes to an illusory woman,
Even the creator himself generates attachment. ^9-30

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-31]]

The creator has not abandoned
The latent patterns of defilements regarding the object of knowledge.
Therefore, when he sees it,
The latent patterns of emptiness are weak. ^9-31

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-32]]

Through cultivating the latent patterns of emptiness,
The latent patterns of truly existing things will be abandoned.
And through cultivating "nothing exists,"
That too will later be abandoned. ^9-32

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-33]]

When the object to be investigated
Is not perceived as "nothing exists,"
How can non-existence, devoid of a basis,
Remain before the mind? ^9-33

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-34]]

When both existence and non-existence
Do not remain before the mind,
Then, with no other aspect,
It utterly calms into non-perception. ^9-34

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-35]]

Just as a wish-granting jewel and wish-granting tree
Completely fulfill hopes,
So too, by the power of aspirations,
Buddhas appear in forms to tame beings. ^9-35

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-36]]

For example, a shrine to a garuda,
Once built, even after it has passed away
For a long time, still pacifies
Poisons and so forth. ^9-36

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-37]]

Likewise, a buddha shrine built
In accordance with bodhisattva conduct,
Even after the bodhisattva has passed into nirvana,
Accomplishes all purposes. ^9-37

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-38]]

How can making offerings to something inanimate
Be fruitful?
Because it is taught that
Those who remain and those in nirvana are equally worthy. ^9-38

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-39]]

Whether conventionally or ultimately,
It is appropriate, and the result is from scripture.
For example, just as
There is a result from an existing Buddha. ^9-39

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-40]]

One is liberated by seeing the truth;
What is accomplished by seeing emptiness?
Because scripture teaches that
Without this path, there is no awakening. ^9-40

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-41]]

If the Mahayana is not established,
How is your scripture established?
Because this is established for both,
But for you, it is not established at first. ^9-41

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-42]]

The condition by which one believes in it
Is the same for the Mahayana.
If it is true because others desire it,
Then the Vedas and so forth would also be true. ^9-42

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-43]]

If you say the Mahayana is disputed,
Then your scriptures, like those of non-Buddhists,
And other scriptures, being disputed
By yourself and others, should be abandoned. ^9-43

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-44]]

If the root of the teaching is the state of being a monk,
Then the state of being a monk is also difficult to maintain.
And for those whose minds are focused on objects,
Nirvana is also difficult to attain. ^9-44

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-45]]

If one is liberated by abandoning defilements,
Then one would immediately attain it.
Even without defilements,
The power of karma is seen in them. ^9-45

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-46]]

If you say that grasping craving
Does not exist, as is certainly the case,
Why would craving not exist
Even if it is not a defilement, like general delusion? ^9-46

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-47]]

Craving arises from feelings,
And feelings exist even in them.
A mind focused on objects
Will remain in some state. ^9-47

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-48]]

A mind devoid of emptiness,
Having ceased, will arise again,
Like the absorption of non-perception.
Therefore, one should cultivate emptiness. ^9-48

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-49]]

If you consider any statement
That enters the sutras to be taught by the Buddha,
Why do you not consider most of the Mahayana
To be similar to your sutras? ^9-49

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-50]]

If, by the exception of one,
Everything becomes flawed,
Why would not everything
Be taught by the Buddha by the exception of one sutra? ^9-50

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-51]]

Any statement that
Great Kashyapa and others could not fathom,
Who would make it unacceptable
Because you have not understood it? ^9-51

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-52]]

For the sake of those suffering from ignorance,
Liberated from the extremes of attachment and fear,
Remaining in cyclic existence is accomplished.
This is the fruit of emptiness. ^9-52

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-53]]

Therefore, refuting
The aspect of emptiness is inappropriate.
Therefore, without doubt,
One should cultivate emptiness. ^9-53

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-54]]

Emptiness is the antidote to the darkness
Of the veils of defilements and objects of knowledge.
Whoever wishes to quickly attain omniscience,
How can they not cultivate it? ^9-54

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-55]]

If something that causes suffering
Gives rise to fear,
How can fear arise
From emptiness, which pacifies suffering? ^9-55

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-56]]

If there were some self,
Then fear would arise from anything.
But since no self exists,
Who is it that would be afraid? ^9-56

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-57]]

Teeth, hair, and nails are not the self.
Bones and blood are not the self.
Mucus is not the self, phlegm is not the self,
And lymph and pus are also not the self. ^9-57

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-58]]

Fat and sweat are not the self.
Lungs and liver are also not the self.
Other internal organs are also not the self.
Feces and urine are not the self. ^9-58

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-59]]

Flesh and skin are not the self.
Heat and wind are also not the self.
Pores are not the self. In every respect,
The six consciousnesses are also not the self. ^9-59

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-60]]

If the cognition of sound were permanent,
Then one would always perceive sound.
If there is no object of knowledge, what does it know?
By what is it called knowledge? ^9-60

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-61]]

If non-knowing is knowledge,
Then wood would also be knowledge.
Therefore, it is certain that
Without an object of knowledge present, there is no knowledge. ^9-61

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-62]]

If it knows form by itself,
Why then is it not also hearing?
If it is because sound is not close,
Then there is no knowledge of it either. ^9-62

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-63]]

How can the nature of the perceiver of sound
Be the perceiver of form?
One is conceived as father and son,
But it is not truly so. ^9-63

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-64]]

Thus, clarity, activity, and
Inertia are not sons, nor are they fathers.
It is not seen to be
Of the nature of the perceiver of sound. ^9-64

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-65]]

If it is seen in another aspect, like an actor,
Then it is not permanent.
If that other aspect is itself,
Then it is one, and that one did not exist before. ^9-65

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-66]]

If that other aspect is not true,
Then tell me its own nature.
If you say it is knowledge itself, then
All beings would be one. ^9-66

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-67]]

Sentient and insentient things
Would also be one, because their existence is similar.
When the distinction is contradictory,
What is the basis of similarity? ^9-67

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-68]]

Insentient things are also not the self,
Because they are insentient, like pots and so forth.
If you know it is sentient,
Then non-knowing would be destroyed. ^9-68

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-69]]

If the self is unchanging,
What has the mind done to it?
Thus, non-knowing and actionless,
Space would be made the self. ^9-69

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-70]]

If there is no self,
The connection of karma and its results is illogical.
Since karma is done and then ceases,
Whose karma would it be? ^9-70

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-71]]

The agent and experiencer are established for both
As having distinct actions and bases for results,
And also as having no self.
Is it not meaningless to dispute this here? ^9-71

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-72]]

This seeing that a cause
Is endowed with a result is not possible.
It is taught that the agent and experiencer
Are based on a single continuum. ^9-72

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-73]]

Past and future minds
Are not the self, because they do not exist.
If the present mind is the self,
Then when it ceases, the self also ceases. ^9-73

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-74]]

For example, if a plantain tree trunk
Is separated into parts, nothing is found.
Likewise, when analyzed
By thorough analysis, the self is also not truly existent. ^9-74

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-75]]

If there are no beings,
For whom should compassion be cultivated?
It is what is conceived by ignorance,
Accepted for the sake of results. ^9-75

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-76]]

If there are no beings, whose is the result?
True, but it is desired from ignorance.
For the sake of pacifying suffering,
The ignorance regarding results should not be averted. ^9-76

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-77]]

The pride that is the cause of suffering
Increases through the ignorance of a self.
If you say that it is not averted even from that,
Then cultivating selflessness is supreme. ^9-77

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-78]]

The body is not the feet or calves.
The thighs and waist are also not the body.
The belly and back are also not the body,
And the chest and shoulders are also not the body. ^9-78

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-79]]

The ribs and hands are also not the body.
The armpits and shoulders are also not the body.
The internal organs are also not it,
And the head and throat are also not the body.
What then is this body? ^9-79

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-80]]

If this body exists
In all its individual parts,
Then the parts would exist in the parts,
But where does it itself exist? ^9-80

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-81]]

If the self, which is the body of all,
Exists in the hands and so forth,
Then there would be as many bodies
As there are hands and so forth. ^9-81

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-82]]

If the body does not exist externally or internally,
How can the body exist in the hands and so forth?
If there is nothing other than the hands and so forth,
How can it exist? ^9-82

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-83]]

Therefore, the mind conceives a body
In the hands and so forth, which are bodiless, through ignorance.
Like the conception of a person in a scarecrow post
Due to a particular configuration of shape. ^9-83

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-84]]

As long as conditions are assembled,
So long does the body appear like a person.
Likewise, as long as it exists in the hands and so forth,
So long does the body appear there. ^9-84

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-85]]

Likewise, since it is a collection of fingers,
What then is the hand?
And since it is a collection of joints,
The joints are also separated into their own parts. ^9-85

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-86]]

The parts are also separated into particles,
And the particle is also divided by its directional parts.
Since directional parts are free from parts,
Like space, therefore, even particles do not exist. ^9-86

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-87]]

Thus, who with discernment
Would be attached to a form like a dream?
When the body is thus non-existent,
What person is there, and what woman? ^9-87

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-88]]

If suffering truly exists,
Why does it not harm intense gladness?
If there is happiness, why does it not
Cause gladness in grief and torment, like delicious tastes? ^9-88

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-89]]

If it is not experienced because it is overwhelmed
By something stronger,
Then what is not the experiencer itself?
How can that be feeling? ^9-89

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-90]]

Subtle suffering truly exists.
Is not its gross aspect removed?
If you say that is merely other happiness,
Then that subtle aspect is also its. ^9-90

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-91]]

If, due to the arising of conflicting conditions,
Suffering does not arise,
Is it not established that the conception of feeling
Is clinging to an object? ^9-91

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-92]]

Therefore, this analysis,
The antidote to this, should be cultivated.
The food of practitioners in meditation
Arises from the field of analysis. ^9-92

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-93]]

If there is space between the sense faculties and their objects,
Where do they meet?
If there is no space, they are one,
So what meets what? ^9-93

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-94]]

Particles do not enter particles;
There is no gap, they are equal.
There is no mixing in what has not entered,
And no meeting in what is not mixed. ^9-94

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-95]]

How can meeting
Be appropriate for something without parts?
If you see meeting
And something without parts, then demonstrate it. ^9-95

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-96]]

The meeting of consciousness with a bodiless thing
Is not appropriate.
Since a collection is non-existent,
As was analyzed before. ^9-96

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-97]]

Thus, if contact does not exist,
From what does feeling arise?
What is the purpose of this toil?
What harms what? ^9-97

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-98]]

When no experiencer exists,
And feeling also does not exist,
Then, seeing this situation,
Why would craving not cease? ^9-98

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-99]]

Seeing or touching
Is of the nature of a dream or illusion.
Since it arises simultaneously with the mind,
Feeling is not seen by it. ^9-99

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-100]]

What arises before and after
Is remembered, not experienced.
One does not experience oneself,
Nor do others experience it. ^9-100

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-101]]

No experiencer exists;
Therefore, feeling is not itself.
Thus, in this collection of selflessness,
Why would this harm it? ^9-101

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-102]]

The mind does not dwell in the sense faculties.
It is not in forms and so forth, nor in between.
It is not the mind internally or externally,
Nor is it found elsewhere. ^9-102

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-103]]

What is not the body, nor elsewhere,
Nor mixed, nor separate, nor anywhere,
That is nothing at all. Therefore,
The nature of beings is nirvana. ^9-103

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-104]]

If knowledge exists before the object of knowledge,
On what does it focus to arise?
If knowledge and the object of knowledge are simultaneous,
On what does it focus to arise? ^9-104

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-105]]

If it exists after the object of knowledge,
Then from what does knowledge arise?
Thus, the arising of all phenomena
Will not be understood. ^9-105

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-106]]

If conventional reality does not exist in that way,
Where are the two truths?
And if it is by another conventional reality,
How can beings pass beyond sorrow? ^9-106

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-107]]

This is the notion of another's mind;
It is not one's own conventional reality.
If it is later ascertained, then it exists;
Otherwise, conventional reality itself is non-existent. ^9-107

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-108]]

Conception and the object to be conceived
Are mutually dependent.
Just as all analysis
Is spoken of based on convention. ^9-108

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-109]]

When analysis
Analyzes by thorough analysis,
Then that thorough analysis
Is also without end, because it is analysis. ^9-109

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-110]]

When the object of analysis is analyzed,
There is no basis for thorough analysis.
Since there is no basis, it does not arise,
And that too is said to be nirvana. ^9-110

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-111]]

According to whom these two are true,
That is very difficult to maintain.
If things are established by the power of knowledge,
What is the basis for knowledge existing? ^9-111

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-112]]

If knowledge is established from the object of knowledge,
What is the basis for the object of knowledge existing?
If they exist by mutual dependence,
Then both would be non-existent. ^9-112

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-113]]

If there is no son, there is no father,
From what does sonship arise?
Without a son, there is no father;
Likewise, both are non-existent. ^9-113

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-114]]

Just as a sprout arises from a seed,
And the seed is understood by it,
Why is it not understood that
Knowledge, born from the object of knowledge, exists? ^9-114

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-115]]

If a seed is understood by knowledge
Other than the sprout,
From what is the existence of knowledge
That understands that object of knowledge understood? ^9-115

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-116]]

For the time being, all causes
Are seen by direct perception in the world.
The distinctions of lotus stems and so forth
Are produced by the distinctions of causes. ^9-116

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-117]]

If you ask what made the distinctions of causes,
It is from the distinctions of previous causes.
Why are causes able to produce results?
It is from the power of previous causes. ^9-117

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-118]]

If Ishvara is the cause of beings,
First, tell me who Ishvara is.
If you say it is the elements, that is true,
But what is the toil in merely naming? ^9-118

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-119]]

However, earth and other elements are many,
Impermanent, unmoving, not divine,
Trampled, and impure.
Therefore, that is not Ishvara. ^9-119

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-120]]

Ishvara is not space, because it is unmoving.
It is not the self, because it was refuted before.
And what is the use of speaking
Of an inconceivable creator by saying it is inconceivable? ^9-120

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-121]]

What is it that is desired to be created by it?
Is not the self, and earth and so forth,
And the essence of Ishvara also permanent?
Knowledge arises from the object of knowledge, and ^9-121

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-122]]

Beginningless pleasure and pain are from karma.
Tell me what is created by that.
If the cause has no beginning,
Where is the beginning of the result? ^9-122

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-123]]

Why does it not always create?
It does not depend on anything else.
If it is not created by it, and nothing else exists,
On what does it depend? ^9-123

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-124]]

If it depends, then a collection
Would be the cause, not Ishvara.
If there is a collection, it does not arise, because it is powerless,
And without it, there is no power to arise. ^9-124

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-125]]

If Ishvara creates without desiring,
Then it would be dependent on others.
If it desires, it would depend on desire,
So how can it be Ishvara if it creates? ^9-125

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-126]]

Those who assert that particles are permanent
Were also refuted before.
The Samkhyas assert that the permanent Primal Substance
Is the cause of beings. ^9-126

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-127]]

The equilibrium of the qualities called
Clarity, activity, and inertia
Is called the Primal Substance.
Disequilibrium is said to be beings. ^9-127

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-128]]

It is illogical for one to have three natures;
Therefore, it does not exist.
Likewise, qualities do not exist,
Because they are also threefold individually. ^9-128

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-129]]

If qualities do not exist, then sounds and so forth
Would also be very far from existing.
And happiness and so forth cannot exist
In insentient things like cloth. ^9-129

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-130]]

If phenomena are of the nature of their causes,
Are not phenomena already analyzed?
Your cause is also happiness and so forth,
And cloth and so forth do not arise from that. ^9-130

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-131]]

Happiness and so forth are from cloth and so forth.
Since that does not exist, happiness and so forth do not exist.
And happiness and so forth, as permanent,
Are never perceived. ^9-131

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-132]]

If clear happiness and so forth exist,
Why is experience not grasped?
If it is subtle,
How can it be gross and subtle? ^9-132

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-133]]

By abandoning the gross and becoming subtle,
Those subtle and gross things are impermanent.
Likewise, why do you not assert
That all phenomena are impermanent? ^9-133

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-134]]

If the gross is not other than happiness,
Then clear happiness is impermanent.
If you assert that nothing
Arises because it does not exist, ^9-134

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-135]]

You do not desire it to arise without being clear,
But it exists.
If the result exists in the cause,
Then eating food would be eating impurity. ^9-135

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-136]]

Buy cotton seeds with the price of cloth
And wear them.
If the world does not see it through ignorance,
Then know that it is established by itself. ^9-136

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-137]]

Since that knowledge also exists in the world,
Why is it not seen?
If the world is not valid cognition,
Then seeing what is clear is also not true. ^9-137

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-138]]

If valid cognition is not valid,
Will not what is measured by it be false?
Therefore, in ultimate reality, emptiness
Would be inappropriate to cultivate. ^9-138

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-139]]

Without touching a conceived thing,
Its non-existence is not grasped.
Therefore, whatever is a false thing,
Its non-existence is clearly false. ^9-139

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-140]]

Therefore, the notion that
A child in a dream has died, and that it does not exist,
Is an obstacle to the notion
That it exists, and that too is false. ^9-140

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-141]]

Thus, by such analysis,
Nothing exists without a cause.
It does not exist in conditions
That are individual or collected. ^9-141

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-142]]

It does not come from elsewhere,
It does not remain, it does not go.
What difference is there between this
That ignorance makes true, and an illusion? ^9-142

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-143]]

Whatever is created by an illusion,
And whatever is created by causes,
Investigate from where it comes
And where it goes. ^9-143

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-144]]

Whatever is seen
By being close, and is not absent if it is not,
Is like a fabricated reflection.
How can it have true existence? ^9-144

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-145]]

If a thing exists,
What need is there for a cause?
And if it does not exist,
What need is there for a cause? ^9-145

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-146]]

Even hundreds of millions of causes
Cannot transform a non-existent thing.
How can that state be a thing?
And what else would be a thing? ^9-146

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-147]]

If existence is not possible when non-existent,
When will a thing ever exist?
Until a thing arises,
Its non-existence will not cease. ^9-147

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-148]]

If it is not free from non-existence,
There is no possibility of a thing existing.
And a thing will also not be non-existent,
Because it would entail two natures. ^9-148

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-149]]

Thus, cessation does not exist,
And things also do not exist. Therefore,
All these beings
Are always unborn and unceasing. ^9-149

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-150]]

Beings are like a dream;
When analyzed, they are like a plantain tree.
Nirvana and non-nirvana
Are ultimately no different. ^9-150

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-151]]

Thus, in empty things,
What is gained, what is lost?
What is honored
Or completely disdained? ^9-151

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-152]]

From what is happiness or suffering?
What is there to dislike, what is there to like?
When investigated in ultimate reality,
Who craves, and for what? ^9-152

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-153]]

When analyzed, in this living world,
Who dies here?
What will arise, what has arisen?
And who are relatives and friends? ^9-153

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-154]]

Let me embrace all
As being like space.
Those who desire their own happiness
Are driven by causes of conflict and eagerness. ^9-154

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-155]]

They are greatly agitated and rejoice,
Grieve, strive, dispute,
Mutually cut and pierce,
And live with great difficulty through misdeeds. ^9-155

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-156]]

Repeatedly coming to happy realms,
And repeatedly enjoying many pleasures,
After death, they fall into
Long, unbearable sufferings in the lower realms. ^9-156

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-157]]

There are many chasms in cyclic existence.
There is nothing like this that is not itself.
There, too, by mutual contradiction,
There is nothing like this truly existing. ^9-157

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-158]]

That ocean of unbearable suffering,
Without example, is boundless.
There, one is so weak,
And life there is also short. ^9-158

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-159]]

There, too, by being alive and free from illness,
By acting, by the toil of hunger and thirst,
By sleep, by harm, and likewise,
By childishness and meaningless association, ^9-159

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-160]]

Life passes quickly and meaninglessly,
And discernment is very hard to find.
There, too, where is the means
To avert the habit of distraction? ^9-160

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-161]]

There, too, Mara strives
To make one fall into the great lower realms.
There are many wrong paths,
And it is hard to overcome doubt. ^9-161

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-162]]

Again, leisure is hard to find,
And buddhas are very hard to find.
The river of defilements is hard to abandon.
Alas, suffering will persist! ^9-162

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-163]]

Thus, though greatly suffering,
Those who do not see their own suffering,
These who dwell in the river of suffering,
Alas, they are worthy of lamentation! ^9-163

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-164]]

For example, someone repeatedly bathes,
And repeatedly enters fire.
Likewise, though dwelling in great suffering,
They imagine themselves to be happy. ^9-164

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-165]]

Thus, acting as if
There is no aging and death,
Having first killed themselves,
Unbearable downfalls in the lower realms will come. ^9-165

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-166]]

When will I pacify
Those tormented by the fire of suffering,
With showers of my own happiness,
Born from the clouds of merit? ^9-166

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-167]]

When will I accumulate
Merit with reverence in a non-objectifying way,
And teach emptiness
To those who are ruined by objects? ^9-167

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^9-a]]

This concludes the ninth chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "The Perfection of Wisdom." ^9-a

## 10. Chapter Ten: Dedication ^10-0

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-1]]

By the good deeds I have accomplished,
through engaging in bodhisattva conduct,
may all beings
engage in bodhisattva conduct. ^10-1

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-2]]

In all directions, as many beings
as there are who are sick with suffering in body and mind,
may they, by my merit,
attain an ocean of happiness and joy. ^10-2

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-3]]

For as long as cyclic existence endures,
may they never lose happiness.
May beings always attain the unbroken happiness
of the bodhisattvas. ^10-3

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-4]]

Whatever hell realms there are
in the worldly realms,
may the beings in those places
rejoice with blissful happiness. ^10-4

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-5]]

May those tormented by cold attain warmth.
From the great clouds of the bodhisattvas,
may the endless rivers that flow forth
cool those tormented by heat. ^10-5

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-6]]

May the forest of sword-leaves
become a dense sandalwood forest.
May the silk-cotton trees
arise as wish-granting trees. ^10-6

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-7]]

May the coots, cranes, and swans,
and other birds, be adorned by uttering melodious sounds.
May the hellish places become delightful,
with lakes filled with fragrant lotuses. ^10-7

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-8]]

May those heaps of coals become heaps of jewels.
May the scorched ground be covered with a crystal floor.
May the crushing mountains become palaces of offerings,
filled with buddhas. ^10-8

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-9]]

May the burning embers, scorched stones, and showers of weapons
henceforth become showers of flowers.
May the striking of one another with weapons
henceforth be a playful throwing of flowers. ^10-9

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-10]]

May those who sink in the fire-pit-like rivers,
whose flesh is utterly dissolved, whose bones are the color of kunda flowers,
by the power of my good deeds, attain divine bodies,
and dwell gently with goddesses. ^10-10

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-11]]

"Why here do Yama's henchmen, terrifying crows, and vultures cause fear?
Whose power is this, that dispels all darkness and creates such great joy?"
Looking up, they see Vajrapani, radiant, dwelling in the sky,
and may they, freed from misdeeds by the force of great joy, accompany him. ^10-11

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-12]]

Seeing showers of flowers mixed with fragrant water falling,
quenching the hellish flames with a hiss,
and thinking, "What is this sudden, overwhelming happiness?"
may the beings in hell see Avalokiteshvara. ^10-12

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-13]]

"Friends, abandon fear and quickly come here to us!
By whose power are all sufferings removed, and the force of joy has arrived?
Who is this young, radiant one with a topknot, who saves all beings
and generates the Mind of Awakening and compassion, making them fearless?" ^10-13

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-14]]

"Behold, Manjushri, whose lotus feet are honored by the crowns of a hundred gods,
whose compassionate, moist eyes are showered with many flowers on his head,
and who is accompanied by thousands of delightful goddesses proclaiming praises in tiered palaces!"
Seeing such a Manjushri, may the beings in hell now cry out. ^10-14

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-15]]

Thus, by my root of virtue, may Samantabhadra and other
bodhisattvas, with unobstructed clouds of happiness,
and cool, fragrant showers, be seen falling,
and may those beings in hell be overjoyed. ^10-15

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-16]]

May animals be free from the fear
of devouring one another.
May hungry ghosts be happy,
like people free from unpleasant speech. ^10-16

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-17]]

May hungry ghosts be satisfied
by the streams of milk flowing from the hand
of the noble Avalokiteshvara,
and may they always be cooled by bathing. ^10-17

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-18]]

May the blind see with their eyes,
and may the deaf always hear sounds.
May pregnant women give birth without harm,
just like Mayadevi. ^10-18

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-19]]

May the naked obtain clothes,
the hungry obtain food,
the thirsty obtain water,
and delicious drinks. ^10-19

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-20]]

May the poor obtain wealth.
May the grief-stricken obtain joy.
May the disheartened be cheered,
and may they attain perfect steadfastness. ^10-20

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-21]]

As many sick beings as there are,
may they quickly be freed from illness.
May all the illnesses of beings
never arise again. ^10-21

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-22]]

May the frightened be fearless.
May the bound be freed.
May the powerless be powerful,
and may their minds be gentle towards one another. ^10-22

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-23]]

May all travelers
be happy in all directions.
May whatever they strive to accomplish
be fulfilled without effort. ^10-23

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-24]]

May those who have embarked on boats and great ships
have their wishes fulfilled,
reach the shore safely,
and rejoice with their relatives. ^10-24

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-25]]

May those who have strayed on sorrowful, lost paths
meet with travelers,
and travel without weariness and easily,
free from the fear of robbers, tigers, and the like. ^10-25

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-26]]

May children, the elderly, and the helpless,
who are asleep, drunk, or deeply intoxicated,
in dangerous, pathless wildernesses and the like,
be protected by gods. ^10-26

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-27]]

May they be free from all weariness,
and endowed with faith, wisdom, and compassion.
May they have abundant food and conduct,
and always remember their past lives. ^10-27

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-28]]

May all beings, like a treasury of space,
have inexhaustible possessions.
May they act freely,
without quarrels and without harm. ^10-28

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-29]]

May those beings with little splendor
have great splendor.
May those ascetics with unattractive forms
attain perfect, beautiful forms. ^10-29

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-30]]

As many women as there are in the world,
may they all become men.
May the lowly attain high status,
and may pride be overcome. ^10-30

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-31]]

By this merit of mine,
may all beings, without exception,
abandon all evil deeds,
and always perform good deeds. ^10-31

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-32]]

May they never be separated from the Mind of Awakening,
and be devoted to bodhisattva conduct.
May they be embraced by the buddhas,
and abandon all demonic actions. ^10-32

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-33]]

May all those beings
have immeasurably long lives.
May they always live happily,
and may the sound of death never be heard. ^10-33

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-34]]

May all directions be filled
with pleasure groves of wish-granting trees,
filled with buddhas and bodhisattvas
proclaiming the sweet Dharma. ^10-34

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-35]]

May the earth everywhere
be free of pebbles and the like,
and be smooth, naturally made of lapis,
like the palm of a hand. ^10-35

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-36]]

In every assembly,
may many bodhisattvas
dwell, adorning the earth
with their excellence. ^10-36

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-37]]

May all embodied creatures
always hear the sound of the Dharma
uninterruptedly from birds,
trees, all rays of light, and the sky. ^10-37

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-38]]

May they always meet with buddhas
and bodhisattvas.
May they worship the guides of beings
with boundless clouds of offerings. ^10-38

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-39]]

May the gods send rain in due season,
and may the crops be abundant.
May kings rule righteously,
and may the world flourish. ^10-39

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-40]]

May medicines be potent,
and mantras and recitations be accomplished.
May dakinis, rakshasas, and the like
be endowed with a compassionate mind. ^10-40

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-41]]

May no being suffer,
be afraid, or be despised.
May no one be unhappy. ^10-41

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-42]]

May monasteries be adorned with reading
and recitation, and be well established.
May the assembly always be in harmony,
and may the purpose of the assembly be accomplished. ^10-42

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-43]]

May monks who desire training
also attain solitude.
May they abandon all distractions,
and meditate with a mind fit for practice. ^10-43

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-44]]

May nuns have gains,
and abandon quarrels and harm.
Likewise, may all renunciants
never lose their ethical discipline. ^10-44

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-45]]

May those whose ethical discipline is broken, filled with remorse,
always exhaust their misdeeds.
And having attained happy realms,
may their ascetic practices never be lost there either. ^10-45

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-46]]

May the wise be honored,
and also obtain alms.
May their mental continuums be thoroughly purified,
and may they be renowned in all directions. ^10-46

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-47]]

May they not experience the suffering of the lower realms,
and without undergoing hardship,
may they quickly attain buddhahood
with bodies superior to those of gods. ^10-47

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-48]]

May all beings repeatedly
worship all buddhas,
and always be endowed with happiness
by the immeasurable happiness of the buddhas. ^10-48

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-49]]

May the bodhisattvas
accomplish the benefit of beings as they intend.
May what the protectors intend
be attained by beings. ^10-49

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-50]]

Likewise, may Solitary Buddhas
and disciples also be happy. ^10-50

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-51]]

May I, by Manjushri's kindness,
until I attain the stage of great joy,
always remember my past lives,
and attain renunciation. ^10-51

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-52]]

May I also live, endowed with
food and clothing.
May I attain perfect endowment,
dwelling in solitude in all my lives. ^10-52

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-53]]

Whenever I wish to see him,
or wish to ask something,
may I see Protector Manjushri himself
without hindrance. ^10-53

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-54]]

Just as Manjushri acts
to accomplish the benefit of all beings
reaching the ends of space in the ten directions,
may my conduct also be like that. ^10-54

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-55]]

For as long as space endures,
and for as long as beings remain,
for that long may I remain,
and dispel the suffering of beings. ^10-55

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-56]]

Whatever suffering there is for beings,
may all of it ripen upon me.
May the assembly of bodhisattvas
cause beings to experience happiness. ^10-56

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-57]]

May the unique medicine for the suffering of beings,
the source of all happiness,
the teachings, remain for a long time,
with gains and respect. ^10-57

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-58]]

To Manjushri, by whose kindness
virtuous thoughts arise, I bow.
To the virtuous friend, by whose kindness I have flourished,
I also bow. ^10-58

![[1-SOURCES/Translations/bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^10-a]]

This concludes the tenth chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "Dedication." ^10-a
