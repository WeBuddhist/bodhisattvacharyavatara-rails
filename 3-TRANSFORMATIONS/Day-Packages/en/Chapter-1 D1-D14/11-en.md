### Notification

**Title:** Thank someone who helped you see clearly  
**Detail:** The deepest help isn't comfort, but clarity. Today, remember someone who gave you that.

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's two verses name three things this mind does for people who are struggling. It brings them happiness. It takes away their pain. And it clears away their confusion. Then the verses ask: where is there anything, or anyone, equal to that?

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

These two verses show, in a way that is easy for ordinary people to understand, three wonderful qualities of the bodhisattva who removes the troubles of beings.

Beings want happiness but do not have it; with great love, the bodhisattva fills them with every kind of happiness, both for now and for the long run. Beings do not want suffering but have so much of it; with great compassion, the bodhisattva cuts away all the suffering of birth, ageing, sickness, and death, right at the root. And for people who do not know what to take up and what to give up when it comes to the causes of happiness and suffering, the bodhisattva, with great wisdom, clears away the darkness of their confusion.

What other good deed could equal the awakening mind, which helps beings through these three: love, compassion, and wisdom? There is no better friend, and no greater merit, anywhere.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when I feel angry or confused, I will immediately remember Bodhicitta to calm my mind.

**Explanation:** Today's verse asks a simple question: is there any friend or good action as powerful as Bodhicitta for clearing away our confusion? The answer is no, there is nothing else like it. By remembering this loving intention the exact moment you feel upset or lost, you are using the best possible way to bring peace back to your mind.

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Therefore, toward those beings who are destitute and poor in happiness together with its causes, and who — burdened with many sufferings, causes and results alike — are worn down and wretched: through great love [the bodhisattva] satisfies them with every happiness, both temporary and ultimate; and through great compassion cuts off the stream of all their suffering, in this life and the next.

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

*Note: In this commentary, verses 1-29 and 1-30 are grouped together; 1-29 is the first verse. The complete word-commentary for both verses is given here.*

Therefore, toward those lowly beings who are destitute and poor in happiness together with its causes, and who are burdened with many sufferings together with their causes, worn down and wretched: with love [the bodhisattva] satisfies them with every happiness of the higher realms and of liberation; with compassion cuts off the stream of all the suffering of samsara and the lower realms; and with wisdom, having shown what is to be taken up and what given up, also dispels their confusion. Where else is there any virtue equal in power to that motivating awakening mind? There is none. Where else is there a trustworthy friend or companion like it? There is none. <!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

*Note: In this commentary, verses 1-29 and 1-30 are grouped together; 1-29 is the first verse. The complete word-commentary for both verses is given here.*

A bodhisattva, an heir of the victorious ones — toward those beings of the three realms who, not having found happiness of body or mind, have become utterly destitute and poor in happiness, and who are burdened with many hundreds of thousands of sufferings such as birth, ageing, sickness, and death: step by step, by the power of the great love present in the heart-stream of the bodhisattva, [the bodhisattva] satisfies with every happiness those beings who are deprived of happiness; and with the great compassion that holds no [fixed] reference, also cuts off the stream of all the sufferings of body and mind — birth, ageing, sickness, death, and the rest — of all those beings burdened with so much suffering; and with great wisdom, also dispels the very darkness of the confusion and ignorance that does not know the causes of happiness and suffering. Where is there any virtuous excellence equal to such a bodhisattva? There is none. Who else possesses such merit — able to destroy all the suffering in the mind-streams of other beings, to increase all their happiness higher and higher, and to dispel all the darkness of confusion and ignorance? In no way does anyone else possess it.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Great Love Satisfies Beings With Every Happiness, Temporary and Ultimate

Verse 1-29 answers the first of the difficulties set out in 1-28: in that situation where beings want happiness but cannot achieve it, the bodhisattva's great love satisfies beings who are deprived of happiness with every happiness — reaching both the temporary and the ultimate. As Kunzang Pelden explains clearly, the happiness that is given is not just a portion but something complete, reaching every happiness of both this life and the next.

###### 2. Great Compassion Cuts Off the Stream of All Suffering

The bodhisattva's great compassion does not merely reduce suffering but cuts off its very stream. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk describes this compassion in an especially exalted way as "the great compassion that holds no [fixed] reference": without any set object, it extends to all beings of the three realms.

###### 3. Built on the Three of Love, Compassion, and Wisdom (SMPLG / NKW)

Both Sabzang and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk treat verse 1-29 as the opening of a pair of verses (1-29/1-30). Within that pair, love answers the point of beings being deprived of happiness; compassion cuts off the stream of suffering; and wisdom (as spelled out in verse 1-30) removes the root of confusion. These three qualities, gathered together in one place, are the bodhisattva's complete answer to the three difficulties set out in 1-28.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse answers the point taught in 1-28 — that beings, though they want happiness, are deprived of it, and, though they do not want suffering, are burdened with much of it — through the bodhisattva's activity: with great love satisfying beings with every happiness, and with great compassion cutting off the stream of all their suffering.

**Key points.**
- With great love, [the bodhisattva] satisfies those deprived of happiness with every happiness, both temporary and ultimate.
- Great compassion, holding no fixed reference, cuts off the stream of all suffering and clears away, without delay, even the pangs of birth, ageing, sickness, and death throughout the three realms.
- SMPLG and NKW read verses 1-29 and 1-30 as a linked pair, healing beings through the three of love, compassion, and wisdom.
- GDR / NTS / MKS treat 1-28 through 1-30 as a single unit and, drawing the point of love, compassion, and wisdom together from 1-28-summary.md, conclude there.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Focusing on beings as limitless as space, who are deprived of the happiness they wish for and tormented by the many sufferings they do not want, [the bodhisattva] brings them to attain happiness and to be free of suffering. And since this must come about by removing the very causes that produce suffering in each of their own mind-streams, whoever possesses the good heart that strives in the work of dispelling the darkness of confusion — the confusion deluded about what is to be taken up and what given up in the mind-streams of all beings — attains boundless merit. What other virtue could equal such a mind? There is nothing higher than this. This good heart, wholly dedicated to accomplishing others' benefit, is the kinsman, protector, refuge, and dear friend of all beings. Where, other than this awakening mind, is there such a constant, unchanging friend? An ordinary friend, even if similarly devoted to one's benefit and happiness, is unreliable. This awakening mind gives rise to an even-minded, benevolent attitude toward all; and because its wish to benefit does not change for as long as space endures, there is no friend greater than it — and where is there anything with greater power to gather merit?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^1-30]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Through great wisdom, having shown what is to be taken up and what given up, [the bodhisattva] also dispels the confusion that is deluded about cause and effect, about what to adopt and what to reject. Where else is there a cause equal in power of virtue to that? There is none. Where else is there a friend who benefits [others] in such a way — accomplishing happiness, removing suffering, and showing what is to be taken up and given up? There is none. And where else, as a resulting merit, is there an awakening mind like that? There is none.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Great Wisdom Dispels Confusion

Verse 1-30 completes the three-part set begun in 1-29, naming the bodhisattva's third quality as great wisdom (ཤེས་རབ་ཆེན་པོ་). Where love and compassion engage happiness and suffering, wisdom enters the very root, dispelling the confusion (གཏི་མུག་) that is deluded about what beings should take up and give up (བླང་དོར་). KKP specifies that this is the confusion deluded about cause and effect (རྒྱུ་འབྲས་བླང་དོར་).

###### 2. The Supreme Friend (Where Is There Such a Friend?)

The verse's second couplet poses three questions: Where is there anything equal in the nature of virtue and goodness? Where is there such a spiritual friend who accomplishes others' benefit and happiness? Where is there merit like a bodhisattva's? As KS and KKP both explain clearly, these three questions are set out as the reasoning that establishes the awakening mind as without equal: no virtue, no friend, and no merit can be shown to match what the awakening mind provides.

###### 3. The Joining-Point of the Three — Love, Compassion, and Wisdom (GDR / NTS / MKS)

The three commentaries that group verses 1-28/29/30 as a single unit place 1-30 as the final subsection of the three-verse set. In them, love (the first line of 1-29), compassion (the second line of 1-29), and wisdom (the first line of 1-30) are explained as clearing away, one by one, the three wretched conditions of beings described in 1-28 — being deprived of happiness, having much suffering, and being deluded about their causes.

###### 4. The Constant Friend (His Holiness the Dalai Lama)

TG comments at length on the word "friend" in the verse. He sets the awakening mind's unchanging friendship — which does not alter for as long as space endures — in contrast to ordinary worldly friendship. A worldly friend, he explains, though sometimes helpful, is unreliable (མི་བརྟན་པ་). By this, he takes the question "Where is there such a friend?" as a powerful argument grounded in impermanence.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse completes the trio of love, compassion, and wisdom by adding wisdom to [the treatment of] the causes, joining its conclusion to verse 1-29. The wisdom that unravels confusion is brought to bear on the ignorance that does not know the cause and effect of happiness and suffering; and by way of the questions "Where else is there equal virtue? Where else such a friend? Where else such merit?", it establishes the supremacy of the awakening mind.

**Key points.**
- With great wisdom, having shown what is to be taken up and given up, [the bodhisattva] dispels at the root the confusion deluded about cause and effect.
- The three questions — equal virtue, equal friend, equal merit — lead to the conclusion that establishes the supremacy of the awakening mind.
- GDR / NTS / MKS explain that, in the 1-28–30 unit, the three of love, compassion, and wisdom fully overcome all the troubles of beings.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama, on the word "friend", explains the awakening mind as a constant, unchanging friend, distinct from the unreliable friendship of ordinary people.
