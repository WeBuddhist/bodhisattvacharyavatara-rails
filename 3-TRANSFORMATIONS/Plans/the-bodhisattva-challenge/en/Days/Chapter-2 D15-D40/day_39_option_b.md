# Day 39 — The wrong we forget to count

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Take honest stock of one broken commitment today
> **Detail:** Today's verse sorts wrongs into two kinds. The one we overlook is the promise we quietly broke.

## Opening

Confession begins with honest recognition, and today's verse sorts what we might recognize into two kinds. Some things are wrong no matter who does them: harming, deceiving, cruelty. Others are wrong only because we took on a commitment, a vow, a promise, a rule we freely accepted. Shantideva names both. The first kind our conscience already flags. The second is the one we tend to forget even counts.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, spells out the two categories the verse names. The first he calls naturally unwholesome: acts that are harmful whoever commits them, vow or no vow, like killing or lying. The second are the proscribed: acts that are wrong specifically because you took on a commitment against them, the way, in his example, eating at the wrong time or drinking would break a vow one had made. Here is why the pairing matters for us. Most of us are fairly alert to the first kind; our conscience raises its hand. The second kind slips past. We tell ourselves we did nothing terrible, and technically that is true, while quietly overlooking the promise we made and let lapse, the standard we agreed to and stopped keeping. Naming that second kind honestly, without drama, is where a lot of real, unglamorous integrity actually lives.

## Today's Practice

Take a quiet minute and look for one commitment you made and quietly let slide, not a great crime, but a promise to someone, a standard you set for yourself, a rule you agreed to and stopped keeping. Name it plainly to yourself, without piling on blame. Simply seeing the broken commitment for what it is, and owning it, is already the beginning of setting it right.
