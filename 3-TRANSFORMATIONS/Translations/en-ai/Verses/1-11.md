## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three commentators agree that the "precious jewel of the mind of enlightenment" (bodhicitta) is of supreme value because it has been thoroughly investigated and praised by the Buddha—the ultimate, omniscient guide of wandering beings. They use the traditional analogy of a skilled captain and a jewel island to illustrate how the Buddha's flawless evaluation of bodhicitta obligates those who wish to escape worldly suffering to hold firmly to this mind.

### 1. The Analogy of the Captain and the Jewel Island
The commentators use the analogy of a skilled maritime captain leading merchants to an island of jewels to explain the Buddha's role in identifying the value of bodhicitta:
- **Gyaltsab Je** compares the Buddha to a skilled captain who leads merchants to an island of jewels. He explains that the peerless captain of wandering beings, the Buddha alone, possesses the immeasurable wisdom required to investigate the supreme method to clear the spiritual poverty of beings.
- **Sazang Mati Panchen** describes the Buddha as the peerless, sole captain who guides infinite wandering beings across the ocean of cyclic existence. He notes that just as a jewel well-examined by a skilled captain is cherished by merchants, the mind of enlightenment is cherished because it was found to possess great value by the perfect Buddha.
- **Ngulchu Thokme** characterizes the Buddha as the unrivaled captain who guides all wandering beings to the "city of liberation" and establishes them in the wealth of excellent qualities.

### 2. The Buddha's Unerring Examination of Bodhicitta
The commentators emphasize that the Buddha did not praise bodhicitta arbitrarily, but did so after a thorough, omniscient investigation of all phenomena:
- **Gyaltsab Je** explains that the Buddha, possessing immeasurable wisdom, thoroughly investigated the supreme method to clear the poverty of wandering beings and saw that this very mind of enlightenment is of great value—being both rare and of immense benefit.
- **Sazang Mati Panchen** highlights that the mind of enlightenment is found to possess great value of qualities when well-examined by the limitless omniscience of the perfect Buddha. Because the Buddha's examination is unerring, we can have complete trust in its value.
- **Ngulchu Thokme** states that the Buddha, whose wisdom encompasses all of reality, thoroughly investigated all phenomena with his immeasurable mind and saw that this mind is of supreme value, being both rare and of immense benefit.

### 3. Scriptural Proof of Bodhicitta's Superiority
Both Sazang Mati Panchen and Ngulchu Thokme substantiate the supreme value of bodhicitta by citing scriptural authority, specifically the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*:
- **Ngulchu Thokme** provides the detailed citation: "Just as the king of jewels outshines all other wealth, likewise, as long as the omniscient wisdom of the three times illuminates the sphere of reality (dharmadhatu), all the virtuous roots—both tainted and untainted—of all gods, humans, Śrāvakas, and Pratyekabuddhas can never equal the value of the precious king of jewels that is the mind of perfect enlightenment."
- **Sazang Mati Panchen** similarly cites the sūtras to establish that all the virtuous roots (tainted and untainted) of all sentient beings, Hearers, and Solitary Realizers can never equal the value of the great precious jewel of generating the mind of enlightenment, which acts as the "king of power."

### 4. Exhortation to the Practitioner
The commentators conclude with a direct urging to the practitioner to adopt and hold firmly to this mind:
- **Gyaltsab Je** asserts that those who wish to be free from all the degradations of the cycle of existence must generate and firmly maintain the precious mind of enlightenment, which is like the sovereign king of wish-fulfilling jewels.
- **Sazang Mati Panchen** exhorts sentient beings who wish to be free from the ocean of cyclic existence—the place where they experience the various sufferings of the six classes of beings—to hold firmly and unshakeably to this precious jewel-like mind of enlightenment with great devotion.
- **Ngulchu Thokme** addresses the practitioner directly, urging those wandering beings who wish to be free from the states of suffering to firmly maintain this precious mind of enlightenment.
