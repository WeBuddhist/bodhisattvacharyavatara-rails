## Today's Verse

> My body, as well as my possessions,
> and all my good deeds of the three times,
> I give away without reservation
> to accomplish the benefit of all beings. ^3-10

> By giving up everything, one passes beyond sorrow.
> My mind accomplishes passing beyond sorrow.
> Giving up everything is the same for all,
> but giving to beings is supreme. ^3-11

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 10 and 11 from the third chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. At some point death will take our body and all our wealth away from us. We will have no say in it. So today, while we still have a say, build up the courage to give everything away to beings completely.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses tell us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago a bodhisattva king called Candraprabha ruled over five continents. Like a parent, he brought benefit and happiness to all beings. Out of jealousy, King Bhimasena sent a brahmin called Ledeva to ask Candraprabha for his head.

The minister offered many heads of gold, silver and jewels in its place. The brahmin would not take them. He wanted nothing but the king's own head.

The king's attendants tried to stop him, but he did not listen. He tied his head to the branch of a tree spirit's tree and gave it to the brahmin. And he made the prayer that, having reached unsurpassed awakening, he would free many beings from suffering.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will give one of my own things, or some food, to someone else without holding back.

**Explanation:** _(Generosity Practice)_ The tenth verse of the third chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_ says this. We should give our body, our enjoyments and all our good to beings without holding back. If today we give what we have to others without craving, our own clinging grows smaller. The ground of mind training that benefits others can then be laid. This brings great happiness to us and to others alike.
