## Today's Verse

> An ordinary enemy, even if driven from one land,
> will dwell in another land,
> gather strength, and return from there.
> The enemy of defilement is not like that. ^4-45

> Defilements are abandoned by the eye of wisdom.
> Once removed from my mind, where will they go?
> Where will they dwell and return to harm me?
> Clinging to the mind is merely striving for myself. ^4-46

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 45 and 46 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Just as darkness has nowhere to hide when the sun rises, let us think together about how the afflictions in our mind, once removed, also have no way to return.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a point that is easy to understand and encouraging. An ordinary worldly enemy, even if driven out of a land, may grow strong again and return. But when the enemy of the afflictions is destroyed at the root by the wisdom that realizes selflessness, it has no place and no strength left to return, so it can never come back. In ultimate truth the afflictions have no true existence of their own; they arise only from confused thought, so they have no more solid core than a bubble. For example, when the sun rises inside a dark house, the darkness is cleared away at once and has nowhere left to go. In the same way, when we look at the afflictions with wisdom, they are naturally purified. So if the afflictions are still left in us, it is not because they have any power of their own by nature. It comes only from our own failure to make the effort to rely on the antidote.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when an affliction arises, I will examine it with wisdom and make the effort to let it go.

**Explanation:** _(Wisdom Practice)_ When an affliction arises in our mind, if we examine it well with the eye of wisdom, we can see that it has no power of its own by nature. When we fail to let go of an affliction, it is because we have been unsteady in our effort or have grown lazy. Today, if we examine our own mind with wisdom and work at letting go of the afflictions, our mind will find peace.
