## Today's Verse

> Everything I have ever enjoyed becomes just a memory, like something from a dream. Once it is gone, it is gone — we can never see it again. ^2-36

> Even in this one lifetime, many people I loved, and many I disliked, have already died and gone. But the wrong I did because of them is still right here, waiting for me. ^2-37

> I never really understood that I too am only passing through this life. Because of that, driven by confusion, attachment, and anger, I have done wrong again and again. ^2-38

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on verses 36 through 38 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. We treat our friends, our rivals, and our possessions as if they were going to stay, and we pile up wrongdoing for their sake. In fact every pleasure and every difficulty of this life passes the way last night's dream passes and becomes nothing but a memory, and we ourselves are here only briefly, with no fixed date of leaving. These verses show us that plainly, and urge us to drop the misdeeds that rest on pointless attachment and dislike.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

Think about last night's dream. You ate something in it, you wore clothes, you sat at home, you had an argument with someone. You woke up, and all that is left is a faint sense that you dreamt something. Now think about the early part of this life — the relatives and neighbours you ate and travelled with, the fights you had. Look for them now and you find the same faint trace. That is what everything we have used comes to.

The people we did wrong for are already gone, many of them within this one life. The wrongdoing is not gone, and the commentaries disagree in an interesting way about how it stays. Khenpo Kunzang Palden says it stays the way a shadow does, never separating, waiting there in front of us. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk says something narrower: it stands before our own mind as an imprint, and as nothing more than that.

Either way the trade is bad. The act is over and cannot be undone. The reason we did it dissolved quickly. What it left in us is still being carried.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, remembering that my possessions and the people I like and dislike are as passing as a dream, I will let go of attachment or dislike the moment I notice it rising.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ Attachment and dislike feel reasonable while they last, because whatever they are aimed at seems solid and important at that moment. Today I will test that. When I catch myself pulled toward something I want or hardened against someone I resent, I will pause and remember that this will pass the way last night's dream passed, and end up as a memory and nothing more. Then I will let the pull go and carry on with what I was doing. Practising this a few times in a day loosens the grip these reactions have on us and leaves the mind noticeably quieter.
