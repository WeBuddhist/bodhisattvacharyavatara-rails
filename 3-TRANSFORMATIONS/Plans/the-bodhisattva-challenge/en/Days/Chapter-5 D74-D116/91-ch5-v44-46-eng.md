## Today's Verse

> If so, everything will be well-accomplished.
> Otherwise, neither will be achieved.
> Then, minor afflictions
> will not increase due to unawareness. ^5-44

> When engaging in various idle chatter
> and many amazing spectacles,
> one should abandon
> attachment to them. ^5-45

> If one digs aimlessly in the ground, cuts grass,
> or draws in the dirt, and so forth,
> recalling the Buddha's training,
> one should abandon it immediately with fear. ^5-46

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the forty-fourth, forty-fifth, and forty-sixth verses of the fifth chapter. As soon as we notice ourselves drawing lines on the ground for no reason, we must stop right there.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us an example that is easy to understand and encouraging.

> If we start the next task before the first is finished,
> neither is done, and this leads to tiredness.
> If we lift both feet without planting one,
> it is a cause of falling.

The meaning of this example is as follows. If we lift both feet without planting one on the ground, we will fall. In the same way, if we take up another task before the first one is finished, neither task will be done. We will only become tired.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, if attachment arises while I look at my phone or chat about nothing useful, I will let it go right away.

**Explanation:** _(Ethics Practice)_ We do not need to stay away from idle talk and shows completely. But if we cling to them, our mind becomes distracted. So if we let go of that clinging when these things come up, we can keep our mind peaceful.
