## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three commentators agree that Verse 1-27 presents a powerful logical progression (*a fortiori* argument) to demonstrate the supreme, incomparable merit of the mind of enlightenment (bodhicitta). They explain how a mere altruistic thought (wishing to benefit others) already outshines vast material offerings made to the Buddhas, and they use this comparison to establish that the actual, active striving to accomplish the complete happiness of all sentient beings without exception yields limitless, immeasurable merit.

### 1. Structural Alignment & Textual Context
*   **Gyaltsab Je's Outline:** Gyaltsab Je places this verse under his division demonstrating the immense benefits of the mind of enlightenment, specifically showing how the active, engaging mind conjoined with the actual practice of conduct is exceptionally superior to the mere aspiring mind or ordinary virtues.
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen's Outline:** Sazang Mati Panchen organizes his explanation of this verse under his section on "how it is supreme among all," specifically focusing on the first of four parts: "how it is supreme among all offerings."
*   **Ngulchu Thokme's Outline:** Ngulchu Thokme analyzes this verse under his section on "the exceptionally distinguished practice," which has two parts: "the vastness of the focus and aspect of the practice" (this verse) and "showing that it is absent in others" (the subsequent verse).

### 2. The Power of Altruistic Thought vs. Material Offerings
The commentators align closely in using scriptural authority to show that a simple loving wish to help others possesses far greater spiritual value than magnificent material offerings:
*   **The Metaphor of Offerings:** The commentators contrast the physical act of making offerings with the mental act of generating loving-kindness. Sazang Mati Panchen and Ngulchu Thokme cite the *King of Meditative Concentrations Sūtra* (*Samādhirāja Sūtra* / *Lamp of the Moon Sūtra*): *"Even if one makes immeasurable, various, and numerous offerings to the Victorious Ones in as many billions of Buddha fields as there are, they cannot equal even a fraction or a drop of the mind of love (or loving compassion)."*
*   **Gyaltsab Je's Formulation:** Gyaltsab Je echoes this scriptural proof, explaining that even the "mere beneficial thought of 'May I become a Buddha to protect all wandering beings'" is taught in the *King of Meditative Concentrations Sūtra* to have merit far exceeding that of making offerings to the Buddhas in as many billions of Buddha-fields as there are.

### 3. Scriptural Proof from the *Inquiry of Vīradatta Sūtra*
Sazang Mati Panchen provides the most extensive scriptural elaboration of this comparison by citing the *Inquiry of Vīradatta Sūtra* (*Vīradattaparipṛcchā Sūtra*):
*   **The Seven Precious Jewels:** Sazang Mati Panchen quotes the sūtra's verse directly to show that if someone were to fill as many Buddha fields as there are grains of sand in the Ganges with the seven precious jewels and offer them to the Protectors of the world, this magnificent offering would still be far surpassed by someone who simply folds their hands with devotion and generates the mind of enlightenment. He emphasizes that the merit of the latter is completely limitless.

### 4. The *A Fortiori* Argument for Active Striving
All three commentators use this comparison to construct a powerful *a fortiori* (how much more so) argument:
*   **From Mere Thought to Active Striving:** If a simple, passive wish or a mere thought of loving-kindness toward sentient beings is already so superior to making material offerings to the Buddhas, then the actual, active effort (*udyamāt* / *བརྩོན་པ་*) of striving to accomplish the complete, supreme happiness of *every single sentient being* without exception must carry merit that is completely infinite and beyond any measure.
*   **Gyaltsab Je** frames this as "making great efforts in practicing and accomplishing the supreme happiness of every single sentient being without exception."
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen** describes it as "engaging with diligent practice in accomplishing the great temporary and ultimate happiness of all sentient beings without exception, who are equal to space."
*   **Ngulchu Thokme** highlights "how exceptionally distinguished is the actual effort made in accomplishing the happiness of every single sentient being without exception."
