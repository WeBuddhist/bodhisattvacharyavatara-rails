## Combined Commentary Summaries

This final verse of the chapter is identified by all three commentators as the "power of restraint" (sdom pa'i stobs), the fourth of the four powers of confession — alongside the power of reliance (on the objects of refuge), the power of the antidote, and the power of regret expressed throughout the preceding verses. The speaker asks the Guides — the Buddhas and bodhisattvas — to recognize the wrongs committed as faults, and vows, since this conduct is not good and ripens unbearably, never to repeat it again, even at the cost of life.

Gyaltsab Je closes his commentary on the chapter by noting that although the scriptures teach many methods of purifying wrongdoing, the confession endowed with all four powers — as practiced throughout this chapter — is the complete antidote, capable of purifying even karma that is otherwise certain to be experienced, a point he supports by citing the *Blaze of Reasoning* and the *Great Commentary on the Eight Thousand Verse Perfection of Wisdom*. He adds a practical exhortation: because ordinary beings are unskilled at discerning karma and constantly create new grounds for wrongdoing, one should continually practice this fourfold confession, with particular attention to purifying whatever obstructs the arising of the mind of enlightenment — linking this final verse of Chapter Two back to the chapter's role in preparing the ground for Chapter Three's cultivation of bodhicitta.
