# Day 31 — Not wicked, just not paying attention

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Tonight, name one thing done just for comfort, and let it go
> **Detail:** Today's verse owns up to a life lived distracted. The remedy starts by turning toward help.

## Opening

After looking squarely at death, Shantideva turns and calls out: "Protectors!" He is speaking to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and what he admits is not some great crime. It is that he lived heedlessly, half asleep, chasing the comfort of this short life and never noticing the danger he was building up. The honest cry for help is where the confession begins.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

The word Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, sits on is heedlessness. Shantideva is not confessing that he set out to be cruel. He is confessing that he was not paying attention, living half asleep, reaching for one small comfort after another and never seeing the harm stacking up behind him. Most of what we get wrong is like this, done not on purpose but on autopilot, for the sake of a life that will not last. Notice what he does with that, though. The very first word is "Protectors!" He turns toward the buddhas and bodhisattvas before he lists a single fault. Owning up and asking for help arrive together. That is what keeps this from collapsing into mere guilt: the point is not to feel terrible about the years of inattention, but to wake up and turn toward something steadier.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Tonight, before you sleep, look back over the day and find one thing you did mainly for your own comfort that cost you or someone else something: a sharp word, a corner cut, a small dishonesty. Name it simply to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, mean the regret, and let it go. You are not confessing to be scolded; you are clearing the desk before sleep.
