# Day 46 — The body is already given: nothing left to guard, and may no one be harmed through me

**Date:** Aug 20  
**Chapter:** 3  
**Verses covered:** 3-12 to 3-14

<!-- sec:challenge -->
## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the three verses 12 to 14 from the third chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. We have already given our body away to others completely. So even if others beat us or mock us, we drop the grip of protecting ourselves. Together we turn our minds to this practice of the helpful attitude. In it, not one being is left without benefit.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us an explanation and a passage of scripture that are easy to understand and encouraging.

We have already given our body to all beings. So even if others beat us, ridicule us, or abuse us, there is no need to protect this body. The reason is that a thing already given to someone else does not need protecting.

From the *Sutra Requested by Ugra*:

> What is given is not to be protected.
> What is kept at home is to be protected.
> What is given brings existence to an end.
> What is kept at home makes existence grow.

As that says, once our body has been given away completely, there is no worry about protecting it. We bring samsara to an end.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when others make fun of me, I will remember that this body has been given away and there is nothing to protect. I will not get angry.

**Explanation:** *(Patience Practice)* Think that we have already given this body to all beings. Then when others look down on us or make fun of us, it is not possible for us to feel hurt or angry. Since it is not held as ours, there is nothing that needs protecting either. If we can cultivate patience from a place of ease, our own mind grows peaceful and the strength of a good mind increases.

<!-- sub:interlinear -->
#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

To show this: **I have** already **given this** very **body to all embodied beings,** that is, to all sentient beings, **to use as they please,** for their enjoyment. So **always,** then — **killing** it by beating, **or reviling** it, which is speaking words that are not praise, or **whatever else they please:** striking it with sticks and the rest, scattering dust upon it and so on — **it rests with what they wish to do.**

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains: I am someone who is working to accomplish nirvana, and someone who is certain to have to give everything up in any case — it is only a question of sooner or later. So from now on I must dedicate my body, my possessions and my roots of virtue to beings from the depths of my heart, and give them away.

In that case, from now on, whatever may come from the side of other beings — harm done to my body, an attack on my body and life, a beating and so on — he explains that all of it has become something for beings to use as they will.

Just as the things in a house, whether set up here or put down there, are in the owner's own hands, so this body is now theirs to dispose of; and, he says, there is nothing in that to make me unhappy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-12]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Here, I have already given this very body to all embodied beings, as many as the reaches of space, to use as they please — that is, however they may wish. So now, always, whatever they please: killing with the body, reviling with the voice, beating with stones and sticks and the rest — all of it, he explains, depends on whatever those beings want, on whatever they wish.

Kunzang Pelden marks this line "third," setting it down in place of the third subdivision of the outline he is treating at this point, and discusses it there.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-12]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

He raises the question, "Having given away the body and the rest, what is one to do then?" and sets this verse down as the answer.

I have already given this body of mine to all embodied beings to use as they please; so, he explains, it rests with beings to do to this body whatever they please — always killing it, reviling it with words, or beating it with their bodies, and so on.

In this way, he says, both attachment to what is one's own and anger toward others are to be blocked in every form.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-12]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Ngulchu Thogme too explains it in this sense: I have already given this body of mine to all embodied beings to use as they please, so it rests with them to do whatever they please — always killing it, reviling it with words, beating it with their bodies and so on. "Do with it as you wish," is the meaning. (This line of his commentary carries verses 3-12 and 3-13 together; but these words themselves apply definitely to the meaning of verse 3-12 proper.)

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-12]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Sabzang Mati Panchen explains it in this way: I have already given this very body, with no reserve, to all embodied beings, as many as the reaches of space, to use as they please, so that each of them may accomplish what they want. So now, always, it rests with them to do whatever they please — killing with the body, reviling with the voice, beating with a stick and so on. "Use it however you wish," is how he puts it.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-12]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that I have given this body of mine to all embodied beings — that is, to all sentient beings — with nothing held back, for them to enjoy as they please.

From now on, always, whether other beings kill me, or revile me and put me down, or beat me with stones and sticks and the rest, or taunt me and so on — he explains that it rests with whatever they like and whatever they feel inclined to do.

He sums it up: the meaning is that this body now belongs to beings.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-12]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Since This Body Was Already Given to All Beings, It Is Now Theirs to Kill, Revile, Beat or Do With As They Please

Many commentaries agree in the end: because I have already given this body to all beings to use as they please, it now rests with whatever those beings wish — always killing it, reviling it, beating it and the rest, whatever they please.

###### 2. Because Everything Must Certainly Be Given Up Sooner or Later, Body, Possessions and Roots of Virtue Should Be Dedicated to Beings From the Heart Right Now

His Holiness the Dalai Lama shows the reason: I am someone working to accomplish nirvana, and it is certain that I will have to give everything up, sooner or later; so from now on body and the rest should be dedicated to beings from the depths of the heart.

###### 3. Since the Body Has Been Given, It Now Belongs to Beings, and Whatever Is Done to It Makes Me No Less Happy

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains the meaning as being that this body now belongs to beings; and His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that, just as the things in a house are in the owner's own hands, the body has passed into the power of the beings themselves, so there is nothing in it to make me unhappy.

###### 4. The Giving of This Body Was Made Without Reserve

Sabzang Mati Panchen explains that this very body was given with no reserve, to be used as they please; and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk agrees, saying it was given with nothing held back.

###### 5. On the Strength of This, Attachment to What Is One's Own and Anger Toward Others Are Both to Be Stopped in Every Form

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen says that whatever comes to this body — killing, reviling, beating and the rest — both attachment to what is one's own and anger toward others are to be blocked in every form.

###### 6. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden Places This Line as the Third Item in His Outline

Kunzang Pelden marks it "third" and sets it down in place of the third subdivision of the outline he is treating at this point.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows that, because I have already given this body without reserve to all beings, it is now all right for beings always to do whatever they please with it — killing it, reviling it, beating it and the rest. All the commentaries agree in the end that this body has now passed into the power of beings.

**Key points.**

- Because this body was already given to all beings to use as they please, killing it, reviling it, beating it and the rest now rest with whatever those beings wish.
- The giving of this body was made without reserve.
- The body has now passed into the power of the beings themselves; and, just as the things in a house are in the owner's own hands, whatever is done to it there is nothing in that to make me unhappy.
- Both attachment to what is one's own and anger toward others are to be blocked in every form.
- Since I am someone working to accomplish nirvana, and it is certain that everything must be given up sooner or later, body, possessions and roots of virtue should be dedicated to beings from the depths of the heart right now.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Even if others play games with my body, or treat it with unfitting and improper contempt by taunting it — that is, with insolence — or make it "a thing of derision," looking at my body and turning it into an occasion for laughter, amazement and mockery: people like us would grow angry, thinking, "He is looking down on me." But someone who is training the mind in bodhicitta does not let the mind be troubled. And since there is nothing here to be held as "mine," to be kept up and looked after, what is the use of fussing over it — of taking charge of it by guarding it from harm and suffering and working for its benefit and comfort? It is not right to hold it as mine.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-13]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Even if they do all sorts of things to my body — all kinds of physical sport with it; or taunt it, testing with all kinds of words, good and bad, to see what one will say; or many people together laugh at it and make it a thing of derision, jeering at it and so on, all sorts of different things — since this body of mine has already been given to all other beings, what is the use of fussing over it, warding off what goes against it and providing what suits it? With these words the notion that one has free authority over the body is blocked.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-13]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

From today onward, even if they play games with my body, or taunt it with insolence, or make it a cause of laughter and derision — since this body of mine has already been given to all beings, what is the use of fussing over guarding it and the like? It has been given away with no reserve, so: do with it whatever you please.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-13]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Since This Body Was Given to All Beings, There Is No Reason to Guard It, Whatever Others Do

Whether they play with it, taunt it, make it a thing of derision, or whatever else is done to it and however it is done: because this body has been given to others, what is the use of the fussing that guards it? Many commentaries agree in the end.

###### 2. These Words Block the Notion That One Has Free Authority Over the Body

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that the very notion of having free authority over what is done with the body is what these words block.

###### 3. The Body Is Not Held as Mine

Sabzang Mati Panchen and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk both make plain that if one asks what the reason is for not needing to guard this body, it is that there is no grasping at it as mine and no clinging to it.

###### 4. Someone Who Trains the Mind in Bodhicitta Is Not Troubled When Others Treat Them With Contempt

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that where an ordinary person would grow angry, one who trains the mind in bodhicitta has no disturbance of mind.

###### 5. Since the Body Was Given Away Without Reserve, Any Claim on It Is Given Up: "Do With It As You Please"

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen makes plain that, as regards this body, it has been given away with no reserve, and so he says, "Do with it whatever you please."

###### 6. "Fussing" Means the Care That Clings to the Body and Holds It as a Self

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk understands "fussing" (ཁ་ཏ) as backing the body up, or the care that grasps at it, clings to it and holds it as a self.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

Whatever deed there may be that **will bring no harm to** those beings, in this life or the next — **let them do it.** And **taking me as their object,** at **any time whatever,** may **not one** being's hope **come to nothing.**

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^3-14]]

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Having given this body away in one's mind, it is not to be left lying there as something with no owner to look after it. In order to accomplish what needs to be done, whatever virtuous deed does not become a circumstance of harm to other beings — let them do that, and one must engage in it oneself. He explains that this body should be put to use as a tool and an instrument for accomplishing virtuous deeds.

As for "taking me as their object, may no one at any time come to nothing": since I have taken up the welfare of all beings as my own burden, this is a prayer — may it never happen that any being who takes me as their object comes to nothing, accumulating wrongdoing and the like.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-14]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

"Whatever deed, good or bad, does not bring harm or wrongdoing to those beings, immediately or in the long run — let them do that": this is to be applied to fitting deeds.

And the prayer that the deed so done not be wasted is this: "Taking me as their object, may not even one being at any time come to nothing." That is stated in general terms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-14]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Whatever virtuous deed there may be that, in this life and the next, will benefit both oneself and others and will bring no harm — let them do it with this body.

The second couplet: of the three — praying that it be a cause of benefit to others alone, praying that the intention not be wasted, and praying that the application not be wasted — this is the first. Its meaning is: taking me as their object, may no being at any time become a cause for anything unwanted arising.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-14]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Following on from "since this body has been given away, what is the use of fussing over guarding it from harm and the like," he explains: whatever deed will bring those beings no harm, immediately or in the long run — let them do that.

The second couplet is the general statement of the two parts of dedicating it to be a cause of benefit; its meaning is: taking me as their object, may no being at any time be left without the aim of benefit and happiness.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-14]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Whatever deed there may be that, in dependence on that body, will not bring harm or wrongdoing to oneself or others in this life or the next — let them do that.

The second couplet is the first of the two parts of making prayers, the prayer that all aims be accomplished; its meaning is: taking the person that I am as their object, may no being at any time come to nothing.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-14]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Since I have given my own body, with no reserve, to all embodied beings to use as they please, it is now all right for those beings to do whatever they please with this body; and out of attachment to the body one should not, in any way at all, show even so much as a sign of anger toward others.

Even so, with this body of mine that has been given away, whatever virtuous deed there may be that will not become a cause of harm to oneself or others, immediately or in the long run, and that will become a cause of benefit and happiness in this life and the next — let them do that too.

This does not contradict the *Compendium of Trainings*, where it is said that even after the body has been given away one must still protect it; for that is a matter of protecting it from pointless harm, whereas the present case is the reverse of that.

The *Compendium of Trainings* says that one must generate enthusiasm for giving by considering the faults of holding body and possessions as one's own and the benefits of giving them away; and it says that even if one cannot actually give away one's body and life, one must first rouse the three kinds of enthusiasm and then train the intention to give, over and over, until it becomes familiar.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-14]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

As for those beings, the recipients to whom this body of mine has been given: whatever virtuous deed there may be that will bring them no harm in any way at all — from now on, let that very deed be done with this body, let it be accomplished with it.

Taking my body and mind as their object, at any time or in any situation, may no being at all come to nothing, or be left without hope, in any way whatever. That is the meaning.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-14]]

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **kunpal:** in the way he takes the first couplet, he says that if there is no harm, then let them do whatever deed there may be, good or bad. ⚑
- **gyaltsab / minyak-kunzang-sonam / khenpo-kunga:** on top of "no harm," they specify further that only virtuous deeds are to be done. ⚑
- **gyaltsab:** he assigns the second couplet as the first of three, namely praying that it be a cause of benefit to others alone. ⚑
- **sabzang:** he assigns the second couplet as the first of the two parts of making prayers, namely the prayer that all aims be accomplished. ⚑
- **ngulchu-thogmed:** he assigns the second couplet as the general statement of the two parts of dedicating it to be a cause of benefit. ⚑
- **gyaltsab:** he explains "to no purpose" as "may it not become a cause for anything unwanted arising for beings." ⚑
- **ngulchu-thogmed:** he explains "to no purpose" as "may they not be left without the aim of benefit and happiness." ⚑
- **khenpo-kunga:** he explains "to no purpose" as "may they not, in any way whatever, be left without purpose or without hope." ⚑
- **tenzin-gyatso:** he explains "to no purpose" as "may it never happen that any being who takes me as their object comes to nothing, accumulating wrongdoing and the like." ⚑

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Since This Body Has Been Given to Beings, One Grants Them Leave: Let Them Do Whatever Deed Brings No Harm

All the commentaries agree in the end in explaining the meaning of the first couplet in this way.

###### 2. The Commentaries Differ Over How Wide the Range of Permitted Deeds Is, on Top of "No Harm" ⚑

Kunzang Pelden explains it broadly, saying "whatever deed there may be, good or bad"; but Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Minyak Kunzang Sonam and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk all three take it as aimed at virtuous deeds alone.

###### 3. The Second Couplet Is a Prayer That No Being Who Takes Me as Their Object Be Left Without Purpose

All the commentaries agree that this couplet is a prayer; but over the precise sense of "to no purpose" they differ. ⚑

###### 4. The Commentators Also Divide the Second Couplet Differently in Their Outlines ⚑

Gyaltsab explains it as the first of three prayers; Sabzang explains it as the first of the two parts of making prayers; and Ngulchu Thogme explains it as the general statement of dedicating it to be a cause of benefit.

###### 5. Giving the Body to Beings Does Not Mean Leaving It Idle: It Is to Be Used as the Equipment for Whatever Virtuous Deed Is at Hand

Minyak Kunzang Sonam and His Holiness the Dalai Lama both explain that, though this body has been given away in one's mind, it is not to be left lying there as something with no owner to look after it; the body is to be put to use as a tool for accomplishing the welfare of beings.

###### 6. This Does Not Contradict the *Compendium of Trainings*, Which Says the Body Must Still Be Protected After It Has Been Given

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that the need to protect it belongs to the case of protecting it from pointless harm, whereas what is spoken of here is the reverse of that.

###### 7. By Considering the Faults of Attachment to Body and Possessions and the Benefits of Giving Them Away, One Rouses Enthusiasm and Trains the Intention to Give

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the *Compendium of Trainings*, the *King of Samādhi Sūtra* and others, and explains that even if one cannot actually give, one must first rouse the three kinds of enthusiasm and then make the intention familiar.

###### 8. The First Couplet Is Applied to Fitting Deeds, and the Second Is a Prayer That the Deed Done Not Be Wasted — Both Stated in General Terms

Kunzang Pelden states the content of both couplets in general terms.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse grants leave, on top of the body's having been given to beings, for them to do whatever deed will bring no harm; and then, in its second half, it shows the way of making a prayer: taking me as their object, may no being at all be left without purpose.

**Key points.**

- Since the body has been given to beings, leave is granted: let them do whatever deed brings no harm.
- ⚑ The commentaries do not agree on whether that harmless deed means whatever deed there may be, good or bad, or whether it must be aimed at virtuous deeds alone.
- ⚑ The second couplet is the prayer, "taking me as their object, may no being at all be left without purpose"; and "to no purpose" is explained variously as a cause of anything unwanted arising, as being without benefit and happiness, and as being without hope.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that giving the body away does not leave it idle: the body is to be used as the equipment for the virtuous deeds that accomplish the welfare of beings, and this does not contradict the *Compendium of Trainings* where it says the body must be protected.
- Many scriptures make plain that one should rouse enthusiasm by considering the faults of attachment to body and possessions and the benefits of giving them away; and that even if one cannot actually give, one must make the intention familiar.
