# Day 32 — The question with only one answer

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Begin one steady practice you can lean on later
> **Detail:** Today's verse asks "what will I do then?" The only answer is what you build now.

## Opening

Shantideva follows fear all the way to its end. Seized, sick, and searching every direction, he finds no protection anywhere, and the verse closes on a bare question: what will I do then? He leaves it hanging on purpose. This is the bottom of the chapter, and the very next thing the text does is turn around and take refuge. The bleak question has an answer, but only one, and only if we reach it in time.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, treats the closing question as the whole point. At the end, he says plainly, there is no method left and no refuge to be found in that place; nothing can be done then. He does not let that land as despair. He turns it straight into instruction: precisely because nothing can be done then, we must work now at the causes of being free from fear. The empty bedside question, what will I do, is answered only by what we put in place beforehand. A refuge is not something you can suddenly locate when terror arrives; it is something you build quietly over time, so that it is already there. The encouragement underneath the grim picture is simple. Today still counts as beforehand.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Pick one small practice you could actually keep: a minute of refuge each morning, a short dedication at night, one honest pause before reacting. Choose just one, and begin it today. A refuge works because it is already familiar when you need it, and the only way it gets familiar is by being built on ordinary days like this one.
