As is said in the *Sūtra of the Miracle of Complete Pacification*: 'O Mañjuśrī, a Bodhisattva who generates an angry or disparaging mind toward another Bodhisattva must don the armor of remaining in hell for as many aeons as there are moments of that mind.' Thus it is said. ^1-11-7

However, if one generates clear faith and devotion toward them, the fruit of that faith increases far more than the fruit of negativity. ^1-11-8

As is said in the *Sūtra of the Mudra of Entering the Definite and Indefinite*: 'O Mañjuśrī, if someone were to put out the eyes of all sentient beings in all the worlds, and another person with a loving mind were to restore their eyes; yet another person who looks with faith at a Bodhisattva who is devoted to the Mahayana generates far greater merit.' Thus it is said. ^1-11-9

Fourth, not being overcome by suffering: Even if severe harm and suffering are inflicted upon the Sons of the Victorious, they do not generate negative thoughts of anger. ^1-11-12

Rather, by the power of those conditions, their virtue increases naturally. ^1-11-13

As is said in the *Compendium of Perfections*: 'Even when inflicted with various fierce harms, the minds of those supreme beings do not change. Because they are exceptionally stable through the power of patience, their devotion to accomplishing supreme enlightenment increases.' Thus it is said. ^1-11-14

Fifth, showing that they are worthy of homage and refuge: As is said in the *Ratnakūṭa Sūtra*: 'For example, if a great king has a son endowed with the marks of royalty, the moment that young prince is born, all the citizens and minor kings gather to honor him. Likewise, the moment the mind of perfect awakening is generated, that prince of the Victorious is honored by the world including the gods, and they cherish him with devotion.' Thus it is said. ^1-11-17

Therefore, I pay homage to the body of anyone in whom this precious, supreme mind has arisen. ^1-11-23

And I take refuge in that source of happiness, the Bodhisattva, who, even when harmed, connects the harmer with happiness and establishes them in the state of bliss. ^1-11-24

If someone asks: 'Does this not conflict with the previous statement that harming a Bodhisattva leads to hell?' ^1-11-25

There is no conflict. ^1-11-26

The former teaches the ripening effect of the negative mind itself, ^1-11-27

whereas this refers to the Bodhisattva's compassionate resolve to temporarily and ultimately guide the harmer to liberation. ^1-11-28

For example, just as King Maitrībala established the five yakṣas (spirits) in temporary and ultimate happiness through the power of his love. ^1-11-29

Those who cannot see this sun of the mind of awakening, which completely dispels the darkness of the two obscurations, but remain trapped in the darkness of ignorance and extreme views—how wondrous is the blind eye of their view! ^1-11-30

This completes the explanation of Chapter One. ^1-11-31
