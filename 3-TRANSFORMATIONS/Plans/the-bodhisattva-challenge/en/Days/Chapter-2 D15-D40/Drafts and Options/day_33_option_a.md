# Day 33 — Someone both willing and able

> **Notification**
> **Title:** When you start to sink today, hand it over instead
> **Detail:** After the fear, the answer arrives. Today's verses turn and take refuge.

## Opening

After two days at the edge of despair, the answer arrives. Having searched everywhere and found nothing, Shantideva turns and takes refuge: from this very day, in the buddhas, the dharma, and the sangha. The darkness was never the end of the story. It was clearing the ground for this turn toward something that can actually hold us.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, points out exactly why the buddhas are worth turning to, and it comes down to two things held together. First, they actually want to help: their whole effort, perfected over a long path, is bent toward protecting beings. Second, they are able to, with a power equal to the task. He says it is the two together that let them dispel fear. The distinction is worth keeping. We lean on plenty of things that are willing but cannot really help, a worried friend, our own racing plans, and on things that could help but do not particularly care for us. A refuge you can rest your whole weight on has to be both. Ngulchu adds, plainly, what taking refuge actually means in the moment: it is saying "I rely on you, please help me." Not a theory, just turning, and trusting.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Once today, when you feel your spirits start to sink under some worry or setback, try not letting them drop all the way. Instead, hand the thing over: "You who can truly help, I rely on you with this." You are not pretending the problem is gone. You are choosing, in that moment, to lean on something steady rather than carry it alone.
