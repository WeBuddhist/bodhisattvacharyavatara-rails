## 10. Chapter Ten: Dedication ^10-0


By the virtue I have gained
through composing this text on entering
the conduct of a bodhisattva,
may all beings enter into the conduct of awakening. ^10-1


May all those, in every direction,
suffering in body and mind,
obtain, through my merit,
oceans of joy and happiness. ^10-2


For as long as they remain in saṃsāra,
may their happiness never decline;
may all beings ceaselessly obtain
the happiness of bodhisattvas. ^10-3


Whatever hells exist
throughout the realms of the world,
may the beings there
find joy in the happiness of Sukhāvatī. ^10-4


May those tormented by cold find warmth,
and may those tormented by heat be cooled
by boundless rivers flowing forth
from the great clouds of the bodhisattvas. ^10-5


May the forest of leaf-bladed swords
become for them a dense grove of sandalwood;
may the trees of the śālmalī forest
become instead wish-fulfilling trees. ^10-6


May the regions of hell become delightful,
with lakes rich in the fragrance of lotuses,
beautified by the sweet cries
of wild ducks, geese, and swans. ^10-7


May those heaps of burning coals become heaps of jewels;
may the burning ground become a floor of crystal;
may the crushing mountains of hell become
mansions of offering, filled with Sugatas. ^10-8


May the rain of coals, stones, and weapons
from now on become a rain of flowers;
and may the mutual striking with weapons
from now on become a playful tossing of flowers. ^10-9


May those sunk in the fathomless river like a pit of embers,
their flesh all consumed, their bones the color of the kunda flower,
through the power of my virtue, attain divine bodies,
and dwell at ease in the river, together with goddesses. ^10-10


"Why is it that here the men of the Lord of Death, and the crows and terrible vultures, no longer inspire fear?
Whose is this fine power that dispels all darkness and gives rise to joy and happiness?"
Looking up, may they see, blazing in the midst of the sky, Vajrapāṇi himself,
and, filled with great joy, freed from wrongdoing, may they come to join him. ^10-11


Seeing a rain of flowers falling, mixed with scented water,
extinguishing with a hiss the burning coals of hell,
thinking, "What is this sudden satisfaction of happiness?" —
may the beings in the hells of the mind then behold Padmapāṇi. ^10-12


"Friends, cast aside your fear, and come quickly to my side!" —
by whose power all suffering is dispelled and the force of joy arrives,
who protects all beings everywhere, in whom bodhicitta and loving-kindness have arisen —
who has come, the youth with the topknot, blazing, dispelling all fear? ^10-13


"Look — his lotus feet honored by the crowns of a hundred gods,
his compassion-moistened eyes, a rain of many flowers falling upon his head,
in a delightful many-storied palace, praised by a thousand goddesses' songs" —
seeing such a Mañjughoṣa, may the beings in the mental hells now cry out in joy. ^10-14


Thus, seeing the roots of my virtue manifest as a cloud,
unobstructed, cool, fragrant, and pleasing,
raining down like Samantabhadra and the other bodhisattvas,
may the beings in the hells rejoice greatly. ^10-15


May animals be freed
from the fear of devouring one another;
may the hungry ghosts be as happy
as the people of Uttarakuru. ^10-16


May the hungry ghosts be satisfied,
and always cool and refreshed by bathing,
in the stream of milk
flowing from the hand of noble Avalokiteśvara. ^10-17


May the blind come to see forms,
and the deaf always hear sounds;
may pregnant women give birth without harm,
like the goddess Māyā did. ^10-18


May the naked obtain clothing,
the hungry obtain food,
and the thirsty obtain water
and other delicious drinks. ^10-19


May the destitute obtain wealth;
may the grief-stricken obtain joy;
may the despairing find new heart,
and firm resolve, and perfect success. ^10-20


May all sick beings
be swiftly freed from their sickness;
may every illness that afflicts beings
never arise again. ^10-21


May the frightened become fearless;
may the bound be set free;
may the powerless gain strength;
may all beings' hearts turn kindly toward one another. ^10-22


May all travelers, in every direction,
find happiness on their way;
and may whatever purpose they travel for
be accomplished without effort. ^10-23


May those who set out in boats and great ships
have their heart's wishes fulfilled;
may they safely reach the shore,
and rejoice there together with their kin. ^10-24


May those lost on desolate roads
find fellow travelers,
and, free from fear of bandits, tigers, and the like,
travel on without fatigue, at ease. ^10-25


May the gods protect
children, the elderly, and the unprotected,
in wastelands and pathless places of dread,
and those asleep or overcome by intoxication. ^10-26


May all be freed from every hardship,
endowed with faith, wisdom, and loving-kindness,
with excellent food and conduct,
and may they always remember their past lives. ^10-27


May all beings possess wealth inexhaustible,
like a treasury as vast as the sky,
and, free of conflict and harm,
enjoy it freely, without constraint. ^10-28


May those beings of little splendor
come to possess great splendor;
may ascetics of unpleasant appearance
become excellent and beautiful in form. ^10-29


May all the women of the world
attain the state of men;
may the lowly attain high station,
yet without arrogance. ^10-30


By this merit of mine,
may all sentient beings without exception,
having abandoned all wrongdoing,
constantly perform virtue. ^10-31


May they never be separated from bodhicitta,
and remain devoted to the conduct of awakening;
may they be fully embraced by the Buddhas,
and abandon all the deeds of Māra. ^10-32


May the lifespan of all these beings
be immeasurably long;
may they always live happily,
and may the very word "death" never be heard. ^10-33


May all directions everywhere
be filled with groves of wish-fulfilling trees,
resounding with the sweet Dharma
proclaimed by Buddhas and their bodhisattva heirs. ^10-34


May the ground everywhere
be free of gravel and the like,
smooth as the palm of a hand,
and of the nature of lapis lazuli. ^10-35


May the great assemblies of bodhisattvas,
seated in their nine-fold maṇḍalas,
adorn the surface of the earth
with their own excellence, and dwell there. ^10-36


May all embodied beings ceaselessly hear
the sound of the Dharma
from birds, from trees, from every ray of light,
and even from the sky itself. ^10-37


May they always meet
with Buddhas and their bodhisattva heirs,
and, with boundless clouds of offering,
may they honor the guides of all beings. ^10-38


May the gods send rain at the proper season,
and may harvests flourish abundantly;
may rulers govern in accordance with the Dharma,
and may the world everywhere prosper. ^10-39


May medicines be potent,
and the recitation of secret mantras effective;
may ḍākinīs, rākṣasas, and the like
be filled with hearts of compassion. ^10-40


May no sentient being ever suffer,
commit wrong, fall ill,
be despised, or be discouraged. ^10-41


May monasteries flourish, well adorned
with study and recitation;
may the saṅgha always dwell in harmony,
and may the purposes of the saṅgha be accomplished. ^10-42


May monks who wish to train
find the solitude they seek;
having abandoned all distraction,
may their minds become workable, and settle in meditation. ^10-43


May nuns be provided with what they need,
and free from conflict and harm;
and likewise may all who have gone forth
never decline in their discipline. ^10-44


May those distressed by broken discipline
constantly exhaust their wrongdoing;
and may they attain a fortunate rebirth,
without their vows ever declining even there. ^10-45


May the learned be honored,
and always receive alms with ease;
may their minds be perfectly pure,
and their good renown spread in every direction. ^10-46


Without ever experiencing the suffering of the lower realms,
and without needing to undergo hardship,
may all beings, with bodies surpassing even the gods,
swiftly attain buddhahood. ^10-47


May all sentient beings, again and again,
make offerings to all the Buddhas,
and, through the Buddhas' inconceivable happiness,
may they always dwell in happiness. ^10-48


May the bodhisattvas' intentions
for the welfare of beings be fulfilled;
and may whatever the Protectors intend for beings
come to pass for them. ^10-49


Likewise, may the pratyekabuddhas
and the śrāvakas also attain happiness. ^10-50


And may I too, through the kindness of Mañjughoṣa,
until I attain the ground of Great Joy,
always remember my past lives,
and attain the state of a renunciant. ^10-51


May I live sustained
by even the simplest food,
and in every life
find the perfect conditions for a life of solitude. ^10-52


Whenever I wish to behold him,
or even to ask some small question,
may I see the Protector Mañjughoṣa himself,
without any obstruction. ^10-53


In order to accomplish the welfare
of all beings throughout the ten directions, to the limits of space,
just as Mañjughoṣa acts,
may my own conduct be just the same. ^10-54


For as long as space endures,
and for as long as the world remains,
so long may I too remain,
dispelling the sufferings of the world. ^10-55


Whatever suffering there is for beings,
may it all ripen upon me;
and through the community of bodhisattvas,
may the world enjoy happiness. ^10-56


May the teachings, the sole medicine for beings' suffering,
the very source from which all happiness arises,
long endure,
provided with support and honor. ^10-57


I bow to Mañjughoṣa,
through whose kindness virtuous thought arises in me;
and I bow also to the spiritual friend,
through whose kindness I have flourished. ^10-58


This concludes the tenth chapter, entitled "Dedication," from the Way of the Bodhisattva. ^10-a
