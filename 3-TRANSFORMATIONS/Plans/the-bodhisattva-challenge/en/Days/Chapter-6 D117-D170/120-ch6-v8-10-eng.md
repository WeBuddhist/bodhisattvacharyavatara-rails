## Today's Verse

> Therefore, I will completely destroy
> the food of that enemy.
> For this enemy has no other function
> than to harm me. ^6-8

> Whatever happens, I will not
> disturb my joyful mind.
> Even if I am unhappy, my wishes will not be accomplished,
> and my good deeds will be ruined. ^6-9

> If something can be remedied,
> what is the point of being unhappy about it?
> If something cannot be remedied,
> what benefit is there in being unhappy? ^6-10

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the eighth, ninth, and tenth verses of the sixth chapter. If a problem can be fixed, there is no point in being unhappy. If it cannot be fixed, what good does being unhappy do?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable point, which is easy to understand and encouraging. A damaged copper pot can be repaired. In the same way, if something unwanted can be fixed, it is enough to fix it right away. So it is right to give up unhappiness and work hard on a way to fix it. Being unhappy about what cannot be fixed is like being unhappy about a broken clay pot or about the sky. It does not help at all, and since it has already passed, nothing can be done. Unhappiness does not only fail to help. The suffering of disturbing ourselves and others harms us even more.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when a problem comes, I will see what cannot be fixed as my own karma, and I will not get angry.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ If a problem can be fixed, we can just fix it, so there is no reason to be unhappy. If it cannot be fixed, unhappiness does not help, and the suffering of a disturbed mind grows. Therefore, we should see what cannot be fixed as the ripening of our own past karma.
