# Day 51 — Born into the buddhas' family, and guarding the rare jewel of bodhicitta

**Date:** Aug 25

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the three verses 25 to 27 from the third chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. A blind man finds a wish-fulfilling jewel in a heap of sweepings at the roadside. Bodhicitta is as hard to come by as that. Together let us look at how precious it is that this mind has arisen in us.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Here the blind man is a person whose eye of wisdom has been blinded by not knowing. The heap of rubbish is the heap of our own afflictions and karma: desire, anger, jealousy, pride and the rest.

The blind man finds a precious jewel in such rubbish. In the same way, this precious bodhicitta has arisen in the middle of the dust of our afflictions. It came by the good fortune of merit and the blessing of the buddhas. That is truly a great wonder.

This precious mind removes all the ruin and poverty of samsara and nirvana. Now that we have found it, we should give rise to great joy, hold it dear, and guard it well.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, instead of thinking only about what I want, I will carry out one act that helps someone else.

**Explanation:** *(Ethics Practice)* We have entered the family of the buddhas' heirs. So if we give up the thought of pursuing our own aims alone and benefit others instead, our vow becomes pure. And the family of the buddhas is not stained by any fault.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

How so? Through the beginningless succession of lives in samsara nothing like this was ever obtained; and so **today my life has borne fruit,** and **this human existence too has been well obtained.** **From today I am born into the family of the buddhas,** and **now I have become an heir of the buddhas.** Thinking in this way, one brings to mind a special kind of pride, and so completely casts off all smallness of mind.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

At this present time we have taken the bodhisattva vow, and so our life has come to bear fruit. In this life we have obtained a human existence — that is, a human body; we have met with the Buddha's teaching; and within that teaching we have met with the Dharma of the Great Vehicle, generated the mind set on supreme awakening, and taken the vow. And so today we are born into the family of the buddhas.

Strictly speaking, this is said of someone in whom all the defining marks are complete. Even so, it is quite right for us to think: I have been made someone who has the good fortune to become just that before very long.

Since one is born into the family of the buddhas, "I have become an heir of the buddhas" — saying this to oneself, one uplifts one's own mind and meditates on joy.

Yet having become an heir of the buddhas is not a matter of seeking a good name for it, nor is it any sort of rank or position that one has been given. What is it, then? Each of us must rouse our courage and make up our mind to work for the welfare of others.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-25]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

How is it that the mind has been generated? At this present time this life of mine, while I am alive, has come to have meaning, to bear fruit. As the verse says: "Used well, this body is the boat that carries one to liberation; used badly, this body is the stone that sinks one in samsara." So the human birth, this human existence, has not been badly obtained but well obtained. And from this day on I am born into the family of the perfect buddhas, the blessed ones, and I have now become an heir of their heart, one who upholds the lineage of all the buddhas.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-25]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->
##### Zhenphen Jampé Gocha (annotation commentary)

In Zhenga's annotation commentary too there is an explanation that goes beyond a bare gloss of the ground text. The way it is put here — that at the time of generating the mind and taking the vow what was amiss has been made good — is explained as a method for bringing to mind a special kind of pride and so completely giving up all smallness of mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^3-25]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

In this way the wise bodhisattva, having taken firm hold of the two kinds of bodhicitta with a mind of clear delight, joy and faith, then rouses enthusiasm and uplifts the mind as follows — so that the mind may not decline but increase, and so that the concluding part of the ritual too may be practised in full.

How? At this time of generating the mind and taking the vow, my life has been made fruitful, and human existence has been well obtained: this freedom and good fortune has been made meaningful. Today I am born into the family of the buddhas, that is, I have become a bodhisattva. And the longing one used to have — "when will I ever become an heir of the buddhas?" — is now met with the thought, "Today I have become an heir of the buddhas." In this way one meditates on joy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-25]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Today my life has come to bear great fruit. How so? Because human existence has been well obtained, and today I am born into the family of the buddhas and have now become an heir of the buddhas. (This explanation is a single passage covering verses 3-25, 3-26 and 3-27 together; only the portion given above bears directly on this verse.)

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-25]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Now, at this time when bodhicitta has arisen, I have made this life of mine, while I am alive, fruitful and meaningful; and the human existence, the birth, that I have obtained I have made into something well obtained. For from this day on I am one of the Great Vehicle, born into the family of the buddhas, the blessed ones, and from today I myself have become an heir who upholds the lineage of all the buddhas.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-25]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

The Protector Maitreya said to Sudhana: "That you have generated the mind in this way means that what was to be gained you have gained well; you have obtained a human body well; and in this world of the living you live well" — and so on.

Accordingly, at this present time, when the mind has been generated and the vow taken, this life of mine, this continuous stream of the living breath by which I live, has come to hold great meaning and great fruit. As for this human existence, this birth: in all my former births I never once obtained it, and this time I have found the precious bodhicitta that is so hard ever to find. So this support endowed with freedom and good fortune has been well obtained. And from this day on I am born into the family of the buddhas — that is, I have become a bodhisattva. What I used to long for, thinking "when will I ever become an heir of the victorious ones?", has come about, and from now on I have become an heir who fills out the lineage of the buddhas. Giving rise to joy in this way, one cultivates the pride that dispels discouragement.

Here "born into the family of the buddhas" is said with reference to the family of those who have devotion to the buddhas, or to the arising of conventional bodhicitta — as was said above: "The moment bodhicitta arises, one is called an heir of the Blissful Ones." Where it is said earlier that on attaining the first ground one is born into the family of the tathāgatas, that is intended for the family of those certain to awaken, or for the occasion when ultimate bodhicitta arises.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-25]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

He sets this out as the way one has gained one's own aim. First, the way one's own living is made meaningful: today, of the two — a human life that bears fruit and one that does not — because bodhicitta has been generated in my mind stream, my human life has become one that does bear fruit. So one thinks.

Then, the way the human body of freedom and good fortune has not been wasted: of finding or obtaining this human existence, this human body, there are two kinds — obtaining it well and obtaining it badly. Today, because bodhicitta has arisen in my mind stream, this human existence, this bodily support, has been well obtained. So one thinks.

Then, the way one has found the capacity to attain buddhahood: until now I was an ordinary being caught in samsara, but today, because bodhicitta has been generated in my mind stream, I am born into the family of the perfect buddhas, and I have become someone who in the future can become a buddha. So one thinks.

And "I have now become an heir of the buddhas" means this: until now I was called a sentient being, but today, because bodhicitta has been generated in my mind stream, today I have become an heir of the buddhas' heart, one who upholds the lineage of the victorious buddhas. Thinking this, one uplifts one's own mind and meditates on joy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-25]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Celebration That, at the Time of Generating the Mind and Taking the Vow, This Life Bears Fruit and Human Existence Is Well Obtained

At the time bodhicitta is generated, this very life of mine comes to have meaning and to bear fruit; and the human existence obtained is well obtained — for from today one is born into the family of the buddhas and has become an heir of the buddhas. All the commentaries agree on this in the end.

###### 2. These Words Are Taught as a Method for Rousing a Special Kind of Pride and Casting Off Smallness of Mind and Discouragement

Zhenga's annotation commentary explains that these words bring a special kind of pride to mind and so completely cast off smallness of mind. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen says they rouse enthusiasm and uplift the mind; Minyak Kunzang Sonam says they are the cultivation of the pride that dispels discouragement; and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and His Holiness the Dalai Lama agree that this is a way of uplifting one's own mind and meditating on joy.

###### 3. "Born Into the Family of the Buddhas" Is Said of the Arising of Conventional Bodhicitta, Not of the Actual Birth Into the Ultimate Family on the First Ground

Minyak Kunzang Sonam distinguishes: here "born into the family of the buddhas" is said only with reference to the family of those who have devotion to the buddhas, or to the arising of conventional bodhicitta; where being born into the family of the tathāgatas is said of attaining the first ground, that is intended for the occasion when ultimate bodhicitta arises. His Holiness the Dalai Lama likewise says that this is not the actual state of a fully qualified heir of the buddhas. ⚑

###### 4. Obtaining Human Existence Well Is Set Against the Danger of Obtaining It Badly

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk distinguishes two ways of obtaining human existence, or a human body — obtaining it well and obtaining it badly — and explains that generating the mind has made it well obtained. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden shows the same thing with the image of the body used well as the boat of liberation and used badly as the stone that sinks one in samsara; and Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen too says that freedom and good fortune have thereby been made meaningful.

###### 5. The Real Point of This Verse Is Not to Seek a Good Name but to Rouse the Courage to Work for Others and Resolve to Do It

His Holiness the Dalai Lama says that one should not go after a reputation for having become an heir of the buddhas; rather, each of us must rouse our courage and make up our mind to work for the welfare of others.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Now, by whatever means, whatever situation I may find myself in, I must hold nothing in mind as my purpose but the welfare of sentient beings. The actions that accord with the family of those bodhisattvas are the four means of attracting disciples, the six perfections and the like — the bodhisattva conduct undertaken for the welfare of beings. In these, he explains, one must train.

"The pure and noble family" means the ultimate nobility that is free of the stain of the fault of self-concern. In the ethical discipline of a bodhisattva, he explains, restraining self-concern is the main thing; and if self-concern gets mixed in, one falls away from the bodhisattva's discipline, so one must be careful.

Citing many scriptural passages, he explains what the *Compendium of Trainings* says: even if a bodhisattva heedlessly enjoys the objects of desire, so long as the mind of self-concern is not mixed in he still has ethical discipline; whereas if one cannot restrain self-concern, then however quiet and gentle one's outward bearing may be, one's discipline is broken.

Here "the pure and noble family" is explained as the very root of how a bodhisattva thinks: the mind that stops the pursuit of one's own comfort and takes on the welfare of others as its one burden.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-26]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

So now, whatever task may fall to me, I shall undertake only actions that accord with the family of the Great Vehicle — that is, actions for the welfare of others.

From the Buddha, the Blessed One, and Mañjughoṣa and the rest, down to myself, all the teachers of the lineage have never been touched by a root downfall through self-serving attitudes and the like; and so, being without the fault of any downfall, this is a family that has become noble.

As for this family, I must think again and again about making the effort to train in such a way that I for my part live up to my father's example as his heir, and that the family is never clouded by the stain of faults and downfalls.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-26]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Now, by whatever means, I shall undertake actions of the three doors that accord with the family of my father, the Buddha.

This family is without fault at the beginning, in the middle and at the end, and is adorned by nothing but good qualities, and so is noble. I shall make the effort in such a way that this family is never clouded in my mind stream by the faults and downfalls that go against the trainings of aspiration and engagement.

##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

*(The text of this commentary is a single passage explaining verses 3-25 to 3-27 together; here only the portion that bears directly on verse 3-26 is given.)*

So now, by whatever means, I shall undertake actions of the three doors that accord with that family; and since this family is pure at the beginning, in the middle and at the end, and so without fault and noble, I shall accomplish it in such a way that it is not clouded by the stain of wrongdoing.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-26]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

From now on, whatever situation I may be in, I shall undertake only the work that accords with the family of the Great Vehicle — the conduct that serves the welfare of others.

This family that I have obtained is without the fault of going against the trainings, and has become noble; I shall make the effort to accomplish it in such a way that it is never clouded by the stain of faults and downfalls.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-26]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Merely to be born into the family of the buddhas and to be their heir is not enough. So from now on I must certainly, by whatever means, undertake actions of the three doors that accord with the family of the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

On the side of intention, it is a family in which there is no fault at all — nothing like the thought of seeking peace and happiness for oneself, but only the intention that seeks the welfare of others. On the side of conduct, it is a noble family that is never in the least stained by anything like the breaking of discipline that comes with the faults and downfalls that go against the trainings, rather than training properly in what the buddhas' heirs are to train in. In both these respects, he explains, it is without fault at the beginning, in the middle and at the end.

Having gained the good fortune of being born into a family so completely pure, one is to be careful with it and to accomplish it well, so that on the side of intention and on the side of conduct alike it is not clouded by the stain of what opposes it. This, he explains, is a pledge made with a cherishing heart.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-26]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

The good intention of bodhicitta has already arisen in my own mind stream. So now, by whatever means, whatever the immediate need may be, I shall undertake those virtuous actions that accord with the lineage of the buddhas — the accomplishing of the welfare of other sentient beings.

"Without fault at the beginning, in the middle or at the end" he identifies with the stages of the ritual: at the beginning, purifying the mind stream with the preliminary practices; in the main part, making the pledge by repeating the words three times; and at the end, meditating on one's own joy and the joy of others.

As for this support, which has become such a faultless, worthy and noble family, one should think: I shall act so that it is not clouded by my own karma and afflictions — attachment, aversion and confusion and the rest — but grows purer and purer. So he explains.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-26]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. From Now On, Whatever Situation One Is In, One Must Undertake Only Actions for Others That Accord With the Family

Many commentaries agree that this is the main point of the verse.

###### 2. This Noble Family Is Identified as Being Without Fault at the Beginning, in the Middle and at the End

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both explain that this family is without fault at the beginning, in the middle and at the end, and is adorned with good qualities.

###### 3. The Root of Fault Is the Self-Serving Attitude, the Thought That Attends to One's Own Aims

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that being untouched by root downfalls through self-serving attitudes and the like is what makes the family faultless. His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that the ultimate meaning of "the pure and noble family" is precisely being free of the stain of the fault of self-concern.

###### 4. "Is Not Defiled" Means Keeping the Mind Stream Unstained by the Faults and Downfalls That Go Against the Trainings of Aspiring and Engaged Bodhicitta

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen makes this point in particular.

###### 5. The Faultlessness of the Noble Family Depends on Both Sides — the Intention That Seeks the Welfare of Others, and Training Properly in the Conduct

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that it is faultless in respect of both intention and conduct.

###### 6. This Verse Belongs With the Concluding Pledge of the Threefold Ritual for Generating Bodhicitta, and One Must See That the Three Poisons Do Not Stain the Family

So Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains.

###### 7. "Actions That Accord With the Family" Are the Four Means of Attracting Disciples, the Six Perfections and So On; and, as the *Compendium of Trainings* Says, It Is Self-Concern That Decides Whether Discipline Is Broken or Not

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains this in detail.

###### 8. This Verse Closes the Section on Conduct That Accords With the Family and Opens the Following Section on Giving Rise to Joy at Finding What Is Rare

Ngulchu Thogme's explanation, which bears directly on this verse, shows the same meaning as the above; and Gyaltsab and the others write the outline heading of the new section after this verse.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows the pledge that from now on, whatever situation one is in, one will strive only in the actions for the welfare of others that accord with the family of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and that this noble family — faultless at the beginning, in the middle and at the end — will never be clouded by the stain of one's own faults and downfalls. All the commentaries agree on this main point, and they explain in detail that the root of fault is the self-serving attitude, the thought that attends to one's own aims.

**Key points.**

- From now on, whatever situation one is in, one must undertake only actions for the welfare of others that accord with the family.
- This noble family is identified as being without fault at the beginning, in the middle and at the end, and as adorned with good qualities.
- The root of fault is the self-serving attitude, the thought that attends to one's own aims; and it is this that causes a bodhisattva's ethical discipline to be broken.
- Actions that accord with the family are the four means of attracting disciples, the six perfections and the like; and faultlessness must hold on the side of intention and on the side of conduct alike.
- This verse belongs with the concluding pledge of the ritual for generating bodhicitta, and one must see that the three poisons do not stain the family.
- This verse closes the section on conduct that accords with the family and opens the following section on giving rise to joy.

#### Root Verse

*Plain English translation (source: `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-translate/BCA-Full-Plain-English.md#^3-27`):*

> It is like a blind person stumbling upon a precious jewel in a heap of trash. By some strange stroke of good fortune, bodhichitta has arisen in someone like me.

---

<!-- sub:interlinear -->
#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

As for what is extremely hard to come across: for example, **just as a blind** person with no eyes **might find** a priceless **jewel** in **a heap of sweepings** — the rubbish heaped up along the great road — **so, by some chance,** that is, by a stroke of luck, **this** precious **mind of awakening has arisen in me.** The meaning is that in samsara, which is like a heap of sweepings, for certain ones who have no realization of suchness this has arisen — somehow, through the power of the buddhas' blessing — like the precious mind of awakening.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

We are born now in an age of dregs, when faculties and intelligence are weak. As is said in the *Madhyamakāvatāra*, we have been born in a time of the five degenerations — the degeneration of lifespan, the degeneration of the afflictions and the rest — and that is a poor thing. Yet even so, with the protection of a teacher, a spiritual friend, and relying on these excellent texts, we are able to give rise to a good heart; and that, he explains, is finding a good fortune that is very hard to find.

In short, this is something almost impossible, like a blind man finding a jewel in a heap of sweepings. By some chance we have this time gained devotion to the Dharma of the Great Vehicle, and, relying on the kindness of a teacher, a spiritual friend, we have found a mind such as this. So he explains.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-27]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

He shows the way it is hard to find with an example: if a blind man, who can find nothing at all, were to find a precious wish-fulfilling jewel in a heap of dust swept up in some place, he would look on it as an utter wonder and immense joy would arise in him.

In just the same way, from within the dust-heap of the afflictions that is the ordinary person's condition, by a good stroke of merit, this precious bodhicitta — which clears away every ruin of samsara and of nirvana — has arisen in me. This, he explains, should be looked on as a very great wonder, and one should rejoice and be glad.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-27]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->
##### Zhenphen Jampé Gocha (annotation commentary)

Besides the bare gloss of the ground text, the annotation commentary also gives an explanation of the meaning: in samsara, which is like a heap of sweepings, for certain ones who have no realization of suchness this has arisen — somehow, through the power of the buddhas' blessing — like the precious mind of awakening. So he says.

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->

Just as a poor and destitute blind man might find a jewel in a heap of sweepings, so, by a stroke of luck, this precious bodhicitta has arisen in me; and that, he explains, is certainly the power of the buddhas.

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

For example, just as it is a wonder when a blind man finds a jewel in a heap of sweepings, so, by some chance — that is, by a stroke of luck — this mind of awakening has arisen in me: how wonderful this is. So he explains.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-27]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

For example, just as it is a great marvel when a blind person finds a precious jewel in a heap of dust swept up in some place, so in just the same way, when from within the afflictions that are the ordinary person's condition, by a good stroke of merit, this mind of awakening — the supreme of all things — has arisen in me, that is a very great marvel; and so, he explains, one should rejoice and be glad.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-27]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

He sets out the example: if a destitute, poor person, blind and without eyes, were to find a jewel of great value in a heap of rubbish that had been swept up and thrown away, then whichever way one looked at it — the finder, the place where it was found, or the thing found — it would be so exceedingly rare as to be a great wonder.

In just the same way, from within the thick dust-heap of karma and the afflictions, through the power of the buddhas' blessing and a good stroke of one's own merit, this mind of awakening — like a precious wish-fulfilling jewel — has been found by me, an ordinary being destitute of the wealth of the noble ones and blind for want of the eye of wisdom. Whichever way one looks at it this is so exceedingly rare that it should be held as a wonder and, he explains, cherished and guarded with care.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-27]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

He sets out the example: if a blind person, both of whose eyes are useless, were to find, in among a heap of filthy sweepings piled up along the great road, a precious jewel of great value able to clear away the poverty of seven generations, everyone would be astonished and filled with wonder.

In just the same way, one's own mind stream is obscured by confusion and ignorance, so that one is as if blind in the eye of wisdom; and from within the filth that is like a heap of sweepings — attachment and aversion, jealousy and rivalry, pride and the rest of the afflictions — one has found the precious jewel of bodhicitta, which is like the source of everything one could need or want. One should think: by good fortune, and through the power of interdependent connection, it has arisen in my own mind stream as though the buddhas had blessed it. So he explains.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-27]]

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

The commentaries give different reasons for the words "by some chance."

- **Khenpo Kunzang Pelden and Sabzang Mati Panchen:** they explain that this precious bodhicitta has arisen in me by the force of a good stroke of my own merit. ⚑
- **Zhenphen Jampé Gocha and Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen:** they explain that it is certainly by the power or force of the buddhas' blessing. ⚑
- **Minyak Kunzang Sonam and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk:** they explain that it depends on both — the power of the buddhas' blessing and a good stroke of one's own merit. ⚑
- **His Holiness the Dalai Lama:** he explains that even for those of weak faculties in this age of dregs, this good mind is found by relying on the kindness of a teacher, a spiritual friend. ⚑

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Bodhicitta Arising Is as Rare and Wonderful as a Blind Man Finding a Wish-Fulfilling Jewel in a Heap of Sweepings

All the commentaries agree in the end: finding a precious jewel inside a heap of sweepings is given as the illustration of bodhicitta arising in the mind stream of an ordinary being, and they explain it as an exceedingly great wonder.

###### 2. The Commentaries Take Different Views of the Reason Behind "By Some Chance"

Some assign it to a good stroke of one's own merit; some assign it to the power of the buddhas' blessing; and some explain that it depends on both. ⚑

###### 3. The Heap of Sweepings Is Explained as the Dust-Heap of Karma and the Afflictions

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both apply the heap of sweepings to the afflictions and to the dust of karma — attachment, aversion, jealousy, rivalry, pride and the rest.

###### 4. The Blind Man Is Explained as the Ordinary Being Whose Eye of Wisdom Is Obscured by Confusion and Ignorance

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that one's own mind stream is obscured by confusion and ignorance, so that one is as if blind in the eye of wisdom; and Minyak Kunzang Sonam likewise speaks of the ordinary being as being like a blind man for want of the eye of wisdom.

###### 5. Because Bodhicitta Is So Exceedingly Rare, One Should Look on It as a Wonder, Rejoice, and Guard It With Care

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden and Sabzang Mati Panchen both say one should rejoice and be glad, and Minyak Kunzang Sonam says it should be cherished and guarded with care.

###### 6. Even Someone of Weak Faculties in This Age of Dregs Has the Good Fortune to Give Rise to This Good Mind by Relying on a Teacher, a Spiritual Friend

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that even in the time of the five degenerations spoken of in the *Madhyamakāvatāra*, devotion to the Dharma of the Great Vehicle is gained by relying on the kindness of a teacher.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows that bodhicitta arising in the mind stream of an ordinary being is as exceedingly rare and wonderful as a blind man finding a precious wish-fulfilling jewel inside a heap of sweepings along the great road. The commentaries give different reasons for how, from within the dust-heap of the afflictions and of karma, this mind of awakening should have arisen by some chance; but they all agree that it is an exceedingly great wonder and that it must be guarded with care.

**Key points.**

- Bodhicitta arising is as exceedingly rare and wonderful as a blind man finding a precious jewel in a heap of sweepings.
- As to the reason it arises, the commentaries differ, assigning it variously to a good stroke of one's own merit, to the power of the buddhas' blessing, or to both together. ⚑
- The heap of sweepings is explained as the dust-heap of the afflictions and of karma, and the blind man as the ordinary being whose eye of wisdom is obscured by confusion.
- Because bodhicitta is so exceedingly rare, one should look on it as a wonder, rejoice, and guard it with care.
- Even someone of weak faculties in this age of dregs has the good fortune to give rise to a good mind by relying on the kindness of a teacher.
