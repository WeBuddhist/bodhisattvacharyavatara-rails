## 10. Chapter Ten: Dedicating the Goodness ^10-0


Through whatever goodness I have gathered
by writing this book about entering
the way of a bodhisattva,
may every being everywhere be led to walk that same path. ^10-1


In every direction, may all beings
suffering in body or mind, wherever they are,
find, through this goodness of mine,
an ocean of joy and happiness. ^10-2


And for as long as they remain within the cycle of rebirth,
may their happiness never fade.
May every being come to know
the constant, unbroken happiness of the bodhisattvas. ^10-3


In every hell realm
that exists throughout the universe,
may the beings trapped there
find joy through the happiness of the realm of true bliss. ^10-4


May those suffering from terrible cold find warmth.
Through the endless rivers of water
flowing from the great, compassionate clouds of the bodhisattvas,
may those suffering from terrible heat find coolness. ^10-5


May the forests of sword-sharp leaves in hell
become instead thick, cool sandalwood groves.
May the trees that once wounded beings
grow instead into wonderful, wish-granting trees. ^10-6


May the hell realms become beautiful, filled with the sweet calls
of geese, swans, and other lovely water birds.
May the ground of hell become lovely and pleasant,
covered in lakes full of sweetly scented lotus flowers. ^10-7


May piles of burning coals become instead piles of jewels.
May the burning ground turn into smooth, cool crystal.
May the crushing mountains of hell instead become
magnificent palaces, filled with Buddhas to be honored. ^10-8


May showers of burning coals and weapons
become instead showers of flowers.
And may the beings who once struck each other with weapons
now instead playfully toss flowers at one another. ^10-9


May those sinking in the raging river of hell, its flesh burned away, only white bones remaining like jasmine flowers —
through the power of my goodness, may they gain radiant, heavenly bodies instead,
and may they rest peacefully beside that gentle river,
together with beautiful goddesses. ^10-10


May the beings there wonder, "Why are the guards of hell, the crows, and the terrifying vultures
suddenly gone, and this wonderful, joyful light appearing instead? Whose power could this be?"
And looking up, may they see, shining in the sky above,
the glowing form of Vajrapāṇi.
And filled with great joy, freed from wrongdoing, may they come to live together with him. ^10-11


Seeing a shower of flowers mixed with scented water
falling and putting out the burning coals of hell,
may the beings there wonder, "What is this sudden, wonderful comfort?"
and look up to see Avalokiteśvara, holding his lotus flower. ^10-12


"Friends, let go of your fear and come quickly, over here to me" —
may the beings hear a voice like this, filled with real power to free them completely from suffering and bring them true joy,
and may they see a beautiful young figure appear before them,
radiant, gentle, and utterly without fear, filled with love and bodhicitta, ready to protect every being everywhere. ^10-13


"Look — his lotus feet are honored by the crowns of a hundred gods bowing before him,
his eyes are wet with compassion, and flowers rain down endlessly upon his head,
while a thousand lovely goddesses in beautiful palaces sing his praises" —
may the beings in hell, seeing Mañjughoṣa appear this way, cry out joyfully in wonder. ^10-14


Through the roots of my goodness in this way, may the beings suffering in hell
see gentle, cool, sweetly scented clouds appear,
sent by Samantabhadra and the other bodhisattvas,
raining down happiness, free from any trouble, and may they rejoice completely. ^10-15


May animals be freed
from the fear of being eaten by one another.
And may the hungry ghosts find happiness,
just as content as the people living happily in the northern land of Uttarakuru. ^10-16


May the stream of milk
flowing from the hands of noble Avalokiteśvara
satisfy and nourish the hungry ghosts,
and may they always be refreshed by its cooling bath. ^10-17


May the blind be able to see forms,
and may the deaf always be able to hear sounds.
And, like the goddess in the old story who gave birth without pain,
may pregnant women give birth safely, without any harm. ^10-18


May the naked find clothing,
may the hungry find food,
may the thirsty find water
and other delicious things to drink. ^10-19


May the poor find wealth.
May those weighed down by grief find joy.
May the discouraged find new hope,
firm resolve, and lasting well-being. ^10-20


May every sick being
be quickly freed from their sickness.
May every illness that ever troubles beings
disappear completely, and never arise again. ^10-21


May the frightened become fearless.
May those who are bound become free.
May the weak become strong,
and may all beings feel warmth and kindness toward one another. ^10-22


May all travelers, in every direction,
find their journeys safe and happy.
And whatever purpose they are traveling for,
may they achieve it without any struggle. ^10-23


May those traveling by boat or by ship
have their wishes fulfilled,
and may they reach the shore safely,
rejoicing there together with their loved ones. ^10-24


May those who have lost their way, wandering in sorrow,
meet with helpful companions,
and travel on safely and easily,
free from the fear of thieves, tigers, and other dangers. ^10-25


In wild, pathless places full of danger,
may children, the elderly, and those without protection,
and those who are asleep, intoxicated, or careless,
be watched over and protected by kind spirits. ^10-26


May all beings be freed from every hardship.
May they have faith, wisdom, and loving kindness.
May they have excellent food and a good way of living,
and may they always remember their past lives clearly. ^10-27


May everyone have all they need,
like an inexhaustible treasury as vast as the sky,
and may they live freely,
without conflict or harm toward one another. ^10-28


May beings of little radiance
come to shine with great radiance.
May those who practice hardship, even if their bodies suffer,
come to have beautiful, healthy bodies instead. ^10-29


May all the women in the world
who wish it be reborn as they wish.
May those looked down upon rise to a respected position,
and may pride and arrogance everywhere be overcome. ^10-30


Through this goodness of mine,
may every single being, without exception,
let go of all wrongdoing
and always do what is good instead. ^10-31


May they never be separated from bodhicitta.
May they stay devoted to the practice of a bodhisattva.
May they be cared for and protected by the Buddhas,
and may they let go of all harmful actions. ^10-32


May all these beings
live for a very, very long time.
May they always live happily,
and may they never even hear the word "death." ^10-33


May every direction be filled
with wish-fulfilling groves,
filled with Buddhas and their heirs,
teaching the sweet sound of the true teachings everywhere. ^10-34


May the ground everywhere
be free of gravel and rough stones,
smooth and gentle,
like the flat surface of a hand, made of beautiful lapis lazuli. ^10-35


In every gathering,
may many bodhisattvas be present,
beautifully adorning the earth
with their own excellent qualities. ^10-36


May every living being
constantly hear the sound of the true teachings,
coming from birds, from trees,
from rays of light, and even from the open sky. ^10-37


May they always be able to meet
the Buddhas and their heirs,
and may they honor these great teachers of the world
with endless clouds of offerings. ^10-38


May the rains fall at just the right time,
and may the harvests always be plentiful.
May rulers govern with fairness and goodness,
and may the whole world flourish and prosper. ^10-39


May medicines be truly effective,
and may prayers and mantras bring real results.
May spirits and other beings who could cause harm
instead be filled with compassion. ^10-40


May no being ever suffer,
do wrong, get sick,
feel afraid, or be looked down upon,
and may no one's mind ever feel troubled or unhappy. ^10-41


May temples and monasteries be filled
with the sound of study and recitation, and remain peaceful and well cared for.
May the community of practitioners always live in harmony,
and may their work for the benefit of others succeed. ^10-42


May monks who wish to train
find the solitude they need.
Letting go of every distraction,
may their minds become clear and ready for meditation. ^10-43


May nuns have all they need,
and be free from conflict and harm.
And may everyone who has devoted their life to spiritual practice
keep their discipline pure, without ever weakening. ^10-44


May those who have broken their discipline feel real, healthy regret,
and work always to clear away that wrongdoing.
And may they, too, eventually reach a good rebirth,
without ever losing their commitment to practice. ^10-45


May the wise be honored,
and may they always receive what they need through simple, humble means.
May their minds become completely pure,
and may their good reputation spread in every direction. ^10-46


Without ever experiencing the suffering of hard rebirths,
and without needing to endure great hardship,
may they, in bodies even greater than those of the gods,
quickly reach full Buddhahood. ^10-47


May every being, in life after life,
make offerings to all the Buddhas,
and may they always enjoy
the boundless happiness of the Buddhas themselves. ^10-48


May the bodhisattvas' wishes
to help all beings be fulfilled.
And may whatever the great protectors intend for the benefit of beings
come to pass for every being. ^10-49


In the same way, may the solitary buddhas
and the śrāvakas also find true happiness. ^10-50


And as for myself, through the kindness of Mañjughoṣa,
may I too, until I reach the joyful first level of a bodhisattva,
always remember my past lives clearly,
and always have the chance to live a life devoted to spiritual practice. ^10-51


Whether relying on simple food
or plain surroundings, may I always live well,
and in every lifetime,
may I have the good fortune to live in peaceful solitude. ^10-52


Whenever I wish to see him,
or even wish to ask him a small question,
may I always be able to see
the protector Mañjughoṣa himself, without any obstacle. ^10-53


Just as Mañjughoṣa acts
to accomplish the welfare of every being
throughout the ten directions and the endless reaches of space,
may my own actions be just the same. ^10-54


For as long as space itself endures,
and for as long as beings remain,
may I too remain,
working to clear away the suffering of the world. ^10-55


Whatever suffering there is for beings,
may it all ripen upon me instead.
And through the community of bodhisattvas,
may every being enjoy true happiness. ^10-56


May the true teachings, the one true medicine for the suffering of the world,
the very source of all happiness,
remain in this world for a very long time,
well supported and deeply honored. ^10-57


I bow down to Mañjughoṣa,
through whose kindness my mind has turned toward goodness.
And I bow down as well to my spiritual teacher,
through whose kindness I have been able to grow and flourish. ^10-58


This is the end of the tenth chapter, called "Dedicating the Goodness," from the Way of the Bodhisattva. ^10-a
