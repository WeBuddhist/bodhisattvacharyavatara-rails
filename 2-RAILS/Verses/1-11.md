## Traditional Interpretation

### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Palden (Tibetan)

Verse 1-11 presents the caravan-leader simile (*ded dpon gyi dpe*). Like a skilled, non-deceptive, benevolent merchant captain who leads caravans to the jewel island and guides them to gather and properly value the precious jewels, so the sole caravan leader of beings — the unrivaled Buddha (*'gran zla med pa'i sang rgyas*) — with the immeasurable wisdom that knows all objects of knowledge (*tshad med pa'i blo gros gang la mnga' ba*), has examined all dharmas and found bodhicitta to be the most precious (*rin chen pa*), because it is the supreme method for clearing beings' poverty (*dkor bul ba*) and fulfilling all wishes. Therefore, those wishing to be free from the abodes of the six realms (*'gro ba'i gnas*) should generate precious bodhicitta, firmly and unwaveringly hold it, keeping it in mind (*dran pa'i sgo nas*) through mindfulness and clear knowing (*shes bzhin gyi sgo nas*).
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-11)

### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Tibetan)

GDR treats this as the second benefit: the precious jewel (*rin chen gyi dpe*). The "sole caravan leader of beings" = the unrivaled Buddha who, with immeasurable wisdom (*tshad med kyi blo*), has seen and examined all dharmas, and found bodhicitta to be the most precious and beneficial — the supreme cause of all worldly and transcendent excellences (*'jig rten dang 'jig rten las 'das pa'i phun sum tshogs pa thams cad kyi rgyu*). Those wishing to be free from all samsaric suffering (*'gro ba'i gnas dang bral bar 'dod pa*) should generate precious bodhicitta and firmly hold it. GDR cites the Sdong po bkod pa: just as all material goods illuminated by sun and moon cannot match the value of the cintamaṇi jewel-king, all the virtue of gods, humans, śrāvakas, and pratyekabuddhas cannot match the value of generating bodhicitta.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-11)

### ngulchu-thogmed — Ngulchu Thokme Sangpo (Tibetan)

NTS reads this verse as addressed even to those who still wish to take birth in samsara (*srid pa'i 'gro ba rnams su 'byor bar 'dod pa rnams*): they should not doubt or misapprehend. The sole caravan leader — the Buddha, together with the bodhisattvas — with immeasurable and boundless wisdom (*tshad med cing gzhal du med pa'i blos*), has thoroughly examined all dharmas and found that bodhicitta is the cause of all worldly and transcendent excellences, and thus exceedingly precious (*rin thang che bas*). Those wishing to be free from the abodes of the five realms (*'gro ba lnga'i gnas dang bral bar 'dod pa rnams*) should hold this very bodhicitta firmly and with clear awareness (*dran pa'i sgo nas legs par dang shes bzhin gyi sgo nas brtan par gzungs shig*). NTS cites Maitreya's liberation chapter from the Sdong po bkod pa comparing the sun-and-moon's light against the cintamaṇi, and the combined virtue of all beings against bodhicitta — the latter cannot be equalled.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-11)

### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Mi Nyag Kunzang Sönam (Tibetan)

MKS develops both similes present in this verse: (1) the caravan leader simile and (2) the precious jewel simile. For the caravan leader: the sole, unrivaled Buddha with immeasurable wisdom (*tshad med pa'i blo gros*) — the wisdom of omniscience that knows all objects of knowledge without error — has examined all dharmas and found bodhicitta to be: (a) rare and precious (*dkon zhing rin thang che ba*) and (b) of immense benefit (*phan 'dogs kyang shin tu che ba*). For the precious jewel: the Sdong po bkod pa demonstrates that just as the wish-fulfilling jewel-king surpasses all material goods, bodhicitta surpasses all other virtue — all the virtue of gods, humans, śrāvakas, and pratyekabuddhas combined (*lha dang mi'i sems can thams cad dang nyan thos dang rang sangs rgyas kyi dge ba'i rtsa ba*) cannot match the value of generating bodhicitta. Therefore those wishing to be free from the suffering of the six realms should generate and firmly hold this precious bodhicitta through mindfulness, clear knowing, and heedfulness (*dran shes bag yod kyi sgo nas*), without wavering.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-11)

### khenpo-kunga — Ngakwang Kunga Wangchuk (Tibetan)

NKW reads "caravan leader" (*ded dpon*) as a title originally given to merchant captains who steer ships to the jewel island — here transferred to the Buddha, who leads all beings across the great ocean of the three realms of samsara (*khams gsum 'khor ba'i rgya mtsho*) to liberation and omniscience. The Buddha alone (*gcig pu*), with immeasurable and profound wisdom, has examined and assessed all dharmas — and found bodhicitta to be like the cintamaṇi, of immeasurable preciousness (*rin thang shin tu che ba*) and equal to the wish-fulfilling jewel (*nor bu rin po che dgos 'dod kun 'byung dang chos mtsungs pa*). Therefore those wishing to be free from the three realms should take and hold this bodhicitta exactly as they have committed to it, firmly and without releasing it (*ji ltar rang gis khas blangs te dam bcas pa bzhin du legs par te shin tu brtan por te dam por ma 'chor ba gzungs shig*).
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-11)

### Divergences

No substantive interpretive divergence. One notable additional reading: **NTS alone adds** that the verse addresses even those who wish to remain in samsaric rebirths (*srid pa'i 'gro ba rnams su 'byor bar 'dod pa*) — suggesting that bodhicitta is so precious it should be valued even by those with no immediate aspiration to exit samsara. The other commentaries address only those seeking liberation. The two readings are complementary rather than contradictory: if bodhicitta benefits even samsara-seekers, it certainly benefits liberation-seekers.

## Word-by-word Disambiguation (ཚིག་དོན་གསལ་བཤད།)

- **འགྲོ་བའི་དེད་དཔོན་གཅིག་པུ་** — "the sole caravan leader of beings": *ded dpon* = the merchant captain who leads traders to the jewel island. Here = the uniquely unrivaled Buddha (*'gran zla med pa gcig pu*). All commentaries: *gcig pu* points to the Buddha's uniqueness — no other figure can occupy this role because only a fully omniscient buddha has the immeasurable wisdom to examine all dharmas without error.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-11) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-11)

- **ལེགས་པར་ཡོངས་སུ་བརྟགས་ན་** — "having thoroughly and correctly examined": KS: "examination (*yongs su brtags na*) of all dharmas with immeasurable wisdom" = the Buddha's omniscient investigation. NTS: this is not the examination of a fallible analyst — it is the examination of omniscient wisdom (*thams cad mkhyen pa'i ye shes*), which cannot err.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-11)

- **འགྲོ་བའི་གནས་** — "the abodes of beings": KS: *'gro ba lnga'i gnas* = the five (or six) realms of samsara. NKW: *khams gsum 'khor ba'i rgya mtsho* = the great ocean of the three realms of samsara.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-11) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-11)

- **རིན་ཆེན་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་** — "precious bodhicitta": *rin chen* is predicative here — bodhicitta is called *rin chen* because the Buddha's examination determined it to be precious. All commentaries agree: the adjective flows from the Buddha's verdict (*gzigs pa*), not from the verse's speaker's opinion.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-11)

- **ནོར་བུ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ་དབང་གི་རྒྱལ་པོ་** (nor bu rin po che dbang gi rgyal po = cintamaṇi / king of precious jewels) — གདམས་ངག་ལས། ཉི་ཟླ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་འོད་དང་སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་དགེ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང་ནོར་བུ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ་དབང་གི་རྒྱལ་པོའི་རིན་དུ་མི་ཆོག་པ་བཞིན། བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ཀྱང་ཡོན་ཏན་གྱི་ངོས་ནས་དཀོན་ཞིང་རིན་ཐང་གཞལ་དུ་མེད།

## Quotations (ལུང་།)

> རིགས་ཀྱི་བུ་འདི་ལྟ་སྟེ། དཔེར་ན་ཇི་སྲིད་དུ་ཉི་མ་དང་ཟླ་བའི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་གྱི་འོད་ཀྱིས་སྣང་བར་བྱེད་པ་དེ་སྲིད་དུ་ནོར་དང་། འབྲུ་དང་། རིན་པོ་ཆེ་དང་། ས་ལེ་སྦྲམ་དང་། དངུལ་དང་། མེ་ཏོག་དང་། ཕྲེང་བ་དང་། བྱུག་པ་དང་། གོས་དང་། ལོངས་སྤྱོད་གང་ཇི་སྙེད་པ་དེ་དག་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་ནོར་བུ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ་དབང་གི་རྒྱལ་པོའི་རིན་དུ་མི་བཟོད་དོ། །དེ་བཞིན་དུ་ཇི་སྲིད་དུ་དུས་གསུམ་དུ་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱིས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་ཀྱི་ཡུལ་སྣང་བར་མཛད་པ་དེ་སྲིད་དུ་ལྷ་དང་མིའི་སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་དང་། ཉན་ཐོས་དང་རང་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་དགེ་བའི་རྩ་བ་ཟག་པ་དང་བཅས་པ་དང་། ཟག་པ་མེད་པ་གང་ཇི་སྙེད་པ་དེ་དག་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཏུ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་པའི་ནོར་བུ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ་དབང་གི་རྒྱལ་པོའི་རིན་དུ་མི་ཆོག་གོ།
> — སྡོང་པོ་བཀོད་པ་ལས། འཕགས་པ་བྱམས་པའི་རྣམ་པར་ཐར་པ་ (Gandavyūha, Maitreya's Liberation chapter)
