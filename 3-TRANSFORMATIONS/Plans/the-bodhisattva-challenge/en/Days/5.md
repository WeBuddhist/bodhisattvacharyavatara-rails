# Day 5 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: Benefits of Bodhicitta — Verses 1-13 to 1-15

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Protection from Negative Actions (Cleansing the Mind):** The wish for Enlightenment can protect you from the results of harmful actions. Even very serious wrongs can be overcome by relying on this mind. This helps reduce guilt and allows the mind to become calmer and lighter.

*   **Understanding Two Levels of Commitment (Clarity of Purpose):** Today's verses explain that the wish for Enlightenment has two clear stages. The first is simply wanting to be Enlightened. The second is actively working toward it. Knowing the difference helps you see where you are on the path.

*   **Continuous Growth of Goodness (Lasting Positive Change):** Once you truly commit to working for the benefit of others, good qualities grow in your mind all the time. This happens even when you sleep or are not actively practicing. Small daily choices slowly build a stronger, kinder character.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-13**

> སྡིག་པ་ཤིན་ཏུ་མི་བཟད་བྱས་ན་ཡང་། །
> དཔའ་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་འཇིགས་པ་ཆེན་པོ་ལྟར། །
> གང་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་ཡུད་ཀྱིས་སྒྲོལ་འགྱུར་བ། །
> དེ་ལ་བག་ཅན་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཅིས་མི་བརྟེན། །
>
> *Even if someone has committed terrible misdeeds, relying on this mind will swiftly liberate them. This is like relying on a brave companion to escape great danger. Why then do careful people not rely on it?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-13**

Just as a traveler on a very dangerous road relies on a brave companion to stay safe, a person who has done serious wrong can rely on the wish for Enlightenment to be quickly freed from its harmful results. This protection works in an instant, and it is available to everyone — both those who are wise enough to see its value and those who are simply afraid of suffering and want a way out.

---

#### **Verse 1-14**

> དེས་ནི་དུས་མཐའི་མེ་བཞིན་སྡིག་ཆེན་རྣམས། །
> སྐད་ཅིག་གཅིག་གིས་ངེས་པར་སྲེག་པར་བྱེད། །
> བྱམས་མགོན་བློ་དང་ལྡན་པས་ནོར་བཟང་བཤད། །
>
> *In a single instant, this mind consumes even the greatest sins, like the fire at the end of an age. Its immeasurable benefits were explained by Maitreya, the Loving Protector, to Sudhana.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-14**

The wish for Enlightenment does not simply protect a person from the results of wrong actions — at a higher level, it can destroy those negative actions completely, like a great fire that burns everything without leaving any ash behind. This teaching comes from a famous scripture, where a great master explained these limitless benefits to a sincere young student who was sincerely seeking the path to Enlightenment.

---

#### **Verse 1-15**

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དེ་མདོར་བསྡུས་ན། །
> རྣམ་པ་གཉིས་སུ་ཤེས་བྱ་སྟེ། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྨོན་པའི་སེམས་དང་ནི། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་འཇུག་པ་ཉིད་ཡིན་ནོ། །
>
> *Briefly, the mind of enlightenment has two aspects: the aspiring mind of enlightenment, and the engaging mind of enlightenment.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-15**

The wish for Enlightenment can be understood in two main ways. The first is simply wishing to become Enlightened for the benefit of all beings — this is the aspiring mind. The second is actually stepping onto the path and practicing to make that wish real — this is the engaging mind. The difference is like the difference between wanting to travel somewhere and actually starting the journey.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Remembering the Brave Companion:** When I make a mistake today — something I say or do that I regret — I will not stay stuck in guilt. Instead, I will remember that the wish to help others is a strong support, and I will use that wish to move forward with a cleaner mind.

*   **2. Noticing My Level of Commitment:** Today, I will check whether my good intentions stay only as wishes or whether I actually do something kind for another person. If I only wish, I will try to take one small step to turn that wish into action.

*   **3. Letting Goodness Grow Quietly:** Before I sleep tonight, I will remind myself that my wish to be kind and helpful continues to grow even when I rest. I will let this thought settle my mind and give me encouragement for tomorrow.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **The Wish for Enlightenment (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས།):** This is the sincere wish to become fully Enlightened in order to help every living being. In today's verses, it is described as both a protector from harm and a powerful force that can remove even the most serious wrong actions from the mind.

*   **Aspiring Mind (བྱང་ཆུབ་སྨོན་པའི་སེམས།):** This is the first stage of the wish for Enlightenment — the genuine desire to reach full Enlightenment for the sake of others. It is like standing at the door of a long journey, knowing where you want to go, but not yet having taken the first step.

*   **Engaging Mind (བྱང་ཆུབ་འཇུག་པ།):** This is the second and more active stage — actually entering the path and practicing the steps that lead to Enlightenment. It brings a continuous flow of good qualities into the mind, even during rest, because the commitment to help others is always present.

*   **Actions of Immediate Retribution (མཚམས་མེད་པའི་ལས།):** These are very serious harmful actions that, according to tradition, would normally lead directly to great suffering. Today's verses teach that even these can be overcome or destroyed through the power of the wish for Enlightenment.

*   **The Fire at the End of an Age (དུས་མཐའི་མེ།):** This is a traditional image of a great cosmic fire so powerful that it destroys everything completely. The great teacher Śāntideva uses this image to describe how the wish for Enlightenment, at its highest level, can completely remove harmful actions — not just protect you from their results, but burn them away entirely.
