# Day 29 — What stays after they're gone

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Name one thing you did "for" someone, that you regret
> **Detail:** The people pass; what we did for their sake stays. Today, set one such thing down honestly.

## Opening

Shantideva keeps confessing, and here he notices something exact. Much of what we do wrong, we do for someone: to protect our own people, to win for our side, to keep a person close. The friends and enemies we did it for are already passing out of our lives. The pleasure of their company fades like a dream. But the wrong itself does not fade with them. It stays, he says, waiting right in front of us.

## From the Tradition

We tell ourselves that some wrong was justified, because it was for family, for a friend, for our side. Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, points at what is left when that story runs out. The people we did it for are not permanent fixtures; many are already gone, and the closeness that seemed to justify everything is now just a memory, like waking from a dream. What does not pass with them is the act itself. Its mark stays in us, he says, waiting right in front of us, ours to answer for. The relationship was temporary; the consequence landed on us and stayed. This is not said to make us sink under it. Confession exists precisely so the thing left waiting can be faced and set down.

## Today's Practice

Bring to mind one thing you did that you are not proud of, something you did "for" someone or for your own side. Name it plainly to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, without defending it, and let yourself set it down. You are not stuck with it; naming it honestly is already the start of clearing it.
