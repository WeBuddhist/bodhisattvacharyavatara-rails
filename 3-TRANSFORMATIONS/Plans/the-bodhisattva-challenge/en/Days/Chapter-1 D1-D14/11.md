# Day 11 — Kindness that asks for nothing back

> **Notification**
> **Title:** After helping, notice what you hoped for
> **Detail:** The bodhisattva helps unasked, expecting nothing. Today, notice any wish for credit.

## Opening

These verses end Chapter One's long praise of the awakening mind. It brings joy to those who have none, cuts their suffering, and clears their confusion. Then comes a sharp comparison: we honour people who repay a kindness, so what about one who helps with no debt and no request at all?

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

> We praise those who merely repay a favor. What need is there to speak of the Hero of Enlightenment, who does good unsolicited?

## From the Tradition

We rightly admire someone who repays a kindness; they owed it, and they came through. Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, points past that. The bodhisattva owes no one. No one helped them first, and no one asked. And he adds the part that is easy to miss: they expect nothing back, not even the quiet sense that the good deed will become merit for them later. Most of our kindness keeps a small place for the self. This points past even that.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

After you do something kind today, holding a door, checking on a friend, helping unasked, pause and look honestly at what you hoped for. Not the big things, the small ones: a thank-you, a warm feeling, the sense of being a good person. You don't have to get rid of it. Just see it.
