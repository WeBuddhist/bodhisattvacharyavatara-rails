## 5. Chapter Five: Guarding Awareness ^5-0

Those who wish to uphold the training ^5-1
must guard their minds with great effort.
If this mind is not guarded,
the training cannot be upheld. ^5-1

Just as an untamed, wild elephant ^5-2
inflicts immeasurable harm,
a wild, untamed mind-elephant
does not inflict such harm in this world. ^5-2

If the mind-elephant is firmly bound ^5-3
with the rope of mindfulness,
all fears will cease,
and all good deeds will come to hand. ^5-3

Tigers, lions, elephants, bears, ^5-4
snakes, and all enemies,
the guardians of the hell realms,
and likewise sorcerers and demons— ^5-4

By binding this single mind, ^5-5
all those are bound.
By taming this single mind,
all those are tamed. ^5-5

The Buddha taught that all these fears ^5-6
and immeasurable suffering
arise from the mind.
This is truly what he taught. ^5-6

Who intentionally created ^5-7
the weapons of the hell beings?
Who made the burning iron ground?
From what did those heaps of fire arise? ^5-7

The Sage taught that all such things ^5-8
are the result of harmful thoughts.
Thus, in the three realms,
there is no other fear than the mind. ^5-8

If giving to the poor ^5-9
is the perfection of generosity,
and if there are still poor beings, then how
did the protectors perfect generosity before? ^5-9

The perfection of generosity is taught ^5-10
as the mind of relinquishment,
giving away all possessions with fruitful intent
to all beings. Therefore, it is the mind itself. ^5-10

Wherever fish and other creatures ^5-11
are driven away so they are not killed,
when the intention to desist is attained,
it is taught as the perfection of discipline. ^5-11

Unruly beings are like space; ^5-12
they cannot be overcome.
If this single angry mind is conquered,
it is like conquering all those enemies. ^5-12

How could there be enough leather ^5-13
to cover all the surface of the earth?
It is like covering the entire earth
with just a piece of leather for a shoe sole. ^5-13

Likewise, I cannot turn away ^5-14
external things.
I should turn away this mind of mine;
what need is there to turn away others? ^5-14

What fruit arises from generating a single clear mind, ^5-15
like that of Brahmā and other gods?
The fruit of actions of body and speech
is not like that due to weaker conduct. ^5-15

Even if one performs all recitations ^5-16
and ascetic practices for a long time,
the wise teach that what is done with a distracted mind
is meaningless. ^5-16

Whoever does not know this secret of the mind, ^5-17
the supreme essence of the teachings,
even if they wish to attain happiness and conquer suffering,
they wander aimlessly without purpose. ^5-17

Therefore, this mind of mine ^5-18
must be well-held and well-guarded.
What use are many disciplines
except for the discipline of guarding the mind? ^5-18

If one lives amidst wild, untamed people, ^5-19
one must carefully guard the wound,
just as one guards a wound with care.
Likewise, one must always guard this wound of the mind. ^5-19

If one is afraid of a minor wound's suffering ^5-20
and guards the wound with care,
why would one not guard the mind's wound
from the fears of crushing and dismemberment? ^5-20

If one lives with such conduct, ^5-21
whether living among bad people
or among women,
the steadfast discipline will not be harmed. ^5-21

My gains, respect, ^5-22
body, and livelihood may be lost,
and other good deeds may be lost,
but the mind must never be lost. ^5-22

To those who wish to guard their minds, ^5-23
I implore you with joined palms:
"Guard mindfulness and awareness
with all your effort!" ^5-23

Just as people afflicted by illness ^5-24
are powerless in all tasks,
likewise, those whose minds are disturbed by delusion
are powerless in all tasks. ^5-24

Even hearing, contemplating, and cultivating ^5-25
with a mind that lacks awareness,
like water in a leaky pot,
it does not remain in memory. ^5-25

Many learned, faithful, ^5-26
and diligent individuals,
marred by the fault of lacking awareness,
fall into downfalls. ^5-26

Robbers of unawareness, ^5-27
following the loss of mindfulness,
even if merit is accumulated,
they go to lower realms as if stripped by thieves. ^5-27

This multitude of troubling emotions, like robbers, ^5-28
seeks an opening.
Once an opening is found, they steal good deeds
and destroy the life of happy realms. ^5-28

Therefore, never let mindfulness ^5-29
depart from the gate of the mind.
Even if it departs, recall the harms of lower realms
and bring it back close. ^5-29

From associating with a teacher, ^5-30
from the instructions of a master,
and from fear, mindfulness easily arises
in those who are fortunate and respectful. ^5-30

The Buddhas and bodhisattvas ^5-31
possess unobstructed vision everywhere.
"I always dwell in the presence
of all of them," ^5-31

Thinking thus, one feels shame, ^5-32
respect, and fear, and likewise caution.
Therefore, the recollection of the Buddhas
also arises repeatedly in that person. ^5-32

When mindfulness remains at the gate of the mind ^5-33
for the sake of guarding,
then awareness will arise,
and what was lost will also return. ^5-33

First, knowing that this mind ^5-34
is flawed in this way,
at that time, I must remain
as inert as a piece of wood. ^5-34

I must never look ^5-35
aimlessly at distractions.
With focused attention, I must always
lower my gaze and look. ^5-35

For the sake of resting my gaze, ^5-36
I may occasionally look in a direction.
If someone appears in my sight,
I should look and offer a welcome. ^5-36

To check for dangers on the road and so forth, ^5-37
I should repeatedly look in the four directions.
After resting, I should turn my head and
look behind me. ^5-37

Having checked ahead and behind, ^5-38
I should go or come.
In all situations,
I should act with appropriate knowledge. ^5-38

"I will remain in this way with my body," ^5-39
Having made this resolve, then
"How is this body
positioned?" I should examine it. ^5-39

The wild elephant of the mind ^5-40
must be examined with all effort,
so that it does not slip from the great pillar
of focusing on the teachings, just as it was bound. ^5-40

"Where is this mind of mine engaged, ^5-41
so that diligence in meditation
does not slip away for even a single moment?"
Thus, I should individually examine my mind. ^5-41

If one is unable to do so due to fear, ^5-42
celebrations, or similar circumstances, one may do as one pleases.
For example, at times of giving,
it is taught that one should be indifferent to discipline. ^5-42

Whatever one has decided to do, ^5-43
one should not think of anything else.
With a mind focused on that,
one should accomplish that very thing for the time being. ^5-43

If so, everything will be well-accomplished. ^5-44
Otherwise, neither will be achieved.
Then, minor afflictions
will not increase due to unawareness. ^5-44

When engaging in various idle chatter ^5-45
and many amazing spectacles,
one should abandon attachment to them. ^5-45

If one digs aimlessly in the ground, cuts grass, ^5-46
or draws in the dirt, and so forth,
recalling the Buddha's training,
one should abandon it immediately with fear. ^5-46

Whenever one wishes to act ^5-47
or wishes to speak,
one should first examine one's own mind
and remain with steady awareness. ^5-47

Whenever one's mind desires attachment ^5-48
or wishes to be angry,
one should not act or speak,
but remain like a piece of wood. ^5-48

Whether prone to laughter or mockery, ^5-49
or if arrogant and puffed up,
or with the intention of exposing faults,
or if flattering and deceitful, ^5-49

Whenever one is eager to praise oneself, ^5-50
or to criticize others,
or is prone to scolding or regret,
at that time, one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-50

If one desires gain, respect, or fame, ^5-51
or wishes to seek attendants,
or if one's mind desires service,
at that time, one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-51

If the mind arises wishing to neglect others' benefit, ^5-52
or wishing to pursue one's own benefit,
or wishing to speak,
then one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-52

If impatience, laziness, fear, ^5-53
recklessness, idle talk,
or a mind clinging to one's own side arises,
at that time, one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-53

Thus, having examined the mind ^5-54
that strives for all troubling emotions and meaningless things,
the hero should firmly grasp
the antidote at that time. ^5-54

With full certainty and deep faith, ^5-55
steadfastness, respect, and humility,
shame, and fear,
calmly and striving to please others. ^5-55

Not wearied by the childish ^5-56
desires that conflict with each other,
and with compassion, thinking, "These troubling emotions
have arisen in this mind." ^5-56

Regarding blameless matters, ^5-57
having exerted control over oneself and beings,
I must always hold this mind
as if I were an illusion, without a self. ^5-57

Having repeatedly reflected on the supreme opportunity ^5-58
obtained after a long time,
I must hold this mind, like Mount Meru,
completely unmoving. ^5-58

Vultures, attached to flesh, ^5-59
drag it back and forth everywhere.
If your mind is not displeased,
why fuss now? ^5-59

Having taken this body as "mine," ^5-60
why do you guard it, mind?
If you and this body are separate,
what can it do for you? ^5-60

Foolish mind, why do you not ^5-61
take a clean wooden image?
What good is it to guard this machine
of impure aggregates, which is decaying? ^5-61

First, with your own wisdom, ^5-62
separate this layer of skin.
Then, with the weapon of wisdom,
separate the flesh from the network of bones. ^5-62

Having separated the bones, ^5-63
look into the marrow.
"What essence is there in this?"
You yourself should examine. ^5-63

If, after striving and searching, ^5-64
you find no essence there,
then why, with attachment,
do you guard this body? ^5-64

You cannot eat what is impure, ^5-65
nor can you drink blood.
You cannot lick the intestines;
what can the body do for you? ^5-65

Therefore, it is right for foxes and vultures ^5-66
to guard this for food.
This human body
is merely something to be used. ^5-66

Even if you guard it, ^5-67
the merciless Lord of Death
will seize it and give it to birds and dogs.
What can you do then? ^5-67

If one does not give clothes and so forth ^5-68
to a servant who cannot be employed,
if this body goes to others even after being fed,
why do you lavishly feed it? ^5-68

Having given wages to this body, ^5-69
now let it do its own work.
One should not give everything
to this body without benefit. ^5-69

Regarding the body as merely a boat ^5-70
for coming and going,
transform it into a wish-fulfilling body
to accomplish the benefit of beings. ^5-70

Since you are independent, ^5-71
always keep a smiling face.
Completely abandon frowning and scowling,
and be gentle with beings, like a friend. ^5-71

Do not carelessly throw down ^5-72
chairs and other things with noise.
Do not forcefully open doors;
always be quiet and joyful. ^5-72

Herons, cats, and robbers, ^5-73
who move stealthily and silently,
accomplish their desired aims.
The Sage always practiced likewise. ^5-73

Skillfully encouraging others, ^5-74
and speaking unsolicited beneficial words,
one should respectfully accept them with bowed head,
and always be a student to everyone. ^5-74

To all well-spoken words, ^5-75
one should say, "Well said!"
If one sees someone performing good deeds,
one should generate gladness with praise. ^5-75

One should praise good qualities in secret, ^5-76
and if good qualities are praised, one should echo them.
If one's own good qualities are praised,
one should recognize them as good qualities. ^5-76

All endeavors are for joy, ^5-77
which is hard to obtain even for a price.
Therefore, one should experience the joy
of others' good qualities. ^5-77

Here, no harm comes to me, ^5-78
and great happiness comes to others.
Displeasure brings unhappiness and suffering,
and great suffering comes to others. ^5-78

When speaking, one should be confident, connected, ^5-79
clear, and pleasing.
One should speak gently and appropriately,
having abandoned attachment and hatred. ^5-79

When looking at beings with one's eyes, ^5-80
one should look with sincerity and loving-kindness,
thinking, "Through relying on these very beings,
I will attain buddhahood." ^5-80

Always inspired by aspiration, ^5-81
or inspired by the antidote,
virtue and benefit arise in those who suffer,
and great good deeds are generated. ^5-81

Being skilled and energetic, ^5-82
I must always perform tasks.
I must not rely on anyone
for any task. ^5-82

The perfections of generosity and so forth ^5-83
should be practiced, advancing higher and higher.
One should not give up the greater for the lesser.
Primarily, one should consider the benefit of others. ^5-83

Having understood this, one should always ^5-84
strive for the benefit of others.
The compassionate ones, who see far,
permit even what is forbidden in such cases. ^5-84

One should share with those who have fallen, ^5-85
the helpless, and those upholding discipline.
One should eat a moderate amount,
and give away everything except the three robes. ^5-85

One should not harm this body, ^5-86
which is the practice of the sacred teachings, for trivial reasons.
If one acts in this way, the aspirations of beings
will quickly be fulfilled. ^5-86

One should not give up this body ^5-87
without pure compassion.
By all means, in this and future lives,
it should be used as a cause for accomplishing great aims. ^5-87

Do not teach the teachings to the disrespectful, ^5-88
to those with covered heads who are not ill,
to those with umbrellas, staffs, or weapons,
or to those with covered heads. ^5-88

To the lowly, do not teach the profound and vast teachings, ^5-89
nor to women without men present.
One should always act with equal respect
towards the teachings of the lesser and the supreme. ^5-89

One should not apply the vast teachings, ^5-90
which are a vessel for the Dharma, to lesser teachings.
One should not completely abandon conduct.
One should not be misled by sutras and mantras. ^5-90

When discarding tooth-sticks and saliva, ^5-91
one should cover them.
It is improper to discard urine and other waste
in water or on the ground. ^5-91

One should not eat with a full mouth, ^5-92
with noise, or with an open mouth.
One should not sit with outstretched legs,
nor rub one's hands together. ^5-92

In conveyances, beds, and dwelling places, ^5-93
one should not be alone with other women.
All those who lack faith in the world
should be avoided by seeing and inquiring. ^5-93

One should not indicate with a finger; ^5-94
with respect, one should use the right hand.
Everyone should likewise
show the way in that manner. ^5-94

One should not move the hand excessively, ^5-95
but move it slightly and make a sound.
By signaling with snapping fingers and so forth,
otherwise, one will be unrestrained. ^5-96

One should lie down facing the desired direction, ^5-96
like the Protector resting in nirvana.
One should immediately apply awareness
to the resolve to rise quickly. ^5-96

The practices of bodhisattvas ^5-97
are taught as immeasurable.
Therefore, one must definitely practice
the conduct of purifying the mind for that long. ^5-97

Three times a day and night, ^5-98
one should recite the three aggregates.
By relying on the Buddhas and the Mind of Awakening,
one pacifies the remaining downfalls. ^5-98

Whether one is independent or dependent, ^5-99
whatever one practices in any situation,
one should diligently train in that situation
which is taught in the training. ^5-99

There is nothing ^5-100
that the spiritual children do not train in.
To a wise person who dwells in that way,
there is no merit that does not arise. ^5-100

Whether directly or indirectly, ^5-101
one should not act for anything other than the benefit of beings.
For the sake of beings alone,
I dedicate everything for awakening. ^5-101

One should never abandon a virtuous friend, ^5-102
who is skilled in the meaning of the Mahayana,
and the supreme discipline of a bodhisattva,
even for the sake of one's life. ^5-102

One should train according to the way of relying on a teacher ^5-103
as described in the biography of Paljung.
This and other teachings taught by the Buddha
should be understood by reading the sutras. ^5-103

The training appears in the sutras. ^5-104
Therefore, one should read the sutras.
One should first examine
the Akasagarbha Sutra. ^5-104

Because it extensively teaches ^5-105
the practice that is always to be done,
one must definitely and repeatedly examine
the Sikṣāsamuccaya. ^5-105

Alternatively, for the time being, one may examine ^5-106
the concise compendium of all sutras.
One should also diligently examine both works
composed by the noble Nagarjuna. ^5-106

Whatever is not forbidden ^5-107
should be practiced.
For the sake of guarding the worldly mind,
one should truly practice what is seen in the training. ^5-107

Repeatedly examining the states ^5-108
of body and mind—
that alone, in brief,
is the definition of guarding awareness. ^5-108

These things should be practiced with the body. ^5-109
What is accomplished by merely speaking words?
Does merely reading medical texts
benefit sick people? ^5-109
