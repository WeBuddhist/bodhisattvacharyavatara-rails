## Today's Verse

> Even if all gods and demigods
> rise up as my enemies,
> they cannot lead me
> into the fire of the lowest hell. ^4-30

> But this powerful enemy, defilement,
> when it encounters anyone, even Mount Meru
> will be reduced to ashes, leaving nothing.
> It casts me into that in a single instant. ^4-31

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 30 and 31 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. No outer enemy, however strong, can throw us into the lowest hell. But the one inner enemy, the afflictions, can throw us there in a single instant.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a saying that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Mount Meru, the king of mountains, is eighty thousand leagues high and extremely solid. Yet this inner enemy, the afflictions, can burn even Mount Meru to ash without a trace in a single instant. In the same way, if even one moment of anger or ill will arises in a bodhisattva, it can throw them into the lowest hell.

However strong the outer enemies of this world may be, they cannot throw us into the lowest hell. But the afflictions in our own mind can throw us into a great hell in a single instant. So we must always watch for and guard against the afflictions in our own mind, even more than we watch for a fearsome outer enemy.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, the moment anger arises in me, I will recognize it and stop it from spilling into my speech or my actions.

**Explanation:** _(Giving up anger)_ Anger is more fearsome than any outer enemy. A single moment of anger can burn away a great store of doing good and cause us to fall into the lower realms. So we must put out the flame of anger right away and guard our own mind.
