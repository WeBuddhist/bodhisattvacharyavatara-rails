# Day 24 — Bow once, with bodies past counting

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Make one slow bow to the Three Jewels today
> **Detail:** Today's verses close with prostration. A single bow, with the mind bowing many times over.

## Opening

Chapter Two's long stretch of offering comes to a close, and the body steps in. Shantideva has given things and sung praise; now he bows. He pictures his one body becoming as many bodies as there are atoms in all the worlds, and every one of them bows to the buddhas, the dharma, and the sangha.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

> I prostrate, bowing as many bodies
> As there are atoms in all realms,
> To all the buddhas of the three times,
> The dharma, and the sublime sangha.

## From the Tradition

When we bow, only one body folds toward the floor. The last verse asks for far more: a body for every atom in every world, all of them bowing at once. Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, explains that this is done in the mind. You make the buddhas vividly present, the way the Aspiration of Good Conduct does, and from your one body you send out countless bodies, each one bowing with respect. He reads the prostration as the cure for **pride**. Pride needs us to stay tall and at the centre. Bowing puts us low, again and again, and it is the buddhas, the dharma, and the sangha we look up to, not ourselves.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

If there is a moment today, make one slow bow. You can do it before an altar, or simply where you stand, bowing to the buddhas, the dharma, and the sangha. As you go down, you might picture many bodies bowing along with you. One real bow, given with care, is enough.
