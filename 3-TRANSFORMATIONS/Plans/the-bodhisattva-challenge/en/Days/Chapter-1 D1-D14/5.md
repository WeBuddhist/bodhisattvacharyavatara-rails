# Day 5 — The fire that burns even the worst of what you've done

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Turn an old regret into a wish for others
> **Detail:** Take one thing you regret and wish that everyone stuck in regret finds a way through.

## Introduction

Three more images today, each making the same point from a different angle. Virtue held by bodhicitta never runs out — it keeps bearing fruit in a way ordinary good deeds never can. Bodhicitta protects even someone who has done terrible harm, like a brave escort through dangerous territory. And at its deepest, it burns negative karma the way a world-ending fire leaves nothing behind.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

*MIND TRAINING (recite 3 times)*

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in immeasurable equanimity, free from attachment and aversion.

*REFUGE (recite 3 times)*

> Until I reach the heart of awakening, I take refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.

*TAKING THE BODHISATTVA VOW (recite 3 times)*

> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And trained step by step in the bodhisattva conduct,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And train step by step in that same conduct.

## From the Tradition

Ngulchu Thokme draws a careful line between the two analogies in today's reading. The brave companion in verse 1.13 protects you from experiencing the results of harmful actions — like a bodyguard who keeps the consequences at bay. The fire in verse 1.14 does something different: it destroys the negative karma from its root, leaving nothing behind. These are not the same thing, and they don't happen at the same time. The first protection comes earlier in practice; the complete destruction comes later, at a point Thokme calls "attaining great patience." It's easy to hear both analogies and assume they mean the same thing — they don't.

## Aspiration

> May the precious and supreme bodhicitta —
> Where it has not yet arisen, may it arise;
> Where it has arisen, may it not decline,
> But grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of this practice,
> May all wandering beings, without exception,
> Enter the path of the bodhisattva.

## Today's Practice Challenge

Ngulchu Thokme cites a sutra: a bodhisattva who relies on the mind of enlightenment has no fear of any enemies of negative actions. Think of something you've done that you still feel bad about — a moment of cowardice, a harm you caused that you haven't been able to shake. Try turning it into a wish: because I know how this feels, may everyone caught in regret like this find a way through. That's the brave companion the verse is pointing at.
