# Day 1 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: Benefits of Bodhicitta — Verses 1-1 to 1-3

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Clearing Inner Obstacles (Removing Barriers to Growth):** Paying homage to the Three Jewels clears away obstacles that block personal growth and spiritual progress. This act of respect builds a strong foundation for any spiritual endeavor. When done sincerely, it opens the door to genuine transformation.

*   **Cultivating Humility (Overcoming Pride):** Recognizing that all wisdom already exists in the scriptures helps reduce pride and arrogance. The great teacher Śāntideva's example of humble self-reflection shows that admitting one's limitations is a sign of true wisdom. This kind of honesty makes it easier to learn and grow without getting in one's own way.

*   **Strengthening Personal Practice (Building Consistency):** Writing down teachings and reflecting on them is a powerful way to deepen understanding and prevent spiritual progress from fading. Just as writing reinforces memory, regular reflection keeps virtuous qualities alive and growing. This practice supports steady mental and emotional development over time.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-1**

> བདེ་གཤེགས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་མངའ་སྲས་བཅས་དང་། །
> ཕྱག་འོས་ཀུན་ལའང་གུས་པར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏེ། །
> བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་ནི། །
> ལུང་བཞིན་མདོར་བསྡུས་ནས་ནི་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ། །
>
> *I pay respectful homage to the Blissful Ones who possess the Truth Body, and to their heirs. I also bow to everyone who is worthy of honor. I shall now briefly describe how to enter the commitments of the Buddha's heirs, in accordance with the scriptures.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-1**

The great teacher Śāntideva opens this text by bowing to the Three Jewels — the Awakened Ones, the teachings, and the community of those seeking awakening. This act of homage is not mere ritual; it clears inner obstacles and builds the merit needed for the text to benefit others. By honoring the Awakened Ones as those who have perfectly abandoned all faults and fully realized the nature of reality, Śāntideva sets the tone of the entire path — one of deep respect, clear intention, and the genuine wish to enter the conduct of a Hero of Enlightenment in accordance with the sacred scriptures.

---

#### **Verse 1-2**

> སྔོན་ཆད་མ་བྱུང་བ་ཡང་འདིར་བརྗོད་མེད། །
> སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་མཁས་པའང་བདག་ལ་ཡོད་མིན་ཏེ། །
> དེ་ཕྱིར་གཞན་དོན་བསམ་པ་བདག་ལ་མེད། །
> རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་ངས་འདི་བརྩམས། །
>
> *I have nothing new to say here, and I do not possess any skill in writing poetry. Therefore, I have no expectation of benefiting others. I wrote this solely to train and cultivate my own mind.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-2**

The great teacher Śāntideva expresses deep humility by denying any claim to originality or poetic genius. This honest self-assessment helps overcome pride and intellectual arrogance before his peers. He clarifies that the text contains nothing not already found in the sacred scriptures, and since those with sharp understanding can access those scriptures directly, he does not write for their sake. His single immediate purpose is to continuously train, habituate, and strengthen his own mind in the aspiring wish for awakening and the six perfections, so that his understanding will not decline but grow.

---

#### **Verse 1-3**

> དགེ་བ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་བདག་གི་དད་པའི་ཤུགས། །
> འདི་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་རེ་ཞིག་འཕེལ་འགྱུར་ལ། །
> བདག་དང་སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཀྱང་། །
> ཅི་སྟེ་འདི་དག་མཐོང་ན་དོན་ཡོད་འགྱུར། །
>
> *By writing this, the strength of my faith to cultivate virtue will temporarily increase. If anyone of equal fortune should see these words, perhaps they will also find them useful.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-3**

The great teacher Śāntideva explains that the very act of writing down his realizations will cause his faith, diligence, and wisdom to temporarily increase and grow. This increase in faith is not vague — it includes conviction in the law of cause and effect, admiration for those with noble qualities, and aspiration toward one's own ability to practice. While self-cultivation remains the immediate focus, Śāntideva also acknowledges a secondary benefit: those who share the same aspiration and good fortune — fellow practitioners on the path of awakening — may also find genuine value in these words and be inspired to engage with the teachings.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Begin with Genuine Respect:** Today, I will begin my morning by reciting the refuge prayer slowly and sincerely, reminding myself of the qualities of the Awakened Ones. Rather than rushing through the words, I will pause to feel genuine respect and appreciation for the path I have access to.

*   **2. Practice Honest Self-Reflection:** Inspired by the great teacher Śāntideva's example, I will look honestly at one area in my life where pride or the need to appear impressive gets in my way. I will remind myself that real learning begins when I admit my limitations and open myself to the teachings without needing to be seen as an expert.

*   **3. Write Down One Insight to Strengthen My Practice:** I will write down one teaching or insight from today's verses in a journal or notebook. By putting it into words, I will help my understanding stay alive and grow stronger. This small act of writing will serve as a support for my mind throughout the day.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Blissful One (བདེ་གཤེགས་ / bde gshegs):** A title for an Awakened One — a being who has gone beyond suffering in a perfect and complete way. This title has three meanings: having abandoned all faults beautifully, having left the cycle of existence permanently without returning, and having removed every last trace of obscuration without exception.

*   **Truth Body (ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་ / chos kyi sku):** The deepest level of an Awakened One's being — the dimension of complete wisdom and purity that is free from all stains. It is not a physical form but rather the realized nature of reality itself, perfectly pure and fully aware.

*   **Heirs of the Awakened Ones (བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ / bde gshegs sras):** Those who have dedicated their lives to attaining full awakening for the benefit of all beings. They are called "heirs" because they carry on the work of the Awakened Ones, born in the footsteps of the great sages through faith, wisdom, and compassion.

*   **The Three Jewels (དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་ / dkon mchog gsum):** The three supreme objects of refuge: the Awakened Ones, the Teachings, and the Community of those on the path. They are called "Jewels" because they are rare, stainless, powerful, the ornament of the world, supreme, and changeless — precious supports for the entire spiritual path.

*   **Homage (ཕྱག་འཚལ་ / phyag 'tshal):** The act of paying deep respect through body, speech, and mind — bowing physically, offering praise verbally, and recollecting the qualities of worthy objects mentally. In this text, homage serves to purify obstacles and generate the merit needed to embark on the path of awakening.

*   **Mind of Enlightenment (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ / byang chub sems):** The wish to attain full awakening for the benefit of all living beings. This is the central theme of the entire text. It begins as an aspiration and gradually deepens into an active commitment to live according to the conduct of those who seek complete awakening.
