# Day 103 — Praise Without Jealousy: Applaud What Is Good, Speak of Good Qualities Behind Their Backs, and Hear Your Own Praise Without Pride

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the seventy-fifth and seventy-sixth verses of the fifth chapter. When you see the good actions of others, your words of praise make a great light shine in their minds.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable story, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Long ago, a lama said at a Dharma place, "Gwa, gya, and lba, these three are the ornament of a monk." At that time, an old monk had all three. He lifted his head a little and became happy. The lama knew this and said, "If all three are present, it is a bad omen." Because of this, the monk bowed his head, feeling deflated. This shows what is meant by "if one's own good qualities are praised."

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when I see someone doing a good action, I will praise them face to face.

**Explanation:** *(Doing good)* When we see others do good work and praise them without jealousy, joy arises in their minds. This also increases our own great merit. This is an important practice taught in the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*.

##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

To everything that is well said — talk on the Dharma and the like — one should not give a disagreeing answer but rejoice, and, in order to please those speakers, one must also say, "Well said." <!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** All the commentaries agree in explaining that, to all well-spoken Dharma talk by others, one should offer words of praise, without jealousy, saying "Well said," and that when one sees others making offerings to the Three Jewels, giving, and performing other deeds of merit, one should, by praising them directly, generate gladness and joy in their minds.

**Key points.**

- **Rejoicing in and praising good speech.** When others speak well Dharma talk that is meaningful and an antidote to the afflictions, one must, free of jealousy, offer words of rejoicing and praise: "Well said."
- **Praising deeds of merit face to face.** When one sees or hears of others making offerings to the Three Jewels above, giving to beggars below, and so on, one should, without rivalry, praise them directly and so generate gladness and joy in their minds.
- **A means of abandoning jealousy.** To praise and rejoice in others' good qualities and deeds of merit is a means of abandoning one's own jealousy and rivalry.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

In order to avert the suspicion of flattery, one should speak of the good qualities of those who have them behind their backs; if someone speaks of another's good qualities, one should agree with it and echo what is said; and if someone speaks of some good quality of one's own, one should, without any puffing up of the mind, recognize that speaker as one who, drawn to the side of good qualities, knows good qualities and nothing else.

##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Further: if the situation is such that it is awkward to praise someone to their face, then, in a place where it is not obvious, behind their back, one must speak of the person's good qualities: "That person did this and that good, virtuous deed; it was excellent."

Further: if it happens that one person speaks in praise of another, one must echo it in agreement. A lama of the past said in a Dharma place, "Gwa, gya, and lba — these three are the ornament of a monk." Among those present, an old monk had all three; he lifted his head a little and became happy. The lama knew this and immediately said, "If all three are present, it is a bad omen," and there is a story that the monk hung his head, deflated.

When one has some slight good quality of one's own and a discerning being recognizes it as a good quality and praises one for it, one should not let arise the pride of thinking, "I really am something," but, thinking, "It is good that he sees a good quality as a good quality," one must regard that other person with a clear mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-76]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

To avert its turning into flattery, one should also praise that person behind their back, by speaking of their good qualities to others. If some other person speaks of the good qualities of whomever — even a person one dislikes — one should not contradict that but agree with it, speaking of their good qualities after them; that is, one must chime in (བརྗོད་རོག). If one sees someone speaking of one's own good qualities, one should not become proud because of it, but should recognize that speaker as a wise person who knows how to distinguish good qualities, one of pure perception endowed with good qualities.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-76]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Fearing that speaking directly would be flattery, one should speak of others' good qualities behind their backs. If others speak of the good qualities of someone other than oneself, one should echo, "That is so." If others speak of one's own good qualities, one should, without puffing up, examine whether the good qualities spoken of are present in oneself, and, if they are, recognize that one has them; one should not do so with conceit.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-76]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

To avert the suspicion of flattery (ཁ་བསག), one should speak of good qualities after reading (ཀློག་ནས); and if others speak of another's good qualities openly, one should echo them, and if the bodhisattva's own good qualities are spoken of, he should recognize them as his own good qualities and not become puffed up.

*(Editorial note: the rail has ཁ་བསག་ and ཀློག་ནས here, where the other commentaries have ཁ་གསག་ and ལྐོག་ན/ལྐོག་ནས ("behind their back"); the wording is translated as given.)*

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

One should also praise them behind their back by speaking of their good qualities; and even when others are speaking of their good qualities, one should not say anything contradictory but agree, speaking in accord. And when one sees someone speaking of one's own good qualities, one should generate gladness by recognizing that speaker as one who knows the distinctions among good qualities and is drawn to good qualities.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-76]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Even if one oneself hears someone such as Devadatta (ལྷས་བྱིན) speak of the good qualities of another, such as a person who makes offerings and gives (མཆོད་སྦྱིན, Yajñadatta), one should, without displeasure at it, not say contradicting words but say, "That is so," agreeing with that very person such as Devadatta, and so make that person glad. And if some other person, whether openly or behind one's back, speaks of one's own good qualities with gladness and delight, one should carefully examine whether the qualities spoken of are present in oneself; if they are, one should recognize that speaker as one of pure perception who is drawn to good qualities and capable of recognizing others as having good qualities; and one should not let one's mind swell up with conceit. These practices are, in turn, the abandoning of flattery, jealousy, and pride.

Alternatively, the meaning from the earlier "doing merit" onward is explained as follows. When one sees someone doing merit, one praises them directly and so makes them glad, and one also speaks of that doer of merit's good qualities behind their back; and when others speak of that doer of merit's good qualities, one also agrees and speaks in accord. It is also said that when others speak of one's own good qualities, one should recognize that speaker as a person of good qualities who is able to discern distinctions of quality. Encouraging others (གཞེན་བསྐུལ་འདེབས་པ) and the like are ways of abandoning speech habits that are contrary to the Dharma, and, putting speech to use in conduct that accords with the Dharma, of taking the essence from it.

*(Editorial note: ལྷས་བྱིན་ and མཆོད་སྦྱིན་ are the Tibetan forms of the stock example names Devadatta and Yajñadatta, here standing for "someone" and "another"; this identification is provisional. The phrase གཞེན་བསྐུལ་འདེབས་པ in the second paragraph is unclear in the rail and is rendered as it reads.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-76]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

If, when praising another to their face for performing deeds of merit and virtue, one fears that it may come across as flattery or as ingratiating words, then behind their back — that is, where that person cannot hear it with their ears — one should tell others of the greatness of those good qualities. Further, when one sees another person speaking in praise or commendation of the good qualities of someone — one's friend, one's enemy, or anyone else — one must also say, "That is so," and speak of their good qualities in the manner of rejoicing along with them. Further, if one hears another person speaking of one's own good qualities in that way, one must know — recognize — that speaker as someone with good qualities, a person of good intention and pure perception.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-76]]

##### The Ornament of a Monk: Gwa, Gya and Lba

A lama of the past said in a Dharma place, "Gwa, gya, and lba — these three are the ornament of a monk." Among those present, an old monk had all three; he lifted his head a little and became happy. The lama knew this and said, "If all three are present, it is a bad omen," and there is a story that the monk then hung his head, deflated. (Given as an example for "if one's own good qualities are spoken of.")

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Behind Their Backs: Speak of Good Qualities to Avoid Any Suspicion of Flattery

In order to avert the suspicion that praise given to someone's face is flattery, one should speak of the good qualities of those who have them behind their backs.

###### 2. When Others Praise Someone's Good Qualities, Echo Them Instead of Contradicting

When others speak of another's good qualities, one should not say anything contradictory, but echo and agree with what is said.

###### 3. When Your Own Good Qualities Are Praised, Do Not Puff Up

When others speak of one's own good qualities, one should do so without puffing up, recognizing that speaker as one who knows good qualities.

#### Divergences Among Commentators

- ⚑ **Minyak Kunzang Sonam's two explanations:** According to the first explanation, when one sees others doing deeds of merit, one praises them to their face, and he explains that one also speaks of that person's good qualities behind their back; and in the second explanation, introduced by "Alternatively" (ཡང་ན), he explains the meaning from "if one sees merit being done" onward in a different way.

*(Editorial note: the rail's Verse Synthesis states the first explanation differently, as explaining the lines in the manner of abandoning flattery, jealousy, and pride. Both statements are kept as the rail gives them; the corresponding bullet has been removed from the Verse Synthesis below.)*

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** The commentaries agree that this verse of the Bodhicaryāvatāra shows Dharma-accordant conduct of speech: because praising someone openly may be feared to turn into flattery, one speaks of good qualities behind their back; when others speak of someone's good qualities, one echoes them in agreement; and when one's own good qualities are spoken of, one does not puff up but recognizes that speaker as one who has good qualities.

**Key points.**

- **Speaking of good qualities behind their backs.** Because one fears that praising openly will turn into flattery or words of ingratiation, one should speak of the good qualities of one who has them where that person cannot hear it with their ears.
- **Echoing others.** When one hears others speak of another's good qualities, one should not feel displeasure or say words that contradict, but echo them in agreement and rejoice along with them.
- **Abandoning pride and puffing up.** When others speak of one's own good qualities, one should not become proud or puffed up, but should recognize that speaker as one who knows how to distinguish good qualities, one of pure perception endowed with good qualities.
- **The example that crushes pride.** As an example of not growing proud over a slight good quality of one's own: a lama of the past said, "Gwa, gya, and lba — these three are the ornament of a monk"; when an old monk was pleased, he said, "If all three are present, it is a bad omen," and the account is set down that the monk hung his head.
