<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

When a person who has piled up offences is being led away to the place where limbs are cut off, dread and terror arise, and out of that mental anguish a great fear is born; and under its force his expression changes from what it was before — mouth gone dry, eyes rolling wildly — his body trembles, and his skin turns ashen. And that is only for a punishment in this present life. If even that much terror, when body and life are about to be parted, produces such enormous dread — if even a slight suffering of torment in this life gives rise to fear, so that dread and panic visibly alter a person's appearance from what it was — then it is settled that when one leaves this life for good there will come, in the future, birthplaces of every kind, good and bad. And since it is settled that dread will arise when consciousness transfers under conditions where one has not even a hair's tip of confidence about where one is going, what need is there to say that one becomes utterly wretched? It need not be said. *(Note: between these two points the source develops a lengthy independent excursus on the continuity of the mind-stream and rebirth as epistemological grounds for the fear, not reproduced here in full.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-44]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The vicious executioners who are the messengers of the Lord of Death — the forms taken by one's own deluded bad karma — are seven times larger than a human, naked, hair standing up on the crown of the head, triangular eyes bulging like glass, jaws clenched tight, teeth and gums bared, blowing out a fierce scattering wind as they hiss, their whole nature wrathful and savage, wielding iron hooks and nooses and hammers and battle-axes and the like, utterly terrifying like rākṣasas, with scowling brows and every kind of dreadful form. Seized by them, bound with the noose, one is led away alone into the next life; the appearances of hell — the plain of burning iron and the rest — are met head-on by a horrifying, terrifying darkness, while the red wind of karma drives one on from behind. And as the executioners of the Lord of Death shout aloud "Strike! Strike! Kill! Kill! Cut! Cut!", one is seized with iron hooks, beaten with hammers, one's limbs cut off — and struck by these fierce sicknesses of great terror. At that time one becomes utterly panicked and wretched with suffering: what need is there even to say it?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-44]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->
##### Zhenphen Jampé Gocha (annotation commentary)

Zhenga's annotation commentary gathers the sense of the words in brief and makes clear, in the underlying gloss, that the terrifying form of the messengers of the Lord of Death is one marked by such features as baring the teeth and gums, jaws clenched tight, hair bound up in a topknot, eyes bulging like glass, and calves grown taut and sinewy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^2-44]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

When some person is today being led by men to the place where limbs are cut off as a punishment, then his mouth goes dry, the glow of his face fades, his eyes look stricken, and his whole appearance visibly changes from what it was before. If that is so, then when one is seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death — beings of an utterly different lot from oneself, terrifying in form — and is struck by the great terror of the sickness of death, so that one becomes utterly wretched, what need is there to say that there is great fear? Therefore one should feel regret for one's wrongdoing.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^2-44]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

For example, when a person guilty of a crime is today being led to the place where limbs are cut off, he is terrified: his mouth goes dry, his eyes look stricken, and his appearance visibly changes from what it was. If that is so, then when one is seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death, who stand there utterly terrifying in form, and is struck by the sickness of great terror, and is made utterly wretched by that suffering — what need is there even to speak of being terrified? *(Note: this prose covers the grouped root-verse run 2-43–2-46 together in the source.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^2-44]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If one's appearance visibly changes from one's former condition, then when one is seized by the great messengers of the Lord of Death — beings of a different lot from oneself, utterly terrifying in form like rākṣasas — and is struck by the fierce sicknesses of great terror, the appearances of hell and the rest, so that one becomes utterly wretched and one's body is fouled with excrement and urine — what need is there even to say it? Therefore it is only right to strive at virtue.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-44]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

"Protectors" — calling upon the sources of refuge: I, who have fallen under the power of a heedlessness that turns all the conduct of the three doors upside down in what it takes up and what it rejects, did not know and did not understand that there is such a thing as this — the suffering of life being severed at death, the suffering of being seized and led away by the messengers of the Lord of Death, the suffering of falling into the lower realms after death — one great terror arising after another in an unbroken chain. And so, during this short life, which is impermanent and does not last long, for the sake of no more than the means of livelihood — food, clothing, enjoyments — and for the sake of enemies and kin and the rest, needlessly, I closely committed and accumulated a great deal of wrongdoing with grave consequences. Thus regret is generated. When some guilty person, having incurred the king's punishment, is today being led by men of his own kind to the place where limbs are cut off, then although he has not even a fraction of the suffering of the lower realms, he becomes utterly frightened and panicked, tormented by thirst so that his mouth goes dry, the glow of his face fades, his eyes look stricken, and his appearance visibly changes from his former state. If that is so, then no comparison can be drawn with that fear and suffering: seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death, of a different lot from oneself and utterly terrifying in form, in the hell realms where terror and suffering are far greater still, one's neck bound with a noose, one's body struck with hammers, … led away alone; and when one is struck by the sickness of that great appearance of terror and dread, at that time one becomes utterly wretched and panicked, one's appearance changed from what it was before — what need is there even to say it? Or else it is rendered: "when one is struck by the fierce sickness of the very moment of death." And in the Indian text and the commentary it says, "What need to speak of being fouled with filth?" — meaning: when, panicked and wretched, one loses control of excrement and urine so that the body is fouled, what need is there to say that one's appearance changes? In the *Questions of Bhadrapāla* too it says: "First, having died and transferred, he is led away without any power over it, and being in suffering and unhappy of mind he enters into a great and utter darkness, and thinks in this way, as though being led away by savages … and he knows, moreover, that a noose has been fastened around his own neck," and so on. Therefore it is only right to strive, from now on, at confessing wrongdoing and accomplishing virtue.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-44]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

So then, for those persons who have accumulated wrongdoing and non-virtuous action that is very powerful and very great, and who are therefore certain, once dead, to be born in the next life in the hells, in the states of the lower realms — for them, at the point when death arrives, even before the breath has actually left the body, the forms of the guardians and the fierce hosts of the hell-executioners of whatever birthplace is to come already appear. At that time the messengers of the Lord of Death, the executioners with the masked heads of tigers, leopards and the rest, utterly terrifying, horrifying and fearsome, their bodies and colour turned black, holding in their hands weapons — axes, swords and the like, blazing with fire by nature — and nooses and many iron hooks and hammers and other implements whose nature is to strike; their eyes bulging like glass, mouths and teeth clenched, matted hair red-yellow and blazing upward by nature, their forms possessed of a tremendous fierce splendour — these bind one with the noose of the Lord of Death, drag one with the iron hook, seize one with the hook-tip, beat and strike one with hammers, so that one is struck by the most terrible anguish of great terror. At such a time, that wrongdoing person becomes utterly wretched, or loses all courage and trembles, or cries out in lamentation, or loses control of the faculties, and so forth: what need is there even to say it? This shows the reason why, in the time before such a thing has come about, it is fitting to confess wrongdoing quickly.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-44]]

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **minyak-kunzang-sonam:** he gives two renderings for the sense of the phrase "struck by the sickness of great terror": (1) the sense of being struck by the sickness of that great appearance of being seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death; (2) or else the sense of being struck by the fierce sickness of the very moment of death — and he also sets out that the Indian text and the commentary read, "What need to speak of being fouled with filth?"
- **khenpo-kunga:** for the sense of that phrase he sets out the principle that, for someone of ill fortune, the forms of the executioners and guardians of the lower-realm birthplace to come appear in advance, even before the breath has left the body — expounding this in more detail than any of the others.

The meaning does not conflict, but whereas Kunzang Pelden and others set out the defining features of the phrase "the sickness of great terror" in brief, Minyak Kunzang Sonam divides it into two renderings and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk expounds the reason for that state in detail; so it is noted separately.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-44]] [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-44]]

#### Metaphors and Examples

- **the terror of a guilty person being led away (as punishment) to the place where limbs are cut off** (སྐྱེ་བོ་ཉེས་པ་ཅན་ཡན་ལག་གཅོད་པའི་སར་(ཆད་པའི་དོན་དུ)ཁྲིད་པའི་བྲེད་སྣང་) → the great terror that arises when one is seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death. The point is established by an argument from the lesser to the greater.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Argument From Lesser to Greater — If the Small Terror of Punishment Does This, What Need to Speak of the Great Wretchedness of Being Seized by the Messengers of the Lord of Death

All the commentaries explain on a shared basis that if merely being led to the place of amputation already changes a condemned person's appearance, then what need is there even to say that one becomes utterly wretched when seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death, beings of a different lot from oneself and terrifying in form.

###### 2. The Defining Features of the Terrifying Form of the Messengers of the Lord of Death

Zhenga's annotation commentary makes clear the dreadful details: baring the teeth and gums, jaws clenched tight, hair bound up in a topknot, eyes bulging like glass, calves grown taut and sinewy. Kunzang Pelden also sets out how they wield weapons — iron hooks, nooses, hammers, battle-axes and the like. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk further describes them as having the masked heads of tigers, leopards and the rest.

###### 3. A Distinctive Explanation — the Appearance of the Messengers of the Lord of Death Comes Even Before the Breath Has Actually Left the Body

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk expounds in detail that for a person who has accumulated powerful bad karma and is certain to be born in the lower realms, the forms of the executioners and guardians of the birthplace to come appear in advance at the point when death arrives.

###### 4. Differing Explanations of the Phrase "the Sickness of Great Terror"

Minyak Kunzang Sonam divides it into the sense of being struck by the sickness of the great appearance of being seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death, and the sense of being struck by the fierce sickness of the very moment of death. ⚑

###### 5. The Instruction That One Must Confess Wrongdoing and Strive at Virtue From This Very Moment

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen says, "Therefore one should feel regret for one's wrongdoing"; Sabzang Mati Panchen says, "Therefore it is only right to strive at virtue"; and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk says, "This shows the reason why it is fitting to confess wrongdoing quickly."

###### 6. Even a Small Fear Here Reveals the Dread of an Uncertain Birthplace to Come

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that if even the mere terror of a punishment in this life produces wretchedness, then it is certain that a great dread will arise when consciousness transfers and one has no certainty at all about what the future birthplace will be.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows, by an argument from the lesser to the greater, that if merely being led to the place of amputation already changes a condemned person's appearance, then what need is there to say that one becomes utterly wretched when seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death — beings of a different lot from oneself, terrifying in form — and struck by the sickness of great terror.

**Key points.**

- The terrifying form of the messengers of the Lord of Death is defined by such features as baring the teeth and gums, jaws clenched tight, hair bound up in a topknot, and eyes bulging like glass.
- How much greater the wretchedness of being seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death is than the small terror of punishment is established by the comparison.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk expounds in detail that the appearance of the messengers of the Lord of Death is a principle that holds even before the breath has left the body, for one certain to be born in the lower realms.
- ⚑ For the phrase "struck by the sickness of great terror," Minyak Kunzang Sonam divides the sense into the appearance of being seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death and the fierce sickness of the very moment of death.
- Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sabzang Mati Panchen and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk all instruct that, on the strength of this terror, one must confess wrongdoing and strive at virtue from this very moment.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains how much greater the dread of an uncertain future birthplace is, if even the mere terror of a punishment in this life gives rise to wretchedness.

##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

When that consciousness breaks the relation of support and supported with this bodily constituent and arrives at the moment called the next birthplace, then in that condition, in a state of dread and unspeakable terror, one thinks, "Who is there who can protect me?" — and though one searches in the four directions, wondering whether some refuge might be found, no refuge is to be found.

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

At that time he cries out, "Which compassionate one — a lama, a physician, someone — will save wretched me well from these great terrors: the final severing at death, the executioners of the Lord of Death and the rest?" And, utterly frightened, panic-stricken — that is, "gooseflesh," the skin of the whole head drawn back toward the nape — his eyes blue and staring wide, unblinking, he looks to the four quarters and the rest, searching for some other protector.

##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

When one has been born in hell and sees the hell-guardians, one cries, "Alas! Yet though one searches so, seeing that there is no refuge in any of the four directions, one then falls into utter despair. Therefore one should go for refuge to the Three Jewels from this very moment.

##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

That when the great terror actually comes one searches for a refuge and does not find one: suppose someone thinks that it will be enough to search for a refuge at the time of death. When that great appearance of dread and terror has actually manifested, then with the lament "Alas!" one cries, "Which compassionate person will fully and well protect destitute and wretched me from this great dread and terror of being seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death and going to hell?" And, utterly terrified and panic-stricken — that is, the look of fright coming over him — with the muscles of the eyes gone slack so that the eyes bulge wide, he looks in the four directions and searches for a refuge.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-45]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

At the moment when, for such a wrongdoing person, appearances of dread and terror and horror have dawned or appeared, then, searching for a refuge, the hope arises: "Is there anyone at all — some person who could free me from this great fear and terror at the result of my own wrongdoing, from this fierce suffering that is coming, and protect me well? Who will there be who protects?" And "panic-stricken": utterly frightened and trembling, he loses control of the faculties; his mind swoons and he becomes as though intoxicated — that is, at the very point of actual death, at a moment like the breath being about to stop, when a change comes over the body, the eyes turn blue and roll upward and are drawn up inside the eyelids, and the feet and hands barely move at all — and in that state he thinks, "Is there anyone in the four directions, east and the rest, to be a refuge and protector for me?" and searches for a refuge, or goes on hoping.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-45]]

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **gyaltsab:** he places the appearance described in this verse "at the time when one has been born in hell and sees the hell-guardians," explaining it as a terror that arises once a being has actually been born in hell.
- **kunpal / ngulchu-thogmed / sabzang / minyak-kunzang-sonam / khenpo-kunga / tenzin-gyatso:** they place it at the time of death, or at the point of being seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death (the moment of dying, or the beginning of the intermediate state), explaining the appearance as occurring without one having actually been born in hell.

The meaning does not conflict, but since there is a difference in identifying which moment "at that time" refers to, it is noted separately.

- **Depth of explanation of the phrase "panic-stricken":** khenpo-zhengah glosses it briefly as "under the force of utter fright, with the eyes bulging up into the middle of the skull." kunpal gives the comparison of "gooseflesh," explaining at length how the skin of the whole head is drawn back toward the nape and the eyes are blue and staring wide, unblinking. khenpo-kunga explains it most extensively of all, as the final moment of dying, at the very point of death when the breath is about to stop.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Cry for a Refuge at the Time of the Great Terror of Death

Many commentaries explain in agreement how a person, seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death and utterly frightened, cries "Who will save me well from this great terror?" and, panic-stricken, searches in the four directions for a refuge.

###### 2. This Verse States the Suffering That Serves as the Preliminary to the Branch of Going for Refuge

On the strength of this terror, Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen connects it to the necessity of going for refuge: "One should go for refuge to the Three Jewels from this very moment."

###### 3. ⚑ Disagreement in Identifying the Moment of the Appearance

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen places it at the time when one has actually been born in hell and sees the hell-guardians, whereas many other commentaries place it at the time of death or at the point of being seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death.

###### 4. Differing Degrees of Elaboration in Describing Being Panic-Stricken

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk expounds that terror most extensively, as the moment of actual dying when the breath is about to stop, while Kunzang Pelden gives the comparison of "gooseflesh."

###### 5. The Established Conclusion That at That Time There Is No Other Source of Refuge at All

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites both *Advice to the King* and the *Sutra of the Pair* to make clear that lamentation at the time of death is of no help and that there is no other protector.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse voices the experience of a person, seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death and utterly frightened, who cries "Who will save me well from this great terror?" and, panic-stricken, searches in the four directions for a refuge; and from the side of many commentaries this is explained as part of the statement of the suffering that serves as the preliminary to the branch of going for refuge.

**Key points.**

- All the commentaries agree in the end that the person, utterly frightened, cries out "Who will save me?" and searches in the four directions for a refuge.
- Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen connects this to the instruction that one must go for refuge to the Three Jewels from this very moment.
- ⚑ There is disagreement about the moment of the appearance: Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen explains it as occurring once one has actually been born in hell, whereas many other commentaries explain it as the time of death or the point of being seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death.
- On the look of being panic-stricken, Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk expounds at length the moment of actual dying when the breath is about to stop, while Kunzang Pelden gives the comparison of "gooseflesh."
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites *Advice to the King* and the *Sutra of the Pair* to make clear that at that time there is no other source of refuge.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

When that consciousness breaks the relation of support and supported with this bodily constituent and arrives at the moment called the next birthplace, then in that condition, in a state of dread and unspeakable terror, one thinks, "Who is there who can protect me?" — and though one searches in the four directions wondering whether some refuge might be found, no refuge is to be found. At that moment, as it is said, "One is oneself one's own protector": when one's own sources of refuge are pushed to the very limit, the actual refuge is the Jewel of the Dharma. When we Buddhists speak of the three refuges, the Teacher who shows the refuge is likened to a physician, the Dharma is likened to the actual refuge, the medicine, and the Sangha is likened to the companions who accomplish the refuge, that is, to nurses. What actually protects one from suffering, which is like an illness, is the benefit of the Dharma, which is like medicine — no one else can serve as the actual refuge. If one abandons wrongdoing and accomplishes virtue without confusing what is to be adopted and what rejected, and so becomes familiar in one's own understanding with the Dharma, then gradually the force of one's own afflictions, and of the bad karma accumulated on their account, becomes weaker and less, and freedom from suffering comes about; there is no other method. On that occasion, though one searches the four directions for a refuge, none is found.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-46]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Though one searches the four directions in this way with real urgency, when it comes to undergoing the bad karma one has oneself created, not even a buddha can protect one — as with Devadatta, who was born in hell while crying out "Gautama, I am burnt, I am burnt!" So, seeing that there is not a single protector, one then falls into utter despair at finding no refuge. One thinks: "I was born in the southern continent of Jambudvīpa, hard to be born in; I obtained the human body of leisure and endowment, hard to obtain; I met a genuine lama, hard to meet; I encountered the sublime Dharma, hard to encounter, and came to know a little of what to adopt and what to reject — and yet, cut off from my share by bad karma, I did not accomplish virtue but committed vast wrongdoing; and now, deprived of refuge and without a protector, my death is settled and there is nothing for me but to go to the lower realms." And remembering the wrongdoing done before, his chest covered with the marks of his own nails, his face gone blue, tears streaming from his eyes, his breath trembling, his head and limbs flailing, he goes on to the far side of this life and comes to suffer — just as the *Sutra of Advice to the King* says, "There is no refuge other than the Dharma." Thus, if at that moment of death there is not a single refuge other than the sublime Dharma, virtue, then at that time, what shall I, with my bad karma, do? Since there is nothing at all that can be done, one is utterly tormented by suffering and becomes wretched.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-46]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->
##### Zhenphen Jampé Gocha (annotation commentary)

Zhenga's annotation commentary sets out the sense of this verse in brief in its underlying gloss: that seeing there is no refuge though one searches the four directions, one falls into despair; and that if in that place of terror there is not a single refuge at all, then at that time, what shall I do?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^2-46]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

When one has been born in hell and sees the hell-guardians, one cries, "Alas! What person will save me well from this great terror?" — and, utterly frightened and panic-stricken, with eyes bulging, searches in the four directions for a refuge. Yet though one searches so, seeing that there is no refuge in any of the four directions, one then falls into utter despair; therefore one should go for refuge to the Three Jewels from this very moment. And so, if in that hell realm there is no refuge that protects from terror, then at that time what shall I do? Since there is no method at all, one should strive from this very moment at the causes of being free from terror.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^2-46]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

For example, when a person guilty of a crime is today being led to the place where limbs are cut off, he is terrified: his mouth goes dry, his eyes look stricken, and his appearance visibly changes from what it was. If that is so, then when one is seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death, who stand there utterly terrifying in form, and is struck by the sickness of great terror, and is made utterly wretched by that suffering — what need is there even to speak of being terrified? At that time one cries, "Who will save me well from this great terror?" and, panic-stricken — that is, with the eyes of utter fright bulging — searches in the four directions for a refuge. Yet though one searches so, seeing that there is no refuge in the four directions, one then falls once again into utter despair. If in such a place there is not a single refuge at all, then at that time what shall I do? — there is no method.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^2-46]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Though one searches the four directions in that way, seeing by the force of bad karma that there is no refuge at all, one then falls into utter despair at finding no refuge. And in that place where, having died, one has fallen into hell and the rest, if there is no refuge at all for the wrongdoer, then at that time what shall I do? Since regret then has no remedy, it is only right to strive from this very moment.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-46]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Suppose someone thinks that it will be enough to search for a refuge at the time of death. When that great appearance of dread and terror has actually manifested, then with the lament "Alas!" one cries, "Which compassionate person will fully and well protect destitute and wretched me from this great dread and terror of being seized by the messengers of the Lord of Death and going to hell?" And, utterly terrified and panic-stricken — that is, the look of fright coming over him — with the eyes bulging wide, he looks in the four directions and searches for a refuge. Yet though one searches the four directions for a refuge in that way, if one is among those who have not gathered merit and have accumulated grave wrongdoing and non-virtuous action, then at that time, seeing that there is no source of refuge at all, one then falls, more than before and in every respect, into a downcast and despairing state of mind. And so, at that moment of actually falling into the terrible realm of hell, if for those who have accumulated great wrongdoing there is no refuge that protects from that terror, then at that time what shall I do? Since one has no power at all in any action, there is nothing to be done even by such means as averting rites, and regret has absolutely no remedy — so he explains. For that meaning he also cites *Advice to the King* and the *Sutra of the Pair*.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-46]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

"Seeing there is no refuge in the four directions" — that is, though one searches for a refuge in the four directions, east and the rest, seeing that there is no one at all to be a refuge or a protector for oneself; and when, as the result of the bad karma of the wrongdoing and non-virtue done while one was alive, one comes to see with actual certainty that one will be born in the hells, in the states of the lower realms — then, it is taught, there also arises such a state in which that wrongdoing being becomes despairing in mind, or has all hope cut off. "If there is no refuge in that place, then at that time what shall I do?" — for those wrongdoing persons who, in this life, did not properly accomplish virtue, did not abandon wrongdoing, and did not accomplish anything like the sublime Dharma by way of going for refuge to the Three Jewels and of conscientiousness and mindful awareness, but instead accumulated and completed much wrongdoing and non-virtuous action, it is the nature of things that when life is spent and they die, they will undergo the fierce suffering of the final severing at death, the delusive appearances of the intermediate state after death, and the fierce sufferings of heat and cold and hunger and thirst in the lower realms hereafter. So, if at such a time there is no refuge, no protector and no guide at all, then at that time what shall I myself do? Since one is then bereft of any means, this teaches that in this very present time, while one still has some actual freedom and some life remaining, one must go for refuge to the Three Jewels and, with fierce remorse for one's wrongful actions, confess them properly. By these, the manner of confessing wrongdoing by what is called the power of repudiation, among the four powers, is taught.

##### BCAC19_KKP — The Case of Devadatta

Devadatta met the Buddha in person and heard the Dharma; yet when it came to undergoing the bad karma he had himself created, not even the Buddha could protect him, and he was born in hell while crying out in lamentation, "Gautama, I am burnt, I am burnt!" <!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Experience of Despair When, at the Time of Death, One Searches the Four Directions and Finds No Refuge at All

As all the commentaries explain on a shared basis, the central point of this verse is that at the moment of death, or at the moment of falling into the lower realms, though one searches the four directions for a refuge, no one at all is found to be a refuge, and so the mind falls into utter despair.

###### 2. When One Undergoes the Bad Karma One Has Oneself Created, Not Even a Buddha Can Protect One

Kunzang Pelden shows this through the case of Devadatta: even having met the Buddha in person, one cannot be protected by the Buddha from the result of one's own bad karma. Tenzin Gyatso likewise explains that the Buddha is only one who shows the path and cannot serve as the actual refuge.

###### 3. At the Time of Death There Is No Refuge Other Than the Sublime Dharma

Citing the *Sutra of Advice to the King* and the *Sutra of the Pair*, it is explained that at the time of death no one — father, mother, children and the rest — can protect one, and that apart from the full ripening of the fruit of one's own actions there is no one at all who will protect.

###### 4. This Verse Shows the Manner of the Power of Repudiation, Among the Four Powers

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that this verse conveys the power of properly confessing, with fierce remorse, the wrongful actions done before.

###### 5. The Instruction That While One Still Has Freedom One Must Abandon Wrongdoing, Accomplish Virtue, and Go for Refuge

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen says one should go for refuge to the Three Jewels from this very moment; Sabzang Mati Panchen likewise says it is only right to strive from this very moment; and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk says that one must go for refuge and confess while one still has freedom.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse states that at the time of death, or at the moment of falling into the lower realms, though one searches the four directions for a refuge, no one at all is found to be a refuge, so that the mind falls into utter despair; and that at that time there is nothing whatever that one can do. All the commentaries explain this as the reason why one must go for refuge to the Three Jewels and strive to confess wrongdoing from this very moment.

**Key points.**

- The *Sutra of Advice to the King* and the *Sutra of the Pair* are cited to show that at the time of death no one — father, mother, children and the rest — can protect one, and that apart from the fruit of one's own actions there is no one at all who will protect.
- Kunzang Pelden shows through the case of Devadatta that even having met the Buddha in person, one cannot be protected by the Buddha from one's own bad karma.
- Tenzin Gyatso explains that the Buddha is only one who shows the path, like a physician, and not the actual refuge, so that one must put the practice into effect oneself.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that this verse shows the manner of the power of repudiation, among the four powers.
- Many commentaries conclude with the instruction that from this very moment one must go for refuge to the Three Jewels and strive to abandon wrongdoing and accomplish virtue.
