## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the forty-second and forty-third verses of the fifth chapter. This teaching clearly shows that once we begin a task, we should not let our mind wander elsewhere. We should first bring that very task to completion.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

Based on the literal meaning of these two verses, we can explain as follows.

In times of danger or celebration, the verse tells us that it is permissible to relax a minor point of discipline for the sake of generosity. It also tells us that once we begin a task, we should not let our mind wander elsewhere before finishing it. We should complete that very task first.

So whatever task we do, we should match it to the situation, and we should bring the task we have begun to completion.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, I will focus my mind single-pointedly on one task. I will not take up any other task until I have finished it.

**Explanation:** *(Diligence Practice)* Until we finish a task we have begun, we should focus on that task alone, without letting the mind wander. If we do this, that one task will be completed well, and we will be free from the distraction and mistakes that come from wanting to do many things at once.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

"Connected with fear, celebrations, and so on" means this: when some other sudden, great danger arises; or when one is holding a great Dharma celebration, such as reciting sutras; or in situations connected with a need such as helping others. In such cases there is a risk that, if the mind stays single-pointed, one will be diverted from the welfare of others. So one need not keep to bodily conduct such as not looking about with the eyes; by the teaching "do as is comfortable," permission is given for that occasion. The Teacher, the Blessed One, taught as follows. Although telling a lie is not at all permissible, for the sake of giving fearlessness — for example, if a badly behaved child is chasing after an injured puppy or a tiny injured bird, and on that occasion one says the lie "I did not see it" in order to save the life of that helpless creature — then, even though in general, within ethical discipline (which is a turning away from whatever is a basis for harming others), one must not tell a lie, he taught that a lie may be told in order to save another's life. On occasions connected with great need, such actions of body and speech are permitted to a bodhisattva for the sake of others.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-42]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

When, in connection with a danger to life from tigers, leopards, and the like, or with celebrations of offering to the Three Jewels such as festival-time offerings, or with accomplishing a great benefit for others, one is unable to remain single-pointedly settled in such strictly restrained conduct, then for now, relying on mindfulness and awareness and without letting the mind become distracted, one is permitted to do as is comfortable. This is so because, although in general ethical discipline is said to be supreme over giving, nevertheless, at times of giving a vast and unrestricted gift, the minor trainings of ethical discipline — such as moving the hands broadly, or serving alcohol — are to be left in equanimity; for the Akṣayamati Sūtra says, "At the time of giving, gather ethical discipline in and leave it in equanimity."

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

When one is connected with a danger of harm to life and the like, with celebrations such as offerings to the Three Jewels, and with the welfare of beings, which is of even greater importance than these, then even if one is unable to keep to such strictly restrained conduct, one is permitted to do as is comfortable in conduct that accords with those circumstances. This is so because, at times of giving, certain ethical disciplines may be set aside in equanimity: the Sūtra of the Questions of Akṣayamati says, "At the time of giving, gather ethical discipline in and leave it in equanimity." Its meaning is, for example, like telling the lie "I did not see it" while actually seeing a being who is to be killed, in order to give that being fearlessness — so says the Detailed Exposition.

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

In general, one must strive not to depart even slightly from virtuous conduct such as meditating in concentration; but for beginners, who are unable to strive in that way continually, the special exception permitting them for now to do as is comfortable is as follows. When one is connected with fears that threaten one's own life — such as enemies among humans and others, poisonous snakes, fire, or water — or with celebrations such as offering especially excellent and vast offerings to the Three Jewels on auspicious days and the like, or with doing a vast benefit for others that is greater still than these, then, if there arise kinds of very subtle, difficult conduct of strictly restraining body and mind that one is unable to do, one is permitted, for now, to proceed with conduct that accords with those circumstances, in a relaxed way, doing as is comfortable. Since this is permitted, no downfall arises from acting in this way; and afterward one should again abide, as before, in the conduct of restraining body and mind.

This is so because, in general, ethical discipline is a hundredfold greater than giving, so it is not fitting to set ethical discipline aside in equanimity; yet a beginner cannot at the outset accomplish both at once, and so at times of training chiefly in giving, for now, until one has trained to become familiar with that, the subtle points of ethical discipline that one cannot accomplish are taught to be left in equanimity. For the Akṣayamati Sūtra says, "At the time of giving, gather ethical discipline in and leave it in equanimity," and so on, thereby showing that when one is training at the outset chiefly in giving, there is no downfall even if one cannot train in ethical discipline and so forth. As for an example, it is like telling the lie "I did not see it" while actually seeing a being who is to be killed, in order to give it fearlessness; the Detailed Exposition (Vibhūticandra's commentary) says so, and it appears in the commentary of the Heir of the Victors (Thogme Zangpo, that is, Ngulchu Thogme). He explains that this has the meaning that one must become skilled in training in the correct, unmistaken order of the path, entering the path by training in it gradually and completing it in sequence.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-42]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

On some occasions — such as times involving fear of enemies, thieves, wild animals, fire, water, and the like, and also circumstances connected with celebrations of joy such as making offerings to the Three Jewels or giving gifts to beggars — in such circumstances, if one is unable to remain by way of conduct such as strictly restraining the senses, one is permitted to let body and speech remain as is comfortable. For at times having even some slight connection of that kind — such as a time of celebrating offerings to the Three Jewels or a time of giving gifts to beggars — even if one cannot guard all the trainings of ethical discipline that are of a subtle, minor nature, one may leave them in equanimity. This is the meaning of what the Akṣayamati Sūtra teaches.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. All the Commentaries Agree That Minor Trainings May Be Set Aside When Strict Conduct Is Impossible

The commentaries agree that not every training of ethical discipline must be kept uniformly and without exception. In situations connected with danger to life, celebrations of offering to the Three Jewels, great benefit for others, and the like, where strictly restrained conduct is not possible, the minor trainings may be set aside in equanimity.

###### 2. Minyak Kunzang Sonam Names the Akṣayamati Sūtra as the Source of This Permission

This permission is drawn directly from the Akṣayamati Sūtra's treatment of the perfection of giving. It is set out as the authority for the point that setting subtle points of ethical discipline aside in equanimity, at a time when a vast act of giving is being accomplished, does not become a downfall.

###### 3. His Holiness the Dalai Lama Adds a Detailed Example — a Lie Told to Save a Life — and Limits the Permission to Great Need

His Holiness the Dalai Lama gives a detailed supplementary example on this permission, showing that even telling a lie is permitted in order to save another's life, and he makes clear that this permission extends only to occasions connected with a great need.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** In connection with danger to life, celebrations of offering to the Three Jewels, the accomplishing of vast benefit for others, and the like, if one cannot remain in strictly restrained conduct of body and mind, the commentaries agree that for now one may do as is comfortable, and that setting the subtle trainings of ethical discipline aside in equanimity does not become a downfall.

**Key points.**

- When fear of enemies, fire, water, wild animals, and the like arises, or when one is making an offering celebration to the Three Jewels, or accomplishing great benefit for others, one is permitted to do as is comfortable if strictly restrained conduct is not possible.
- The source of this permission is the Akṣayamati Sūtra's statement "At the time of giving, gather ethical discipline in and leave it in equanimity," which says that at the time of accomplishing a vast act of giving, the subtle points of ethical discipline are to be left in equanimity.
- A beginner cannot at the outset accomplish both giving and ethical discipline at the same time; this has the meaning that one must enter the path by training gradually, without mistaking its order.
- In circumstances connected with great need, such actions of body and speech as telling the lie "I did not see it" while actually seeing a being who is to be killed, in order to save its life, are also permitted to a bodhisattva.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Once a Task Is Begun, the Mind Must Not Wander Off Until It Is Done

This shows the key point that, whatever conduct or hearing and reflecting one first begins, until it is accomplished one must not let the mind go elsewhere but must accomplish it single-pointedly. It is set out in order to abandon the fault of piling up many tasks, none of them with a proper beginning or end.

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk uses the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* itself as his example of hearing and reflecting, showing that once one text has been well brought to completion, it is acceptable to enter other texts in sequence.

###### 3. Sabzang Mati Panchen Adds That the Task Must Be One Begun on the Basis of Scripture and Reasoning

Sabzang Mati Panchen sets out that a task such as giving must be one begun after reflecting in accord with scripture and reasoning and seeing its benefit, making clear that the very reason for beginning must rest on scripture and reasoning.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** The commentaries agree that, whatever task of hearing and reflecting or of conduct one first sets about doing, until it is accomplished one must not let the mind go elsewhere but, with a mind single-pointedly fixed on it, bring it first to completion.

**Key points.**

- **Completing the first task.** Until the task first begun is accomplished, one must not think of other things, such as things to recite or to look at, but must fix the mind single-pointedly and bring it to completion.
- **Entering in sequence.** Only after each earlier task has been well completed must one enter each later one in sequence.
- **The *Bodhicaryāvatāra* itself as the example.** Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk gives as his example a single text such as the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*: after hearing and reflecting on it have been brought to completion, one enters other texts and the practice of meditation in sequence.
- **The point about virtues of equal strength.** Ngulchu Thogme teaches that, among virtues of equal strength, what was first begun must be accomplished without being abandoned.
- **Tasks in accord with scripture and reasoning.** Sabzang Mati Panchen teaches that a task such as giving, which has been begun after seeing its benefit in accord with scripture and reasoning, must be accomplished first, with effort.
