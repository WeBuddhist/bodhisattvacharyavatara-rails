## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three commentators agree that Verse 1-20 establishes the scriptural authority (*āgama* / *lung*) and logical reasoning (*sopapattika* / *thad pa dang bcas pa*) for the continuous and boundless arising of merit from the engaging mind of enlightenment (*avatāra-citta* / *'jug pa'i sems*). They systematically unpack the Metaphor of the Armor from the *Subāhuparipṛcchā Sūtra* and explain the Buddha's compassionate intent in teaching this doctrine to beings of lesser aspirations.

### 1. Scriptural Authority: Citing the *Subāhuparipṛcchā Sūtra*
*   **Consensus on Source:** All three commentators identify the *Subāhuparipṛcchā Sūtra* (Sūtra Requested by Subāhu / *lag bzangs kyis zhus pa'i mdo*) as the primary canonical source cited by Śāntideva. This scripture provides the scriptural proof (*教立* / *lung*) and logical justification (*理成* / *rigs pas sgrub pa*) that the engaging mind of enlightenment generates an uninterrupted, sky-like flow of merit.
*   **The Metaphor of the Armor:** Both Sazang Mati Panchen and Ngulchu Thokme (in his original Tibetan text) quote the exact scriptural passage describing the "armor of resolve" (*go bགོས་* / *go cha*):
    > "If one dons the armor for the sake of the benefit and happiness of the infinite realms of sentient beings, their boundless virtuous roots—even if they become unmindful, have their minds distracted, or fall asleep—will grow, increase, and become fully perfected in every single instant of the mind, day and night; by generating the boundless accumulations of awakening, they will not find it difficult to attain perfect awakening."
    This scriptural quote directly validates the claim made in Verses 1-18 and 1-19 regarding the continuous flow of merit even during sleep or carelessness.

### 2. Compassionate Intent: Turning Back Beings of Lesser Aspiration
*   **Defining the Audience (*hīnādhimukti* / *dman mos*):** The commentators explain that the Buddha (the Tathāgata) spoke this teaching specifically for the benefit of sentient beings who are inclined toward the lower vehicle (Hearers and Solitary Buddhas).
*   **Gyaltsab Je's Analysis of Fear:** Gyaltsab Je explains that certain practitioners of indefinite lineage (*aniyata-gotra*) are terrified of the vast collections of merit and wisdom required for full Buddhahood. Because of this fear, they turn away from the Great Vehicle and seek the individual peace of a Hearer's enlightenment.
*   **The Buddha's Remedy:** Sazang Mati Panchen, Gyaltsab Je, and Ngulchu Thokme agree that the Buddha taught this continuous arising of merit to show these cautious practitioners that accumulating vast merit is not an unendurable, agonizing hardship. By demonstrating that the engaging mind generates merit effortlessly and continuously—even while sleeping or fuddled—the Buddha dispels their fear, turns back their inclination to the lower vehicle, and firmly establishes them in the Great Vehicle.
