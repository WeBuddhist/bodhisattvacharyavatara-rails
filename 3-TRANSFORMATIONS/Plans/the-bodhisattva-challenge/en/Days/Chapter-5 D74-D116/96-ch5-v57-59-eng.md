## Today's Verse

> Regarding blameless matters,
> having exerted control over oneself and beings,
> I must always hold this mind
> as if I were an illusion, without a self. ^5-57

> Having repeatedly reflected on the supreme opportunity
> obtained after a long time,
> I must hold this mind, like Mount Meru,
> completely unmoving. ^5-58

> Vultures, attached to flesh,
> drag it back and forth everywhere.
> If your mind is not displeased,
> why fuss now? ^5-59

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the fifty-seventh, fifty-eighth, and fifty-ninth verses of the fifth chapter. This precious human life is short. Even so, what would it be like if our mind were as steady as a mountain that the wind cannot move?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us an example that is easy to understand and encouraging. We must think again and again about this. We are free from the lower realms. We have left the eight states without freedom behind. We have found a human body that is hard to find. We must make it meaningful. For example, these three cannot make Mount Meru shake: dust, birds, and the ocean. In the same way, we must hold our own mind firm and unmoving, so that bad friends, the suffering of hardship, hearing profound Dharma, and so on cannot shake it.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will think about how hard this rare human life is to find. Then I will keep my mind steady and not be moved by distraction.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ If we think about how hard it is to find this precious human body, we will give up pointless distraction. If the mind is as unmoving as Mount Meru, bad conditions cannot overcome it. In this way, our daily actions turn toward doing good.
