<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

1. **The sweet scents and incense that exist even in the worlds of gods and nāgas are also set forth as offering-substances.** — With the phrase "in the worlds of gods, nāgas and the like too, the naturally occurring scents and the compounded or arising incense," all the commentaries agree that not only the human world but the excellent things of the realms of gods and nāgas are gathered up as offering-substances.
2. **The wish-fulfilling tree — the tree that grants every wish.** — On the meaning of "wish-fulfilling tree," Kunzang Pelden explains it as the tree from which everything one conceives and wishes for arises, and Sabzang Mati agrees, calling it the tree from which every desired thing arises the moment it is thought of.
3. **Trees made of various jewels — the perfect excellence of the environment's qualities.** — Ngulchu Thogme and Khenpo Kunga explain in one voice that these are trees made of various kinds of precious substances of great value, such as crystal, ruby, gold, and silver.
4. **Crops that grow of themselves, unploughed — enjoyments arising without effort.** — On the phrase "the forms of crops that grow of themselves, unploughed," Kunzang Pelden gives the example of fruit-crops such as rice, and Ngulchu Thogme explains it as the many kinds or varieties of crops.
5. **"And, beyond these, the ornaments worthy of being offered" — a general summary of every distinct thing past telling.** — All the commentaries explain that "and, beyond these, however many kinds of ornaments worthy of being offered there are" is the summarizing phrase that gathers up all the offering-substances named in verses 2-2 through 2-4; and Ngulchu Thogme confirms it with the scriptural citation from the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse names many of the distinct offering-substances that belong to no owner — the sweet scents and incense that exist even in the worlds of gods and nāgas, the wish-fulfilling trees and trees made of various jewels, and the forms of crops that grow of themselves, unploughed — together with "the ornaments worthy of being offered." All the commentaries understand it in one and the same way.

**Key points.**

- The sweet scents of the realms of gods and nāgas, whether naturally occurring or compounded, are counted among the offering-substances.
- The wish-fulfilling tree is the tree that accomplishes every wish without obstruction, as all the commentaries explain.
- The trees formed of the seven precious substances are given as an image for the qualities of pure realms such as Sukhāvatī.
- The crops that grow of themselves, unploughed, set forth enjoyments that arise without effort as offering-substances too.
- With "and, beyond these, the ornaments worthy of being offered," all the offering-substances past telling are gathered up in general, as confirmed by the citation from the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

1. **Offerings that belong to no owner.** — This verse explains that everything from the lakes and ponds adorned with lotuses and the swans and other pleasing birds, out to all that exists throughout the whole reach of the infinite expanse of space, is an offering-substance that no one has made his own; all the commentaries teach this same basis in agreement.
2. **The meaning of "belonging to no owner."** — As Ngulchu Thogme and Minyak Kunzang Sonam clearly explain, this means all the substances — like the produce in pure realms and in Uttarakuru — that no owner has ever made his own and taken as his possession.
3. **"Reaching to the ends of the infinite expanse of space" = boundless pervasion.** — Just as Kunzang Pelden explains it as "all that exists, pervading everywhere throughout space," Sabzang Mati too explains it as the reach of a boundless field, "pervading to the very ends of the infinite expanse of space."
4. **The kinds of offering-substances are endless.** — From the lotus-adorned lakes and ponds and the sweet voices of the swans, whatever offering-substance of sight or sound there may be is past telling one by one, as Sabzang Mati and Minyak Kunzang Sonam clearly explain.
5. **The preliminary of taking hold with the mind and receiving before offering.** — Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk links the phrase "these too I, the person making the offering, take hold of with my mind and receive" to the preliminary stage — that all the perfectly excellent arrangements worthy of being offered to the Three Jewels, beyond the substances above, are first taken up in the mind.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse brings the earlier description of offering-substances to a close: from the lakes and ponds adorned with lotuses and the sweet voices of the swans, out to all that exists throughout the whole reach of the infinite expanse of space, all these offering-substances that no one has made his own are presented as offerings. All the commentaries explain this in agreement.

**Key points.**

- The chief distinguishing quality of these offering-substances is that they belong to no owner, so — free of the fault of stinginess — one should emanate them with the mind and offer them.
- The image of lakes and ponds adorned with lotuses, and of swans and other birds beautiful in their many colors, conveys the pleasing character of the field of offering.
- The phrase "reaching to the ends of the infinite expanse of space" shows that the extent of the offering-substances pervades without limit.
- What the qualities of each offering-substance are — such as the water endowed with the eight qualities — Minyak Kunzang Sonam sets out clearly by scriptural citation.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk links all these offering-substances to the preliminary stage of taking hold of them with the mind and receiving them before making the offering.
