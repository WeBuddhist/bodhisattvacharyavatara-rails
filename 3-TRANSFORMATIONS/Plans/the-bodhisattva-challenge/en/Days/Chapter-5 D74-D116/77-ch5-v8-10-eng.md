## Today's Verse

> The Sage taught that all such things
> are the result of harmful thoughts.
> Thus, in the three realms,
> there is no other fear than the mind. ^5-8

> If giving to the poor
> is the perfection of generosity,
> and if there are still poor beings, then how
> did the protectors perfect generosity before? ^5-9

> The perfection of generosity is taught
> as the mind of relinquishment,
> giving away all possessions with fruitful intent
> to all beings. Therefore, it is the mind itself. ^5-10

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the eighth, ninth, and tenth verses of the fifth chapter. Let us think together about this remarkable question: since there are still poor people in the world, does that mean even the Buddha did not perfect generosity?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a point that is easy to understand and encouraging.

If the perfection of generosity meant ending the poverty of every being in the world, then even the buddhas of the past would not have perfected it. The reason is simple: today, too, we still see many poor people in the world.

But the perfection of generosity does not depend on ending the poverty of others. It depends on our own mind. We must perfect the habit of giving without any stinginess. We give away our body, our possessions, and the roots and fruits of our good deeds to all beings. Perfecting this stingy-free mind of giving is the true perfection of generosity.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will help someone or give something to another person, with a mind free of attachment.

**Explanation:** _(Generosity Practice)_ The perfection of generosity does not come from ending all poverty in the world. It comes from perfecting our own wish to give away our body and possessions, free of attachment. So, starting today, we should train our mind to give even small things to others, without any attachment.
