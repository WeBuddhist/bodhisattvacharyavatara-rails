# Day 38 — The ledger you leave at the door

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Loosen one entry in your allies-and-enemies tally today
> **Detail:** Today's verse: we keep a running scoreboard of friends and foes, then walk out alone.

## Opening

Most of us carry a quiet running scoreboard: who is on my side, who is against me, who owes me, who wronged me. We spend real energy maintaining it, and sometimes do real harm and good on its basis. Shantideva points out where it all ends. Leaving this life, leaving everyone, we go on alone, and the scoreboard stays behind. So what, he asks, was the point of all that sorting?

## From the Tradition

Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, calls this life a bubble on water, and pictures the leaving plainly: father, mother, siblings, every friend, all of it set down, and you going on alone to a place you cannot see. Read carelessly, the verse can sound cold, as if love were pointless. That is not the point, and it is worth being clear. What is meaningless, Sazang says, is the attachment and the aversion, the grasping at the people we have filed as ours and the grudges we hold against the ones we have filed as against us. That sorting drives a great deal of the harm we do. The love itself is not the problem; the anxious bookkeeping around it is. You can hold people dearly and still let go of the scoreboard, and in fact letting go of the scoreboard is what frees the love to be steady and even, toward those near and far alike.

## Today's Practice

Bring to mind one person you have quietly filed as "on my side" and one you have filed as "against me." Just for today, loosen the tally one notch: let the ally be more than a useful ally, and let the other person off the hook of being your opponent for a moment. You are not dropping anyone you love. You are setting down the scoreboard you would have left behind anyway.
