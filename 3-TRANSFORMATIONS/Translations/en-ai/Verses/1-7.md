## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three classical commentators agree that verse 1-7 highlights the supreme efficacy of generating the mind of awakening (bodhicitta) as the ultimate method for accomplishing the welfare of all sentient beings. They emphasize that this discovery is not an arbitrary invention but the result of the Buddhas' deep, prolonged contemplation over countless aeons, offering a joyful and effortless path to liberation.

### 1. The Buddhas' Prolonged Contemplation and Discovery
- **Consensus:** The Buddhas (referred to as the Lords or Kings of Sages, or *Sugatas*) spent countless aeons actively investigating and contemplating every possible method to benefit sentient beings. Out of all potential means, they saw that only generating bodhicitta is of supreme, comprehensive benefit.
- **Sazang Mati Panchen's Explanation:** Sazang notes that the perfect Buddhas "contemplated only the methods to benefit sentient beings for many aeons such as three countless aeons," confirming that bodhicitta is the unique, verified vehicle that accomplishes all purposes of benefit and happiness.
- **Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo's Formulation:** Thokme Zangpo echoes this closely, stating that the Kings of Sages contemplated deeply for many countless aeons and saw that this very generation of bodhicitta is what truly benefits wandering beings.

### 2. A Joyful and Effortless Path to the Highest Bliss
- **Consensus:** Relying on bodhicitta allows infinite assemblies of sentient beings to easily and smoothly attain the highest bliss of buddhahood (high rebirth and definitive goodness) without having to undergo agonizing, painful hardships.
- **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen's Seed Analogy:** Gyaltsab Je explains that this generation of the mind acts "like a seed" that enables unnumbered beings to easily attain supreme bliss through a joyful path. This stands in sharp contrast to non-Buddhist or lesser paths that require extreme physical hardships, such as "tearing out one's hair."
- **Sazang's Contrast with Austerities:** Sazang similarly contrasts the path of bodhicitta with arduous, mediocre efforts and painful austerities, emphasizing that it allows unnumbered multitudes of beings to obtain supreme happiness "without depending on fierce hardships."

### 3. Doctrinal Nuances and Key Citations
- **Going From Bliss to Bliss:** Both Gyaltsab Je and Sazang emphasize the joyful nature of the Bodhisattva path. Gyaltsab Je quotes the famous scriptural passage: *"Going from bliss to bliss, what intelligent person would be lazy?"* This reinforces the idea that the practice of bodhicitta is not only joyful in its final result (buddhahood) but is also a joyful journey in itself.
- **Attainment of the Two Goals:** Sazang explains that the supreme happiness achieved through bodhicitta encompasses both "higher rebirth" (*abhyudaya*) and "definitive goodness" (*niḥśreyasa*), showing that it fulfills both temporary worldly aspirations and ultimate spiritual liberation.
