## Traditional Interpretation

### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Palden (Tibetan)

Verse 1-7 gives the authoritative reason for bodhicitta's supremacy: the mighty sages (*thub dbang* = perfect buddhas, *rdzogs pa'i sangs rgyas*) who, for countless kalpas (*bskal ba du mar*), painstakingly and thoroughly contemplated (*rab tu dgongs par mdzad pa*) what best benefits beings in both temporary and ultimate ways — they themselves saw (*gzigs*) that only this bodhicitta benefits all beings. Their conclusion: this bodhicitta enables (*byed*) measureless and immeasurable masses of beings (*tshad med pa'i skye bo'i tshogs rnams*) to easily obtain (*bde blag ñid du thob par byed*) the supreme happiness (*bde ba'i mchog*) — perfect buddhahood. KKP emphasises *rab tu dgongs pa*: this is not casual observation but sustained, profound contemplation over aeons. The buddhas themselves are the witnesses; their verdict is bodhicitta.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-7)

### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Tibetan)

GDR reads verse 1-7 as the second benefit of bodhicitta. After establishing (1-6) that bodhicitta surpasses other virtues in its power to suppress sin, verse 1-7 gives the cosmic endorsement: for countless kalpas (*bskal pa grans med gsum sogs du mar*), the mighty sages who thoroughly examined how to benefit beings in both provisional and ultimate ways found only bodhicitta able to accomplish this. GDR specifies what "benefit" (*phan pa*) includes: purifying past sins, cutting future sin-streams, suppressing kleshas, nurturing even small virtue-roots, and ultimately leading to great awakening. The verse's punchline: this bodhicitta enables measureless beings to *easily* (*bde blag = without hardship*) obtain the supreme happiness, i.e., to proceed swiftly to buddhahood without excessive difficulty.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-7)

### ngulchu-thogmed — Ngulchu Thokme Sangpo (Tibetan)

NTS reads the verse as providing the testimony of the perfect buddhas as proof of bodhicitta's efficacy. The pattern: the perfect buddhas are the best possible witnesses to what benefits beings; they contemplated for many kalpas and arrived at one conclusion — bodhicitta. Their testimony grounds the claim of verse 1-6: bodhicitta suppresses sin because it alone brings measureless beings to supreme happiness easily. The logic is both soteriological (only bodhicitta leads to buddhahood) and epistemically authoritative (the buddhas saw it, not ordinary beings).
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-7)

### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Mi Nyag Kunzang Sönam (Tibetan)

MKS offers the fullest analysis. The "mighty sages" (*thub dbang rnams*) are specifically the perfect buddhas who contemplated for countless *great* kalpas (not merely ordinary kalpas) through their extraordinary, unobstructed wisdom (*mkhyen pa thams cad mkhyen pa*). They examined all possible paths and methods and reached the singular conclusion: bodhicitta alone — not severe asceticism, not meditative absorption alone, not other virtues — is the means that enables measureless beings to easily obtain supreme happiness. MKS emphasises *bde blag* (= easily, not arduously): bodhicitta is the *swift path* (*mgyogs lam*), in contrast to the difficult, elongated paths of śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas. This is the doctrinal base for the Mahāyāna claim that bodhicitta is the most efficient means.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-7)

### khenpo-kunga — Ngakwang Kunga Wangchuk (Tibetan)

NKW gives a concise systematic reading: the perfect buddhas, the most powerful and wise of all sages, having examined through countless kalpas what antidote is most beneficial to the sin of beings established in 1-6, saw bodhicitta as the most beneficial means. The causal link: this bodhicitta enables measureless beings to obtain supreme happiness easily and quickly. "Easily" (*bde blag*) here means: without the extreme difficulty and extended time required by other paths. NKW notes this verse functions as pramāṇa: the buddhas' contemplative observation constitutes proof (*gtan tshigs*) of bodhicitta's efficacy.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-7)

### Divergences

No substantive interpretive divergence. All five commentaries agree: (1) the subject is perfect buddhas; (2) the contemplation was sustained over countless kalpas; (3) the conclusion was that bodhicitta alone is the best benefit; (4) *bde mchog* = buddhahood; (5) *bde blag* underlines ease and speed relative to other paths.

  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-7)

- **རབ་ཏུ་དགོངས་པར་མཛད་པ** — "thoroughly contemplated / carefully examined": KS gloss "sngon slob pa lam gyi dus na bskal ba du mar 'bad pas rab tu dgongs par mdzad" — during their time as trainees on the path, they exerted effort for many kalpas and thoroughly contemplated. "Rab tu" reinforces the depth and thoroughness; this is not casual examination.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-7)

- **འདི་ཉིད་ཕན་** — "this alone is beneficial": "phi ñid phan" — the "this alone" (ñid) is key: not just that bodhicitta is beneficial but that it *alone* among all possible approaches (*phan bdde thams cad kyi rgyu yin par*) is the cause of all benefit and happiness. KS makes the exclusivity explicit.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-7)

- **བདེ་བའི་མཆོག་** (bde ba'i mchog) — "the supreme happiness": KKP and all commentaries: "rdzogs pa'i byang chub" — perfect enlightenment/buddhahood. The Tibetan "bde mchog" fuses "supreme" (*mchog*) with "happiness/bliss" (*bde ba*); the buddhahood here is both the highest goal and a state of supreme well-being.

སྔར་གྱི་ལམ་གྱི་དུས་སུ་བསྐལ་བ་གྲངས་མེད་དུ་འབད་བརྩོན་ཆེན་པོས་གཟིགས་ཤིང་བརྟགས་མཁན་གྱི་ (= ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ཐོབ་ཀྱིས་མ་ཟིན་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སྔགས་རིམས་བྱིན་ཆེན་འདེབས་མཁན་གྱི) རྫོགས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ (= བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས) རྣམས་ཀྱིས། སེམས་ཅན་ལ་གནས་སྐབས་དང་མཐར་ཐུག་གི་ཕན་བདེ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་རྒྱུ་གང་ཡིན་མིན་རྩར་བཞག་མཐར། བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་འདི་ཉིད་ (= ཀུན་རྫོབ་སྨོན་འཇུག་གཉིས་ཀ) ཕན་བདེ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་རྒྱུ་ཡིན་པར་གཟིགས་ཤིང་། འདིས་ཚད་མེད་ཅིང་གཞལ་དུ་མེད་པའི་སྐྱེ་བོའི་ཚོགས་རྣམས་ (= སེམས་ཅན་གྲངས་མེད་པ) བདེ་མཆོག་ (= རྫོགས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང) བདེ་བླག་སྟེ་མྱུར་བ་ཉིད་དུ་ཐོབ་པར་བྱེད་དོ། །
