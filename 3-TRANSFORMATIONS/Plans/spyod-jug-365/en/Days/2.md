# Day 2 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: Benefits of the Mind of Enlightenment — Verses 1-4 to 1-6

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Recognizing the Preciousness of Your Life (Gratitude):** This life — with a healthy body, access to teachings, and freedom to practice — is incredibly rare. Most living beings do not have these conditions. Remembering this can help you feel real gratitude and use each day with more care and purpose.

*   **Understanding the Weakness of Good Habits (Urgency):** The small good actions you do each day are weak on their own. They appear briefly, like a flash of lightning, and disappear. Knowing this helps you understand why you need a deeper and more powerful motivation — the mind of enlightenment — to make your good efforts last and grow.

*   **Trusting the Power of the Mind of Enlightenment (Hope):** Negative habits and past harmful actions can feel very heavy and hard to change. But the mind of enlightenment — the sincere wish to awaken for the sake of all beings — has the power to overcome even the strongest negativity. This gives real hope that change is possible, no matter your past.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-4**

> དལ་འབྱོར་འདི་ནི་རྙེད་པར་ཤིན་ཏུ་དཀའ། །
> སྐྱེས་བུའི་དོན་སྒྲུབ་ཐོབ་པར་གྱུར་པ་ལ། །
> གལ་ཏེ་འདི་ལ་ཕན་པ་མ་བསྒྲུབས་ན། །
> ཕྱིས་འདི་ཡང་དག་འབྱོར་པར་ག་ལ་འགྱུར། །
>
> *This precious human life of leisure and opportunity is extremely difficult to find. Now I have this chance to accomplish my goals. If I do not use it to do good, how will I ever find such an opportunity again?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-4**

This verse opens the actual teaching of the path by urging the practitioner to recognize how rare this human life truly is. A life with freedom from suffering and the fortune to practice the teachings requires a specific combination of ethical discipline, generosity, and sincere aspiration as its causes — and such causes are extremely hard to bring together. To illustrate this rarity, the tradition compares it to a blind turtle rising from the ocean once every hundred years and finding its neck inside a small wooden yoke drifting on the surface — virtually impossible, yet obtaining this human life is said to be even harder. The great teacher Śāntideva urges that failing to use this rare opportunity for genuine good now will make it nearly impossible to find again.

---

#### **Verse 1-5**

> ཇི་ལྟར་མཚན་མོ་མུན་ནག་སྤྲིན་རུམ་ན། །
> གློག་འགྱུ་སྐད་ཅིག་རབ་སྣང་སྟོན་པ་ལྟར། །
> དེ་བཞིན་སངས་རྒྱས་མཐུ་ཡིས་བརྒྱ་ལམ་ན། །
> འཇིག་རྟེན་བསོད་ནམས་བློ་གྲོས་ཐང་འགའ་འབྱུང་། །
>
> *A flash of lightning on a dark, cloudy night reveals everything for a single instant. Similarly, the power of the Buddha occasionally inspires a brief moment of virtue in this world.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-5**

The image here is a dark, stormy night — a picture of how negative actions and suffering dominate ordinary life. Within that darkness, a flash of lightning appears for only a moment and is quickly gone. The verse says that a virtuous thought — the brief impulse to do good, to help others, or to practice the teachings — is exactly like that lightning flash. It appears rarely, lasts only an instant, and is not something ordinary beings produce on their own. Such a moment of goodness arises only through the compassionate power of the Buddhas shining into the minds of beings caught in the darkness of cyclic existence.

---

#### **Verse 1-6**

> དེ་ལྟས་དགེ་བ་ཉམ་ཆུང་ཉིད་ལ་རྟག །
> སྡིག་པ་སྟོབས་ཆེན་ཤིན་ཏུ་མི་བཟད་པ། །
> དེ་ནི་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མིན་པ། །
> དགེ་གཞན་གང་གིས་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་པར་འགྱུར། །
>
> *Therefore, ordinary virtue is always extremely weak. Negativity is immensely powerful and unbearable. Except for the perfect mind of enlightenment, what other virtue can overcome such great evil?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-6**

This verse draws out the clear conclusion from the lightning flash image: ordinary virtuous actions are perpetually weak because they arise only briefly and with great difficulty, while negativity is constant, fierce, and capable of producing intense suffering in future lives. No ordinary good deed — no matter how sincere — has the power on its own to overcome this heavy force of negativity. Only the mind of enlightenment, grounded in the wish to awaken for the sake of all beings and infused with the understanding of reality, possesses the strength to purify past harmful actions and cut off the causes of future ones entirely.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Pausing to Value This Day:** Today, I will take one minute in the morning to sit quietly and think: "I have a healthy body, a clear mind, and access to teachings that most beings never encounter." I will let this awareness settle before I start my day, so that my actions feel less automatic and more chosen.

*   **2. Noticing Moments of Goodness:** When I feel a sudden impulse to help someone, to be patient, or to say something kind today, I will notice it as a rare and precious moment. Instead of letting it pass, I will act on it immediately, remembering that such impulses are brief and not to be wasted.

*   **3. Returning to the Mind of Enlightenment When I Feel Heavy:** If I feel weighed down by past mistakes or bad habits today, I will not just try to do more ordinary good deeds. Instead, I will remind myself of the sincere wish to awaken for the benefit of all beings, because I know this motivation is the only force strong enough to truly turn things around.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Leisure and Opportunity (དལ་འབྱོར།):** This term refers to a human life that has two special qualities: freedom from conditions that prevent practice, and the presence of conditions that support it. The tradition lists eighteen specific factors — eight freedoms and ten endowments — that together make genuine spiritual practice possible. Such a life is considered rarer and more valuable than any worldly treasure.

*   **Goals of a Person (སྐྱེས་བུའི་དོན།):** This phrase points to the two main aims of a meaningful human life: gaining a better future existence through ethical living, and ultimately achieving complete liberation and awakening. The verse teaches that this rare human life is the perfect vehicle for reaching both goals, and that failing to use it wisely is a profound loss.

*   **The Power of the Buddha (སངས་རྒྱས་མཐུ།):** This refers to the blessings and compassionate influence that the Buddhas continuously radiate toward all beings. The verse uses this concept to explain why virtuous thoughts arise at all in ordinary minds — not through the person's own strength, but through the grace and power of those who have fully awakened.

*   **The Mind of Enlightenment (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས།):** This is the sincere wish to attain complete awakening for the sake of freeing all living beings from suffering. The verse presents it as the supreme remedy — the one force capable of overcoming the heaviest negativity. The tradition distinguishes two aspects: the aspiring wish to awaken, and the active engagement in the path that leads there.

*   **Negativity (སྡིག་པ།):** In this context, negativity refers to harmful actions — physical, verbal, and mental — that create the causes for suffering in future lives. The verse emphasizes that these forces are not minor inconveniences but are extremely powerful and capable of producing intense, long-lasting suffering. This is why only the mind of enlightenment, and not ordinary good deeds, can fully overcome them.
