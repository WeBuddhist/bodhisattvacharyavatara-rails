## Chapter One: Explaining the Benefits of Bodhicitta ^1-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-1]]

To the Sugatas, endowed with the Dharmakāya, along with their heirs,
And to all who are worthy of homage, I respectfully bow down;
Then, entering into the vows of the heirs of the Sugatas,
I shall explain it concisely in accordance with scripture. ^1-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-2]]

There is nothing said here that has not appeared before,
Nor do I have any skill in composition;
Therefore, I have no thought of benefiting others,
I have composed this to cultivate my own mind. ^1-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-3]]

In order to cultivate virtue, the force of my faith
Will for a time increase because of this;
And if others, equal in fortune to myself,
Should see these words, they may be meaningful. ^1-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-4]]

These freedoms and advantages are extremely hard to find;
Since the attainment that fulfills human purpose has been found,
If one does not accomplish what is beneficial in this life,
How will such a perfect opportunity arise again later? ^1-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-5]]

Just as on a dark night in the womb of clouds
A flash of lightning brilliantly illuminates for an instant,
In the same way, by the power of the Buddha, occasionally
Worldly intelligence for merit arises for a moment. ^1-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-6]]

Thus virtue is perpetually feeble,
While evil is extremely powerful and unbearable;
If it were not for the mind of perfect awakening,
What other virtue could overcome it? ^1-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-7]]

The Lords of Sages, who have contemplated for many eons,
Have seen that this alone is beneficial;
By this, immeasurable multitudes of beings
Will easily attain the supreme bliss. ^1-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-8]]

Those who wish to destroy the hundreds of sufferings of existence,
Who wish to dispel the unhappiness of sentient beings,
And who wish to experience hundreds of great joys,
Should never give up the mind of awakening. ^1-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-9]]

If the mind of awakening is generated, in an instant,
The wretched ones bound in the prison of cyclic existence
Will be called children of the Sugatas,
And will become worthy of reverence by the world's gods and humans. ^1-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-10]]

Like the supreme form of the gold-making elixir,
It takes this impure body and transforms it
Into the priceless jewel of a Conqueror's body;
Therefore, firmly hold to this so-called mind of awakening. ^1-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-11]]

Since the sole captain of beings, with limitless intellect,
Has thoroughly examined it and found it to be highly precious,
Those who wish to be separated from the states of wandering
Should perfectly and firmly hold the precious mind of awakening. ^1-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-12]]

All other virtues are just like the plantain tree:
Having borne their fruit, they are exhausted;
But the tree of the mind of awakening constantly
Bears fruit, is not exhausted, and continually grows. ^1-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-13]]

Even if one has committed extremely unbearable evils,
Just as one relies on a hero when in great fear,
By relying on it, one is liberated in an instant;
Why do conscientious beings not rely upon it? ^1-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-14]]

Like the fire at the end of time, it
Certainly burns up great evils in a single instant;
Its immeasurable benefits
Were explained to Sudhana by the wise Lord Maitreya. ^1-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-15]]

In brief, that mind of awakening
Should be understood as being of two types:
The mind aspiring to awakening, and
The mind entering awakening itself. ^1-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-16]]

Just as one understands the difference
Between wishing to go and actually going,
Likewise, the wise should understand the difference
Between these two in their respective order. ^1-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-17]]

From the mind aspiring to awakening,
Great fruits arise while in cyclic existence, but
Unlike the mind entering awakening,
A continuous stream of merit does not arise. ^1-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-18]]

From the moment that,
In order to completely free the infinite realms of beings,
With a mind that does not turn back,
One perfectly takes up that mind, ^1-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-19]]

From then on, even while asleep
Or inattentive, a force of merit,
Uninterrupted and manifold,
Continually arises, equal to the sky. ^1-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-20]]

Along with the reasoning for this,
In the Questions of Subāhu,
For the sake of beings of lesser inclination,
It was taught by the Tathāgata himself. ^1-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-21]]

"I shall cure just the headaches
Of sentient beings"—even if one just thinks this,
Possessing that beneficial intention
Endows one with immeasurable merit. ^1-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-22]]

The discomforts of each and every sentient being,
Immeasurable as they are, one wishes to dispel;
And for each, immeasurable good qualities
One wishes to accomplish—what need is there to mention? ^1-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-23]]

Whether it be a father or even a mother,
Who has such a beneficial mind?
Do the gods or the sages,
Or even Brahmā have this? ^1-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-24]]

If, for those very sentient beings in the past,
Such a mind, even for their own sake,
Was not dreamt of even in a dream,
How could it arise for the sake of others? ^1-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-25]]

Even for their own sake, in others
This intention for the sake of sentient beings does not arise.
This extraordinary jewel of a mind
Is born as an unprecedented wonder. ^1-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-26]]

The cause of joy for all transmigrating beings,
The medicine for the suffering of sentient beings—
The merit of that jewel-like mind,
How could it ever be measured? ^1-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-27]]

If merely intending to be beneficial
Is more exalted than making offerings to the Buddhas,
Of all sentient beings without exception,
What need is there to mention striving for their happiness and welfare? ^1-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-28]]

Although having the wish to abandon suffering,
They run directly toward suffering itself.
Although desiring happiness, out of ignorance,
They destroy their own happiness as if it were an enemy. ^1-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-29]]

For those who are destitute of happiness
And who have many sufferings,
It satisfies them with all happiness
And cuts through all sufferings, ^1-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-30]]

And dispels even their ignorance.
Where is there a virtue comparable to that?
Where is there a friend like that?
Where is there even merit like that? ^1-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-31]]

If whoever repays a benefit done to them
Is worthy of some praise,
The Bodhisattva who does good without being asked,
What need is there to mention? ^1-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-32]]

One who provides regular food to a few beings,
Giving merely a meal for a brief instant,
Or satisfying them for half a day with contempt,
Is honored by people as a doer of virtue. ^1-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-33]]

To limitless numbers of sentient beings for a long time,
The unsurpassable bliss of the Sugatas,
Which fulfills all the wishes in their minds,
What need is there to mention constantly giving? ^1-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-34]]

If anyone toward such a benefactor, a Child of the Conquerors,
Generates an evil thought, that one
For eons equal to the number of evil thoughts generated
Will abide in hell, so the Capable One has taught. ^1-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-35]]

But if anyone gives rise to a mind of deep faith,
Its fruit increases even more than that.
Even in great adversity for the Children of the Conquerors,
Non-virtue does not arise, and virtue naturally increases. ^1-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-36]]

In whomever that supreme, precious mind
Has been born, I bow to their body, and
To those whom even doing harm connects one to happiness,
I go for refuge to those sources of happiness. ^1-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^1-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, this is the first chapter, called Explaining the Benefits of the Awakening Mind. ^1-a

## Chapter Two: Confession of Sins ^2-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-1]]

In order to take hold of that precious mind,
To the Tathāgatas and the holy Dharma,
The stainless Jewel, and the Children of the Buddhas,
Who are oceans of qualities, I make excellent offerings. ^2-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-2]]

However many flowers and fruits there are,
And whatever kinds of medicine there are,
However many jewels there are in the world,
And whatever pure and pleasing waters there are, ^2-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-3]]

Jewel mountains, and likewise
Forest regions, secluded and joyful,
Trees adorned and fully decked with flowers,
And trees with branches bowing down with good fruit, ^2-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-4]]

Fragrances in the worlds of gods and others,
Incense, wish-fulfilling trees, and jewel trees,
Various crops that grow without being plowed,
And other ornaments worthy of being offered, ^2-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-5]]

Lakes and ponds adorned with lotuses,
Endowed with the highly melodious and pleasing calls of wild geese,
Extending to the limits of the infinite sphere of space,
All those things that are unowned, ^2-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-6]]

Taking them with my mind, to the Capable Ones, the supreme of beings,
And to their Children, I make excellent offerings;
May those holy objects of offering, the ones of great compassion,
Think of me with love and accept these things of mine. ^2-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-7]]

I am without merit and extremely destitute,
And I have no other wealth for offering.
Therefore, may the Protectors whose thoughts are for the welfare of others,
By their own power, accept this for my sake. ^2-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-8]]

To the Conquerors and their Children, I
Offer my entire body forever.
Supreme Beings, please accept me completely;
With devotion I will become your subject. ^2-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-9]]

Because I am completely accepted by you,
I will not fear in cyclic existence and will benefit sentient beings.
I will perfectly overcome my previous negative deeds,
And I will not commit other negative deeds henceforth. ^2-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-10]]

In a bathhouse that is exceedingly fragrant,
Arranged with a crystal floor, clear and shining,
Endowed with pleasing pillars of blazing jewels,
And a canopy of radiant pearls spread above, ^2-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-11]]

To the Tathāgatas and their Children,
With many jeweled vases of scented water,
Beautifully and perfectly filled, alongside songs
And various music, I offer a bath. ^2-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-12]]

With garments that are unequaled,
Clean and infused with supreme fragrances, I dry their bodies.
Then to them, excellently dyed
Holy robes, exceedingly fragrant, I offer. ^2-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-13]]

With various fine garments, thin and soft,
And with hundreds of those supreme ornaments,
The noble Samantabhadra, Mañjuśrī,
Avalokiteśvara, and others, I will adorn. ^2-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-14]]

With fragrances whose scent wafts throughout the three thousandfold world systems,
By these supreme perfumes, the bodies of all the Lords of Capable Ones,
Like purified and polished refined gold,
Radiant and blazing, I will anoint. ^2-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-15]]

To the Lords of Capable Ones, the supreme objects of offering, with pleasing
Mandāra flowers and lotuses,
Utpalas, and all fragrant things,
And beautifully strung, pleasing garlands, I make offerings. ^2-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-16]]

Supreme incense with a captivating, pervasive fragrance,
Masses of clouds of its vapor I also offer to them;
With meals comprising various foods and drinks,
Divine feasts I will also offer to them. ^2-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-17]]

Arranged in rows of golden lotuses,
Jeweled lamps I will also offer.
On the cleared ground anointed with incense,
I shall scatter pleasing loose flowers. ^2-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-18]]

Immeasurable palaces possessing pleasing songs of praise,
Beautifully blazing with hanging ornaments of pearls and jewels,
Which have become ornaments of boundless space, these too
I will offer to those whose nature is compassion. ^2-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-19]]

Beautiful jeweled parasols with golden handles,
Possessing pleasing forms of ornaments all around,
Upright, well-shaped, and lovely to behold,
I will always offer to the Lords of Capable Ones. ^2-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-20]]

Besides those, masses of offerings,
Accompanied by pleasing, sweet sounds of music,
Which soothe the suffering of sentient beings,
May clouds of these remain individually. ^2-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-21]]

Upon all the rare and supreme holy Dharma,
Stūpas, and images,
May a rain of jewels, flowers, and the like,
Fall without interruption. ^2-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-22]]

Just as Mañjuśrī and others
Made offerings to the Victorious Ones,
Likewise, I make offerings to the Tathāgatas,
The Protectors along with their Children. ^2-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-23]]

To those who are oceans of qualities, I
Offer praise with oceans of limbs of melodious praise.
May sweet clouds of songs of praise
Certainly arise everywhere for them. ^2-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-24]]

To all the Buddhas who have gone in the three times,
Along with the Dharma and the Supreme Assembly,
With bodies as numerous as all the atoms in the fields,
Bowing down, I pay homage. ^2-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-25]]

To the bases of the awakening mind,
And to the stūpas, I pay homage.
To the preceptors and likewise the teachers,
And to the supreme practitioners, I pay homage. ^2-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-26]]

Until reaching the essence of awakening,
I go for refuge to the Buddhas;
To the Dharma and the Bodhisattvas'
Assembly, likewise, I go for refuge. ^2-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-27]]

To those who reside in all directions,
The perfect Buddhas and Bodhisattvas,
Who possess great compassion,
Joining my palms, I make this request: ^2-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-28]]

Since beginningless cyclic existence,
In this life or in others,
Whatever non-virtue I have unwittingly committed,
Or caused to be committed, ^2-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-29]]

And, overcome by the confusion of ignorance,
Whatever I have rejoiced in,
Having seen these mistakes,
I confess them to the Protectors from the depths of my heart. ^2-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-30]]

To the Three Jewels,
To parents, gurus, or others,
Through afflictions, with body, speech,
And mind, whatever harm I have done, ^2-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-31]]

Defiled by many faults,
Whatever non-virtues I, a sinner,
Have committed that are extremely unbearable,
I confess them all to the Guides. ^2-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-32]]

Before my non-virtues are purified,
I may meet my death.
So that I may definitely be freed from them,
I pray you, protect me swiftly! ^2-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-33]]

This Lord of Death cannot be trusted;
He does not wait for what is done or not done.
Whether we are sick or not sick,
None can trust this fleeting life. ^2-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-34]]

I must leave everything behind and depart.
Since I did not understand this,
For the sake of friends and enemies,
I have committed various kinds of non-virtue. ^2-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-35]]

My enemies will cease to be,
My friends will cease to be,
I too will cease to be,
Likewise, all will cease to be. ^2-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-36]]

Like experiencing a dream,
Whatever things I have enjoyed
Will become mere objects of memory;
All that has passed will not be seen again. ^2-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-37]]

Even during this brief life,
Many friends and enemies have passed away.
But the unbearable non-virtues committed for their sake
Remain right before me. ^2-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-38]]

That my existence is so fleeting,
Since I did not realize this,
Through ignorance, attachment, and hatred,
I have committed many kinds of non-virtue. ^2-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-39]]

Pausing neither day nor night,
This life is constantly slipping away,
And since no extra time is added from elsewhere,
Why would someone like me not die? ^2-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-40]]

While I am lying in my bed,
Although surrounded by all my relatives and friends,
The feelings of my life being severed
Will be experienced by me alone. ^2-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-41]]

When seized by the messengers of Yama,
What good are relatives, what good are friends?
At that time, merit alone is a protection,
But I have not relied upon it. ^2-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-42]]

O Protectors, I, being careless,
Unaware of such danger as this,
For the sake of this impermanent life,
Have committed many non-virtues. ^2-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-43]]

Even a person being led today to the place
Where their limbs will be amputated is terrified;
With a dry mouth, dreadful eyes, and the like,
Their appearance is transformed, different from before. ^2-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-44]]

Seized by the messengers of Yama
With their terrifying physical appearances,
And struck by the great terrifying disease,
What need to mention my utter misery? ^2-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-45]]

"Who from this great terror
Will perfectly protect me?"
Opening my terrified eyes wide,
I will search the four directions for protection. ^2-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-46]]

Seeing no protection in the four directions,
I will then become completely enveloped in despair.
If there is no protection in that place,
What shall I do at that time? ^2-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-47]]

Therefore, in the Victors, the protectors of beings,
Who strive for the purpose of protecting beings,
The mighty ones who clear away all fear,
From today onward, I take refuge. ^2-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-48]]

In the Dharma that they have realized,
Which clears away the fears of saṃsāra,
And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas,
Likewise, I perfectly take refuge. ^2-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-49]]

Terrified by fear,
I offer myself to Samantabhadra;
To Mañjuśrī, I myself
Shall offer my body. ^2-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-50]]

Whose compassionate deeds are unerring,
To the protector Avalokiteśvara as well,
With an anguished cry, I call out:
"Please protect me, a sinner!" ^2-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-51]]

To the noble Ākāśagarbha,
And to Kṣitigarbha,
And to all the greatly compassionate protectors,
Seeking refuge, I call out from my heart. ^2-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-52]]

Seeing whom, the messengers of Yama
And other malicious beings
Flee in terror to the four directions,
I take refuge in the Vajra-wielder. ^2-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-53]]

Previously I transgressed your commands,
But now, seeing great fear,
I take refuge in you, so
Please swiftly clear away this fear. ^2-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-54]]

Even if frightened by an ordinary illness,
One must act according to the doctor's words;
Of a hundred faults such as attachment and the like,
When constantly stricken by their disease, what need to mention? ^2-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-55]]

Even by just one of them, in Jambudvīpa
All the people dwelling there are destroyed;
And if other medicines to cure them
Are not found anywhere in all directions, ^2-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-56]]

In that case, of the omniscient physician
Who draws out all pain—
The intention not to act according to his commands
Is utterly foolish and an object of reproach. ^2-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-57]]

Even on a small, ordinary precipice,
If one must remain careful,
Of falling a thousand yojanas
On a long-lasting precipice, what need to mention? ^2-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-58]]

Thinking, "Today alone I will not die,"
It is not right to remain at ease.
The time when I will cease to be
Will undoubtedly occur. ^2-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-59]]

Who has given me fearlessness?
How will I definitely escape from this?
If I will undoubtedly cease to be,
How can my mind remain at ease? ^2-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-60]]

From what was previously experienced and perished,
What remains for me?
By being deeply attached to that,
I have transgressed the gurus' commands. ^2-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-61]]

This lifetime, and likewise
Relatives and friends having entirely abandoned,
If I must go somewhere alone,
What is the point of all friends and non-friends? ^2-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-62]]

From non-virtue suffering arises.
"How will I definitely escape from that?"
Constantly, day and night, by me
Just this alone is right to be pondered. ^2-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-63]]

Because I am ignorant and foolish,
Natural misdeeds or
Whatever proscribed negative deeds—
Whichever ones I have done, ^2-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-64]]

Directly in the presence of the protectors,
With palms joined and a mind terrified of suffering,
Bowing down again and again,
I will confess all of them. ^2-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-65]]

I beseech the guides to accept my negative deeds
As being faults.
Since this is not good,
From now on I will not do them. ^2-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the second chapter, Confession of Negative Deeds. ^2-a

## Chapter Three: Completely Taking Hold of Bodhicitta ^3-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-1]]

Of the lower realms of all sentient beings,
In the virtue that relieves suffering, and
In suffering beings abiding in happiness,
With joy I rejoice. ^3-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-2]]

In the accumulated virtue that becomes the cause of awakening,
In that I rejoice.
From the suffering of samsara for embodied beings,
In the definite liberation I rejoice.
In the awakening of the protectors and
In the stages of the Conquerors' sons too, I rejoice. ^3-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-3]]

Bringing happiness to all sentient beings,
In the ocean of virtue of generating the awakening mind, and
In the deeds that benefit sentient beings,
With joy I rejoice. ^3-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-4]]

To the Buddhas of all directions,
With joined palms I pray:
For sentient beings confused in the darkness of suffering,
I beseech you to light the lamp of Dharma. ^3-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-5]]

To the Conquerors wishing to pass into nirvana,
With joined palms I pray:
So as not to leave these wanderers in blindness,
I beseech you to remain for countless eons. ^3-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-6]]

Having done all these things in this way,
Whatever virtue has been accumulated by me,
By that, of all sentient beings,
May all suffering be dispelled. ^3-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-7]]

As long as wanderers are sick,
Until they are cured of illness,
The medicine and the doctor themselves, and
Their nurse, may I become. ^3-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-8]]

Bringing down a rain of food and drink,
May I dispel the suffering of hunger and thirst.
During the intermediate eon of famine,
May I myself become food and drink. ^3-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-9]]

For sentient beings who are destitute and poor,
May I become an inexhaustible treasure, and
As all the various kinds of needed requisites,
May I remain closely present before them. ^3-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-10]]

My body and likewise my wealth, and
All virtues of the three times,
In order to accomplish the welfare of all sentient beings,
I shall give away without regret. ^3-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-11]]

By giving up everything, nirvana is attained,
And my mind seeks to accomplish nirvana.
Since I must part with everything at once,
It is best given to sentient beings. ^3-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-12]]

By me, to all embodied beings,
Since this body has been given for their pleasure,
Continually killing, abusing, or
Striking and so forth, let them do as they please. ^3-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-13]]

Whether they play with my body, or
Use it as a source of ridicule and jest,
Since I have already given this body away,
What is the use of cherishing it? ^3-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-14]]

That which does not bring harm to them,
Let them do whatever action it may be.
Whenever anyone focuses on me,
May it never be meaningless for anyone. ^3-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-15]]

Focusing upon me, for those whose
Minds of anger or faith arise,
May that very thing always become for them
The cause for accomplishing all their goals. ^3-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-16]]

Those who speak ill of me, or
Others who cause me harm, or
Likewise those who mock me,
May they all have the fortune of awakening. ^3-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-17]]

May I be a protector for those without a protector,
A guide for those who travel on the path,
And a boat for those who wish to cross over,
May I become a ship and a bridge. ^3-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-18]]

An island for those seeking an island,
A bed for those desiring a bed,
And for embodied beings who desire a servant,
May I become a servant for them all. ^3-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-19]]

A wish-fulfilling jewel and a fine vase,
An accomplished knowledge-mantra and great medicine,
And wish-granting trees,
May I become a cow of plenty for embodied beings. ^3-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-20]]

Just like earth and the other great elements,
And like space, permanently,
For limitless sentient beings,
May I also be the ground of manifold sustenance. ^3-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-21]]

Likewise, extending to the limits of space,
For the realm of sentient beings in all ways,
Until they all attain nirvana,
May I also be the cause of their sustenance. ^3-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-22]]

Just as the previous Sugatas
Generated the awakening mind,
And in the trainings of the bodhisattvas,
Just as they progressively abided, ^3-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-23]]

Likewise, for the benefit of wandering beings,
I shall generate the awakening mind,
And likewise, in the trainings too,
I shall progressively train. ^3-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-24]]

The intelligent ones who in this way
Have excellently taken hold of the awakening mind,
In order to make it flourish further,
Should extol the mind like this: ^3-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-25]]

Today my life is fruitful;
I have well obtained a human existence.
Today I am born into the family of the Buddhas;
Today I have become a child of the Buddhas. ^3-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-26]]

Now I must, whatever happens,
Undertake actions in accord with the family,
And upon this flawless, noble family,
Bring no stain—thus shall I act. ^3-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-27]]

Like a blind man from a heap of refuse
Finding a precious jewel,
Just so, by some coincidence,
This awakening mind has been born in me. ^3-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-28]]

Destroying the Lord of Death for wandering beings,
It is the supreme nectar.
Dispelling the poverty of wandering beings,
It is the inexhaustible treasure. ^3-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-29]]

Completely pacifying the illnesses of wandering beings,
It is the supreme medicine.
For wandering beings wandering and weary on the path of existence,
It is the tree of rest. ^3-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-30]]

For all wandering beings, from the lower realms
It is the universal bridge that liberates.
Dispelling the torment of the afflictions of wandering beings,
It is the rising moon of the mind. ^3-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-31]]

The dimness of ignorance of wandering beings,
It is the great sun that uproots it.
From churning the milk of the true Dharma,
It is the essence of butter that has emerged. ^3-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-32]]

For wandering beings, the guests traveling the path of existence,
Who wish to experience the enjoyment of happiness,
This is stationed nearby as the supreme happiness,
Satisfying the great guests, sentient beings. ^3-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-33]]

Today, by me, in the presence of all the protectors,
Wandering beings to Sugatahood itself, and
Until then, to happiness, have been invited as guests;
Let gods, asuras, and others rejoice! ^3-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the third chapter, called "Fully Taking Hold of the Awakening Mind." ^3-a

## Chapter Four: Teaching Conscientiousness ^4-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-1]]

The son of the Conquerors, in that way,
Having very firmly taken hold of the awakening mind,
Unwaveringly and always,
Should strive not to transgress the trainings. ^4-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-2]]

Whatever is undertaken recklessly,
Or whatever is not well examined,
Even if a promise is made regarding it,
It is appropriate to examine whether to do it or abandon it. ^4-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-3]]

By the Buddhas and their sons,
Whatever has been examined with great wisdom,
And which I myself have examined again and again,
What is there to postpone regarding it? ^4-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-4]]

If, having promised in that way,
I do not accomplish it in action,
Having deceived all those sentient beings,
What will my destiny become? ^4-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-5]]

Even regarding a slight, ordinary thing,
Having intended in one's mind to give it,
Whatever person does not give it,
If it is said that they will become a hungry ghost, ^4-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-6]]

To unsurpassable happiness,
Having invited them from the depths of my heart,
If I deceive all wandering beings,
How will I go to the higher realms? ^4-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-7]]

Even though some people give up the awakening mind,
As for their being liberated,
The way of karma is inconceivable;
Only the Omniscient One knows. ^4-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-8]]

For a bodhisattva, this
Is the heaviest among the downfalls;
Thus, if this happens,
The benefit of all sentient beings is diminished. ^4-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-9]]

Whoever else, even for a mere moment,
Creates an obstacle to this merit,
Because the benefit of sentient beings is diminished,
Their lower rebirths will be limitless. ^4-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-10]]

Even the happiness of a single sentient being,
If I destroy it, I will be ruined;
Limitless as the entirety of space,
Destroying the happiness of embodied beings, what need is there to mention it? ^4-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-11]]

Thus, with powerful downfalls,
And the powerful awakening mind,
If they alternate in saṃsāra,
It will take a long time to attain the grounds. ^4-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-12]]

Therefore, just as I have promised,
I shall respectfully accomplish it.
If from today I do not make effort,
I will go lower and lower. ^4-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-13]]

Benefiting all sentient beings,
Even though countless Buddhas have passed away,
Due to my own faults, I
Have not become an object of their healing. ^4-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-14]]

If I still act in this way,
Again and again it will be the same:
In the lower realms, illness and bondage,
Cutting and cleaving, and so forth, I will experience. ^4-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-15]]

The appearance of the Tathāgatas,
Faith, obtaining a human body,
And the fitness to practice virtue, like this,
Since they are rare, when will they be obtained? ^4-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-16]]

A day without illness like this,
With food and without harm, nevertheless,
Life is deceptive in a moment,
And the body is like something borrowed for a brief moment. ^4-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-17]]

With behavior like mine,
Even a human body will not be obtained.
If a human body is not obtained,
There is only sin and no virtue. ^4-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-18]]

Even when having the fortune to practice virtue,
If I do not practice virtue,
Completely bewildered by the suffering of the lower realms,
At that time, what will I be able to do? ^4-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-19]]

Having not practiced virtues,
And having accumulated sins,
For even a hundred million eons,
Even the sound of the happy realms will not be heard. ^4-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-20]]

For that very reason, the Bhagavan
Of a yoke drifting on the great ocean,
Like a turtle's neck entering the hole,
Declared that the human state is extremely difficult to obtain. ^4-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-21]]

Even by the sin committed in a single moment,
If one dwells in Avīci for an eon,
Due to the sins accumulated in beginningless saṃsāra,
What need is there to mention not going to the happy realms? ^4-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-22]]

Having experienced just that much,
One will not be liberated from it,
Because while experiencing it like this,
Other sins are fully generated. ^4-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-23]]

Having found such leisure,
If I do not habituate myself to virtue,
There is no deception other than this,
And there is no bewilderment other than this. ^4-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-24]]

If, having understood this,
I am still lazy later out of bewilderment,
At the time of my death,
Great sorrow will arise. ^4-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-25]]

If the unbearable fire of hell for a long time
Burns my body,
Due to the blazing fire of unbearable regret,
My mind will doubtless be tormented. ^4-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-26]]

The state of benefit that is extremely difficult to find,
Having found it by some chance,
While I myself am possessed of understanding,
If I am led back again into that hell, ^4-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-27]]

As if bewildered by a mantra,
I am reduced to having no mind here.
Even I do not know what bewilders me;
What is there inside me? ^4-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-28]]

The enemies of hatred, craving, and so forth,
Do not have arms, legs, and so forth,
And though neither brave nor wise, how is it
That they have made me like a slave? ^4-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-29]]

While dwelling in my mind,
Doing harm to me at their pleasure,
Patiently not being angry even at that,
Is inappropriate patience and an object of reproach. ^4-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-30]]

Even if the gods and demigods
All rose up against me as enemies,
Even they would not be able
To lead and place me in the fire of Avīci. ^4-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-31]]

This powerful enemy of the afflictions,
Upon meeting which, even Mount Meru
Does not remain even as ashes,
Casts me there in a single moment. ^4-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-32]]

My enemies, the afflictions,
Are long-lasting, without beginning or end;
All other enemies
Are not capable of lasting for a long time like that. ^4-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-33]]

If amiably served and honored,
All others bring benefit and happiness,
But if the afflictions are served,
They instead bring suffering and harm. ^4-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-34]]

Thus, these long-lasting continuous enemies,
The sole cause of the great increase of the multitude of harms,
If they definitely take up residence in my heart,
How could I be fearless and joyful in saṃsāra? ^4-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-35]]

The guards of the prison of saṃsāra,
These which become the executioners that kill in hell and so forth—
If, in the net of attachment residing in the mind,
They remain, how can there be happiness for me? ^4-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-36]]

Thus, as long as I do not manifestly
And certainly destroy this enemy, I will not forsake my effort here.
When angered by even a slight temporary harm,
Those full of pride do not sleep until they have destroyed it. ^4-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-37]]

Those afflicted ones who suffer and naturally die,
When arrayed in battle, even those intensely desiring to forcefully destroy them,
Having ignored the suffering of being struck by arrows, spears, and weapons,
If they do not turn back and flee without accomplishing the goal, ^4-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-38]]

The constant cause of all suffering,
Today, as I strive to destroy these definite natural enemies,
Whatever becomes the cause of hundreds of sufferings,
Needless to say, will not make me despair and lose heart. ^4-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-39]]

Even the scars inflicted by meaningless enemies
Are worn like ornaments on the body; so
For me, striving perfectly to accomplish the great purpose,
Why should suffering be a harm? ^4-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-40]]

Fishermen, outcastes, farmers, and so forth,
Thinking only of their own livelihood,
Endure the harms of cold, heat, and so forth;
Why should someone like me not endure them for the happiness of wandering beings? ^4-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-41]]

Reaching the ends of space in the ten directions,
To liberate wandering beings from afflictions,
When I promised this, I myself
Was not liberated from afflictions. ^4-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-42]]

Without knowing my own measure,
How is speaking thus not crazy?
Thus, in destroying afflictions,
I will never turn back. ^4-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-43]]

I will be attached to this,
And, holding a grudge, I will engage in battle;
Except for the afflictions of such a kind,
That destroy afflictions. ^4-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-44]]

Whether I am burned and killed,
Or even if my head is cut off, it is better;
In any case, to the afflictive
Enemies I will not bow down. ^4-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-45]]

Even when ordinary enemies are expelled from the country,
They reside in other countries and take possession,
And, having regained their strength, they return from there;
But the way of the afflictive enemies is not like that. ^4-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-46]]

Afflictions, afflictions, abandoned by the eye of wisdom,
Cleared from my mind, where will they go?
Where will they stay to return and harm me?
It is just that I, of weak mind, lack effort. ^4-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-47]]

Afflictions do not reside in the objects, nor in the sense faculties, nor in between.
Nor elsewhere; if so, where do they reside and harm all wandering beings?
They are like an illusion; therefore, banish fear in the heart and rely on effort for the sake of knowledge.
Why should I be harmed meaninglessly in hells and so forth? ^4-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-48]]

Having pondered thus, for the sake of accomplishing
The trainings just as explained, I will make effort.
If one does not listen to the doctor's words, how can
A patient who needs to be cured by medicines be healed? ^4-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the fourth chapter, called the Teaching on Conscientiousness. ^4-a

## Chapter Five: Guarding Introspection ^5-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-1]]

Those who wish to guard the training
Must strictly guard the mind;
If this mind is not guarded,
One will not be able to guard the training. ^5-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-2]]

Letting the elephant of the mind wander loose
Causes harm in the Avīci hell;
An untamed, maddened elephant
Does not cause such harm here. ^5-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-3]]

With the rope of mindfulness on all sides,
If the elephant of the mind is tightly bound,
All fears will cease to exist,
And all virtues will come into one's hands. ^5-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-4]]

Tigers, lions, elephants, bears,
Snakes, and all enemies,
The guardians of the hells for sentient beings,
Evil sorcerers, and likewise rākṣasas— ^5-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-5]]

By binding this mind alone,
All of them will be bound;
By taming this mind alone,
All of them will be tamed. ^5-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-6]]

Thus, all fears and
Immeasurable sufferings too,
That they arise from the mind
Was taught by the Speaker of Truth Himself. ^5-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-7]]

The weapons of the hells for sentient beings,
Who intentionally created them?
Who created the burning iron ground?
From what did those masses of fire arise? ^5-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-8]]

All such things
Were taught by the Sage to be the sinful mind.
Thus, in the three worlds,
There is no fear other than the mind. ^5-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-9]]

If by eliminating the poverty of wandering beings,
The perfection of generosity is achieved,
Since there are still destitute beings, how did the past
Saviors achieve the perfection? ^5-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-10]]

All possessions together with their fruits,
By the mind giving them to all people,
The perfection of generosity is taught;
Therefore, it is simply the mind itself. ^5-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-11]]

Where could fish and so forth
Be driven away so that they are not killed?
From attaining the mind of renunciation,
The perfection of moral discipline is explained. ^5-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-12]]

Unruly sentient beings are like space;
They can never be entirely overcome.
If this single mind of anger is overcome,
It is equivalent to overcoming all those enemies. ^5-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-13]]

To cover this surface of the earth with leather,
Where could that much leather be found?
With just the leather for the soles of one's shoes,
It is equivalent to covering the entire surface of the earth. ^5-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-14]]

Likewise, external phenomena too,
Cannot be turned back by me,
But I will turn back this mind of mine;
What need is there to turn back others? ^5-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-15]]

Whatever result is generated by a single clear mind,
Such as Brahmā and so forth,
The results of body and speech,
Because of weak conduct, are not like that. ^5-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-16]]

All recitations and ascetic practices,
Even if practiced for a long time,
If done with a mind distracted elsewhere,
Are taught by the Knower of Reality to be meaningless. ^5-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-17]]

If someone, regarding the supreme principal of Dharma,
Does not know this secret of the mind,
Even though they wish to attain happiness and overcome suffering,
They will wander meaninglessly and in vain. ^5-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-18]]

Therefore, this mind of mine,
I shall hold it well and guard it well.
Except for the discipline of guarding the mind,
What is the use of many disciplines? ^5-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-19]]

When dwelling in the midst of a wild crowd,
Just as one carefully protects a wound,
So too, when dwelling in the midst of bad people,
One should always guard this wound of the mind. ^5-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-20]]

Because of the minor suffering of a wound,
If one who is afraid carefully protects the wound,
Those who fear the Crushing and Destroying Hells,
Why do they not guard the wound of the mind? ^5-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-21]]

If one acts with such conduct,
Whether dwelling in the midst of bad people,
Or even dwelling in the midst of women,
The steadfast observer of vows will not decline. ^5-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-22]]

My gain and honor,
And body and livelihood had better perish,
And other virtues had better decline,
But the mind must never decline. ^5-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-23]]

To those who wish to guard their minds,
Mindfulness and introspection,
Guard them both with all your effort!
Thus I join my palms in prayer. ^5-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-24]]

People who are disturbed by illness,
Are powerless in all activities;
Likewise, when the mind is disturbed by ignorance,
One is powerless in all activities. ^5-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-25]]

Of one who has a mind without introspection,
Even hearing, thinking, and meditating,
Like water in a leaky vase,
Will not remain in memory. ^5-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-26]]

Those who are learned and have faith,
And many who dedicate themselves to effort,
Because they incur the fault of lacking introspection,
Will become tainted by downfalls. ^5-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-27]]

The thieves of lacking introspection,
Following upon the decline of mindfulness,
Even when merits are accumulated,
Robbed by thieves, one goes to the lower realms. ^5-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-28]]

This host of affliction-thieves,
Seeks for an opportunity;
And having found an opportunity, steals virtue,
And destroys even the life of the happy realms. ^5-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-29]]

Therefore, mindfulness from the door of the mind,
Should never be let go.
Even if it departs, recalling the harms of the lower realms,
It should be closely maintained. ^5-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-30]]

From associating with gurus,
And the instructions of preceptors,
In the fortunate who act with respect out of fear,
Mindfulness is easily generated. ^5-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-31]]

The Buddhas and Bodhisattvas,
Are endowed with unobstructed vision everywhere.
"In the presence of them all,
I am always dwelling." ^5-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-32]]

Thinking like this, with shame,
Respect, and fear, one should dwell.
Thus, the recollection of the Buddhas,
Will also arise in one again and again. ^5-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-33]]

When mindfulness from the door of the mind,
Remains for the sake of guarding,
At that time introspection will come,
And even what has gone will return. ^5-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-34]]

At first, a mind like this,
Having known it to be faulty,
At that time, I, like a piece of wood,
Should remain steadfast. ^5-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-35]]

Looking around distractedly without purpose,
I should never do;
Always with a resolute mind,
I should look with eyes cast down. ^5-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-36]]

For the sake of resting the sight,
Occasionally I should look to the directions.
If someone appears in the path of sight,
Having looked, I should say "Welcome." ^5-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-37]]

In order to examine dangers on the path and so forth,
I should look again and again to the four directions.
Having rested, turning the head around,
I should look to the direction behind. ^5-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-38]]

Having examined in front and behind,
I should go or otherwise come;
Likewise, in all situations,
Having known what is needed, I should act. ^5-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-39]]

"The body should remain like this,"
Having prepared the action, then,
Occasionally, "How is this body
Remaining?" I should look. ^5-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-40]]

The mad elephant of the mind,
To the great pillar of thinking on the Dharma,
So that it does not escape from how it is tied,
Thus, it should be examined with all effort. ^5-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-41]]

So that one striving in concentration
Does not stray even for a single moment,
"Where is this mind of mine engaged?"
Thus, one should individually examine the mind. ^5-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-42]]

When involved with dangers and festivities and so forth,
If one is unable, one should act at ease.
Thus, at times of giving,
It is taught to treat ethical discipline with equanimity. ^5-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-43]]

Whatever is intended and begun to be done,
One should not think of anything other than that;
With the thought directed to that,
That very thing should be accomplished for the time being. ^5-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-44]]

In that way, everything is well done;
Otherwise, neither will happen.
The secondary affliction of lacking introspection,
In that way, will not increase. ^5-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-45]]

Various kinds of idle gossip,
And many kinds of wondrous spectacles—
When engaging in all of them,
One should abandon attachment to them. ^5-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-46]]

Digging the earth, cutting grass without purpose,
And drawing lines in the earth and so forth—if one does these,
Having remembered the training of the Sugatas,
Out of fear, one should abandon it instantly. ^5-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-47]]

Whenever one desires to move,
Or even when one desires to speak,
Having first examined one's own mind,
With steadfastness, one should act with understanding. ^5-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-48]]

Whenever one's own mind has attachment,
Or the desire to be angry—at that time,
One should not act and should not speak;
One should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-49]]

With wildness and mockery,
Or if one possesses pride and arrogance,
And the intention to expose faults,
Or if there is an evasive or deceptive mind, ^5-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-50]]

Whenever one is devoted to self-praise,
Or the very disparaging of others,
Or becoming abusive and resentful,
At that time, one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-51]]

Desiring gain, honor, and fame,
Or desiring to seek attendants and retinues,
If one's mind desires to be served,
At that time, one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-52]]

Forsaking the benefit of others,
And desiring to pursue one's own benefit,
If a mind desiring to speak arises,
Because of that, one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-53]]

Intolerance, laziness, and fear,
Likewise shamelessness and senseless talk,
If a mind attached to one's own side arises,
At that time, one should remain like a piece of wood. ^5-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-54]]

Thus, completely afflicted,
Having examined the mind striving for meaningless things,
At that time, the hero, by means of an antidote,
Should hold it firmly. ^5-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-55]]

With utmost certainty and excellent faith,
Steadfast, respectful, and courteous,
With a sense of shame and with apprehension,
Peaceful, and striving to make others happy. ^5-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-56]]

Of the childish who are mutually discordant,
Not being disheartened by their desires,
'From these arisen afflictions,
This mind has arisen,' thinking thus, being endowed with compassion, and ^5-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-57]]

Regarding faultless things,
Having mastered myself and sentient beings,
Without "I", like an apparition,
This mind should always be held. ^5-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-58]]

After a long time, the supreme leisure,
Reflecting again and again on having attained it,
Such a mind, like Mount Meru,
Should be held completely unshakable. ^5-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-59]]

By vultures desiring flesh,
Even when dragged back and forth everywhere,
Mind, if you do not become displeased,
Why do you care for it now? ^5-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-60]]

Having grasped this body as "mine",
Mind, why do you guard it?
If you and this are separate,
What use is it to you? ^5-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-61]]

Deluded mind, for what reason
Do you not grasp a clean wooden form?
This machine of a collection of impurities,
Guarding this rotten thing, what is the use? ^5-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-62]]

First, this layer of skin,
Separate it with your own intellect.
And the flesh from the network of bones,
Separate it aside with the weapon of wisdom. ^5-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-63]]

Having also divided the bones,
One should look down to the marrow, and
"What essence is there in this?"
You yourself should examine. ^5-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-64]]

Even having searched with effort in that way, there,
If you do not see any essence,
Why still, with attachment,
Do you protect this body? ^5-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-65]]

Impurities are not fit for you to eat,
Blood also is not fit to drink,
And intestines cannot be sucked, so
What will the body do for you? ^5-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-66]]

At best, by foxes and vultures,
This is fit to be guarded for the sake of food.
This human body
Is fit only to be employed. ^5-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-67]]

Even though you guard it like this,
By the merciless Lord of Death
If it is snatched and given to birds and dogs,
At that time what can you do? ^5-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-68]]

If one does not give clothes and so forth
Even to a servant who cannot be employed,
If this body goes elsewhere even when nurtured,
Why do you exhaust yourself nurturing it? ^5-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-69]]

Having given wages to this,
Now make it serve your own purpose.
Without any benefit, to this
You should not give everything. ^5-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-70]]

Merely as a support for going and coming,
Conceive of the body as a boat, and
In order to accomplish the welfare of sentient beings,
Transform it into a wish-fulfilling body. ^5-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-71]]

Thus, since you are in control,
Always bear a smiling face.
Completely abandon frowns and grimaces,
Be an honest friend to wandering beings. ^5-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-72]]

Chairs and so forth, recklessly
You should not throw down with noise.
Also, do not open doors violently;
Always delight in acting quietly. ^5-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-73]]

Waterfowl, cats, and thieves,
By going silently and stealthily,
Accomplish their desired goals;
The sage should always act like that. ^5-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-74]]

Skilled in exhorting others, and
Words that act beneficially unasked,
Accept them with respect upon your crown, and
Always become the disciple of everyone. ^5-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-75]]

To all words that are well spoken,
You should say, "Excellently spoken."
If you see someone creating merit,
Generate joy well by praising them. ^5-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-76]]

Speak of good qualities in secret,
And if good qualities are spoken of, join in.
If your own good qualities are spoken of,
Understand that as them knowing good qualities. ^5-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-77]]

All undertakings are for the sake of joy,
Yet that is rare even if bought with a price.
Therefore, through the good qualities achieved by others,
I should enjoy the happiness of joy. ^5-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-78]]

In this, there is no loss for me here,
And in the next life there is great happiness.
Through faults there is unhappiness and suffering,
And in the next life there is great suffering. ^5-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-79]]

When speaking, comforting and coherent,
Clear in meaning and pleasing to the mind,
Having abandoned attachment and hatred,
One should speak gently and in moderation. ^5-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-80]]

Even when looking at sentient beings with your eyes,
Thinking, "Relying precisely upon them,
I will become a Buddha,"
You should look with a direct and loving manner. ^5-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-81]]

Motivated by constant strong aspiration,
Or motivated by the antidote,
Towards the fields of qualities and benefactors,
And the suffering, virtue becomes great. ^5-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-82]]

Being skilled and endowed with faith,
I should always perform actions myself.
In all actions, upon anyone at all,
I should not rely. ^5-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-83]]

The perfection of generosity and the rest,
Should be practiced, progressing higher and higher.
One should not abandon the greater for the lesser.
Principally, one should consider the benefit of others. ^5-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-84]]

Having understood thus, for the benefit of others,
One should always remain striving.
By the Compassionate One who sees far,
Even prohibited actions have been permitted for that. ^5-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-85]]

To those fallen into ruin, the unprotected,
And those abiding in ascetic practice, one should share;
One should eat only a moderate amount,
And give away everything except the three Dharma robes. ^5-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-86]]

The body that practices the sublime Dharma
Should not be harmed for trivial reasons.
If one acts thus, the wishes of sentient beings
Will quickly be fulfilled. ^5-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-87]]

As long as the thought of compassion is impure,
This body should not be given away;
Rather, in this and other lives as well,
It should be given as a cause to accomplish a great purpose. ^5-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-88]]

One should not explain the Dharma to the disrespectful,
Nor to those who wear head-wraps while not sick,
Nor to those holding umbrellas, staffs, or weapons,
Nor to those whose heads are covered. ^5-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-89]]

Nor the profound and vast to the inferior,
Nor to a woman without a man present.
To the inferior and supreme Dharmas,
One should apply equal respect in all cases. ^5-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-90]]

One who is a vessel for the vast Dharma
Should not be engaged in the inferior Dharma;
Conduct should not be completely cast aside,
Nor should they be deceived with sutras and mantras. ^5-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-91]]

Tooth-sticks and saliva,
When discarded, should be covered;
Also, urine and the like, into usable
Water or on land, it is deprecated to discard. ^5-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-92]]

With a full mouth, making noise,
And with an open mouth, one should not eat;
One should not sit with legs outstretched,
And one should not rub the hands together. ^5-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-93]]

In vehicles, beds, and seats,
One should not be alone with another's woman.
Everything that causes the world to lose faith,
Having observed and inquired, one should abandon. ^5-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-94]]

One should not indicate with a finger,
But respectfully, with the right
Hand used entirely,
Even the path should be shown in this way. ^5-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-95]]

One should not wave the arms wildly,
But move them slightly, making a sound
By snapping the fingers and so forth as a sign;
Otherwise, one will be uncontrolled. ^5-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-96]]

Just as the Lord lay when passing into nirvana,
One should lie down in the desired direction;
With clear comprehension and the thought of rising quickly,
One should definitely set the mind from the very beginning. ^5-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-97]]

The practices of the Bodhisattvas
Are taught to be limitless;
The practice of purifying the mind,
One should definitely practice at least that much. ^5-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-98]]

Three times during the day and night,
One should recite the Triskandha;
By relying on the Conquerors and the awakening mind,
One should pacify the remainder of downfalls thereby. ^5-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-99]]

Whether under one's own or another's power,
In whatever situation, whatever one is doing,
Whatever has been taught as a training,
In that situation, one should exert oneself and train. ^5-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-100]]

That which is not to be trained in by the Sons of the Conqueror,
Does not exist at all;
For the wise one who abides in this way,
There is nothing that does not become merit. ^5-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-101]]

Whether directly or else indirectly,
One should do nothing other than the benefit of sentient beings;
For the sake of sentient beings alone,
One should dedicate everything for awakening. ^5-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-102]]

Always, the virtuous spiritual friend,
Who is skilled in the meaning of the Mahāyāna,
And the supreme discipline of the bodhisattvas,
Should not be given up even for the sake of one's life. ^5-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-103]]

From the liberation story of Śrīsaṃbhava,
One should train in the manner of relying on the guru;
This and other words spoken by the Buddha,
Should be known after reading the sūtras. ^5-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-104]]

The trainings appear in the sūtras,
Therefore, one should read the sūtras;
The Ākāśagarbha Sūtra,
Should be looked at from the very beginning. ^5-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-105]]

Because what is to be practiced constantly
Is extensively taught in that,
The Śikṣāsamuccaya, too,
Should definitely be looked at again and again. ^5-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-106]]

Or else, for the time being, the brief
Sūtrasamuccaya should be looked at;
And the two composed by the Noble Nāgārjuna
Should also be looked at with effort. ^5-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-107]]

Whatever is not forbidden by whichever,
That itself should be practiced;
In order to guard the minds of worldly beings,
Having seen the trainings, one should practice perfectly. ^5-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-108]]

The states of the body and mind,
Should be examined again and again;
That alone, in brief,
Is the defining characteristic of guarding introspection. ^5-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-109]]

These should be practiced with the body;
What will be accomplished by merely uttering words?
By merely reading a medical treatise,
Will patients be benefited? ^5-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the fifth chapter: Guarding Introspection. ^5-a

## Chapter Six: Teaching Patience ^6-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-1]]

Accumulated over a thousand eons,
Generosity, offerings to the Sugatas, and so forth—
Whatever good conduct there is, all of that,
A single moment of anger destroys. ^6-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-2]]

There is no misdeed like hatred;
There is no ascetic practice like patience.
Therefore, earnestly upon patience,
One should meditate in various ways. ^6-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-3]]

If one holds a mind with the dart of hatred,
The mind does not experience peace,
Neither joy nor happiness is obtained,
Sleep does not come, and one becomes unsteady. ^6-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-4]]

Those who, through wealth and honors,
Have been favored and depend upon him,
Even they, against the hate-filled
Master, will rebel to kill him. ^6-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-5]]

Because of it, friends become disheartened;
Though gathered by generosity, they do not stay.
In brief, an angry person who dwells in happiness—
There is no such thing at all. ^6-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-6]]

The enemy of anger will create
These and other such sufferings.
Whoever applies effort and destroys anger,
That one is happy in this life and the next. ^6-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-7]]

From doing what is undesired and
Hindering what is desired, there arises
Mental unhappiness; having found this food,
Hatred grows strong and destroys me. ^6-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-8]]

Therefore, of that enemy, I
Shall completely destroy the food;
For, other than harming me,
This enemy has no other task. ^6-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-9]]

Whatever happens, I
Shall not disturb my joyful mind.
Even if unhappy, what is desired is not accomplished,
And virtues will decline. ^6-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-10]]

If it can be remedied,
What is the point of being unhappy about it?
If it cannot be remedied,
What use is there in being unhappy about it? ^6-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-11]]

For myself or my friends,
Suffering, contempt, harsh words,
And unpleasantness are not desired;
For enemies, it is the opposite of that. ^6-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-12]]

The causes of happiness occur sometimes;
The causes of suffering are very many.
Without suffering, there is no renunciation.
Therefore, mind, resolve to be steadfast! ^6-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-13]]

Devotees of asceticism and the Karṇapas
Endure the sensations of burning, cutting, and so forth
Pointlessly; for the sake of liberation,
Why am I cowardly? ^6-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-14]]

That which does not become easier through familiarity—
There is no such thing at all.
Therefore, by becoming familiar with small harms,
Endure great harms! ^6-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-15]]

From snakes and gadflies,
Sensations of hunger, thirst, and so forth,
And conditions like rashes and so forth—
Why have I not seen pointless sufferings? ^6-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-16]]

Towards heat, cold, rain, wind, and so forth,
Sickness, bondage, beatings, and so forth,
I should not be overly sensitive;
If I do so, the harm increases. ^6-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-17]]

When some see their own blood,
They become exceptionally brave and steadfast;
When some see the blood of others,
They faint and fall unconscious. ^6-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-18]]

That is due to the steadfastness of the mind,
Or due to a cowardly nature.
Therefore, disregarding harms,
Make yourself unaffected by sufferings! ^6-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-19]]

Even when suffering arises, the wise
Should not cloud the clarity of the mind.
When waging war with the afflictions,
During battle, harms are many. ^6-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-20]]

Having disregarded all sufferings,
Those who conquer enemies such as anger—
They are the victorious heroes;
The rest slay corpses. ^6-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-21]]

Furthermore, the quality of suffering is
That through sorrow, arrogance is dispelled,
Compassion arises for those in cyclic existence,
Sin is shunned, and virtue is delighted in. ^6-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-22]]

Towards bile and so forth, which are
Great sources of suffering, without being angry,
Why be angry at those with minds?
They too are all incited by conditions. ^6-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-23]]

For example, even without wishing it,
Just as this sickness arises,
Likewise, even without wishing it,
Afflictions forcefully arise. ^6-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-24]]

Even without thinking, "I shall get angry,"
People get angry without choice;
Even without thinking, "I shall produce it,"
Anger likewise arises. ^6-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-25]]

All however many faults there are, and
Various kinds of sins—
All these arise from the power of conditions;
They do not possess independence. ^6-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-26]]

Even those assemblages of conditions
Have no intention, "We shall produce."
And that which is produced by them has no
Intention, "I shall be produced." ^6-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-27]]

Whatever is asserted as the "Primary Matter," and
Whatever is imputed as the "Self"—
Thinking, "I myself shall arise,"
They do not purposefully arise. ^6-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-28]]

If it does not exist prior to arising,
At that time, what wishes to arise?
Since it would be permanently distracted by objects,
It would also never cease. ^6-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-29]]

If that self is permanent,
Like space, it is evidently inactive.
Even if it meets with other conditions,
What can be done to the unchanging? ^6-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-30]]

If even at the time of acting it remains as before,
What did the action do to it?
By saying, "This is its action,"
What connection could there be? ^6-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-31]]

Thus, everything is under the power of others,
And that which has power is itself powerless.
Having understood this, one will not get angry
Towards all things, which are like emanations. ^6-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-32]]

"By what is what to be reversed?
Reversing too is illogical," if one says;
In dependence upon that, the wish for sufferings'
Continuum to be severed is not illogical. ^6-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-33]]

Therefore, whether it is an enemy or a friend,
If I see them doing what is improper,
Thinking, "This arose from such conditions,"
I will remain happy, reflecting in this way. ^6-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-34]]

If things were accomplished by their own will,
Since no one desires suffering,
For all embodied beings,
Suffering would not occur for anyone. ^6-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-35]]

Due to carelessness, even to themselves,
They do harm with thorns and so forth;
In order to obtain women and the like,
They become obsessed and starve themselves and so forth. ^6-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-36]]

Some hang themselves or jump from cliffs,
Eat poison or unwholesome food,
And through non-meritorious conduct,
They bring harm upon themselves. ^6-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-37]]

When, overpowered by afflictions,
They even kill their beloved selves,
At that time, to the bodies of others,
How could they not cause harm? ^6-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-38]]

When, due to the arising of afflictions,
They engage in killing themselves and so forth,
If compassion does not at least arise,
What point is there in becoming angry? ^6-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-39]]

If doing harm to others
Is the nature of the childish,
It is illogical to become angry at them,
Like resenting fire for its nature of burning. ^6-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-40]]

But if that fault is adventitious,
And the nature of sentient beings is gentle,
Even then it is illogical to become angry,
Like resenting space when smoke arises in it. ^6-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-41]]

Although sticks and the like are directly utilized,
If I become angry at the one who wields them,
Since they too are impelled by hatred,
It is logical to be angry at hatred. ^6-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-42]]

In the past, to sentient beings,
I have done harm such as this;
Therefore, since I have harmed sentient beings,
It is logical that this harm comes to me. ^6-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-43]]

Their weapons and my body
Are both causes of suffering;
They produced the weapon and I the body,
At which one should I be angry? ^6-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-44]]

Like a human-shaped ulcer,
Painful and unable to bear being touched;
When, blinded by craving, I have grasped it,
When it is harmed, at whom should I be angry? ^6-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-45]]

Childish ones do not desire suffering,
Yet they crave the causes of suffering;
When harmed by my own faults,
Why should I resent others? ^6-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-46]]

Like the guardians of hell,
And the forest of sword-leaves,
This is produced by my own karma,
So at whom should I be angry? ^6-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-47]]

Instigated by my own karma,
Those who harm me appear;
If because of this they go to hell,
Have I not ruined them? ^6-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-48]]

By relying upon them,
Through patience my many negative actions are cleansed;
But by relying upon me, they
Will go to the long-lasting suffering of hell. ^6-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-49]]

I am the one who harms them,
While they are the ones who benefit me;
Why perversely,
O wicked mind, do you become angry? ^6-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-50]]

If I possess the good qualities
Of intention, I will not go to hell;
If I protect myself,
What will happen to them here? ^6-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-51]]

But if I cause harm in return,
They will not be protected,
And my conduct will also decline;
Thus my ascetic practice will be destroyed. ^6-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-52]]

Because the mind is bodiless,
No one can destroy it anywhere;
Because of deep attachment to the body,
The body is harmed by sufferings. ^6-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-53]]

Contempt, speaking harsh words,
And those unpleasant words—
Since they do not harm the body,
O mind, why do you become so angry? ^6-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-54]]

That others dislike me,
Since that, in this life or another,
Will not consume me,
Why do I dislike it? ^6-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-55]]

Because it hinders my gain,
If I do not desire this,
My gain will be left behind here,
But my negative actions will remain steadfast. ^6-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-56]]

It is better that I die this very day,
Than to live long by wrong livelihood;
Even if I remain for a long time,
The suffering of death will be just the same. ^6-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-57]]

Having experienced happiness in a dream for a hundred years,
Whoever awakens,
And having experienced happiness for just an instant,
Whoever awakens— ^6-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-58]]

For both of those who awaken,
That happiness will not return;
For both a long life and a short life,
At the time of death they end up just the same. ^6-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-59]]

Even if I acquire abundant gains,
And enjoy happiness for a long time,
Just like being robbed by a thief,
I will depart naked and empty-handed. ^6-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-60]]

If you say, "By living on account of gains,
I will exhaust negative actions and perform merit,"
If I become angry for the sake of gains,
Will not merit be exhausted and negative actions created? ^6-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-61]]

That very purpose for which I live,
If it is destroyed,
That performs only negative actions—
What is the use of such a life? ^6-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-62]]

If you say, "Because it causes sentient beings to lose faith,
I become angry at those who speak unpleasant words,"
When unpleasant words are spoken about others,
Why do you not likewise become angry? ^6-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-63]]

Because lack of faith depends on others,
If you tolerate those without faith,
Because it depends on the arising of afflictions,
Why do you not tolerate those who speak unpleasant words? ^6-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-64]]

Towards images, stupas, and the sublime Dharma,
Even if someone disparages or destroys them,
It is inappropriate for me to have anger,
For the Buddhas and so forth suffer no harm. ^6-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-65]]

Towards gurus, relatives, and so forth,
And even those who do harm to friends,
Through conditions, in the previous manner,
Seeing that it occurs, turn back anger. ^6-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-66]]

To embodied beings, animate
And inanimate things both do harm;
Why single out the animate and hold resentment?
Therefore, be patient with harm. ^6-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-67]]

Some commit faults through ignorance,
And some, being ignorant, become angry.
Among them, who is to be considered faultless?
And who is to be considered at fault? ^6-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-68]]

That which causes others to do harm—
Why did I do those actions in the past?
If everything depends upon karma,
Why should I hold resentment toward this? ^6-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-69]]

Seeing this to be so, by all means,
So that everyone will have loving thoughts towards one another,
In order to bring this about, I will
Exert myself in meritorious deeds. ^6-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-70]]

For example, when a fire burning a house
Spreads into another house,
Whatever straw and so forth it spreads to,
That should be taken out and discarded. ^6-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-71]]

Likewise, whatever the mind is attached to,
If the fire of hatred spreads to it,
Fearing that merit will be burned,
It should be discarded instantly. ^6-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-72]]

If a person to be killed has his hand cut off
And escapes, why is that not good?
If through human suffering
One is freed from hell, why is that not good? ^6-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-73]]

Even for this much suffering right now,
If I cannot endure it,
Then the cause of suffering in hell,
Anger, why do I not avert it? ^6-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-74]]

For the sake of desires, burning and so forth,
I have experienced in hell thousands of times, but
I did not accomplish my own benefit,
Nor did I accomplish the benefit of others. ^6-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-75]]

This is not so harmful,
And great benefits will also be accomplished;
In the suffering that dispels the harm of wandering beings,
It is appropriate here only to rejoice. ^6-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-76]]

If others, by praising those possessing good qualities,
Attain the joy of happiness,
O mind, by praising them too,
Why do you not rejoice in that way? ^6-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-77]]

This joy of your rejoicing
Produces happiness and has no blame;
It is approved by those possessing good qualities,
And is also supreme in gathering others. ^6-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-78]]

Thinking, "Others will become happy in that way,"
If you do not want this happiness,
Because you forsake giving wages and so forth,
You will be ruined in the seen and the unseen. ^6-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-79]]

When your own good qualities are spoken of,
You want others to be happy too.
When others' good qualities are spoken of,
You do not want yourself to be happy. ^6-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-80]]

Because you desire happiness for all sentient beings,
Having generated the mind of awakening,
If sentient beings find happiness themselves,
Why do you become angry because of that? ^6-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-81]]

To awaken into Buddhahood worshipped by the three worlds,
If you desire for sentient beings,
Having seen them receive mere meager respect,
Why do you become tormented by that? ^6-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-82]]

Whoever is to be nurtured, you nurture;
They are to be given to by you yourself.
If they find a livelihood from a relative,
Do you not rejoice, but become angry instead? ^6-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-83]]

If you do not want this for wandering beings,
How can you desire awakening for them?
For one who is angry at another's prosperity,
Where is the mind of awakening? ^6-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-84]]

If he receives that from him,
Or if it remains in the benefactor's house,
In all ways, since you do not have it,
What does it matter whether it is given or not given? ^6-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-85]]

Merit and faith,
Or your own good qualities, why do you cast them away?
Not taking hold of the gains that occur,
Tell me, why do you not become angry? ^6-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-86]]

You have committed negative deeds yourself,
And not only are you without sorrow,
But with others who have performed meritorious deeds,
Do you want to compete together?
The Second Fascicle. ^6-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-87]]

Even if an enemy is unhappy,
What is there for you to rejoice in?
Merely by your wishing,
It does not become a cause of harm to them. ^6-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-88]]

If by your desires that suffering
Is accomplished, what is there for you to rejoice in?
If you say, "I will be satisfied,"
What is more ruinous than that? ^6-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-89]]

This hook, cast by the fisherman of the afflictions,
Is an unbearable barb.
Caught by it, in the cauldrons of hell,
The guardians of hell will boil me. ^6-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-90]]

Praise, fame, and honor
Will not become merit, nor will they become long life;
Will not become my strength, and are not health;
Nor will they make the body comfortable. ^6-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-91]]

If I recognize my own welfare,
What welfare of mine is there in that?
If I desire only mental happiness,
I should rely on gambling, alcohol, and the like. ^6-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-92]]

For the sake of fame, losing wealth,
And even getting myself killed,
What is the use of mere syllables?
When dead, who will be happy from them? ^6-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-93]]

When a sandcastle collapses,
Children howl in despair.
Likewise, when praise and fame decline,
My own mind is like a child. ^6-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-94]]

First of all, since sound is mindless,
It cannot possibly have the intention to praise me.
The renown that "Others are pleased with me"—
Perhaps that is the cause of joy. ^6-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-95]]

Whether it is directed at another or myself,
How does the joy of another benefit me?
That joy and happiness belongs to them alone;
I do not obtain a share of it. ^6-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-96]]

If I am happy through their happiness,
I should do the same toward everyone.
How is it that when they become happy
By rejoicing in others, I am not happy? ^6-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-97]]

Therefore, thinking, "I am being praised,"
The joy that arises in me—
Since that also is unjustified in this way,
It is nothing but the behavior of a child. ^6-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-98]]

Praise and the like distract me,
And also destroy my disillusionment;
They cause jealousy toward those with good qualities,
And destroy excellence as well. ^6-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-99]]

Therefore, regarding my praise and the like,
Those who stand close by to destroy them—
Are they not engaged in protecting me
From falling into the lower realms? ^6-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-100]]

Since I seek liberation,
I do not need the bonds of gain and honor.
Those who free me from being bound—
How could I be angry at them? ^6-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-101]]

When I wish to enter into suffering,
Like the blessing of the Buddha,
They become a gate that prevents me from passing.
How could I be angry at them? ^6-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-102]]

Thinking, "This one is an obstacle to my merit,"
It is also inappropriate to be angry at them.
Since there is no austerity equal to patience,
Should I not abide in it? ^6-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-103]]

If, through my own fault,
I do not practice patience here,
When the cause of merit is present,
I am solely the one making an obstacle to it. ^6-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-104]]

If one thing does not arise without another,
And comes to be when it is present,
Since that very thing is its cause,
How can it be called an obstacle? ^6-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-105]]

By a beggar who arrives at the right time,
No obstacle to giving is created;
One who grants ordination
Cannot be called an obstacle to ordination. ^6-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-106]]

Beggars in the world are indeed plentiful,
But those who cause harm are rare,
For if I have not harmed others,
No one will cause me harm. ^6-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-107]]

Therefore, accomplished without toil,
Like a treasure appearing in the house,
Since they become my helper in bodhisattva conduct,
I should delight in my enemies. ^6-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-108]]

Since it is accomplished by this one and myself,
Therefore, the fruit of patience
Should first be offered to them,
For they are the cause of patience. ^6-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-109]]

If you say, "Because they have no intention to accomplish my patience,
This enemy is not to be venerated,"
Then since it is a suitable cause for accomplishment,
Why do you venerate the sublime Dharma? ^6-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-110]]

If you say, "Because they have the intention to harm,
This enemy is not to be venerated,"
If they strove to benefit me like a doctor,
How could my patience be accomplished? ^6-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-111]]

Therefore, since patience arises
In dependence on a very hateful mind,
Because they are the very cause of patience,
They are worthy of veneration just like the sublime Dharma. ^6-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-112]]

Therefore, the field of sentient beings
And the field of the Conquerors, the Sage has proclaimed.
By pleasing them, many
Have thus reached the far shore of excellence. ^6-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-113]]

From sentient beings and from the Conquerors,
The accomplishment of a Buddha's qualities is alike.
Just as one respects the Conquerors,
Why is it not the practice to do so for sentient beings? ^6-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-114]]

The qualities of their intentions are not by themselves equal;
It is due to the fruit that they are equal.
Sentient beings also have qualities,
And therefore they are equal. ^6-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-115]]

Whatever offering is made to one possessing a loving mind,
That is the greatness of sentient beings.
Whatever merit comes from faith in the Buddha,
That too is the greatness of the Buddha. ^6-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-116]]

Because they have a share in accomplishing a Buddha's qualities,
Therefore they are asserted to be equal.
Yet, regarding the boundless ocean of qualities,
No one is equal to the Buddhas. ^6-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-117]]

Of the unique collection of supreme qualities,
If merely a fraction of those qualities
Appears in someone, in order to venerate them,
Even offering the three realms would be too small. ^6-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-118]]

A fraction of generating the supreme qualities of a Buddha
Is possessed by sentient beings; therefore,
In accordance with just this fraction,
It is fitting to venerate sentient beings. ^6-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-119]]

Furthermore, to those who are unwavering friends
And who perform immeasurable benefit,
Other than pleasing sentient beings,
What else could be a repayment? ^6-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-120]]

For those for whose sake they give up their bodies and enter the Avīci hell,
Benefiting them becomes a repayment;
Therefore, even if they cause great harm,
I should act toward all with complete goodness. ^6-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-121]]

Even my own lords,
Because they do not consider even their own bodies,
How could a fool like me
Act with pride toward them and not act as a servant? ^6-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-122]]

Those by whose happiness the Sages are pleased,
And by harming whom displeasure arises;
By bringing joy to them, all the Sages are pleased,
And by harming them, harm is done to the Sages. ^6-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-123]]

Just as when one's body is completely engulfed in fire,
No desirable things can make the mind happy,
Likewise, when harm is done to sentient beings,
There is no way for the greatly compassionate ones to be pleased. ^6-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-124]]

Therefore, whatever harm I have done to wandering beings,
Which has caused all the greatly compassionate ones to be displeased,
I individually confess that negative deed today;
I pray that the Sages forgive whatever displeasure I have caused. ^6-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-125]]

In order to please the Tathāgatas,
From today onward I shall be a servant to the world.
Even if the multitudes of wandering beings strike my head with their feet,
Or kill me, I will not retaliate; may the protectors of the world be pleased! ^6-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-126]]

Those possessing the nature of compassion
Have made all these wandering beings into themselves; of this there is no doubt.
These very ones seen in the form of sentient beings
Are the protectors themselves; why do I not respect them? ^6-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-127]]

This alone is what pleases the Tathāgatas;
This alone is also the perfect accomplishment of my own purpose;
This alone is also what dispels the suffering of the world;
Therefore, I should always practice this alone. ^6-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-128]]

For example, if one of the king's men
Causes harm to many people,
Farsighted people,
Even if they are capable, do not harm him in return, ^6-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-129]]

Because he is not alone,
The might of the king is his power;
Likewise, a weak harm-doer
Should not be despised at all. ^6-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-130]]

Because the wardens of hell
And the compassionate ones are their forces;
Therefore, just as subjects please a fierce king,
I should please sentient beings. ^6-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-131]]

Even if a king were to become angry,
By displeasing sentient beings,
Whatever is to be experienced—
Could he inflict that harm of hell? ^6-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-132]]

Even if a king were to be pleased,
Through having pleased sentient beings,
Whatever is to be attained,
He could not possibly grant Buddhahood itself. ^6-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-133]]

Arising from pleasing sentient beings,
Let alone the future accomplishment of Buddhahood,
In this very life, great glory,
Fame, and happiness will come; why do you not see this? ^6-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-134]]

While in saṃsāra, through patience, beauty and the like,
Health, and fame,
An extremely long life, and
The vast happiness of a universal monarch are attained. ^6-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the sixth chapter, named "Teaching Patience." ^6-a

## Chapter Seven: Teaching Joyous Effort ^7-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-1]]

Thus, having patience, I should undertake diligence.
For enlightenment abides in diligence.
Just as there is no movement without wind,
Merit does not arise without diligence. ^7-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-2]]

What is diligence? It is delight in virtue.
Its opposing factors shall be explained:
Laziness, clinging to what is bad, and
Despondency, which is self-contempt. ^7-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-3]]

Tasting the pleasure of idleness, and
Craving that relies on sleep;
From feeling no weariness toward the sufferings of saṃsāra,
Laziness comes to arise. ^7-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-4]]

Having been snared by the fowler of afflictions,
Having entered the net of birth,
And having gone into the mouth of the Lord of Death,
How is it that you still do not know? ^7-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-5]]

That your own group is gradually being killed,
Have you not seen it?
Yet whoever relies on sleep,
Is like an outcast and a buffalo. ^7-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-6]]

When all paths are blocked,
And the Lord of Death is watching,
How can you enjoy eating,
And how can you enjoy sleeping like this? ^7-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-7]]

Because death will come very quickly,
Until then, the accumulations should be gathered.
Even if laziness is abandoned at that time,
Being out of time, what can be done? ^7-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-8]]

"This is undone, this is just begun,
And this remains only half-done"—
When the Lord of Death suddenly arrives,
One will think, "Alas, I am ruined!" ^7-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-9]]

Swollen by the force of sorrow,
Red eyes shedding tears upon their faces,
Relatives having lost hope,
And looking at the faces of Yama's messengers. ^7-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-10]]

Tormented by the memory of one's own evils,
And hearing the sounds of hell,
Clothed in excrement out of terror,
When one becomes delirious, what can be done? ^7-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-11]]

If you are fearful in this life,
Like a live fish rolling about,
Having committed evils, what need is there to mention
The unbearable sufferings of hell? ^7-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-12]]

When touched by hot water,
The tender-fleshed find it extremely hot.
Having committed the deeds of hell,
Why do you remain comfortably like this? ^7-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-13]]

Desiring the result without exerting effort,
Being fragile and experiencing many harms,
Yet acting like a god while seized by death—
Alas, you will be destroyed by sufferings! ^7-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-14]]

Relying on the boat of a human body,
Free yourself from the great river of suffering.
Since this boat will be hard to find later,
Fool, do not sleep at this time! ^7-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-15]]

Which is a boundless cause of joy,
Having abandoned the supreme joy of the holy Dharma,
In distraction by the causes of suffering,
And in frivolity and the like, why do you delight? ^7-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-16]]

Without despondency, and with the forces,
Applying oneself earnestly, and mastering oneself,
Equalizing self and other,
And exchanging self and other, you should practice! ^7-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-17]]

Thinking, "How could I attain enlightenment?"
I should not be despondent,
For in this way the Tathāgata,
Who speaks the truth, has spoken this truth: ^7-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-18]]

"Flies, mosquitoes, and bees,
And likewise whatever is born as a worm,
Even they, if they generate the power of effort,
Will attain unsurpassable enlightenment, which is hard to find." ^7-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-19]]

Since someone like me is born as a human by birth,
And recognizes what is beneficial and harmful,
If I do not forsake the enlightenment conduct,
Why should I not attain enlightenment? ^7-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-20]]

"But my arms, legs, and so forth,
Must be given away, so I am afraid," if I think—
Without examining what is heavy and what is light,
Due to foolishness I am merely afraid. ^7-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-21]]

For countless tens of millions of eons,
I have been cut many times,
Pierced, burned, and split apart,
But did not attain enlightenment. ^7-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-22]]

The suffering of my accomplishing enlightenment
Has a limit;
To remove the harm of an internal thorn,
It is like the suffering of making a cut in the body. ^7-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-23]]

Even all doctors, through medical treatments,
Cure illness with discomfort.
Therefore, to destroy many sufferings,
I should endure a minor discomfort. ^7-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-24]]

Such ordinary medical treatments
Are not performed by the supreme physician;
With an extremely gentle method,
He cures immeasurable great diseases. ^7-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-25]]

Even in the giving of vegetables and so forth,
The Guide initially engages one.
Having become accustomed to that, later on,
One will gradually give away even one's own flesh. ^7-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-26]]

When toward one's own body
A mind arises that views it like vegetables and so forth,
At that time, in giving away flesh and so forth,
What difficulty is there in that? ^7-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-27]]

By abandoning misdeeds, there is no suffering;
By being wise, there is no displeasure.
Thus, wrong conceptualizations
And misdeeds harm the mind and body. ^7-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-28]]

Through merit, the body is at ease,
And through wisdom, the mind is at ease.
Even while remaining in cyclic existence for the sake of others,
Why would the compassionate ones become weary? ^7-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-29]]

Because through the power of this awakening mind
Former misdeeds are exhausted,
And because it gathers an ocean of merit,
It is explained to be supreme over the Hearers. ^7-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-30]]

Therefore, riding the horse of the awakening mind,
Which dispels all weariness and fatigue,
Proceeding from happiness to happiness,
Who with a knowing mind would fall into despair? ^7-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-31]]

The forces to accomplish the benefit of sentient beings
Are aspiration, firmness, joy, and relinquishment.
Aspiration is generated through the fear of suffering
And contemplating its benefits. ^7-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-32]]

Thus, having abandoned the discordant factors,
Through aspiration, pride, joy, and relinquishment,
And the powers of earnest application and mastery,
I should strive to increase joyful endeavor. ^7-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-33]]

The faults of myself and others,
Being immeasurable, I must destroy;
Wherein for even each single fault,
An ocean of eons will be exhausted. ^7-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-34]]

Of that initiation of exhausting faults,
If I do not see even a fraction in myself,
Being an abode of immeasurable suffering,
Why does my heart not burst? ^7-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-35]]

The good qualities of myself and others,
Being many, I must accomplish;
Wherein if for even each single quality
One must familiarize for an ocean of eons, ^7-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-36]]

I, for even a fraction of a good quality,
Have never generated familiarization;
Having somehow found this birth,
It is astonishing that I have made it meaningless. ^7-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-37]]

I have not made offerings to the Blessed Ones,
I have not provided the happiness of great festivals,
I have not performed deeds for the teaching,
I have not fulfilled the wishes of the poor. ^7-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-38]]

Not having given fearlessness to the fearful,
And not having given happiness to the afflicted,
I have merely produced the pangs
And suffering of my mother's womb. ^7-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-39]]

Because previously and even now,
I have been devoid of aspiration for the Dharma,
Such destitution has come about;
Who would forsake aspiration for the Dharma? ^7-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-40]]

Of all wholesome factors,
The Sage has stated that the root is aspiration,
And its root constantly is
Meditating on the ripened results. ^7-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-41]]

Suffering and mental unhappiness,
Various kinds of fears,
And separation from what is desired
Will arise from engaging in misdeeds. ^7-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-42]]

By performing wholesome deeds intended in the mind,
Wherever one may go,
There, through that merit,
One will be manifestly honored with the offerings of its results. ^7-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-43]]

Although those who commit misdeeds desire happiness,
Wherever they may go,
There, through those misdeeds,
They are completely destroyed by the weapons of suffering. ^7-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-44]]

Dwelling in the core of a vast, fragrant, and cooling lotus,
Whose splendor is produced by the food of the Victor's sweet speech,
With a supreme body born from a lotus blossomed by the Sage's light,
Through wholesome deeds one becomes a son of the Sugata dwelling before the Victor. ^7-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-45]]

Utterly tormented by the henchmen of Yama stripping away one's skin entirely,
Molten copper melted by intensely hot fire poured onto one's body,
Pierced by blazing swords and short spears, one's flesh scattered in a hundred pieces,
Through many unwholesome deeds, one falls onto a ground of intensely blazing iron. ^7-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-46]]

Therefore, one should aspire to the wholesome,
And meditate upon it with reverence;
According to the method of the Vajradhvaja,
Having begun, one should meditate on self-confidence. ^7-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-47]]

First, having examined one's capacity,
One should either begin or not begin;
Not beginning is best,
But having begun, one should not turn back. ^7-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-48]]

Becoming accustomed to that in other lives,
Misdeeds and suffering will increase;
Other deeds and the time of results as well
Will become inferior, and that will not be accomplished either. ^7-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-49]]

Actions, afflictions, and capacity:
One should generate self-confidence regarding these three.
Thinking, "I shall do this alone,"
This is the self-confidence of action. ^7-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-50]]

Since this world, powerless through afflictions,
Is unable to accomplish its own benefit,
Wandering beings are not capable like me;
Therefore, I shall do this. ^7-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-51]]

If others are performing inferior actions,
How can I myself remain idle?
I do not do this out of pride,
For me, having no pride is supreme. ^7-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-52]]

When encountering a dead snake,
Even a crow acts like a garuda.
If I myself am weak,
Even a minor downfall will cause harm. ^7-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-53]]

For one who is despondent and casts aside effort,
Is there liberation from destitution?
But by generating self-confidence and effort,
One is difficult to defeat even by the great. ^7-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-54]]

Therefore, with a steadfast mind,
I shall conquer the downfalls;
If I am defeated by downfalls,
My desire to conquer the three realms becomes a laughingstock. ^7-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-55]]

I shall conquer all,
And no one shall conquer me.
I, a son of the Victorious Lion,
Should abide in this self-confidence. ^7-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-56]]

Any being who is overcome by pride
Is afflicted, not self-confident;
The self-confident do not fall under the power of the enemy,
They fall under the power of the enemy of pride. ^7-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-57]]

Those inflated by the affliction of pride
Are led to the lower realms by pride,
Destroying their human joys,
And becoming slaves who eat others' food, ^7-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-58]]

Stupid, ugly, and weak,
They will be despised everywhere.
If ascetics puffed up with pride
Are also included among the proud,
Tell me, what kind of wretched beings are they? ^7-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-59]]

Whoever holds self-confidence to conquer the enemy of pride,
He is the truly self-confident one and the victorious hero.
Whoever definitively destroys the thriving enemy of pride,
Perfects the fruit of victory according to the desires of beings. ^7-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-60]]

When dwelling in the midst of a host of afflictions,
One should endure in a thousand ways;
Like a lion among foxes and the like,
One should not be harmed by the host of afflictions. ^7-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-61]]

Even if great peril arises,
Just as a person protects his eyes,
Likewise, even if peril arises,
One should not fall under the power of afflictions. ^7-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-62]]

Just as one desires the joyful fruit of a game,
Whatever task is to be done,
One should be attached to that action,
And finding no satiety, rejoice in that action. ^7-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-63]]

Even if one acts for the sake of happiness,
It is uncertain whether happiness will result;
But for one whose very action brings happiness,
How can there be happiness without doing that action? ^7-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-64]]

Like honey smeared on a razor's edge,
If one is not satiated by sensual desires,
Having a ripening of happiness and peace,
How could one ever be satiated with merit? ^7-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-65]]

Therefore, in order to complete the task,
Just as an elephant tormented by the midday sun
Plunges into a lake upon encountering it,
One should also engage in that action. ^7-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-66]]

If one's strength declines,
One should set it aside in order to resume it later.
If it is well completed, desiring the next
And the next, one should abandon it. ^7-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-67]]

Together with veteran enemies,
Like clashing swords on a battlefield,
One should dodge the weapons of afflictions,
And strike to destroy the enemies of afflictions. ^7-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-68]]

If one drops one's sword in battle,
One quickly picks it up out of fear;
Likewise, if the weapon of mindfulness is lost,
Remembering the fear of hell, one should quickly take it up. ^7-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-69]]

Just as poison, by relying on the blood,
Spreads throughout the body,
Likewise, if an opportunity is found,
Faults will spread through the mind. ^7-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-70]]

Just as one carrying a vessel filled with mustard oil,
With a sword-bearer standing nearby,
Fearing the threat of death if it spills,
An ascetic should likewise exert himself. ^7-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-71]]

Therefore, if a snake comes into one's lap,
Just as one would quickly jump up,
Likewise, if sleep and lethargy come,
One should quickly repel them. ^7-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-72]]

For each and every fault that arises,
Reproaching oneself, by all means,
'This will not happen to me again,
I will act in this way'—so should one ponder for a long time. ^7-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-73]]

With the thought, 'How in these situations
Will mindfulness become a habit?'
For this reason, one should seek encounters
Or appropriate actions. ^7-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-74]]

So that before undertaking any task,
One will have the capability for everything,
Thus, recalling the teachings on heedfulness,
One should make oneself rise lightly. ^7-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-75]]

Just as the going and
Coming of the wind control the cotton fluff,
Likewise, being controlled by joy,
In that way, success will be achieved. ^7-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, this is the seventh chapter, entitled The Teaching on Joyous Effort. ^7-a

## Chapter Eight: Teaching Meditation ^8-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-1]]

Having thus generated joyous effort,
One should place the mind in meditative concentration.
A person whose mind is distracted
Dwells between the fangs of the afflictions. ^8-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-2]]

Through the isolation of body and mind,
Distraction does not arise.
Therefore, one should abandon the worldly life
And completely discard discursive thoughts. ^8-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-3]]

Because of attachment and for the sake of gain and so forth,
Through craving, one does not abandon the world.
Therefore, having completely abandoned these,
The wise should analyze in this way. ^8-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-4]]

By special insight completely endowed with calm abiding,
Knowing that the afflictions are destroyed,
One should first seek calm abiding; and that too
Is accomplished by joyful detachment from the world. ^8-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-5]]

What impermanent being
Would be truly attached to the impermanent?
Through that, for thousands of lifetimes,
They will not see their loved ones. ^8-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-6]]

Not seeing them, one will not be joyful,
And the mind will not rest in equipoise.
Even seeing them, one will not be satisfied,
And will be tormented by craving as before. ^8-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-7]]

If one forms attachments to sentient beings,
Ultimate reality is completely obscured,
The mind of disillusionment is also destroyed,
And in the end, one will be tormented by sorrow. ^8-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-8]]

By merely thinking about them,
This life will pass away meaninglessly.
Because of impermanent friends and relatives,
Even the eternal Dharma will be destroyed. ^8-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-9]]

If one acts with equal fortune to the childish,
One will certainly go to the lower realms.
If they lead one to what is unequal,
What is the use of relying on the childish? ^8-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-10]]

In a single instant they become friends,
And in a brief moment they even become enemies.
Since they become angry at causes for joy,
Ordinary people are difficult to please. ^8-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-11]]

If one speaks beneficially, they get angry,
And they turn me away from what is beneficial.
If one does not listen to their words,
They become angry and go to the lower realms. ^8-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-12]]

Envious of superiors, competitive with equals,
Arrogant toward inferiors, conceited when praised,
And prone to anger when spoken to unpleasantly—
When is benefit ever obtained from the childish? ^8-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-13]]

If one associates with the childish,
Praising oneself and disparaging others,
And talking about the joys of cyclic existence and so forth,
Non-virtue will certainly arise in every way. ^8-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-14]]

Thus, associating oneself with others
Will merely lead to ruin.
They will not accomplish my benefit,
And I will not accomplish their benefit. ^8-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-15]]

One should flee far away from the childish.
When meeting them, one should please them with joy,
Without becoming very close,
Interacting well as mere acquaintances. ^8-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-16]]

Like a bee taking nectar from a flower,
Having taken only the meaning of Dharma,
As if never having seen them before,
One should remain without familiarity toward everyone. ^8-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-17]]

"I have much gain and respect,"
"Many are fond of me"—
If one harbors such conceit,
Fear will arise after death. ^8-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-18]]

Therefore, whatever the thoroughly deluded mind
Becomes attached to,
Along with that, a thousandfold
Suffering will manifest and arise. ^8-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-19]]

Therefore, the wise should not be attached;
From attachment, fear arises.
Since these will naturally be left behind,
Remain steadfast and fully understand this. ^8-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-20]]

Even if many gains are acquired,
And fame and a good reputation arise,
Together with the collection of gains and fame,
There is no knowing where one will go. ^8-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-21]]

If there are others who disparage me,
What joy is there for me in praise?
If there are others who praise me,
What displeasure is there in disparagement? ^8-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-22]]

Sentient beings have diverse inclinations;
If even the Victors cannot please them,
What need to mention an inferior one like me?
Therefore, I should abandon worldly thoughts. ^8-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-23]]

Sentient beings disparage those without gains,
And speak unpleasantly of those with gains.
With those who are naturally difficult to associate with,
How can joy ever arise? ^8-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-24]]

Because the childish do not experience joy
Unless it is for their own benefit,
"None of the childish are true friends,"
Thus the Tathāgatas have spoken. ^8-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-25]]

In the forest, deer and birds,
And trees do not speak unpleasantly;
With those whose company is joyful,
When will I dwell together? ^8-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-26]]

In a cave or an empty shrine,
Or dwelling at the foot of a tree,
When will I never look back,
And become free from attachment? ^8-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-27]]

In places not held as "mine",
Which are naturally vast,
Acting freely and without attachment,
When will I abide? ^8-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-28]]

With merely a begging bowl and the like,
Wearing garments unwanted by anyone,
And not even concealing this body,
When will I dwell fearlessly? ^8-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-29]]

Having gone to the charnel ground,
The bones of others and my own body,
As having the nature of perishing,
When will I view them equally? ^8-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-30]]

Even this very body of mine,
By whose stench even foxes
Will not approach near,
Will become exactly like that. ^8-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-31]]

Although this body arose as one,
The flesh and bones that were born together
Will perish and separate from one another;
What need to mention other friends? ^8-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-32]]

When born, one is born alone,
And when dying, one dies alone;
Since no other takes a share of the suffering,
What is the use of friends who are hindrances? ^8-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-33]]

Just as those who enter upon a journey
Take up a place to stay,
In the same way, those entered on the path of existence
Fully take up a place of birth. ^8-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-34]]

While the whole world mourns,
As long as four men
Have not carried this away from there,
Until then, go to the forest. ^8-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-35]]

Having made no friends and no enemies,
This body dwelling alone in solitude,
Having considered it as already dead,
Even when dying, there is no mourning. ^8-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-36]]

Since there is no one staying nearby
To cause grief or harm,
By this, the recollection of the Buddha and so forth
Will not be distracted by anyone. ^8-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-37]]

Therefore, in exceptionally delightful
Forests, with little trouble and happy,
Pacifying all distractions,
I shall dwell completely alone. ^8-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-38]]

Having abandoned all other thoughts,
With a single-pointed intention,
In order to settle the mind in equipoise
And to subdue it, I shall strive. ^8-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-39]]

In this world and in the next,
Desires produce ruin:
Here, killing, bondage, and amputation,
And in the next, establishing hells and so forth. ^8-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-40]]

Previously through male and female messengers,
For whose sake I made many requests,
And regarding sins or ill repute,
For whose sake I did not hesitate; ^8-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-41]]

I also entered into danger,
And exhausted my wealth;
By fully embracing whom
I was supremely delighted—those very ones ^8-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-42]]

Are nothing but skeletons, nothing else.
Being neither independent nor a self,
Strongly desiring and fully clinging to them,
Why do you not proceed to nirvāṇa? ^8-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-43]]

Which at first was lifted up with effort,
And though drawn near, looked down out of modesty;
Whether seen before or unseen,
Whose face was covered by a cloth; ^8-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-44]]

That face which tormented you,
Just as it has now become manifest,
Having seen it unveiled by vultures,
Why do you flee from it now? ^8-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-45]]

Even when others looked at it with their eyes,
It was completely guarded;
Now that it is being eaten by them,
Miser, why do you not guard it? ^8-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-46]]

Seeing this heap of flesh,
When vultures and others eat it,
Why do you offer to the food of others
Garlands of flowers, sandalwood, and ornaments? ^8-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-47]]

Having seen it as merely a heap of bones,
If you are terrified even when it does not move,
When it was moved by something, like a zombie,
Why were you not afraid? ^8-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-48]]

From a single food alone,
If feces and saliva are produced,
Out of these, you dislike the feces,
So why do you delight in the saliva? ^8-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-49]]

Of cotton, which is soft to the touch,
You do not delight in a pillow,
Saying it does not emit a foul odor;
The lustful are deluded by filth. ^8-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-50]]

If you are attached to it even when covered,
Why do you not desire it when uncovered?
If you have no need for it uncovered,
Why do you embrace it when covered? ^8-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-51]]

If you have no attachment to filth,
To a cage of bones tied together with sinews
And plastered with the mud of flesh,
Why do you embrace another in your lap? ^8-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-52]]

You yourself contain much filth,
Which you continually make use of,
Yet for other bags of filth as well,
Out of a craving for filth, you desire them. ^8-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-53]]

Saying, "I delight in this flesh,"
If you desire to touch and look at it,
Which is of a mindless nature,
How can you desire the flesh? ^8-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-54]]

That mind which you desire
Cannot be touched or seen,
And whatever can be is not conscious;
What is the point of a meaningless embrace? ^8-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-55]]

That another's body is of an unclean nature,
Not realizing this is not so amazing;
That you yourself are filth itself,
Not realizing this is truly amazing! ^8-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-56]]

Opened by the rays of a cloudless sun,
Abandoning a fresh lotus,
With a mind attached to filth,
Why do you delight in a cage of filth? ^8-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-57]]

A place smeared with filth,
If you do not wish to touch,
From which that filth arose,
How can you wish to touch the body? ^8-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-58]]

If you have no attachment to filth,
Arisen from a field of filth,
And generated by its seed,
Why do you embrace another in your lap? ^8-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-59]]

A filth-worm arisen from filth,
Even a tiny one, you do not desire,
Yet a body whose nature is much filth,
And born of filth, you desire. ^8-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-60]]

Towards your own filth,
Not only do you not disparage it,
But out of craving for filth,
You desire another bag of filth. ^8-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-61]]

Even pleasing things like camphor,
Or cooked rice and vegetables,
If put into the mouth and spat out,
Make even the ground foul and filthy. ^8-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-62]]

If such filth as this
Is obvious, yet you doubt it,
The bodies discarded in the charnel grounds,
Look at that other filth as well. ^8-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-63]]

If the skin is peeled away from it,
Great fear will arise;
Knowing this, how can you
Feel joy in that very thing again? ^8-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-64]]

That scent smeared on the body
Is sandalwood and so forth, nothing else;
Because of that scent of another thing,
Why are you attached to another? ^8-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-65]]

If its nature is foul-smelling,
Is it not better to not be attached to it?
Those who pointlessly burn with craving in the world,
Why do they smear it with sweet scents? ^8-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-66]]

If the sweet scent belongs to sandalwood,
What does that have to do with the body here?
Because of the scent of another thing,
Why are you attached to another? ^8-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-67]]

If long hair and nails,
Yellowish teeth, and a foul, muddy smell,
Imbue the nature of the body,
And make it terrifying indeed when naked, ^8-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-68]]

Like a weapon that harms oneself,
Why make efforts to clean and polish it?
Through efforts driven by self-delusion,
This earth is thoroughly agitated by madmen. ^8-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-69]]

Having seen just a skeleton,
If you feel disgust in the charnel ground,
Pervaded by moving skeletons,
Are you delighted by the charnel ground of the city? ^8-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-70]]

That which has thus become filthy,
Cannot be obtained without a price;
Exhaustion from accomplishing that goal,
And harm in hells and so forth will follow. ^8-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-71]]

Since as a child one cannot amass wealth,
When coming of age, what happiness is there in this?
If life is exhausted by amassing wealth,
What can be done with desires in old age? ^8-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-72]]

Some miserable people with desires,
Utterly exhausted by the day's work,
Come home with worn-out bodies,
And sleep exactly like corpses. ^8-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-73]]

Some suffer the afflictions of traveling abroad,
And the misery of going far away;
Although longing for their wives and children,
They do not see them for years on end. ^8-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-74]]

Utterly deluded about what benefits themselves,
Even selling themselves for that very purpose,
They do not obtain it, and uselessly
Are driven by the wind of others' work. ^8-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-75]]

Some sell their own bodies,
And powerlessly become employed by others;
Even when their wives give birth to children,
They are simply born at the foot of trees in the wilderness. ^8-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-76]]

Fools deceived by desires,
Wishing for a livelihood and thinking, "I will make a living,"
Enter into battle despite fearing the loss of their lives,
And go into slavery for the sake of profit. ^8-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-77]]

Some with desires even have their bodies mutilated,
Some are impaled on the points of stakes,
Some are stabbed with short spears,
And some are seen being burned. ^8-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-78]]

Through the torments of amassing, protecting, and losing it,
Wealth should be known as endless ruin.
Those who are distracted by attachment to wealth
Have no opportunity for liberation from the miseries of existence. ^8-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-79]]

For those with desires, these and so forth
Are the many disadvantages and little benefit,
Just like beasts of burden drawing a chariot
Eating a few mouthfuls of grass. ^8-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-80]]

Which is not rare even when accomplished by beasts;
For the sake of whatever is that small benefit,
This hard-to-find, perfect freedom and endowment
Is destroyed by those tormented by karma. ^8-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-81]]

Desires will certainly perish,
And they cause one to fall into hell and so forth;
For the sake of what is not great, at all times,
Whatever fatigue and hardship has occurred, ^8-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-82]]

If by just a ten-millionth fraction of that
Hardship, Buddhahood is accomplished,
Those with desires have greater suffering than in the Bodhisattva's conduct,
And yet they have no awakening. ^8-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-83]]

If one considers the sufferings of hell and so forth,
For desires, weapons,
Poison, fire, precipices,
And enemies cannot even serve as examples. ^8-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-84]]

Having thus generated revulsion for desires,
Generate joy for solitude.
Empty of disputes and afflictions,
In the peaceful forests, ^8-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-85]]

The fortunate ones, cooled by moonlight and sandalwood,
On broad flat stones, delightful mansions,
Fanned by the silent, peaceful forest breeze,
Pace while contemplating what benefits others. ^8-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-86]]

In empty houses, at the foot of trees, and in caves,
Staying wherever and for however long they desire,
Having abandoned the suffering of guarding possessions,
They live carefree without dependence. ^8-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-87]]

Acting independently and without attachment,
Unconnected to anyone at all,
Whatever is the joyful conduct of contentment,
Even Indra has difficulty finding it. ^8-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-88]]

Through these and other ways,
Having considered the qualities of solitude,
With discursive thoughts fully pacified,
One should cultivate the awakening mind. ^8-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-89]]

The equality of self and other
One should first strive to meditate upon.
Since happiness and suffering are equal,
All should be protected like oneself. ^8-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-90]]

Although the divisions of hands and so forth are many,
Just as they are one as a body to be fully protected,
Likewise, the different migrating beings, with their happiness and suffering,
Are all one in equally desiring happiness, like oneself. ^8-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-91]]

If my suffering
Does not harm the bodies of others,
Even so, that suffering is mine,
Unbearable due to clinging to self. ^8-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-92]]

Likewise, although the sufferings of others
Do not fall upon me,
Even so, that suffering is mine,
Difficult to bear due to clinging to self. ^8-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-93]]

I should dispel the suffering of others
Because it is suffering, like my own suffering.
I should benefit others
Because they are sentient beings, like my own body. ^8-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-94]]

When both myself and others
Are equal in desiring happiness,
What difference is there with myself,
That I strive for the happiness of myself alone? ^8-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-95]]

When both myself and others
Are equal in not desiring suffering,
What difference is there with myself,
That I protect myself and not others? ^8-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-96]]

If, because their suffering
Does not harm me, I do not protect them,
Future suffering also
If it does not harm, why protect against it? ^8-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-97]]

The thought that "I will experience it"
Is a false conception;
Thus, the one who dies is another,
And the one who is born is another. ^8-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-98]]

If whenever the suffering of anyone
Is to be protected by that one himself,
The suffering of the foot is not the hand's,
Why should it be protected by it? ^8-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-99]]

If you say, "Though it is illogical, here
One engages through grasping at self,"
That which is illogical regarding self and others
Is to be abandoned to the best of one's ability. ^8-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-100]]

What are called a continuum and a collection
Are false, like a rosary, an army, and so forth.
Since there is no one who possesses suffering,
Therefore, who will have control over this? ^8-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-101]]

Since suffering has no owner,
All are entirely without distinction.
Because it is suffering, it is to be dispelled.
What is the use of restriction there? ^8-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-102]]

"Why should the suffering of all
Be prevented?" There is no room for debate:
If prevented, all should be prevented.
If not, I too am like sentient beings. ^8-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-103]]

"Since compassion has much suffering,
Why forcibly generate it?" If one asks:
When one contemplates the suffering of beings,
How is the suffering of compassion great? ^8-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-104]]

If by a single suffering
Many sufferings will be eliminated,
The compassionate one should generate that suffering
For oneself and others. ^8-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-105]]

Therefore, Supuṣpacandra,
Even while knowing the king's harm,
Did not dispel his own suffering,
So that the suffering of many would be exhausted. ^8-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-106]]

Thus, those whose continuums are accustomed,
Because they delight in pacifying others' suffering,
Like a swan into a lotus pool,
Will plunge even into the Avīci hell. ^8-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-107]]

When sentient beings are liberated,
The ocean of joy that exists—
Is that itself not sufficient?
What is the use of desiring liberation? ^8-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-108]]

Therefore, even when acting for the benefit of others,
There is no conceit or amazement.
Delighting solely in the benefit of others,
There is no hope for a ripened result. ^8-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-109]]

Therefore, just as to a small degree
I protect myself even from unpleasant words,
Likewise, a mind of protecting others and
A mind of compassion should be cultivated in this way. ^8-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-110]]

Through habituation, regarding others'
Drops of semen and blood,
Even though they lack any entity,
Just as one apprehends them as "I," ^8-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-111]]

Likewise, regarding the bodies of others as well,
Why not apprehend them as "I"?
Positing my own body as others
Is not difficult in that way. ^8-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-112]]

Knowing oneself to be flawed, and others
To be an ocean of qualities,
Completely discarding grasping at self
And taking up others should be meditated upon. ^8-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-113]]

Just as hands and so forth
Are accepted as being limbs of the body,
Likewise, as limbs of transmigratory beings,
Why are embodied beings not accepted? ^8-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-114]]

Just as towards this selfless body
The mind of "I" has arisen through habituation,
Likewise, towards other sentient beings as well,
Why would the mind of "I" not arise through habituation? ^8-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-115]]

If it is like that, the benefit of others,
Even when done, amazement and conceit do not arise,
Just as when one has eaten food oneself,
There arises no hope for a reward. ^8-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-116]]

Therefore, just as to a small degree
I protect myself even from unpleasant words,
Likewise, a mind of protecting transmigratory beings and
A mind of compassion should be habituated. ^8-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-117]]

Therefore, the protector Avalokiteśvara,
Out of great compassion, for transmigratory beings,
In order to dispel the fears of the assembly,
Blessed even his own name. ^8-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-118]]

One should not turn back from difficulties,
For by the power of habituation in this way,
Even someone whose name one feared to hear,
Without them, one becomes joyless. ^8-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-119]]

Whoever, regarding oneself and others,
Wishes to quickly protect them,
The exchange of self and others,
Which is the supreme secret, should be practiced. ^8-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-120]]

Through attachment to this body of mine,
Fear arises at even small sources of fear;
Towards that body which gives rise to fear,
Who would not be hostile as if towards an enemy? ^8-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-121]]

For whichever body's illnesses like hunger and thirst,
Wishing to perform the treatment to cure them,
Birds, fish, and wild animals
Are killed and ambushed on the road. ^8-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-122]]

Whoever, for the sake of profit and respect,
Kills even their father and mother, and,
Having stolen the wealth of the Three Jewels,
Will therefore burn in the Incessant Hell; ^8-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-123]]

What wise person, towards that body,
Would desire, protect, and venerate it?
Towards this, who, as if towards an enemy,
Would not look and not despise it? ^8-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-124]]

"If I give, what will I consume?"—
Thinking of one's own purpose is the way of a demon.
"If I consume, what will I give?"—
Thinking of others' purpose is the quality of a deity. ^8-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-125]]

If, for one's own sake, one harms others,
One will be tormented in hell and so forth.
If, for others' sake, one harms oneself,
One will obtain all excellences. ^8-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-126]]

Through wishing for oneself to be exalted,
One will fall to bad migrations and become base and foolish.
If that very thing is shifted to others,
One will obtain happy migrations and respect. ^8-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-127]]

If one employs others for one's own purpose,
One will experience servitude and so forth.
If one uses oneself for the purpose of others,
One will experience lordship and so forth. ^8-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-128]]

Whatever happiness there is in the world,
All of that arises from wishing for others' happiness.
Whatever suffering there is in the world,
All of that arises from wishing for one's own happiness. ^8-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-129]]

What need is there to say much?
Childish beings act for their own purpose, and
The Sage acts for the purpose of others.
Look at the difference between these two! ^8-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-130]]

One's own happiness and the sufferings of others,
If one does not perfectly exchange them,
One will not achieve Buddhahood, and
Even in cyclic existence there will be no happiness. ^8-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-131]]

Let alone the next world,
With servants not doing their work and
Masters not paying wages,
Even the purpose of this life will not be accomplished. ^8-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-132]]

Of that which accomplishes seen and unseen happiness,
Entirely abandoning the excellent joy,
Through the cause of inflicting suffering on others,
The foolish take on unbearable suffering. ^8-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-133]]

Whatever harm there is in the world, and
However much fear and suffering there is,
If all of that arises from grasping at a self,
What use is that great demon to me? ^8-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-134]]

If one does not completely let go of oneself,
One cannot abandon suffering,
Just as if one does not let go of fire,
One cannot avoid being burned. ^8-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-135]]

Therefore, to pacify harm to myself and
To pacify the suffering of others,
I should give myself to others and
Hold others as myself. ^8-135

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-136]]

"I am under the control of others"—
O mind, you should definitely know this, and
Apart from the purpose of all sentient beings,
Now you should not think of anything else. ^8-136

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-137]]

With eyes and so forth that are under the control of others,
It is not appropriate to accomplish one's own purpose.
With eyes and so forth that are for their purpose,
It is not appropriate to act wrongly toward them. ^8-137

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-138]]

Therefore, sentient beings should be made principal, and
Whatever I see on my body,
Having taken it away,
I will use it to benefit others. ^8-138

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-139]]

Having made the lower and so forth into oneself, and
Having made oneself into the other,
With a mind free of conceptualization,
I should meditate on jealousy, rivalry, and pride. ^8-139

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-140]]

This one is respected, but I am not.
I have not found wealth like this one, and
This one is praised, while I am disparaged.
This one is happy, but I am suffering. ^8-140

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-141]]

I do all the work, and
This one abides in happiness itself.
This one is great in the world, and
I am known as inferior and without good qualities. ^8-141

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-142]]

What can be done about being without good qualities?
All of us are indeed endowed with good qualities.
There are those compared to whom this one is inferior, and
There are those compared to whom I am supreme. ^8-142

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-143]]

The decline of discipline, view, and so forth
Is due to the power of afflictions; it is not under my control.
I should be healed as much as possible, and
I will willingly accept even harm. ^8-143

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-144]]

However, if I am not to be healed by this one,
Why does he despise me?
What use are his good qualities to me?
This one himself possesses good qualities. ^8-144

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-145]]

Abiding in the jaws of the vicious lower realms,
This one is without compassion for beings.
Outwardly conceited about his good qualities,
He wishes to contend with the wise. ^8-145

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-146]]

Having looked at those equal to me,
In order to make myself superior,
My wealth and respect
Will definitely be established even through dispute. ^8-146

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-147]]

By all means, my good qualities
Will be clear to the whole world, and
Furthermore, whatever good qualities this one has,
I will ensure that no one hears of them. ^8-147

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-148]]

My faults will also be hidden, and
I will be venerated, but not this one.
Today I have well found wealth, and
I will be respected, but not this one. ^8-148

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-149]]

Upon this one being made miserable,
I will look with joy for a long time.
He will be the laughingstock of all beings, and
Mutual disparagement will be brought about. ^8-149

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-150]]

This miserable one is even renowned
To be contending together with me.
Are his learning and wisdom, or
Form, caste, and wealth equal to mine? ^8-150

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-151]]

Thus proclaimed to all,
Having heard of my good qualities,
Thrilled with the arising of joy,
I will fully enjoy happiness and well-being. ^8-151

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-152]]

Even if this one has wealth,
If he does my work,
Having given him just enough to live on,
I will take it by my power. ^8-152

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-153]]

This one shall be made to fall from happiness, and
Be constantly subjected to my harm.
In all hundreds of ways,
This one has harmed me in saṃsāra. ^8-153

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-154]]

O mind, because you desired to accomplish your own purpose,
Even though countless eons have passed,
With such great weariness,
You have accomplished only suffering. ^8-154

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-155]]

Thus, you must certainly
Fully engage in the purpose of others.
Since the words of the Muni are infallible,
You will see its good qualities later. ^8-155

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-156]]

If in former times
You had performed this action,
A state other than the perfect happiness of a Buddha,
Such a situation as this could not possibly occur. ^8-156

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-157]]

Therefore, just as
Upon the drops of semen and blood of others,
You have grasped as "I",
Likewise make it a habit with others too. ^8-157

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-158]]

Having become a great spy for others,
Whatever appears on my body,
Having stolen those very things,
You should practice what is beneficial for others. ^8-158

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-159]]

"I am happy, others are unhappy,
I am high, others are low,
I do what is beneficial, others do not."
Why do you not feel jealous of yourself? ^8-159

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-160]]

Separate yourself from happiness, and
Apply the suffering of others to yourself.
Thinking, "What is this one doing at any time?"
Examine your own faults. ^8-160

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-161]]

Even when faults have been committed by others,
Transform them into your own faults;
And even if a fault committed by yourself is small,
Confess it fully to many people. ^8-161

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-162]]

By exalting the reputation of others,
Suppress your own reputation.
Like the lowest of servants,
Employ yourself for all purposes. ^8-162

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-163]]

This one is of a faulty nature,
Do not praise it for its adventitious good qualities.
By all means, regarding its good qualities,
Act so that no one at all will know them. ^8-163

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-164]]

In short, for the sake of your own purpose,
Whatever harm you have done to others,
For the sake of the purpose of sentient beings,
Let that harm descend upon yourself. ^8-164

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-165]]

Lest it become domineering,
Do not foster its strength.
Just like a new bride,
Keep it bound by modesty and fear. ^8-165

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-166]]

"Do that! Remain like that!
You must not do like that!"
In this way, subjugate it,
And if it transgresses that, it must be punished. ^8-166

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-167]]

However, even if instructed like that,
O mind, if you do not do so,
Since all faults rely upon you,
You yourself will just have to be punished. ^8-167

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-168]]

The former times when you ruined me
Were a different time;
Now I see you, where will you go?
I shall destroy all your arrogance. ^8-168

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-169]]

"I still have my own purpose"—
Cast away that thought which thinks it exists!
Since I have sold you to others,
Do not feel sorrow, offer your services. ^8-169

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-170]]

If, becoming careless,
I do not give you to sentient beings,
You will surely hand me over
To the guards of hell. ^8-170

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-171]]

In that way, you have indeed
Handed me over, and I have suffered for a long time;
Remembering those grudges,
I shall destroy your thinking of your own purpose. ^8-171

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-172]]

Therefore, if I wish to be happy,
I must not make myself happy.
Therefore, if I wish to protect myself,
I must always protect others. ^8-172

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-173]]

To whatever extent this body
Is thoroughly cared for,
To that very extent, it falls into
Becoming extremely fragile. ^8-173

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-174]]

And even for that which has fallen in that way,
Its desires, even by this entire earth,
Cannot be fulfilled;
So who is able to fulfill its desires? ^8-174

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-175]]

Desiring the impossible brings afflictions,
And disappointed hopes will also arise.
But for one who does not depend on anything,
Their excellence knows no exhaustion. ^8-175

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-176]]

Therefore, for the desires of the body,
One should not give the opportunity to increase.
Not grasping at whatever is pleasant—
That is an excellent thing. ^8-176

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-177]]

Ultimately ending in ashes,
Motionless, to be moved by others,
An unbearable, impure form—
Why do I grasp at this as 'I'? ^8-177

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-178]]

Whether it is alive or whether it is dead,
What use is this machine to me?
If there is no difference between this and a clod of earth,
Alas, why do I not dispel my pride? ^8-178

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-179]]

By taking care of the body,
Having accumulated meaningless suffering,
Attachment and anger—
What use are they for this thing similar to wood? ^8-179

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-180]]

Whether it is cared for by me in this way,
Or whether it is eaten by vultures and the like,
Since it has no attachment and no anger,
Why do I become attached to it? ^8-180

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-181]]

That which is angered by blame,
And that which is delighted by praise—
If it itself lacks any knowing,
For whose sake have I toiled? ^8-181

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-182]]

Whoever desires this body,
If I think, 'They and I are friends,'
Since everyone desires their own body,
Why do I not rejoice in them? ^8-182

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-183]]

Therefore, without attachment,
I shall give up my body to benefit beings.
Thus, even though it has many faults,
It should be retained as an instrument for action. ^8-183

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-184]]

Therefore, enough with childish behavior!
I shall follow in the footsteps of the wise,
And, remembering the teachings on conscientiousness,
I shall turn away from sleep and lethargy. ^8-184

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-185]]

Therefore, in order to clear away obscurations,
Having withdrawn the mind from wrong paths,
Constantly upon the perfect object,
I shall rest in meditative equipoise. ^8-185

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, the eighth chapter, called The Teaching on Meditative Concentration. ^8-a

## Chapter Nine: The Perfection of Wisdom ^9-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-1]]

All these branches
The Sage taught for the sake of wisdom.
Therefore, those who wish to pacify
Suffering should generate wisdom. ^9-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-2]]

The relative and the ultimate—
These are asserted to be the two truths.
The ultimate is not the domain of the intellect;
The intellect is said to be the relative. ^9-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-3]]

In this regard, two kinds of world are seen:
Yogis and ordinary people.
In this regard, the ordinary world
Is invalidated by the world of yogis. ^9-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-4]]

Even yogis, due to differences in intellect,
Are invalidated by successively higher ones.
Through examples accepted by both,
Because they are unanalyzed, for the sake of the result. ^9-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-5]]

People of the world see entities,
And conceptualize them as truly real,
Not as being like illusions; therefore, here,
Yogis and the world dispute. ^9-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-6]]

Even the direct perception of forms and so forth
Is through convention, not through valid cognition.
That is false, just like the convention
Of being clean and so forth regarding what is unclean and so forth. ^9-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-7]]

"For the sake of guiding the world,
The Protector taught entities. In reality,
They are not momentary."
If one says, "This contradicts even the relative," ^9-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-8]]

There is no fault in the yogi's relative;
In relation to the world, they see reality.
Otherwise, the apprehension of women as unclean
Would be invalidated by the world. ^9-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-9]]

Merit from a Victorious One who is like an illusion
Is just as it is for one who truly exists.
"If sentient beings are like illusions,
How are they born after dying?" ^9-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-10]]

As long as conditions are assembled,
For that long, even illusions will occur.
How, merely through a long continuum,
Does a sentient being truly exist? ^9-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-11]]

In killing an illusory person and so forth,
Because there is no mind, there is no sin;
But regarding that which possesses an illusory mind,
Merit and sin arise. ^9-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-12]]

Because mantras and so forth lack the capacity,
Illusory minds do not arise.
Arising from various conditions,
Illusions themselves are also various. ^9-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-13]]

That a single condition has the capacity for everything
Nowhere exists.
"If the ultimate is nirvāṇa,
And saṃsāra is the relative, in that case, ^9-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-14]]

Even a Buddha would cycle in saṃsāra, so
What is the point of bodhisattva conduct?"
If the continuum of conditions is not severed,
Even an illusion is not reversed; ^9-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-15]]

But because the continuum of conditions is severed,
It does not arise even relatively.
"When even the deceptive mind does not exist,
By what is the illusion perceived?" ^9-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-16]]

When, for you, even the illusion itself
Does not exist, then what is perceived?
"If it exists as another in reality,
That aspect is the mind itself." ^9-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-17]]

If the mind itself is an illusion,
Then what is seen by what?
Even the Protector of the World
Stated that the mind does not see the mind. ^9-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-18]]

Just as the blade of a sword
Does not cut itself, so too the mind.
If [you say], "Just as a lamp
Perfectly illuminates its own entity," ^9-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-19]]

A lamp is not something to be illuminated,
Because it is not obscured by darkness.
"Unlike a crystal, a blue thing
Does not depend on another for its blueness; ^9-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-20]]

So also some are seen to depend
On another, and some not to depend."
When it is not blue itself,
It does not make itself blue. ^9-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-21]]

That a lamp illuminates
Is known by cognition and thus expressed;
That the intellect itself is luminous,
Having been known by what, is so expressed? ^9-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-22]]

If it is not seen by anyone at all,
Whether it is luminous or not luminous,
Like the alluring pose of a barren woman's daughter,
Expressing it is meaningless. ^9-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-23]]

"If self-awareness does not exist,
How is consciousness remembered?"
It is remembered from its connection to experiencing another,
Like the poison of a rat. ^9-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-24]]

"Endowed with other conditions,
Because it sees, it illuminates itself."
Through the application of accomplished eye-ointment,
A vase is seen, but not the eye-ointment itself. ^9-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-25]]

How things are seen, heard, and known
Is not to be refuted here;
Here, what becomes the cause of suffering,
The conceptualization of true existence, is to be reversed. ^9-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-26]]

"Illusion is not other than the mind,
And if it is not conceived as being not other,"
If it is a real entity, how is it not other?
If it is not other, it does not exist as an entity. ^9-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-27]]

Just as an illusion, although not true,
Is an object of sight, so is the perceiving mind.
"If saṃsāra has a real basis, it functions;
Otherwise, it would be like space." ^9-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-28]]

"Since a non-entity depends on a real entity,
How could it be endowed with a function?"
Your mind, being without a companion,
Would become entirely alone. ^9-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-29]]

When the mind is free of an apprehended object,
At that time, all are Tathāgatas.
Even in that case, in Mind Only,
What merit is there in conceptualizing it? ^9-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-30]]

Even if one knows things to be like an illusion,
How are the afflictions repelled?
Since, toward an illusory woman,
Even her creator generates attachment. ^9-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-31]]

Her creator has not abandoned
The latent tendencies of the afflictions toward objects of knowledge;
Therefore, when he sees her,
His latent tendencies for emptiness are weak. ^9-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-32]]

By familiarizing with the latent tendencies of emptiness,
The latent tendencies of entities will be abandoned;
By familiarizing with "there is nothing at all,"
That too will later be abandoned. ^9-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-33]]

When one asserts, "Such and such does not exist,"
And the entity to be examined is not observed,
At that time, how can a non-entity without a basis
Remain before the mind? ^9-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-34]]

When both entities and non-entities
Do not remain before the mind,
At that time, since there is no other aspect,
It is completely pacified, without an object of focus. ^9-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-35]]

Just as a wish-fulfilling jewel and a wish-granting tree
Completely fulfill hopes,
Likewise, due to those to be tamed and prayers of aspiration,
The form of the Victorious One appears. ^9-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-36]]

For example, when a garuḍa shrine
Has been accomplished and its creator has passed away,
Even though a long time has passed since his death,
It pacifies poison and so forth. ^9-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-37]]

In accordance with the bodhisattva's conduct,
The shrine of the Victorious One is also accomplished;
Even when the Bodhisattva has passed
Into nirvāṇa, he accomplishes all aims. ^9-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-38]]

"Since offerings are made to one without a mind,
How can there be a result?"
Because whether he remains or has passed into nirvāṇa,
It is taught that it is exactly the same. ^9-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-39]]

Whether conventionally or in reality,
It is suitable, for scriptures state that there is a result;
For example, toward a true Buddha,
Just as it is endowed with a result. ^9-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-40]]

"One is liberated by seeing the truths;
What is the use of seeing emptiness?"
Because scriptures state that without this path,
There is no awakening. ^9-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-41]]

"If the Mahāyāna is not established,
How is your scripture established?"
"Because this is established for both of us."
"Initially, this was not established for you." ^9-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-42]]

By whatever conditions you believe in that,
That is the same for the Mahāyāna too.
If it is true because two others accept it,
The Vedas and so forth would also become true. ^9-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-43]]

If you say the Mahāyāna is subject to dispute,
The scriptures are disputed by non-Buddhists,
And other scriptures are disputed by ourselves and others;
Since they are subject to dispute, they would have to be discarded. ^9-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-44]]

If being a monk is the root of the teaching,
Even being a monk is difficult to establish.
For those whose minds have apprehension,
Even nirvāṇa is difficult to establish. ^9-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-45]]

If one is liberated by abandoning afflictions,
It would occur immediately upon that.
Even though there are no afflictions, in them
The power of karma is seen. ^9-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-46]]

As for the craving for appropriation,
If you say it is certain that it does not exist;
Even if that craving is not an affliction,
Why would it not exist, like ignorance? ^9-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-47]]

Craving occurs due to the condition of feeling;
Those ones also have feelings.
A mind that is endowed with apprehension
Will rest upon something. ^9-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-48]]

A mind separated from emptiness,
Though ceased, will arise again,
Like the meditative absorption without discrimination;
Therefore, one should meditate on emptiness. ^9-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-49]]

If any speech that enters into the sūtras
Is asserted to be spoken by the Buddha,
Is it not asserted that the majority of the Mahāyāna
Is equal to your sūtras? ^9-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-50]]

If, due to a single one that is not included,
All become faulty,
Then due to a single one that is equal to the sūtras,
Why are they not all spoken by the Victorious One? ^9-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-51]]

Any speech whose depth was unfathomed
By Mahākāśyapa and the rest,
Just because you do not understand it,
Who would make it unacceptable? ^9-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-52]]

For the sake of those who suffer due to ignorance,
Being freed from the extremes of attachment and fear,
Remaining in saṃsāra becomes established;
This is the result of emptiness. ^9-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-53]]

Thus, toward the side of emptiness,
Refutation is not appropriate.
Therefore, without hesitation,
One should meditate on emptiness. ^9-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-54]]

Of the obscurations of afflictions and knowables,
Emptiness is the antidote to the darkness.
By one who wishes to quickly become omniscient,
How could one not meditate on it? ^9-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-55]]

Whatever entity produces suffering,
From that, fear will arise.
Since emptiness pacifies suffering,
How can fear arise toward it? ^9-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-56]]

If any self existed,
Fear would arise from anything.
Since no self exists at all,
Who is there to be afraid? ^9-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-57]]

Teeth, hair, and nails are not the self;
The self is not bone or blood.
It is not snot, nor is it phlegm,
And it is neither serum nor pus. ^9-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-58]]

The self is not fat or sweat,
And lungs and liver are not the self.
The other internal organs are also not the self,
And the self is not feces or urine. ^9-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-59]]

Flesh and skin are not the self,
And warmth and wind are not the self.
The cavities are not the self, and in all ways
The six consciousnesses are not the self either. ^9-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-60]]

If the consciousness of sound is permanent,
It would apprehend sound at all times.
If there is no object of knowledge, what does it cognize,
And due to what is it called a consciousness? ^9-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-61]]

If there is consciousness without cognizing,
It would logically follow that wood is also consciousness.
Therefore, it is certain that without a nearby
Object of knowledge, there is no consciousness. ^9-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-62]]

If that very same consciousness cognizes form,
Why is there no hearing at that time?
If it is because sound is not nearby,
Then the consciousness of it is also non-existent. ^9-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-63]]

How could that which has the nature of apprehending sound
Become the apprehender of form?
One being both father and son
Is designated, but it is not ultimate reality. ^9-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-64]]

Thus, sattva, rajas, and
Tamas are neither son nor father;
And it is not seen to have a nature
Possessing the apprehension of sound. ^9-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-65]]

If, like an actor, it takes on a different guise,
And if that very same thing is seen, it is not permanent.
If that different guise is that very same thing,
That unity is an unprecedented one. ^9-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-66]]

If the different guise is not true,
Then explain its own nature.
If you say it is consciousness itself, then in that case,
It would logically follow that all people are one. ^9-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-67]]

Conscious and unconscious things, too,
Would become one, because their existence is the same.
When differences are false,
What then is the basis for similarity? ^9-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-68]]

The unconscious is not the self either,
Because it is unconscious, like a vase and so forth.
However, if you say it cognizes because it possesses consciousness,
When it cognizes, it logically follows that the unconscious perishes. ^9-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-69]]

But if the self has no change,
What is done to it by consciousness?
Thus, unconscious and devoid of activity,
Space might as well be made into the self. ^9-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-70]]

If the self does not exist,
The connection between actions and results is not logical,
Because if one perishes after performing an action,
Whose action will it be? ^9-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-71]]

That the bases of the action and the result are different, and
That there is no self operating there,
Is established for both of us, so
Is it not pointless to debate here? ^9-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-72]]

That the one possessing the cause also possesses the result—
To see this is impossible.
It is in dependence upon a single continuum
That an agent and an experiencer are taught. ^9-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-73]]

The mind of the past and the future
Is not the self, because it does not exist.
But if the arisen mind is the self,
When that perishes, there is no self either. ^9-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-74]]

For example, the trunk of a banana tree,
When divided into parts, is nothing at all;
Likewise, when analytically
Searched for, the self is also not real. ^9-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-75]]

If sentient beings do not exist,
Toward whom does one practice compassion?
Toward whatever is imputed by the delusion
That is accepted for the sake of the result. ^9-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-76]]

If sentient beings do not exist, whose is the result?
That is true, but it is asserted due to delusion.
For the sake of entirely pacifying suffering,
The delusion regarding the result should not be reversed. ^9-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-77]]

Pride, which is the cause of suffering,
Increases because of the delusion of a self.
If you say, "Even that cannot be reversed,"
Meditating on selflessness is the best. ^9-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-78]]

The body is not the feet or the calves,
The thighs and the waist are also not the body.
The belly and the back are not the body,
And the chest and the arms are not the body. ^9-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-79]]

The ribs and the hands are not the body,
The armpits and the shoulders are not the body.
The inner organs are also not it,
And the head and the neck are not the body;
What is the body in all this? ^9-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-80]]

If this body were to abide in all of these
In a partial manner,
The parts would indeed abide in the parts,
But where does it itself abide? ^9-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-81]]

If the entirety of the body itself
Were to abide in the hands and so forth,
However many hands and so forth there are,
There would be that many bodies. ^9-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-82]]

If the body does not exist outside or inside,
How does the body exist in the hands and so forth?
If it does not exist other than the hands and so forth,
How does it exist? ^9-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-83]]

Therefore, there is no body; with regard to the hands and so forth,
Due to delusion, there is the concept of a body,
Because of a specific configuration of shapes,
Just like the concept of a person with regard to a cairn. ^9-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-84]]

As long as the collection of conditions lasts,
The body appears like a person.
Likewise, as long as it is present in the hands and so forth,
For that long, the body appears there. ^9-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-85]]

Likewise, because it is a collection of fingers,
What would the hand be?
And since that too is a collection of joints,
The joint, too, by its division into its own parts, ^9-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-86]]

And the part also by its division into particles,
And that particle also by its division into directional parts,
And since the division of directions is devoid of parts,
It is like space; therefore, even particles do not exist. ^9-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-87]]

Thus, what intelligent person would be attached
To form, which is like a dream?
When the body does not exist in that way,
Then what is a man and what is a woman? ^9-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-88]]

If suffering truly existed,
Why does it not harm the very joyful?
If happiness [truly existed], for those overcome with grief and so forth,
Why do delicious foods and so forth not cause joy? ^9-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-89]]

If it is because it is overshadowed by something stronger
That it is not experienced,
How can that which is not of the nature of experience
Be a feeling? ^9-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-90]]

"Suffering exists in a subtle state;
Has its gross state not been removed?"
If you say, "That is merely a joy distinct from that,"
Then that subtle state too is [a state] of that [joy]. ^9-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-91]]

If, because a contrary condition has arisen,
Suffering does not arise,
Is it not established that the conception of feeling
Is merely conceptual clinging? ^9-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-92]]

For this very reason, the antidote to this
Is to meditate on this analytical discernment;
That which arises from the field of analysis
Is the food of the yogi's concentration. ^9-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-93]]

If there is a gap between the sense faculties and objects,
Where would they meet?
Even if there is no gap, they are a single entity,
So what would meet with what? ^9-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-94]]

An atom does not enter an atom,
For they have no empty space and are of equal size.
Without entering, there is no mixing,
And without mixing, there is no meeting. ^9-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-95]]

How could it be reasonable to say
That what is partless can meet?
If meeting and being partless
Have been seen together, show it! ^9-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-96]]

For consciousness, which has no body,
Meeting is not reasonable at all.
Neither is it for an aggregation, because it does not exist as a real entity,
Just as was analytically examined before. ^9-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-97]]

Thus, if contact does not exist,
From what would feeling arise?
What is the purpose of this weariness?
What would harm what? ^9-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-98]]

When there is no feeler at all,
And feeling also does not exist,
At that time, having seen this situation,
Why would craving not be turned away? ^9-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-99]]

Seeing or even touching,
Have a nature like a dream or an illusion.
Because it arises simultaneously with the mind,
Feeling is not perceived by it. ^9-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-100]]

Even by what arises before or after,
It is remembered, but not experienced.
It does not experience itself,
And it is not experienced by others either. ^9-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-101]]

There is no feeler at all,
Therefore, feeling itself is not real.
Thus, for this selfless aggregate,
How could it be harmed by this? ^9-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-102]]

The mind does not abide in the sense faculties,
Nor in forms and so forth, nor in between them.
The mind is not inside, nor is it outside,
And it is not found anywhere else. ^9-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-103]]

That which is not in the body, nor anywhere else,
Not mixed, nor apart from it anywhere,
Is nothing whatsoever; therefore,
Sentient beings are by nature in nirvāṇa. ^9-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-104]]

If consciousness exists before the object of knowledge,
Directed towards what would it arise?
If consciousness and the object of knowledge are simultaneous,
Directed towards what would it arise? ^9-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-105]]

Or if it exists after the object of knowledge,
Then from what does consciousness arise?
In this way, of all phenomena,
Arising cannot be realized. ^9-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-106]]

If, in this way, conventional reality does not exist,
How could the two truths exist in that?
And if that is due to another conventional reality,
How could sentient beings pass into nirvāṇa? ^9-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-107]]

This is the conception of another mind;
It is not one's own conventional reality.
If it is ascertained later, it exists;
If not, conventional reality itself does not exist. ^9-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-108]]

Conception and the object to be conceived,
The two are mutually dependent.
Relying on what is conventionally known,
All analyses are expressed. ^9-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-109]]

When analysis
Is analyzed by analysis,
Then that analysis too,
Because it is analyzed, has no end. ^9-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-110]]

If the object of analysis has been analyzed,
There is no basis for analysis.
Because there is no basis, it does not arise,
And that too is expressed as nirvāṇa. ^9-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-111]]

According to those who hold these two to be true,
That very position rests in great difficulty.
If the object is established by the power of consciousness,
What is the basis for the existence of consciousness itself? ^9-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-112]]

Or if consciousness is established from the object of knowledge,
What is the basis for the existence of the object of knowledge?
If they exist by the power of one another,
Both also would not exist. ^9-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-113]]

If without a son there is no father,
From what does the son himself arise?
In the absence of a son, there is no father;
Likewise, those two themselves do not exist. ^9-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-114]]

A sprout arises from a seed, and
Just as that very seed is known by it,
By consciousness, which arises from the object of knowledge,
Why is the existence of that [object] not known? ^9-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-115]]

By a consciousness that is other than the sprout,
If the existence of the seed is known,
By which that object of knowledge is known,
From what is the existence of the consciousness itself known? ^9-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-116]]

For now, by worldly direct perception,
All causes are seen.
The variations such as the stalk of the lotus
Are produced by the variations of causes. ^9-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-117]]

If one asks, what made the variations of causes?
From the variations of prior causes themselves.
Why are causes able to produce effects?
From the power of prior causes themselves. ^9-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-118]]

If Īśvara is the cause of beings,
Tell me, for now, what is Īśvara?
If one says it is the elements, so be it;
But what is the use of exhausting oneself over a mere name? ^9-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-119]]

However, earth and so forth are multiple,
Impermanent, devoid of movement, not divine,
Objects to be stepped upon, and impure;
They are not Īśvara himself. ^9-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-120]]

Īśvara is not space, because it is devoid of movement;
He is not the self, because it was already refuted before;
And as for a creator who is inconceivable,
What is the use of speaking of the inconceivable? ^9-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-121]]

And what is it that he wishes to produce?
Are not the self, those such as earth,
And the essence of Īśvara permanent?
Consciousness arises from the object of knowledge, and ^9-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-122]]

Happiness and suffering are from beginningless karma;
Tell me, what was produced by him?
If the cause has no beginning,
How can the effect have a beginning? ^9-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-123]]

Why does he not create continuously?
He does not depend on another.
If there is nothing else that was not made by him,
Upon what would he depend? ^9-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-124]]

If he depends, then the collection itself
Would be the cause, and not Īśvara.
When assembled, there is no power not to produce,
And without them, there is no power to produce. ^9-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-125]]

If Īśvara creates without desiring it,
It follows that he is under the control of another.
Even if he desires, he is dependent upon desire.
If he creates, how is he Īśvara? ^9-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-126]]

As for those who assert permanent atoms,
They have also already been refuted earlier.
The permanent Pradhāna is the cause of beings;
This is what the Sāṃkhyas assert. ^9-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-127]]

The qualities known as Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas,
Abiding in equilibrium,
Are termed Pradhāna;
In disequilibrium, they are said to be beings. ^9-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-128]]

For one thing to have three natures
Is illogical, therefore it does not exist.
Likewise, the qualities do not exist,
Because they too would each have three aspects. ^9-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-129]]

If the qualities do not exist, sound and so forth
Would be very far from existing.
In insentient objects like cloth and so forth,
It is impossible for pleasure and so forth to exist. ^9-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-130]]

If things are the nature of their cause,
Have things not already been analyzed?
Your cause is also pleasure and so forth,
And from that, cloth and so forth do not arise. ^9-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-131]]

Pleasure and so forth are from cloth and so forth;
Since they do not exist, pleasure and so forth do not exist.
Moreover, permanent pleasure and so forth
Are never observed. ^9-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-132]]

If the manifestation of pleasure and so forth exists,
Why is the experience not apprehended?
If that itself becomes subtle,
How can it be both coarse and subtle? ^9-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-133]]

Since it becomes subtle after abandoning coarseness,
That coarseness and subtlety are impermanent.
Likewise, why do you not assert
That all things are impermanent? ^9-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-134]]

If coarseness is not other than pleasure,
Manifest pleasure is impermanent.
If you assert that nothing nonexistent
Arises, because it does not exist, ^9-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-135]]

The arising of what is unmanifest
Remains, even though you do not assert it.
If the result abides in the cause,
One who eats food would eat excrement. ^9-135

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-136]]

With the price of a cotton cloth,
Buy a cotton seed and wear it.
If the ignorant world does not see it,
It is established by the knowers of reality. ^9-136

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-137]]

That knowledge is also in the world;
Since it exists, why is it not seen?
If the world is not a valid cognition,
Seeing the manifest is also not true. ^9-137

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-138]]

If valid cognition is not valid,
Is what is measured by it not false?
In reality, emptiness;
Meditating on it therefore becomes untenable. ^9-138

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-139]]

Without contacting the imputed entity,
Its nonentity is not apprehended.
Therefore, whatever entity is false,
Its nonentity is clearly false. ^9-139

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-140]]

Thus, when a child dies in a dream,
The conceptualization thinking, "He does not exist,"
For the conceptualization of his existence
Is the block, and that is also false. ^9-140

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-141]]

Therefore, by analyzing in this way,
Nothing whatever exists without a cause.
In individual or assembled
Conditions, it also does not abide. ^9-141

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-142]]

Neither does it come from elsewhere,
Nor does it abide, nor does it go.
This which the ignorant take as true,
What difference does it have from an illusion? ^9-142

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-143]]

Whatever is emanated by an illusion,
And whatever is emanated by causes,
Where does it come from,
And where does it go? Examine this! ^9-143

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-144]]

Whatever, through the proximity of something,
Is seen, and is not when that is absent,
Is artificial and similar to a reflection;
How can there be true existence in that? ^9-144

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-145]]

For an entity that exists,
What need is there for a cause?
Yet even if it does not exist,
What need is there for a cause? ^9-145

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-146]]

Even by a hundred million causes,
A nonentity cannot be transformed.
How could that state be an entity?
And what else could become an entity? ^9-146

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-147]]

If existence is impossible at the time of nonexistence,
When will it become an existing entity?
Without the entity being produced,
It will not separate from that nonentity. ^9-147

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-148]]

If it does not separate from nonentity,
There is no opportunity for an entity to exist.
An entity also does not become nonexistent,
Because it would follow that it has two natures. ^9-148

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-149]]

Thus, there is no cessation,
And there is no entity; therefore,
All these migrating beings
Are always unproduced and unceased. ^9-149

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-150]]

Migrating beings are like a dream;
When analyzed, they are like a plantain tree.
Even passing into nirvana or not passing into nirvana,
In reality, have no difference. ^9-150

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-151]]

Thus, with entities being empty,
What is there to be gained, what is there to be lost?
Who is there to do honor,
Or what is there to completely despise? ^9-151

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-152]]

From what are happiness and suffering?
What is there to displease, what is there to please?
If searched for in reality,
Who is craving and for what do they crave? ^9-152

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-153]]

When analyzed, this world of living beings,
Who is it that will die here?
Who is it that will come to be, and who has been?
Who are relatives and friends? ^9-153

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-154]]

That all things are like space,
May those like me completely grasp.
Those who desire happiness for themselves,
Through causes of conflict and excitement, ^9-154

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-155]]

Become intensely agitated and elated.
With sorrow, exertion, and disputes,
Cutting and piercing one another,
And through negative deeds, they live in great difficulty. ^9-155

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-156]]

Having come again and again to the happy realms,
And having experienced much happiness,
After dying, into the sufferings of the lower realms,
Which are long-lasting and unbearable, they fall. ^9-156

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-157]]

In existence, there are many abysses,
And there, reality is not like this.
And because they mutually contradict each other there,
In existence, there is no such reality. ^9-157

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-158]]

Furthermore, there is the incomparable and unbearable
Boundless ocean of suffering.
There, one thus has little strength,
And there, life is also short. ^9-158

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-159]]

Even there, for livelihood and health,
Through activities, the weariness of hunger and sickness,
Sleep, harm, and likewise
Meaningless association with fools, ^9-159

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-160]]

Life passes quickly and meaninglessly;
Discernment is extremely hard to find.
And there, as for habituation to distraction,
Where is the method to reverse it? ^9-160

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-161]]

Even there, into the great lower realms,
Māra exerts himself to make them fall.
There, wrong paths are many, and
It is hard to overcome even doubt. ^9-161

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-162]]

Once again, leisure is hard to find, and
The appearance of a Buddha is extremely hard to encounter.
The river of afflictions is hard to abandon, so
Alas, suffering will continue in succession. ^9-162

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-163]]

Although suffering so extremely like this,
Those who do not see their own suffering,
These beings dwelling in the river of suffering,
Alas, are worthy of sorrow. ^9-163

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-164]]

For example, like some who bathe again and again,
And having done so, enter the fire again and again,
Thus, in extreme suffering,
Even while dwelling, they pride themselves on being happy. ^9-164

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-165]]

Thus, as if there were no aging and death,
To those who live behaving in such a way,
First of all, having been killed,
The unbearable fall into the lower realms will come. ^9-165

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-166]]

Thus, for those tormented by the fire of suffering,
Well-arisen from the clouds of merit,
With the rain of my own accumulation of happiness,
When will I pacify them? ^9-166

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-167]]

When, in a non-apprehending manner,
Having respectfully gathered the accumulation of merit,
To those who are ruined by apprehension,
Will I teach emptiness? ^9-167

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva, this is the ninth chapter, on the Perfection of Wisdom. ^9-a

## Chapter Ten: Dedication ^10-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-1]]

To the Conduct of a Bodhisattva,
By my having composed the Entering,
Through whatever virtue that is, may all wandering beings
Enter the bodhisattva conduct. ^10-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-2]]

In all directions, those with body and mind,
However many are sick with suffering,
May they, by my merit,
Attain an ocean of happiness and joy. ^10-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-3]]

For as long as they remain in saṃsāra,
May they never decline from happiness.
May wandering beings, of a bodhisattva,
Attain uninterrupted happiness. ^10-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-4]]

In the realms of the world, the hells,
However many there are,
In them, may embodied beings
Rejoice with the happiness of Sukhāvatī. ^10-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-5]]

May those tormented by cold attain warmth.
From the great clouds of the bodhisattvas,
By the boundless rivers that have arisen,
May those tormented by heat be cooled. ^10-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-6]]

May the forest of sword-leaves also
Become a dense sandalwood forest for them.
May the trunks of the śālmali trees also
Grow as wish-fulfilling trees. ^10-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-7]]

Waterfowl, ducks, and geese,
Swans and so forth—may they be beautified by their sweet calls.
With lakes possessing greatly fragrant lotuses,
May the regions of hell become delightful. ^10-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-8]]

May those heaps of coal become heaps of jewels.
May the burning ground be paved as a crystal ground.
May the mountains of the Crushing Hell also, of offerings
Become immeasurable palaces, filled with Sugatas. ^10-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-9]]

May the rain of embers, burning stones, and weapons
From now on become a rain of flowers.
And may that striking of one another with weapons
From now on become the tossing of flowers for the sake of play. ^10-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-10]]

May those sinking in the unfordable river that is like a fire pit,
Their flesh all destroyed, with bones the color of jasmine flowers,
By the power of my virtue attain the bodies of gods,
And abide together with goddesses in the Mandākinī. ^10-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-11]]

"Why are the men of Yama, and the unbearable crows and vultures terrified here?
Whose noble power is this, that entirely dispels darkness and generates joy and happiness?" Thinking thus,
Looking upward and seeing the blazing Vajrapāṇi residing in the center of the sky,
Freed from negativity through the force of intense joy, may they depart together with him. ^10-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-12]]

Through the falling of a rain of flowers mixed with scented water,
Seeing the burning embers of hell sizzling and being extinguished,
And thinking, "What is this sudden satisfaction of happiness?"
May the hell beings see Padmapāṇi. ^10-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-13]]

"Friends, cast away your fear and come quickly here to us!
By whose power we are freed from all suffering and the force of joy has arrived,
The one who entirely protects all wandering beings, who has given rise to bodhicitta and compassion,
The blazing youth with a topknot who makes us fearless, has arrived! ^10-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-14]]

See how the crowns of hundreds of gods make offerings to his lotus feet,
His eyes moistened with compassion, and a rain of multitudes of flowers falling upon his head,
In delightful tiered palaces containing thousands of goddesses proclaiming songs of praise!"
Seeing Mañjughoṣa like this, may the hell beings now cry out in joy. ^10-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-15]]

Likewise, by my roots of virtue, seeing Samantabhadra and other
Bodhisattvas' unobstructed pleasant clouds,
Letting fall a cool and fragrant rain,
May those sentient beings in hell become truly joyful. ^10-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-16]]

May animals be freed
From the fear of eating one another.
Just like the humans of Uttarakuru,
May the hungry ghosts become happy. ^10-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-17]]

Of noble Avalokiteśvara,
By the stream of milk flowing from his hands,
May the hungry ghosts be satisfied,
And having bathed, may they always be cooled. ^10-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-18]]

May the blind see with their eyes,
And the deaf always hear sounds.
Just as with the goddess Māyā,
May pregnant women give birth without harm. ^10-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-19]]

May the naked obtain clothing,
And the hungry obtain food,
May the thirsty obtain water
And delicious drinks. ^10-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-20]]

May the destitute obtain wealth,
May those tormented by sorrow obtain joy,
May the despondent be restored in mind,
And may their fortitude be perfect. ^10-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-21]]

However many sick sentient beings there are,
May they quickly be freed from illness.
May the diseases of wandering beings, without exception,
Never arise again. ^10-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-22]]

May the frightened be fearless,
May the bound be freed,
May the powerless become endowed with power,
And may their minds be tender toward one another. ^10-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-23]]

For all travelers,
May all directions be safe.
Whatever purpose they are traveling for,
May it be accomplished without effort. ^10-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-24]]

May those who embark on boats and large ships
Have the wishes in their minds fulfilled,
And having safely reached the shore of the water,
May they rejoice together with their relatives. ^10-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-25]]

May those wandering on astray and dreary paths
Meet with fellow travelers,
And without fear of bandits, tigers, and the like,
May they proceed easily without fatigue. ^10-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-26]]

In fearful, trackless places such as wildernesses,
May children, the elderly, and the unprotected,
And those who are asleep, intoxicated, or insane,
Be protected by deities. ^10-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-27]]

Freed from all unfree states,
Endowed with faith, wisdom, and compassion,
And perfect in sustenance and conduct,
May they always remember their past lives. ^10-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-28]]

Like the treasury of space,
May their enjoyments be inexhaustible,
And without dispute and without harm,
May they enjoy them independently. ^10-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-29]]

Whatever sentient beings have little splendor,
May they become endowed with great splendor.
Whatever ascetics have poor forms,
May they become endowed with perfect, beautiful forms. ^10-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-30]]

However many women there are in the world,
May they become men.
May the lowly obtain high status,
And may their pride be subdued. ^10-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-31]]

By this merit of mine,
May all sentient beings without exception
Abandon all harmful deeds
And always perform virtuous ones. ^10-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-32]]

Never separating from the awakening mind,
And dedicating themselves to the bodhisattva conduct,
May they be fully embraced by the buddhas,
And abandon the actions of Māra. ^10-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-33]]

May all those sentient beings
Have immeasurably long lives.
May they always live happily,
And may even the word "death" not be heard. ^10-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-34]]

With gardens of wish-fulfilling trees,
Buddhas, and the children of buddhas
Proclaiming the sweet sound of the Dharma,
May all directions be filled. ^10-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-35]]

Everywhere, may the ground
Be free of gravel and the like,
As even as the palm of a hand, and of lapis lazuli,
May its nature remain soft. ^10-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-36]]

In all the circles of retinues that exist,
May many bodhisattvas
Adorn the surface of the earth with their excellence,
And remain dwelling there. ^10-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-37]]

By all embodied beings, from birds,
Trees, and all rays of light,
And even from space, may the sound of the Dharma
Be heard unceasingly. ^10-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-38]]

May they always encounter buddhas
And the children of buddhas.
With boundless clouds of offerings,
May they make offerings to the gurus of the world. ^10-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-39]]

May the gods send rain on time,
And may the harvests be perfect.
May kings act in accordance with the Dharma,
And may the world also prosper. ^10-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-40]]

May medicines be endowed with potency,
And may the recitation of secret mantras be accomplished.
May ḍākinīs, rākṣasas, and the like
Be endowed with minds of compassion. ^10-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-41]]

May no sentient being ever suffer,
Or be afraid, or be belittled,
And may none ever be unhappy. ^10-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-42]]

May monasteries, with reading and
Recitation, remain well adorned.
May the Saṅgha always be in harmony,
And may the goals of the Saṅgha be accomplished. ^10-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-43]]

May monks who desire to train
Attain solitary places.
Having abandoned all distractions,
May they meditate with pliable minds. ^10-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-44]]

May nuns be endowed with provisions,
And may they abandon quarrels and harm.
Likewise, may all who are ordained
Remain with unblemished discipline. ^10-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-45]]

May those with broken discipline feel remorse,
And may they always exhaust their negative deeds.
Having also attained higher realms,
May their vows remain uncompromised even there. ^10-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-46]]

May the learned be honored
And also receive alms.
May their mindstreams be completely pure,
And may they be renowned in all directions. ^10-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-47]]

Without experiencing the sufferings of the lower realms,
And without engaging in hardships,
With bodies superior to those of the gods,
May they swiftly attain buddhahood. ^10-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-48]]

May all sentient beings repeatedly
Make offerings to all the buddhas.
With the inconceivable bliss of the buddhas,
May they always be endowed with happiness. ^10-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-49]]

May the bodhisattvas
Accomplish their intentions for the welfare of beings.
Whatever the protectors intend,
May that be accomplished for sentient beings. ^10-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-50]]

Likewise, may the pratyekabuddhas
And the śrāvakas also be happy. ^10-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-51]]

And through the kindness of Mañjuśrī,
Until I attain the ground of Perfect Joy,
May I always remember my past lives
And attain ordination. ^10-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-52]]

May I, even with simple food,
Live endowed with vitality.
In all my lifetimes,
May I attain the perfection of solitary places. ^10-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-53]]

Whenever I wish to look,
Or even wish to ask a slight question,
May I behold the protector Mañjuśrī himself,
Without any hindrance. ^10-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-54]]

As far as the limits of space in the ten directions,
In order to accomplish the aims of all sentient beings,
Just as Mañjuśrī acts,
May my conduct also be like that. ^10-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-55]]

As long as space abides,
And as long as beings remain,
So long may I also remain,
To dispel the suffering of beings. ^10-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-56]]

Whatever suffering there is for beings,
May it all ripen upon me.
Through the sangha of bodhisattvas,
May beings experience happiness. ^10-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-57]]

The sole medicine for the suffering of beings,
The source of all happiness,
May the teaching, along with gain and honor,
Remain for a long time. ^10-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-58]]

Through whose kindness a virtuous mind arises,
I prostrate to Mañjuśrī.
Through whose kindness I have grown,
I also prostrate to the spiritual friend. ^10-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-a]]

The tenth chapter, called Dedication, from Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva. ^10-a
