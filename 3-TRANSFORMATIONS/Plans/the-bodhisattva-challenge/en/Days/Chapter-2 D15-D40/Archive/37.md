# Day 37 — The thing we quietly assume

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Catch yourself thinking "there's always tomorrow" today
> **Detail:** Today's verse names the silent bet behind our days: "not today." Nothing actually backs it.

## Opening

We rarely say it out loud, but almost everything we postpone rests on one quiet assumption: not today. There is always tomorrow, later, some other time. Shantideva names that assumption and points out it has nothing behind it. Death is certain, and its timing is not. The verse is not trying to frighten us; it is trying to dislodge the autopilot that keeps putting off what matters most.

## From the Tradition

Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, puts his finger on why "I won't die today" is such a slippery thought. He pairs two facts. Death is certain, that much we admit. But its timing is completely uncertain, and that is the part we quietly ignore. Put them together and the comfortable thought collapses: if the day is not fixed, then no particular day, including this one, can be ruled out. So the silent "not today" we live by is a guess dressed up as a fact. Sazang's aim is not to make us morbid. It is to break the spell of postponement. Most of us are not refusing to live well; we are simply assuming there will be time to get to it. Once you see that the assumption has nothing under it, the things you keep meaning to do stop being someday things and quietly become today things.

## Today's Practice

Listen today for the words "later," "tomorrow," or "there's always time," out loud or in your head. When you catch one, pause and gently question it: is that actually promised? You do not have to overhaul your whole schedule. Just take one thing you were filing under "someday," something that genuinely matters, and move a small piece of it into today.
