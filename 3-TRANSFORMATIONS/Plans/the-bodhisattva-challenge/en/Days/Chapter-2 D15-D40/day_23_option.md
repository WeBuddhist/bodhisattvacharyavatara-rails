# Day 23 — Wish good to places you'll never reach

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Wish good today on someone you'll never meet
> **Detail:** Shantideva wishes offerings to keep flowing everywhere, always. Today, send a wish where you can't go.

## Opening

Something quietly changes in today's verses. Until now Shantideva has been offering things himself, in his mind. Now he steps back and simply wishes: may these clouds of offering stay and keep arising, may this rain of flowers keep falling, in every place, for as long as the world turns. He lets the good keep flowing where his own hands will never reach.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

> May rains of gems and flowers and more

> On all the jewels of the true dharma

## From the Tradition

Our care tends to stop at the edge of what we can do. If we cannot fix or reach a thing, we look away from it.

These verses stretch past that edge. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, reads them as a wish for the offerings to remain and keep arising on their own, the rain falling for as long as the world lasts. Shantideva is no longer the one carrying each gift; he is wishing good into places he will never visit, for beings he will never meet. This is something you can do today even when your hands are full. A sincere wish for the welfare of people far beyond your reach is not a small or empty thing. It widens the heart past its usual fence, and that wider heart is what the practice is growing.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Once today, bring to mind people you will never meet, somewhere you have heard of suffering you cannot reach, and quietly wish them well: may they find ease, may good things reach them. Let your care go where your hands cannot.
