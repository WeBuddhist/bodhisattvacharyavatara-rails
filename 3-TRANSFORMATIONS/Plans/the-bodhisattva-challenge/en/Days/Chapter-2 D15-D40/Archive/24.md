# Day 24 — Offer, praise, and bow

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Offer, praise, and bow once to the Three Jewels today
> **Detail:** Today's three verses close Chapter Two's worship: a gift, a word of praise, and a bow.

## Opening

Today three verses round off the long offering of Chapter Two, one for each of three acts. First Shantideva offers as the great bodhisattvas offer. Then he praises the buddhas and bodhisattvas, whom he calls oceans of good qualities. Last he bows, picturing his one body becoming as many bodies as there are atoms in all the worlds. Each act is given to the buddhas, the dharma, and the sangha.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Today asks for three things: an offering, a word of praise, and a bow. Each one reaches far past what we could actually carry out, and the commentators treat that as the point, not a problem. On the offering, Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, says you cannot fill space with gifts the way Manjushri does, so you offer by **aspiration**: you picture the gift and form the wish to give as he gives, and that wish is the offering. On the praise, Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, says to imagine far more praise than one voice could give, as if you had many mouths praising at once. When we say plainly that something in another person is good, the wish to compete with them gets weaker, and we feel glad for them instead. On the bow, Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, says to imagine your body becoming countless bodies, all of them bowing low. Pride wants us to feel above other people. Bowing does the opposite: we put ourselves low, and we look up to the buddhas, the dharma, and the sangha.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Once today, take a quiet minute before the buddhas, the dharma, and the sangha and do all three in turn. Offer something in your mind that you could never hand over, the sky or the sea. Say one true word of praise, for them or for a good quality you see in someone. Then make one slow bow. A gift, a word, and a bow is enough.
