## Today's Verse

> If, having made such a vow,
> I do not accomplish the task,
> having deceived all those beings,
> what will my destiny be like? ^4-4

> It is said that if a person
> merely thinks of giving even a small, ordinary thing,
> but does not give it,
> that person will become a hungry ghost. ^4-5

> If, having sincerely invited
> all beings to unsurpassed happiness,
> I then deceive them all,
> how can I go to happy realms? ^4-6

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 4 to 6 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. There is a difference between promising to give a little food and inviting all beings as guests. That difference makes us take our everyday promises seriously too.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

> Sagaramati, it is like this. Suppose a king, or a king's chief minister, invites all the people of the city and says, "Tomorrow I will give you a meal." Then he lets it slip and sets out no food or drink. In that case he has deceived that whole crowd of people.

This comparison shows the meaning of the sixth verse clearly. We have invited all beings, saying that we will bring them to the happiness of a buddha. If we do not practise, it is the same as the king breaking his word. Then we have deceived every being we invited. If we act like that, we ourselves have no fortune of gaining a good rebirth.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will actually give even the small amount of food I have thought of giving to someone else.

**Explanation:** _(Generosity Practice)_ Suppose we think in our heart of giving a little food to someone, and then do not actually give it. That becomes a cause of being born as a hungry ghost. But if today we actually carry out even a small promise to give, our own mind is made clean. And we are freed from the fear of the lower realms.
