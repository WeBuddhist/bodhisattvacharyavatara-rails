# Requirements — Bodhicaryāvatāra, Marathi, academic

## Target language
Marathi, in Devanagari. Modern standard Marathi in Devanagari.

## Audience and register
Students and practitioners reading a careful study translation; university readers of Buddhist texts (see `audience.md`). Faithful, precise, readable Marathi; no archaism.

## Source and grounding
- Translate from the Tibetan. Use the aligned Sanskrit and the aligned commentary passages to settle the meaning of each verse; the checked English academic translation (../en-academic/) is given to confirm the meaning, not as the text to translate; the Marathi machine draft may be consulted but is not an authority.
- Where the commentaries read a verse differently, follow the reading they share; if they truly split, follow the Tibetan's most literal sense.
- Translate the whole verse, in order: no omissions, no additions, no summary.
- Never add explanations, glosses or notes in the translation itself.

## Vocabulary
- Every term in `termbase.md` is rendered with its locked academic rendering, inflected for number and grammar as needed. Where a term has several senses, the sense assigned to that occurrence is used.
- Buddhist technical terms in Sanskrit (tatsama) form, as the termbase sets them. Many Marathi Buddhist readers know Pāli forms (धम्म, निब्बान) from the Ambedkarite tradition; this Mahāyāna text, translated from Tibetan, uses the termbase's Sanskrit forms.
- Proper names: Sanskrit forms in Devanagari (सुभूति, शारिपुत्र, मैत्रेय).

## Form and style
- A four-line Tibetan verse becomes four lines of Marathi free verse (no rhyme, no metre), separated by line breaks, following the Tibetan line order wherever Marathi syntax allows. Longer or shorter verses keep their own line count. Prose blocks (title, homage, colophon) stay prose.
- Standard modern Marathi punctuation; each line must read naturally and the verse must parse as a whole.
- Keep Śāntideva's first-person voice ("I", "my mind") and his rhetorical questions as questions.
- In chapter 9, an opponent's objection stays recognisable as an objection; do not merge it into the reply.
- Headings: translate the Tibetan chapter title literally and concisely.
