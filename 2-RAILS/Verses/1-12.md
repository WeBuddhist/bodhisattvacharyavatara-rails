## Traditional Interpretation

### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Palden (Tibetan)

KKP reads verse 1-12 as teaching the third benefit of bodhicitta through the fruit-bearing tree simile (*ljon shing gi dpe*). All other virtue not grasped by bodhicitta (*byang chub kyi sems kyis ma zin pa'i dge ba*) is like the banana tree (*chu shing*): it produces fruit once, then the root is exhausted and the tree dies, unable to bear fruit again. By contrast, the bodhicitta-tree (*byang chub sems kyi ljon shing*) continuously bears fruit of temporal happiness for gods and humans — without that fruit-bearing ever exhausting the root; and moreover, the tree grows (*'phel*) with each fruit borne. Those seeking inexhaustible, ever-increasing virtue should therefore cultivate bodhicitta. KKP cites the Ratnakūṭa (Dkon mchog gi za ma tog): "Just as diverse fragrant trees flourish when taken up by the four elements, so too virtue-roots grasped by bodhicitta and dedicated to omniscience expand."
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-12)

### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Tibetan)

GDR treats this as the third benefit: the fruit-tree simile (*'bras bu can gyi ljon shing gi dpe*). All virtue *not* grasped by bodhicitta — whether mundane or transcendent — is like the banana tree: it bears fruit and then the tree itself is exhausted (*rang zad par 'gyur ba nyid*). Virtue grasped by bodhicitta is like the wish-fulfilling tree (*dpag bsam gyi shing*): it continuously bears fruit of divine and human happiness, yet the merit itself not only does not exhaust but grows (*mi zad cing gong nas gong du 'phel*). GDR cites the Aksayamati sūtra (*Blo gros mi zad pas bstan pa'i mdo*): a drop of water fallen into the great ocean does not exhaust until the end of the kalpa; similarly, virtue dedicated to enlightenment does not exhaust until one attains the heart of enlightenment (*byang chub kyi snying po*).
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-12)

### ngulchu-thogmed — Ngulchu Thokme Sangpo (Tibetan)

NTS reads the verse as presenting the extraordinary, unshared (*thun mong ma yin pa*) quality of the wish-fulfilling tree (*dpag bsam gyi shing lta bu che ba'i bdag nyid*). All virtue other than bodhicitta, like the banana tree (*chu shing*), produces fruit once — whether the fruit of divine-human happiness (*lha mi'i bde ba'i 'bras bu*) or śrāvaka-pratyekabuddha nirvāṇa (*zan thos dang rang sangs rgyas kyi phung po lhag med du zad pa*) — and then exhausts. The bodhicitta-tree continuously and always produces fruit (*rtag par yang 'bras bu 'byin pas*) — temporal divine-human wellbeing — without the virtue itself exhausting, and with the fruit-producing capacity growing more each time. NTS cites the Aksayamati sūtra: virtue-roots dedicated to enlightenment do not exhaust until reaching the heart of enlightenment.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-12)

### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Mi Nyag Kunzang Sönam (Tibetan)

MKS gives the most detailed analysis of the two poles of the simile. First pole: "all other virtue not held by bodhicitta" (*byang chub kyi sems kyis ma zin pa'i dge ba gzhan gang ji snyed pa kun*) — whether merit-concurrent (*bsod nams cha mthun*) virtue that causes divine-human rebirths, or śrāvaka-pratyekabuddha virtue that leads to the remainderless nirvāṇa — is like the banana tree: it produces one fruit, then exhausts and the capacity to produce further fruit is cut (*'bras bu bskyeds de rang yin lhag med du zad par 'gyur ba nyid yin*). Second pole: virtue held by bodhicitta (*byang chub sems kyi zin pa'i dge ba*) is like the wish-fulfilling tree (*dpag bsam gyi shing*) — from it always and continuously, ever-increasing fruits of temporal divine-human abundance (*lha mi'i bde 'bras rgya chen po*) flow, reaching ultimately all the way to the buddha body (*sangs rgyas kyi sku bsod nams kyi phung po'i nang du bgrang du yod pa'i 'bras bu rgya chen po*) — and this fruit-bearing never exhausts; the merit grows (*gong du lhag par 'phel bar 'gyur ba*). MKS cites Aksayamati sūtra and also the Ratnakūṭa.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-12)

### khenpo-kunga — Ngakwang Kunga Wangchuk (Tibetan)

NKW reads the verse as demonstrating the great benefit of the third kind: virtue not subsumed by bodhicitta — prostrations, recitations, circumambulations, giving, keeping discipline — all other virtue (*dge ba gzhan kun thams cad*) is like the banana tree (*chu shing*): it produces fruit once — varying amounts of longevity, health, wealth, etc. in divine-human rebirths — and then exhausts (*dge ba rang nyid zad nas med par 'gyur ba yin*). But bodhicitta itself (*byang chub sems bskyed 'di*) is like the excellent wish-fulfilling tree (*ljon shing bzang po*): whether the wish-fulfilling tree of the god-realm (*dpag bsam gyi shing*), or the Jambudvīpa tree (*shing 'dzam bu praks'a*), or others — always and in every circumstance their fruits ripen without exhaustion; similarly, bodhicitta continuously and uninterruptedly bears fruit of divine-human blessings, wealth, glory, and power (*lha dang mi'i gnas su tshe ring nad med longs spyod che ba*), and not only does the virtue not exhaust, it grows more (*mi zad pa... gong nas gong du 'phel bar 'gyur ba*) all the way until reaching buddhahood and beyond.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-12)

### Divergences

No substantive doctrinal divergence. One structural difference: GDR identifies the exhaustion-end of the simile explicitly as both mundane virtue (*mngon mtho 'thob byed kyi dge rtsa*) and śrāvaka-pratyekabuddha nirvāṇa virtue (*dman pa'i myang 'das 'thob byed kyi dge rtsa*) — distinguishing two modes of exhaustion. MKS develops this distinction in the greatest detail. The other commentaries note only the general exhaustion point.

## Word-by-word Disambiguation (ཚིག་དོན་གསལ་བཤད།)

- **ཆུ་ཤིང་** (chu shing) — "banana tree / plantain": a plant that dies after bearing fruit once. The Tibetan term is used throughout classical literature for this simile. MKS cites the Udānavarga: "The banana tree is destroyed by its fruit (*chu shing 'bras bus bcom par 'gyur*)" as the scriptural basis for this image. The identifying characteristic: the fruit-bearing capacity is exhausted by the act of producing fruit.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-12)

- **དགེ་བ་གཞན་ཀུན་** — "all other virtue": *gzhan* = other than bodhicitta-subsumed virtue. KS and NTS: all virtue not held (*ma zin pa*) by bodhicitta. MKS specifies two categories: (1) *bsod nams cha mthun* = merit-concordant virtue producing divine-human rebirths; (2) *dman pa'i myang 'das 'thob byed kyi dge rtsa* = śrāvaka-pratyekabuddha virtue leading to lower nirvāṇa. Both categories fall under the "banana tree" pole.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-12)

- **རྟག་པར་ཡང་** — "always, continuously": NKW: *rgyun ldan du* = uninterruptedly, at every moment — in contrast to the banana tree, which produces fruit only once.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-12)

- **མི་ཟད་འཕེལ་བར་འགྱུར་** — "inexhaustibly grows / increases without exhaustion": *mi zad* modifies the virtue-root itself (it does not exhaust); *'phel* modifies the trajectory of the fruit-production (it increases). NTS: the fruit-producing capacity (*nus pa*) is renewed and grows with each fruit produced.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-12)

## Key Concepts (ཆོས་ཀྱི་གནད་ཚིག)

### ཚིགས་བཅད་ནང་གི་གནད་ཚིག

> (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-12)

> འཇམ་དཔལ་འདི་ལྟ་སྟེ་དཔེར་ན་སྤོས་ཀྱི་ཤིང་ལྗོན་པ་སྣ་ཚོགས་པ་ནི་ཁམས་བཞིས་བཟུང་ནས་རྣམ་པར་འཕེལ་ལོ། །འཇམ་དཔལ་དེ་བཞིན་དུ་ཚོགས་སྣ་ཚོགས་བསགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་དགེ་བའི་རྩ་བ་ཡང་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ཀྱིས་བཟུང་ཞིང་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ཉིད་དུ་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྔོས་ན་རྣམ་པར་འཕེལ་ལོ།
> — དཀོན་མཆོག་གི་ཟ་མ་ཏོག་ལས། (Ratnakūṭa sūtra)

## Disambiguated Restatement (Tibetan)

བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ཀྱིས་མ་ཟིན་པའི་དགེ་བ་གཞན་ (= ལྷ་མིར་སྐྱེ་བར་གྱུར་པའི་རྒྱུ་ཡིན་པའི་དགེ་རྩ་དང་། ཉན་རང་གི་ཐར་པའི་རྒྱུར་གྱུར་པའི་དགེ་རྩ་ཐམས་ཅད) ཀུན་ནི། ཆུ་ཤིང་གིས་ལན་གཅིག་འབྲས་བུ་བསྐྱེད་དེ་འཐུང་སྐམ་འགྲོ་བ་བཞིན་དུ། རང་གི་འབྲས་བུ་ (= ལྷ་མིའི་བདེ་བའམ་ཉན་རང་གི་མྱང་འདས) བསྐྱེད་ནས་དེ་ཙམ་གྱིས་ཟད་དེ་སླར་འབྲས་བུ་བསྐྱེད་ནུས་པ་མི་འབྱུང་བར་འགྱུར་བ་ཉིད་ཡིན། བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ཀྱི་ལྗོན་ཤིང་ (= བྱང་ཆུབ་ཏུ་བསྔོས་ཤིང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ཀྱིས་ཟིན་པའི་དགེ་རྩ) ལས་ནི་དུས་རྟག་ཏུ་རྒྱུན་ཆགས་སུ་ལྷ་མིའི་བདེ་བ་ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པའི་འབྲས་བུ་མི་ཟད་ཅིང་གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་འགྱུར་རོ།
