## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:notification -->
### Notification

**Title:** Feeling stuck? Make one real wish  
**Detail:** The instant this mind arises, you change. Today, when you feel stuck, wish yourself and others free.

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's verses are about a single moment: when the wish to awaken first becomes real in a person. Shantideva says everything changes in that instant. Someone who was stuck and suffering becomes, right then, an heir of the buddhas. He compares it to a substance people once believed could turn iron into gold: it takes an ordinary body and makes it precious. That is why he tells us to hold on to it.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

Today's three verses show a wonderful thing: the very moment the awakening mind is born in us, our name and our standing change completely.

**Verse 9 shows how our name changes in an instant.** Someone may have been a poor, suffering being, locked in the prison of samsara. Yet the moment bodhicitta arises in them, they receive the name "child of the Buddhas" and become worthy of the respect of gods and humans. There is a story about this. A merchant named Palbar had his eye gouged out by a robber, yet he felt not even a hair's worth of anger. Instead, he gave rise to a pure awakening mind. The robber was so amazed that he gave back everything he had stolen, bowed to the merchant, and became his follower.

**Verse 10 shows the example of the elixir that turns metal to gold.** Just as one measure of this special elixir can turn a thousand measures of iron into gold, the awakening mind turns this impure body of ours — a body tied down by our karma and afflictions — into the priceless body of a Buddha. So we are told to hold this mind firmly and never let it waver.

**Verse 11 shows the example of the wish-fulfilling jewel.** The perfect Buddha is the only guide who can lead beings across the ocean of samsara. When he looked with his boundless mind, he saw that the most precious and best way to end the misery of beings is this awakening mind alone. So anyone who wants to be free from the suffering of samsara is urged to hold this mind well, with mindfulness, so that it never fades.

You become worthy of respect from everyone, including gods and humans. By saying this prayer with true feeling, you are welcoming this beautiful change into your own life

##### tenzin-gyatso — Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

If the precious bodhicitta that cherishes others more than oneself should arise, then from the very first instant of its arising — even if, up to now, one had fallen under the power of karma and the afflictions, a wretched being confined in the prison of samsara, of lowly support, like an animal even — once the precious bodhicitta is born in one's mindstream, then although one may lack any [special] quality such as the view that realizes emptiness, and although in terms of one's support one may be a pitiable being like a destitute pauper, in that very instant of bodhicitta's birth one takes on the name "heir of the Blissful Ones," and in terms of one's standing, too, one becomes worthy to receive the homage of the world together with its gods. #### Stories and Illustrations

<!-- story:BCAC13_KTB -->
##### BCAC13_KTB — The Merchant Who Had Given Rise to Bodhicitta

Long ago, among many merchants who had gone to sea to fetch jewels, all the others died, but one who had given rise to bodhicitta survived by clinging to a plank and reached the shore. He had three measures of gold. One night, as he slept in an empty guesthouse, a robber came to take his wealth. In the manner of one with bodhicitta, he gave over all three measures of gold in full. Seeing how readily he gave, the robber was not satisfied and demanded more; the merchant answered, "I have nothing left." The robber said, "Then I will kill you," and he replied, "Even if you kill me, I have no wealth." The robber pressed a boulder to his heart and gouged out his eye, yet not the slightest anger arose in him; he said, "Though I have no wealth, even if you gouge out my eye I bear you no ill will." At this the robber was filled with wonder and asked, "What good quality do you have?" He answered, "I have no quality other than having given rise to bodhicitta, the mind set on awakening." Hearing this, the robber felt deep faith; he gave back the gold, became the merchant's disciple, requested the generation of bodhicitta, and served him with body, speech, and mind.

This shows vividly that the very moment bodhicitta is born in one's mindstream, even a wretched being bound in the prison of samsara becomes, in a single instant, one who is worthy of homage.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

1. **In a single instant, the name changes.** The very moment bodhicitta is born in one's own mindstream, in a single instant the name changes and one comes to be spoken of as "an heir of the Blissful Ones" — with no regard whatever to the body being male or female, age old or young, or family good or bad, and with no need for any preliminary period of time.

2. **The change of name and the change of standing — two.** Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen distinguishes two: the distinguished name, being spoken of as "heir of the Blissful Ones," and the distinguished standing, becoming worthy to receive the homage of the world with its gods and humans. He explains that this is accomplished the very moment bodhicitta is born, without regard to whether or not a bodhisattva ground has been attained.

3. **The wretched being bound in the prison of samsara.** Before bodhicitta arose, one was a wretched being bound as if imprisoned by karma and the afflictions; the moment bodhicitta arises one becomes worthy of homage — illustrating the great reversal.

4. **Worthy to receive the homage of the world with its gods and humans.** At the same time as the name changes, in terms of standing too one becomes an object of homage for all the gods, demigods, and humans of the world — as all the commentaries establish.

5. **Bodhicitta is the teacher of all the buddhas.** Kunzang Pelden explains that because bodhicitta is the very teacher (source) of all the buddhas, it is worthy of homage even by the gods. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk cites the verse: "To whomever that supreme and precious mind is born — to the body of that person I pay homage."

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This stanza teaches the second benefit of bodhicitta — the benefit whereby name and standing change. The very moment the precious bodhicitta is born in one's own mindstream, in a single instant — with no regard whatever to the body being male or female, age old or young, or family good or bad — the name changes and one comes to be spoken of as "an heir of the Blissful Ones"; and the standing changes, so that one becomes an object worthy of the homage of the world, its gods, demigods, and humans alike. All the commentaries explain this on common ground.

**Key points.**

- All the commentaries agree on the way the name changes in a single instant; Gyaltsab makes clear that it is accomplished without regard to whether or not a bodhisattva ground has been attained.
- The great reversal — whereby even a wretched being bound in the prison of samsara becomes, the moment bodhicitta arises, an object of homage — is shown vividly by the Tale of the Merchant Who Had Given Rise to Bodhicitta.
- Because bodhicitta is the very teacher of all the buddhas, Kunzang Pelden says it is worthy of homage even by the gods.
- The *Sutra Requested by Kāśyapa* and the scripture of the Eastern Mountain school likewise show parallel examples of the change of name and standing: the slave woman's child becomes an heir of the victors, and even a bodhisattva reborn as an animal becomes worthy of homage.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### tenzin-gyatso — Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

That a being dwelling in a support such as this — the present body, a gross aggregate of flesh and bone, an impure support — could become the body of a buddha, endowed with every quality, its every fault exhausted, adorned with the major and minor marks: this seems almost beyond the mind's grasp. If one asks by what condition and by what means this is accomplished — it is accomplished by that bodhicitta which cherishes others more than oneself. Because it is in dependence on this bodhicitta that the precious body of a victor — beyond all price, adorned with the marks and signs — is attained upon the basis of an impure body such as this, hold this very bodhicitta utterly firm. So he says.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^1-10]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The third point has three parts: showing, by the simile of the gold-making elixir, that buddhahood is attained; showing, by the simile of the precious jewel, that its purpose is great; and showing, by the simile of the fruit-bearing tree, that it benefits other beings. Here is the first — showing, by the simile of the gold-making elixir, that buddhahood is attained.

The simile is the gold-making elixir; what it illustrates is bodhicitta; and the point of likeness is the turning of something inferior into something excellent. Just as the supreme gold-making elixir called "mercury that appears as gold" can, with a single measure, transform a thousand measures of iron into fine gold, so bodhicitta — the thing illustrated — can transform this impure, sin-laden body into the wondrous Form Body of a victor. So it is explained.

*(Note: Kunpal's prose covers verses 1-10, 1-11, and 1-12 together as a single commentarial unit. The gold-elixir material above is the portion specific to 1-10; the jewel simile (1-11) and the fruit-tree simile (1-12) follow in the same block.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-10]]

##### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

This is the third point — showing the benefit by similes; and the first of these, the turning of the inferior into the supreme by the simile of the gold-making elixir. ##### BCACXX_WR_bo — Nāgārjuna's Story

A tale given as an illustration of this stanza's phrase "like the supreme kind of gold-making elixir." Nāgārjuna was born as the son of a brahmin in the land of Bheta⚑ (Vidarbha). A diviner prophesied that he had only seven days to live; but by praying to the nāgas his life was extended to seven years, and by accomplishing [the practice of] the Great Peahen (Mahāmāyūrī) it was extended to fifteen. Out of fear of dying young, he took ordination and studied at Nālandā. By praying to Mañjuśrī and to the Deathless Lamp,⚑ he attained the siddhi of long life. During a famine he prepared the gold-making elixir, and knowing from his tests with snakes and with leaves and flowers that the elixir was accomplished, he used it to benefit beings. Just as Saraha, with a single substance of supreme attainment (siddhi), could turn whatever it touched into gold, so it is shown that by a single [instance of] bodhicitta all embodied beings become the body of a victor.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

1. **Bodhicitta, like the gold-making elixir.** Bodhicitta is like the supreme gold-making elixir: just as the supreme elixir, with a single measure, can turn a thousand measures of iron into gold, so bodhicitta takes up this impure, affliction-ridden body and can turn it into the Form Body of a victor. This transformation is shown in two aspects — in essence (from impure to pure) and in scale (from small to vast).

2. **Transforming the impure body into the body of a victor.** All the commentaries explain the two aspects of the transformation alike: the impure body (impure by nature and veiled by karma and the afflictions) is turned into the body of a victor (the Form Body of a buddha). The *Noble Maitreya's Liberation* illustrates this in detail: by the essence of bodhicitta all the iron of karma and affliction is turned into the gold of omniscience.

3. **Transformed beyond all price.** No conventional price whatever can be set on the body of a victor: it is a treasure beyond measure. Khenpo Zhenga's annotation renders it plainly — "precious and beyond all price."

4. **Hold it utterly firm and unwavering.** The stanza's imperative ("hold it!") teaches that one must grasp bodhicitta and keep it utterly firm and unwavering. Just as, seeing such benefit, one guards a substance as precious as gold-making elixir, so all the commentaries alike explain that bodhicitta itself must be held firmly.

5. **The three grades of gold-making elixir.** Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains three grades — the lowest (1:1), the middling (1:100), and the supreme (1:1000) — and shows that bodhicitta is that very supreme grade. The transformation of this impure body into a fruit of supreme value is no small thing, but of the very highest degree.

6. **By what condition and means is it accomplished?** Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso poses directly the reason for this transformation, and answers that the cause is bodhicitta itself, which cherishes others more than oneself. Taken up upon this impure body, it accomplishes the priceless body of a victor adorned with the major and minor marks.

7. **Kunzang Pelden explains the three stanzas together.** Khenpo Kunzang Pelden comments on stanzas 1-10, 1-11, and 1-12 as a single unit: the gold-making simile (1-10), the precious-jewel simile (1-11), and the fruit-bearing-tree simile (1-12) are set out in sequence.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Stanza 1-10 opens the third phase of teaching the benefits — praising the power of bodhicitta by way of similes. The first of the three similes is the gold-making elixir. Just as the supreme gold-making elixir, with a single measure, can turn a thousand measures of iron into gold, so bodhicitta takes up this impure body and transforms it into the body of a victor — a fruit beyond all price.

**Key points.**

- All the commentaries recognize the same structure to the simile: the impure body is like iron, bodhicitta is like the elixir, and the Form Body of a victor is like gold. Khenpo Zhenga's annotation puts it plainly at the close — seeing such benefit, one should hold bodhicitta utterly firm and unwavering ("hold it!").
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam distinguishes the three grades of gold-making elixir (lowest 1:1, middling 1:100, supreme 1:1000) and explains that bodhicitta is the very supreme grade — showing the transformation to be of the highest degree in terms of scale.
- The *Noble Maitreya's Liberation*, cited at length in Khenpo Zhenga's annotation, explains the simile in detail: just as "mercury appearing as gold" turns a thousand measures of iron into gold without itself being used up, so the essence of the mind set on omniscience turns all the iron of karma and affliction into the gold of omniscience without itself being diminished.
- Gyaltsab, Ngulchu Thogme, Sabzang, and Khenpo Kunga all cite the *Gaṇḍavyūha* alike to settle the gold-making simile; Minyak Kunzang Sonam further cites the *Questions of Vīradatta*: "Whoever delights in bodhicitta — their body becomes like gold."
- Stanza 1-10 is the first of the three-simile phase (1-10 gold-elixir, 1-11 precious jewel, 1-12 fruit-bearing tree); Khenpo Kunzang Pelden comments on all three together as one unit.
- Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso poses the reason for the transformation directly ("by what condition and by what means?") and answers that bodhicitta — cherishing others more than oneself — is itself the cause.

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### tenzin-gyatso — Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

The sole guide of beings — the one and only guide who shows wandering beings the excellent path to temporary and ultimate benefit and happiness — is the Buddha, the Blessed One; and "with measureless intelligence" means that with his ultimate wisdom, to which nothing is an obstacle, he saw and examined thoroughly. When he did so, he found that there is nothing higher than the precious bodhicitta — the unsurpassed means, beyond all price, which single-pointedly gives beings happiness for the present and accomplishes the state of unsurpassed perfect awakening in the end. Therefore those who wish to be free from the state of wandering beings — the abodes of the six classes — must hold well and firm this precious bodhicitta, beyond all price. So he says.

##### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

This is the second point — showing, by the simile of the precious jewel, that its purpose is great. Just as a caravan-leader skilled in means and endowed with intelligence, having no wish to deceive others and full of the intention to help, leads merchants to the isle of jewels and, having examined the heaps of jewels well, takes them up — whereupon the merchants treasure those jewels — so the matchless, sole caravan-leader who ferries the boundless host of beings across the ocean of existence by his many skillful deeds, leading them each according to their fortune to the city of liberation and joining them to the precious wealth of [spiritual] qualities — namely the perfect Buddha, who possesses the intelligence that directly and measurelessly knows every aspect of the knowable, is free of all not-knowing, and unwaveringly accomplishes nothing but benefit and happiness — when with diligence he examined phenomena thoroughly and well, saw that this bodhicitta alone is, from the side of its qualities, rare and of great value and of very great benefit. And since there is no error in what the Buddha discerns, those who wish to be free from every ruin of the six classes of wandering beings' manifold sufferings, and who wish to enjoy the vast happiness of higher states and definite goodness, should first give rise well to this precious bodhicitta — like the king of wish-granting jewels, hard to find and, once found, of great purpose — and then, with a caring and cherishing attitude, hold it firm and undeclining.

As the sutra says (from the *Gaṇḍavyūha*): "Son of good family, it is like this: for as long as the light of the orbs of the sun and moon shines, all the wealth, grain, jewels, refined gold, silver, flowers, garlands, ointments, garments, and every enjoyment there may be — none of them can match the price of the king of precious jewels. <!-- story:BCACXX_WR_bo -->
##### BCACXX_WR_bo — The Caravan Leader's Story

A tale given as an illustration of this stanza's phrase "the sole guide of wandering beings, with measureless intelligence." The caravan-leader Leksöl⚑, captain of a great ship, led many merchants out to sea; when the wind died and they lay becalmed, stranded upon the sea, he consoled the merchants, saying, "There is a way." At night, listening to the talk of two birds, he learned that a brahmin with magical powers was crossing the sea holding a royal edict.⚑ Knowing the means to summon the birds, the caravan-leader beat the golden drum from the rock-cave, and by the force of the wind that the flocking birds stirred up, the ship sailed on and all the merchants were saved. It is set forth as an illustration of how the sole guide, with measureless intelligence, delivers the whole host of beings from danger.

In the same way, for as long as the wisdom of the omniscient ones illumines the object that is the sphere of reality, the roots of virtue — contaminated and uncontaminated — of all gods and humans, and of all the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas, however many there be, none of them can match the price of the precious jewel that is the generation of the mind set on awakening.
> — the *Noble Maitreya's Liberation* (Āryamaitreyavimokṣa) (that the light of sun and moon, though it gather every enjoyment, cannot match the price of the precious jewel.)

> Just as, among all riches, the king of jewels is the most precious, so, for as long as the wisdom of the omniscient ones illumines the sphere of reality, the roots of virtue — contaminated and uncontaminated — of all gods and humans and of all the shravakas and pratyekabuddhas, however many there be, cannot match the price of the precious jewel that is the generation of bodhicitta.
> — the *Gaṇḍavyūha* Sutra (as cited to Ngulchu Thogme.)

#### Main Teaching Points

1. **The precious-jewel simile shows the value of bodhicitta.** This stanza compares bodhicitta to the king of wish-fulfilling jewels (ཡིད་བཞིན་གྱི་ནོར་བུ་དབང་གི་རྒྱལ་པོ, *cintāmaṇirāja*). All the systems agree.

2. **Its value is seen when examined with measureless intelligence.** None but the Buddha's measureless intelligence (ཚད་མེད་བློ, omniscient wisdom) can know the value of bodhicitta. The Victor examined it thoroughly and saw it to be of great value — and there is no error in what he discerns.

3. **The sole guide.** All the commentaries agree: the Buddha alone is the matchless, sole guide (འགྲན་ཟླ་མེད་པ་གཅིག་པུ, *ekam anupamam*); only a buddha can act as the guide that shravakas, pratyekabuddhas, and others cannot.

4. **The counsel to hold it well and firm.** The chief instruction of this stanza: those who wish to be free from the state of wandering beings should, through mindfulness and mindful awareness, hold bodhicitta firm and undeclining.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This stanza counsels that bodhicitta — likened to the king of wish-fulfilling jewels (ཡིད་བཞིན་གྱི་ནོར་བུ་དབང་གི་རྒྱལ་པོ, *cintāmaṇi-rāja*) — should be held firm.

**Key points.**

- Khenpo Kunzang Pelden strings stanzas 1-10, 1-11, and 1-12 together as one unit and comments on them together; he explains the great purpose of bodhicitta through the simile of the caravan-leader who guides merchants to the isle of jewels.
- The Buddha alone, examining thoroughly with his measureless intelligence, saw the great value of bodhicitta; because others cannot know this, all the commentaries agree that this reinforces the root reason for the counsel — "entertain no doubt."
- The *Noble Maitreya's Liberation* (Āryamaitreyavimokṣa) gives the scriptural warrant: just as the light of sun and moon, though it gathers every enjoyment, cannot match the price of the precious jewel, so all the roots of virtue of gods, humans, shravakas, and pratyekabuddhas cannot match the price of bodhicitta.
- The chief counsel: though bodhicitta is being found, one must not let it decline, but hold it firm through mindfulness and mindful awareness. All systems agree.
