![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-47]]

From this very day, the Victorious Ones are the ones I go to for shelter. They are the Protectors of beings. They work hard to keep beings safe. These strong ones chase away all fears. ^2-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-48]]

In the same way, the teachings they have realized are what I go to for shelter. These teachings chase away the fears of the cycle of rebirth. The group of awakening heroes is also who I go to for shelter. ^2-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-49]]

I am terrified by fear. So, I offer myself to Samantabhadra. I also give my body to Manjushri. ^2-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-50]]

Because of his compassion, his actions never go wrong. I cry out in a sad voice to the protector Avalokiteshvara. I say, "Please protect me, one who has done harmful deeds!" ^2-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-51]]

Looking for shelter, I cry out from my heart to noble Akashagarbha. I also call to Kshitigarbha. I cry out to every Protector who has great compassion. ^2-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-52]]

The Thunderbolt-holder is the one I go to for shelter. When the messengers of Yama and other harmful beings see him, they are terrified. They run away in all four directions. ^2-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-53]]

In the past, I went against your commands. Now I have seen great fear. You are the one I go to for shelter. Please take away my fears quickly! ^2-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-54]]

If we are scared of a normal illness, we must follow the doctor's orders. So of course we must when we are always sick! We have a disease made of a hundred faults like longing. ^2-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-55]]

Imagine if just one of these illnesses could destroy all the people living in Jambudvipa. And imagine if no medicine to cure them could be found anywhere. ^2-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-56]]

In that case, for the All-Knowing Doctor who takes away all pain, not wanting to follow his commands is extreme ignorance. Anyone who ignores him should be scolded. ^2-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-57]]

We must be careful even near a small, normal cliff. So of course we must be careful when we could fall a thousand leagues into a drop that lasts a very long time! ^2-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-58]]

It is wrong for me to relax and think, 'I will not die today.' The time when I will be gone will definitely come. ^2-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-59]]

Who has made me safe from fear? How will I make sure I escape from this? If I will definitely stop existing one day, how can my mind be at peace? ^2-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-60]]

Everything I felt in the past is gone. What is left of it for me? Because I held on to those things so tightly, I broke the teacher's commands. ^2-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-61]]

I will have to leave this life behind. I will also leave my family and friends. If I must go somewhere completely alone, what is the use of having friends or enemies? ^2-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-62]]

Suffering comes from a bad deed. I must ask myself how I will ever escape from that. It is right for me to think only about this question all day and all night. ^2-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-63]]

Because of my unknowing and ignorance, I have done bad things. I have committed deeds wrong in themselves. I have also done all kinds of forbidden harmful deeds. ^2-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-64]]

Right in front of the Protectors, I stand with my hands pressed together. My mind is terrified of suffering. I bow down again and again, and I own up to all of those. ^2-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^2-65]]

I pray that the Guides see my harmful deeds as faults. Because this is not good, I will never do it again. ^2-65

From Entering the Way of the Awakening Hero, the second chapter: Confessing Harmful Deeds. ^2-a

## Chapter Three: Completely Adopting the Awakening Mind ^3-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-1]]

I joyfully rejoice in the good deeds of all living beings that bring rest from the suffering of the unhappy realms. I also rejoice when suffering beings find happiness. ^3-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-2]]

I rejoice in the gathered good deeds that lead to awakening. I rejoice in the complete freedom of beings with a body from the suffering of the cycle of rebirth. I rejoice in the awakening of the Protectors. I also rejoice in the levels of the children of the Victorious Ones. ^3-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-3]]

I joyfully rejoice in the ocean of good deeds from the birth of the awakening mind, which brings happiness to all living beings. I also rejoice in the actions that help living beings. ^3-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-4]]

I put my hands together and pray to the Buddhas everywhere. Living beings are lost in the dark with their suffering. Please light the lamp of the teachings for them. ^3-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-5]]

I put my hands together and pray to the Victorious Ones who wish to pass into freedom from sorrow. Do not leave these beings in blindness. Please stay with us for countless great ages. ^3-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-6]]

By all the good deeds I have gathered in this way, may the suffering of every living being be taken away completely. ^3-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-7]]

As long as any beings are sick, may I help them until they are cured. May I be their medicine, their doctor, and their nurse. ^3-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-8]]

May I rain down food and drink to take away the suffering of hunger and thirst. During the middle great age of famine, may I become food and drink myself. ^3-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-9]]

For every living being who is poor and has nothing, may I become a treasure that never runs out. May I stay right in front of them as everything they need. ^3-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-10]]

I will freely give away my body and my belongings. I will give away my good deeds from the past, present and future. I will do this without feeling a loss to help all living beings. ^3-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-11]]

Giving up everything brings freedom from sorrow. My mind wants to reach this freedom from sorrow. Since I must give up everything anyway, it is best to give it to living beings. ^3-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-12]]

I have already given this body to all beings with a body for their happiness. Let them do whatever they please with it. They can hit it, insult it, or even kill it. ^3-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-13]]

They can play with my body. They can laugh at it and make fun of it. Since I have already given this body away, why should I take care of it? ^3-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-14]]

Let them do any action to me, as long as it does not hurt them. Whenever anyone focuses on me, may it always be helpful to them. May it never be useless. ^3-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-15]]

When people focus on me, they might feel anger or faith. May those very feelings always help them. May that anger or faith be the reason they reach all their goals. ^3-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-16]]

If people say bad things about me, hurt me, or make fun of me, may they all have the good fortune of awakening. ^3-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-17]]

May I become a Protector for anyone who has no Protector. May I be a guide for travelers on the path. For those who want to cross over, may I become a boat, a ship, and a bridge. ^3-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-18]]

May I be an island for those who seek an island. May I be a resting place for those who want to rest. For any being with a body who wants a servant, may I become a servant to them all. ^3-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-19]]

May I become a wish-fulfilling jewel and a wonderful vase. May I become powerful sacred words and a great medicine. May I be wish-granting trees and a wish-granting cow for every being with a body. ^3-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-20]]

Just like the earth and the other basic elements, and just like space, may I always be a ground that supports countless living beings in many different ways. ^3-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-21]]

In the same way, reaching to the very edges of space, may I support the whole realm of living beings in every way. May I sustain them until they all reach freedom from sorrow. ^3-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-22]]

Just as the Well-Gone Ones of the past created the awakening mind and, step by step, followed the trainings of the awakening heroes, ^3-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-23]]

In the same way, I will create the awakening mind to help all beings. Step by step, I will also practice the trainings. ^3-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-24]]

A wise person takes hold of the awakening mind with clear faith. To make it grow even more, they should encourage their mind like this: ^3-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-25]]

Today my life has great meaning. I have gained a good human life. Today I am born into the family of the Buddhas. Today I have become a child of the Buddhas. ^3-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-26]]

Now I must do whatever I can to act in a way that fits this family. I will make sure I never stain this flawless and noble family. ^3-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-27]]

Imagine a blind person finding a precious jewel in a pile of trash. By that same amazing luck, this awakening mind has been born in me. ^3-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-28]]

It is the best nectar that defeats the Lord of Death for beings. It is the treasure that never runs out, taking away the poverty of beings. ^3-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-29]]

It is the best medicine that cures the illnesses of beings. It is a tree of rest for beings who are tired from wandering the path of life. ^3-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-30]]

It is a bridge for everyone that frees all beings from the unhappy realms. It is the rising moon of the mind that clears away the pain of beings' troubling emotions. ^3-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-31]]

It is the great sun that clears the dimness of beings' not knowing. It is the rich butter made by churning the milk of the holy teachings. ^3-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-32]]

Beings are guests traveling the path of life who wish to enjoy happiness. This is the greatest happiness waiting nearby. It satisfies the great guests, the living beings. ^3-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-33]]

Today, in front of all the Protectors, I have invited beings as guests to the state of a Well-Gone One, and to happiness in the meantime. Let gods, demigods, and others rejoice! ^3-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^3-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Awakening Hero, the third chapter, called "Fully Taking Hold of the Awakening Mind." ^3-a

## Chapter Four: Teaching Carefulness ^4-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-1]]

A child of the Victorious Ones has firmly taken hold of the awakening mind in this way. They should always try hard not to break the trainings. ^4-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-2]]

If something is started without thinking, or not checked carefully, you should think about whether to do it or drop it. This is true even if a promise was made. ^4-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-3]]

The Buddhas and their children have checked this with great wisdom. I have also checked it again and again. So what reason do I have to delay it? ^4-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-4]]

If I make a promise like that but do not act on it, I am tricking all those living beings. What sort of wandering being will I become? ^4-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-5]]

Suppose you decide in your mind to give away a small, ordinary thing. If you do not give it, the teachings say you will become a hungry ghost. ^4-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-6]]

I have sincerely invited every being to the greatest happiness. If I trick them now, how can I ever go to a happy realm? ^4-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-7]]

Even if someone gives up the awakening mind, they might still find freedom. This is a mysterious result of action. Only the All-Knowing One understands it. ^4-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-8]]

For an awakening hero, giving this up is the heaviest fall of all. If this happens, you fail to help all living beings. ^4-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-9]]

Someone might block this hero's store of goodness for even a single moment. This stops the hero from helping living beings. Because of this, their time in an unhappy realm will be endless. ^4-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-10]]

If destroying the happiness of just one living being ruins me, what will happen if I destroy the happiness of endless beings with bodies who fill all of space? I do not even need to ask. ^4-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-11]]

Suppose powerful falls and the powerful awakening mind take turns in the cycle of rebirth. Then it will take a long time to reach the stages. ^4-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-12]]

Because of this, I must respectfully do what I vowed. If I do not make an effort starting today, I will go lower and lower. ^4-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-13]]

Countless Buddhas have passed away while helping all living beings. But because of my own faults, I have not been healed by them. ^4-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-14]]

If I keep acting this way, the same things will happen again and again. I will be sick, tied up, and cut in the unhappy realms. ^4-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-15]]

A Thus-Gone One appearing is very rare. Getting a human body, having trust, and being ready to do good deeds are also rare. When will I ever get these things? ^4-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-16]]

Today I am not sick. I have food and I am safe from harm. Even so, life can trick me in an instant. My body is just like a short loan. ^4-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-17]]

If I act like this, I will not even get a human body. If I do not get a human body, I will only do harmful deeds and no good deeds. ^4-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-18]]

Right now I have the chance to do good deeds. If I do not do them, what will I be able to do later? I will be completely confused by the suffering of the unhappy realms. ^4-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-19]]

If I do not do good deeds and I pile up harmful deeds, I will not hear even the sound of the happy realms for a billion great ages. ^4-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-20]]

For this reason, the Blessed One said that being human is extremely hard to achieve. He said it is like a turtle poking its neck through the hole of a wooden yoke drifting on a great ocean. ^4-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-21]]

Just one instant of a harmful deed can trap me in Avichi for a great age. I have gathered harmful deeds in the cycle of rebirth, which has no beginning. So of course I will not go to a happy realm! ^4-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-22]]

I will not win freedom just by living through that. While I am experiencing it, I will just create other harmful deeds. ^4-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-23]]

I have found this freedom to practice. If I do not build a habit of doing good deeds, there is no worse trick than this. There is no worse confusion than this. ^4-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-24]]

If I understand this but stay lazy because I am confused, great sorrow will come to me when it is time to die. ^4-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-25]]

If the unbearable fire of hell burns my body for a long time, my mind will surely be tormented by the blazing fire of unbearable regret. ^4-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-26]]

This helpful state is very hard to find. Somehow, I have found it by chance. But what if, even while I understand this, I am led back again to that hell? ^4-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-27]]

It is as if I have been confused by sacred words. I am left with no mind here. I do not even know what is confusing me. What is inside me? ^4-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-28]]

My enemies, like hatred and wanting, do not have hands or feet. They are not brave or wise. So how have they made me like a slave? ^4-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-29]]

These enemies live right inside my mind. They hurt me whenever they want. If I do not get angry at them and just practice patience, that is the wrong kind of patience. I should be scolded for it. ^4-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-30]]

Even if all the gods and demigods rose up against me as enemies, they could not drag me away. They could not throw me into the fire of the Avichi hell. ^4-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-31]]

But my powerful enemy, these troubling emotions, can throw me there in a single instant. If Mount Meru touched that fire, it would burn away without leaving even ashes. ^4-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-32]]

My troubling emotions are enemies that last a very long time. They seem to have no beginning and no end. All my other enemies could never last that long. ^4-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-33]]

If I kindly serve and honor others, they bring me help and happiness. But if I serve my troubling emotions, they bring me further suffering and harm. ^4-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-34]]

These enemies have stayed with me for a long time without stopping. They are the only reason I suffer so many harms. If they truly live inside my heart, how could I be fearless and happy in the cycle of rebirth? ^4-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-35]]

These inner enemies guard the prison of the cycle of rebirth. They become executioners in hell. If they stay inside my mind in a net of clinging, how could I ever be happy? ^4-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-36]]

So, until I completely destroy this enemy, I will not give up my effort. Think of people who are filled with pride. If someone wrongs them just a little, they get angry. They do not sleep until they have destroyed that enemy. ^4-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-37]]

If even soldiers fight enemies who will die anyway, and ignore the suffering of being struck by arrows and spears, and do not run away before they reach their goal... ^4-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-38]]

My true natural enemies are the constant cause of all suffering. Today, I am trying hard to destroy them. So of course I will not lose heart, even if this fight brings me hundreds of sufferings. ^4-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-39]]

People sometimes wear scars from meaningless fights with enemies like decorations on their bodies. I am working hard to achieve a truly great goal. Why should suffering be a harm to me? ^4-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-40]]

Fishermen, outcasts, farmers, and others only think about making a living. Yet they put up with the pain of cold and heat. I am working for the happiness of all beings. Why should someone like me not endure these things too? ^4-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-41]]

I promised to free the beings who fill space in the ten directions. I promised to free them from their troubling emotions. But when I made this promise, I was not even free from my own troubling emotions! ^4-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-42]]

Since I do not even know what I can really do, am I not crazy to talk like this? So I will never turn back from destroying my troubling emotions. ^4-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-43]]

I will hold tightly to this. I will hold a grudge and fight this battle! This is the only kind of troubling emotion I should not let go of, because it destroys all other troubling emotions. ^4-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-44]]

Even if I am burned and killed, so be it. Even if my head is cut off, that is fine. But I will never bow down to my enemies, the troubling emotions. ^4-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-45]]

When normal enemies are chased out of a country, they go live in other lands. They build up their strength and come back. But my enemies, the troubling emotions, are not like that. ^4-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-46]]

These awful troubling emotions can be cleared away by the eye of wisdom. Once they are chased out of my mind, where will they go? Where could they hide to come back and hurt me? The truth is just that my mind is weak and I am not trying hard enough. ^4-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-47]]

Troubling emotions do not live in the things we sense. They do not live in the group of senses, or in between. If they are nowhere else, where do they stay to harm all beings? They are like an illusion. So, let go of fear in your heart and work hard to know them. Why do I hurt myself for no reason in the hells and other places? ^4-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-48]]

Thinking like this, I will work hard. I will do the training just as it was taught. If a sick person does not listen to the doctor, how can medicine cure them? ^4-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^4-a]]

The fourth chapter from Entering the Way of the Awakening Hero, called Carefulness. ^4-a

## Chapter Five: Guarding Watchfulness ^5-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-1]]

If you want to guard the training, you must guard your mind well. If this mind is not guarded, you cannot guard the training at all. ^5-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-2]]

Letting the elephant of the mind run wild causes harm in the Avichi hell. A wild, crazy elephant here does not cause that much harm. ^5-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-3]]

If I tightly tie up the elephant of the mind with the rope of keeping in mind, all fears will disappear. Every good deed will come right into my hands. ^5-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-4]]

There are tigers, lions, elephants, bears, snakes, and all enemies. There are hell guardians, witches, and also demons. ^5-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-5]]

If I tie up this mind alone, all of those things are tied up. If I tame this mind alone, all of them are tamed. ^5-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-6]]

All fears and endless suffering come from the mind. This is what the Speaker of Truth himself has taught. ^5-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-7]]

Who purposely made the weapons in the hell of living beings? Who created the burning iron ground? Where did those groups of women come from? ^5-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-8]]

The Sage taught that all these things come from a harmful mind. So, in the three worlds, there is no other fear besides the mind. ^5-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-9]]

If the perfection of giving means taking away the poverty of all beings, how did the past Protectors reach perfection? There are still poor beings today. ^5-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-10]]

The perfection of giving is taught as having the mind of giving everything away. It means wanting to give all your things and their good results to everyone. Therefore, it is just the mind itself. ^5-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-11]]

Where could fish and other animals be put so they are never killed? The perfection of good conduct is explained as coming from getting the mind of giving up. ^5-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-12]]

Wild living beings are like space. They can never be fully defeated. But if this single mind of anger is defeated, it is like defeating all those enemies at once. ^5-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-13]]

How could there ever be enough leather to cover the whole earth? But with just enough leather for the soles of your shoes, it is as if the whole earth is covered. ^5-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-14]]

In the same way, I cannot stop every outside thing. I will just stop this mind of mine. What need is there to stop anything else? ^5-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-15]]

Having just one clear mind brings a great result. It can make you like Brahma. But if your actions are weak, just using your body and words will not bring a result like that. ^5-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-16]]

You might do recitation and hard practice for a long time. But if you do them with a wandering mind, they are meaningless. The Knower of the way things really are said this. ^5-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-17]]

Some people do not know the most important part of the teachings. They do not know this secret of the mind. They want to find happiness and end suffering. But they will just wander around for no reason. ^5-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-18]]

Because of this, I will carefully hold and protect this mind of mine. What is the point of doing many kinds of strict practice? The only one I need is the strict practice of guarding the mind. ^5-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-19]]

If you are in a wild crowd, you carefully protect a bodily wound. In the same way, when you are around bad people, you must always protect this wound of the mind. ^5-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-20]]

A person carefully protects a scratch because they fear the small suffering of a wound. So why do people who fear the Crushing Hell and Destroying hell not guard the wound of the mind? ^5-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-21]]

If a person acts like this, they will stay strong. Whether living among bad people or among women, a steady person of self-restraint will not fail. ^5-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-22]]

It is better to lose my gain and honor. It is better to lose my body and the things I live on. It is even better to lose other good deeds. But I must never let my mind fail. ^5-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-23]]

To those who want to guard the mind, I put my hands together and ask this. Guard both keeping in mind and watchfulness with all your effort! ^5-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-24]]

People who are troubled by illness have no power to do anything. In the same way, a mind troubled by confusion has no power to do anything. ^5-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-25]]

If someone has a mind without watchfulness, what they hear, think about, and practice will leak out. Like water in a cracked pot, it does not stay in keeping in mind. ^5-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-26]]

Many people have learning and faith. Many people work hard with great effort. But if they make the mistake of lacking watchfulness, they will be stained by a fall. ^5-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-27]]

When keeping in mind fades away, the thieves of lost watchfulness sneak in. Even if I have gathered a store of goodness, they rob me. Then I go down to an unhappy realm. ^5-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-28]]

This gang of thieves, made of troubling emotions, looks for a chance to strike. When they find it, they steal my good deeds. They even destroy life in a happy realm. ^5-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-29]]

So, I must never let keeping in mind wander away from the door of the mind. If it does leave, I should remember the pain of the unhappy realms and bring it right back. ^5-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-30]]

For lucky people who act with respect because they are afraid to do wrong, keeping in mind grows easily. This happens by spending time with a teacher and listening to the teacher who ordained me. ^5-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-31]]

The Buddhas and awakening heroes can see everywhere without anything blocking their view. I should think, "I am always standing right in front of them all." ^5-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-32]]

Thinking this way, I should act with shame, respect, and fear. By doing this, keeping the Buddha in mind will happen to me again and again. ^5-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-33]]

When keeping in mind stays at the door of the mind to guard it, watchfulness will come. Even what was lost will come back. ^5-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-34]]

First, when I see that a mind like this has faults, I should stay firm like a log of wood. ^5-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-35]]

I should never look around for no reason. With a firm mind, I should always look down. ^5-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-36]]

To rest my eyes, I should sometimes look around. If I see someone, I should look at them and say, "Welcome." ^5-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-37]]

To check for danger on the path, I should keep looking in all four directions. After resting, I should turn and look behind me. ^5-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-38]]

After checking in front and behind me, I should go forward or go back. In the same way, I should always understand what is needed before I act. ^5-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-39]]

When I start an action, I should think, 'My body will stay like this.' Then I should check it sometimes. I should ask, 'How is my body staying?' ^5-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-40]]

I must try with all my effort to watch the mad elephant of the mind. I must tie it to the great pillar of thinking about the teachings so it does not escape. ^5-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-41]]

I must try hard not to lose my deep focus for even a single moment. To do this, I must carefully check my mind. I should ask, 'What is my mind doing?' ^5-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-42]]

When I am caught up in danger, a festival, and the like, if I cannot keep up my practice, I should act at ease. It is taught that at times of giving, good conduct should be treated with even-mindedness. ^5-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-43]]

When I plan to do something and start it, I should not think of anything else. I should keep my thoughts on it. I must finish that very thing first. ^5-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-44]]

If I act this way, everything is done well. Otherwise, neither task will be finished. In this way, the secondary troubling emotion of lacking watchfulness will not grow. ^5-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-45]]

There are many kinds of silly talk and amazing shows. When I join in these things, I must stop clinging to them. ^5-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-46]]

If I dig the earth, cut grass, or draw lines on the ground for no reason, I should remember the training of the Well-Gone One. Out of fear, I should stop right away. ^5-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-47]]

Whenever I want to move or even speak, I should check my own mind first. Then I should act firmly and do the right thing. ^5-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-48]]

Whenever my mind has clinging or wants to feel anger, I should not act or speak. I should stay perfectly still, like a log. ^5-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-49]]

I might feel restlessness or mockery. I might have pride and arrogance. I might think about exposing others' faults. I might have sneakiness or a deceiving mind. ^5-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-50]]

Sometimes I feel like praising myself or putting others down. I might say mean things and feel full of regret. At those times, I should stay still like a log. ^5-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-51]]

If I want gain, honor, or fame, or want to seek helpers and followers, or if my mind wants to be served, at that time I should stay like a log. ^5-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-52]]

If my mind wants to drop the good of others to seek my own good, or if it just wants to speak, then I should stay like a log. ^5-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-53]]

If I feel impatience, laziness, fear, rudeness, or idle talk, or if my mind clings to my own side, at that time I should stay like a log. ^5-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-54]]

I must check my mind for any troubling emotion and for chasing meaningless things. At that time, a hero should use a remedy to hold the mind firmly. ^5-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-55]]

With strong certainty and deep trust, I should be steady, respectful, and polite. With a sense of shame and a fear of doing wrong, I should be peaceful and try hard to make others happy. ^5-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-56]]

I should not be upset by the clashing desires of any childish person. Instead, I should think, "This mind of theirs has appeared because a troubling emotion grew in them." I will be a compassionate one. ^5-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-57]]

For things that are free of blame, which are in my hands and in the hands of living beings, I must always hold my mind like a magical creation, with no 'I' in it. ^5-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-58]]

It took a long time to get this best chance to practise. I should think about this again and again. I must keep my mind as steady and unshakable as Mount Meru. ^5-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-59]]

When hungry vultures drag this dead body all around, you are not upset, my mind. So why do you care so much for it right now? ^5-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-60]]

My mind, you hold onto this body and call it 'mine.' But why do you guard it so closely? If you and the body are separate, what can it really do for you? ^5-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-61]]

My mind of confusion, why do you not hold onto a clean wooden shape instead? This body is just a machine made of filth. What is the use of guarding this rotten thing? ^5-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-62]]

First, use your own thinking mind to separate this layer of skin. Then, use the blade of wisdom to separate the flesh from the web of bones. ^5-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-63]]

Take apart even the bones. Look right down to the marrow. Check for yourself. What essence is really in there? ^5-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-64]]

You look so hard, but you find no essence. Why do you still protect this body with clinging? ^5-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-65]]

Filth is not fit for you to eat. Blood is not fit to drink. Guts are not fit to suck. So what will this body do for you? ^5-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-66]]

At best, it is only guarded as food for foxes and vultures. Every human body is just something to be put to use. ^5-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-67]]

You might guard it carefully. But the cruel Lord of Death will snatch it away. He will give it to birds and dogs. What can you do then? ^5-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-68]]

You would not give clothes to a servant who cannot work. This body will leave you even if you feed it well. Why wear yourself out taking care of it? ^5-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-69]]

I pay my body what it needs, like a worker. Then I make it work for my goals. I should not give it everything if it brings no benefit. ^5-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-70]]

I think of my body as just a boat for moving around. To help living beings, I will turn it into a wish-granting body. ^5-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-71]]

So, practice being in control of oneself and always keep a smiling face. Stop frowning and scowling completely. Be a friend to wandering beings and speak honestly. ^5-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-72]]

I will not recklessly throw down chairs and other things with noise. I will not open doors with force. I will always enjoy acting quietly. ^5-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-73]]

Herons, cats, and thieves get what they want by moving silently and secretly. The Sage should always act in that same way. ^5-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-74]]

Some people give good advice, and others say helpful things without being asked. I will accept their words with respect, placing them on my head like a crown. I will always be a student to everyone. ^5-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-75]]

When someone says something good, I should tell them they spoke well! If I see someone building a store of goodness, I should happily praise them. ^5-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-76]]

I should talk about people's good qualities when they are not around. If others talk about a good quality, I should join in. If they talk about my own good qualities, I should just know they recognize a good quality. ^5-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-77]]

Everything we do is to find joy. True joy is rare even if you try to buy it. So, I should enjoy the happiness of joy by looking at the good qualities others have gained. ^5-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-78]]

I lose nothing in this life, and I will have great happiness in the next. Through faults, there is sadness and suffering now. In the next life, there is great suffering. ^5-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-79]]

When I speak, my words should make sense and bring comfort. My meaning should be clear and pleasing. I must let go of clinging and hatred. I should speak gently and not say too much. ^5-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-80]]

When I look at living beings, I should think, "By relying on these very beings, I will become a Buddha." I should always look at them in a direct and loving way. ^5-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-81]]

A good deed becomes very great when you do it with a strong, lasting wish, or as a remedy. This is true when you act toward people with good qualities, people who help you, and those in suffering. ^5-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-82]]

I will become skilled and full of faith. I will always do my work myself. I should not rely on anyone else for any action at all. ^5-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-83]]

I should practice the perfection of giving and the other practices better and better over time. I should not give up something big for something small. Above all, I must think about the good of others. ^5-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-84]]

Knowing this, I must always work hard for the good of others. The Compassionate One sees far ahead. Because of this, he allows a person working for others to do even forbidden things. ^5-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-85]]

I should share with people in miserable states, people with no protection, and those doing strict practice. I should eat only the right amount. I must give away everything except my three monk's robes. ^5-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-86]]

My body practices the holy teachings. I should not harm it for something small. If I act like this, I will quickly fulfill the wishes of every living being. ^5-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-87]]

If my compassion is not pure, I should not give away my body. I should only give it away if it will help achieve a great purpose in this life and future lives. ^5-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-88]]

I should not share the teachings with disrespectful people. I should not teach people who wrap their heads unless they are sick. I must not teach anyone holding an umbrella, a stick, or a weapon, or those with covered heads. ^5-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-89]]

I should not share deep and vast teachings with people who are not ready. I should not teach a woman when no man is present. I must always show equal respect to both simple and highest teachings. ^5-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-90]]

People who are ready for the vast teachings should not practice the lesser teachings instead. Good behavior should not be given up completely. I should not lure people away with teaching texts and sacred words. ^5-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-91]]

When I spit or throw away sticks used to clean teeth, I should cover them up. It is wrong to pee or leave waste in water or on land that people use. ^5-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-92]]

I should not eat with my mouth full, make noises, or chew with my mouth wide open. I should not sit with my legs stretched out, and I should not rub my hands together. ^5-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-93]]

I should not be alone with another woman in a vehicle, on a bed, on a seat, or in a room. By watching and asking, I should find out what makes the world lose faith. Then I must avoid all of it. ^5-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-94]]

I should never point with just one finger. Instead, I should respectfully use my whole right hand. Even when I am just showing someone the way to go, I should point like this. ^5-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-95]]

I should not wave my hands around too much. I should move them just a little and make a sound, like snapping my fingers, to signal someone. Otherwise, I will lose my self-control. ^5-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-96]]

I should lie down facing the direction I want, just like the Protector lay when passing into freedom from sorrow. Right from the very start, I should use watchfulness. I must firmly set my mind on waking up quickly. ^5-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-97]]

The teachings say that the way of acting of the awakening hero is endless. Until I am able to do all of it, I must certainly practice actions that make my mind pure. ^5-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-98]]

Three times during the day and three times at night, I should recite the Three Heaps text. By relying on the Victorious Ones and the awakening mind, I will clear away my remaining falls. ^5-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-99]]

Whether I am acting on my own or following someone else, I should always try hard. No matter what I am doing, I must follow whatever I was taught as a training. ^5-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-100]]

There is no action that is not a training for a child of the Victorious Ones. For a wise person who lives like this, everything becomes a store of goodness. ^5-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-101]]

Whether directly or indirectly, I should only do things that help living beings. For the sake of living beings alone, I should give everything to awakening by sharing the goodness. ^5-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-102]]

I must never give up a friend who guides me, even to save my own life. This friend knows the meaning of the Great Vehicle and keeps the highest strict practice of the awakening heroes. ^5-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-103]]

I should learn how to rely on a teacher from the life story of Shrisambhava. I should read the scriptures to learn this and other things the Buddha taught. ^5-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-104]]

The trainings are found in the scriptures, so I must read the scriptures. I should look at the Teaching of Akashagarbha first of all. ^5-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-105]]

I must also read the Compendium of Trainings again and again. It shows in great detail what I need to practice all the time. ^5-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-106]]

Or, for now, I should read the short Collection of the Buddha's Talks. I must also try hard to read both teaching texts written by the noble Nagarjuna. ^5-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-107]]

I should practice whatever is not forbidden in them. To protect the minds of the world, I must see the training and practice perfectly. ^5-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-108]]

I must check the states of my body and mind again and again. In short, this alone is the meaning of guarding watchfulness. ^5-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-109]]

I must actually do these things with my body. What good is just saying the words? Will a sick person get well just by reading a medical book? ^5-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^5-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Awakening Hero, the fifth chapter: Guarding Watchfulness. ^5-a

## Chapter Six: Teaching Patience ^6-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-1]]

A single moment of anger destroys all your good actions. It wipes out what you gathered over a thousand great ages, like giving and making offerings to the Well-Gone Ones. ^6-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-2]]

There is no harmful deed quite like hatred. There is no hard practice quite like patience. Because of this, you should try your best to practice meditation on patience in many different ways. ^6-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-3]]

If you hold hatred like a sharp dart in your mind, your mind will not feel peace. You will not find joy or happiness. You will not sleep, and you will feel unsteady. ^6-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-4]]

Think of the people who depend on a master for wealth and honor. If that master is full of hatred, even those people will attack and try to kill him. ^6-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-5]]

Because of it, friends feel sad. Even if he tries to gather them by giving, they will not stay. In short, there is no such thing as a happy person who holds onto anger. ^6-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-6]]

Anger is an enemy, and it brings these and other kinds of suffering. Whoever tries hard to destroy anger will be happy in this life and the next. ^6-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-7]]

Hatred grows when things happen that I do not want, or when I am stopped from getting what I want. This gives hatred the food of unhappiness. Then it grows strong and destroys me. ^6-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-8]]

Therefore, I must completely destroy the food of that enemy. This enemy has no other job except to hurt me. ^6-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-9]]

Whatever happens, I will not upset my joyful mind. Even if I am unhappy, it does not get me what I want. It only makes my good deeds fade away. ^6-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-10]]

If a problem can be fixed, why be unhappy about it? If it cannot be fixed, what is the use of being unhappy? ^6-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-11]]

I do not want suffering, disrespect, harsh words, or a bad name for myself or for my friends. But for an enemy, it is the exact opposite. ^6-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-12]]

The causes of happiness only happen sometimes. The causes of suffering are very common. Without suffering, there is no wish to be free. So, my mind, you must be strong. ^6-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-13]]

Followers of Durga and the Karnapas put up with the feeling of being burned and cut. They do this for no real reason. So why should I not endure pain for the sake of freedom? ^6-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-14]]

Everything becomes easier when you get used to it. There is nothing in the world that does not. So, by getting used to small harms, you can learn to endure great harms. ^6-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-15]]

Think of snakes, biting flies, itchy rashes, and the feeling of hunger and thirst. The suffering they bring is pointless. Why do you not see this? ^6-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-16]]

I will not be too sensitive to heat, cold, rain, or wind. I will not complain about sickness, being tied up, or being hit. If I am too sensitive, the harm only gets worse. ^6-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-17]]

When some people see their own blood, they become extra brave and strong. But when other people see someone else's blood, they get dizzy and faint. ^6-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-18]]

This comes from a steady mind or from being a coward. So, ignore harm. Do not let suffering upset you. ^6-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-19]]

Even when suffering happens, wise people keep a clear mind. This is because they are fighting a war against troubling emotions, and many harms happen during a battle. ^6-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-20]]

Real heroes ignore all suffering. They defeat enemies like hatred. Anyone else is just fighting dead bodies. ^6-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-21]]

Suffering also has a good quality. Getting tired of suffering stops us from feeling superior. We feel compassion for those in the cycle of rebirth. We avoid harmful deeds and enjoy good deeds. ^6-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-22]]

Things like bile cause great suffering, but we do not get angry at them. So why get angry at a being with a mind? They are also pushed by circumstances. ^6-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-23]]

For example, sickness happens even when we do not want it. In the same way, troubling emotions rise up with force even when we do not want them. ^6-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-24]]

People get angry over little things without even thinking, "I will get angry." In the same way, anger just happens without us deciding to make it. ^6-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-25]]

Every single fault and all kinds of harmful deeds happen because of circumstances. They do not have independence. ^6-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-26]]

Even those circumstances that come together do not think, "I will make this happen." And the thing they make does not think, "I am being made." ^6-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-27]]

Whatever people call Primal Matter, and whatever people call the self, do not decide to exist. They do not think, "I will come to be." ^6-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-28]]

If it does not exist before it begins, what could wish to exist? Because it would always be busy with things we sense, it could never stop. ^6-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-29]]

If that self is lasting forever, it clearly does nothing, just like space. Even if it meets other circumstances, what can change something that never changes? ^6-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-30]]

If something stays exactly the same while it acts, what did the action do to it? What connection could there be? How could anyone say, "This is its action"? ^6-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-31]]

Everything is controlled by other forces. Because of this power, everything is powerless. Knowing this, a person will not get angry at any things. They are like things that magic has conjured up. ^6-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-32]]

Someone might ask, "What can stop what? Even stopping things makes no sense!" But because things depend on each other, it does make sense. It is not wrong to wish for sufferings to be cut away. ^6-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-33]]

Therefore, if you see an enemy or a friend doing something wrong, think, "This has come from these circumstances." Think this way and stay at ease. ^6-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-34]]

If things happened just by their own choice, suffering would never happen to anyone. Since no one wants suffering, it would never arise for any beings with a body. ^6-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-35]]

Through carelessness, people even hurt themselves with thorns and other things. To get women and other things they want, they get very worked up and start going without food. ^6-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-36]]

Some people hang themselves or jump off cliffs. They eat poison and bad food. They bring harm to themselves through a way of acting that builds no store of goodness. ^6-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-37]]

When people are controlled by a troubling emotion, they might even kill themselves. If they can hurt themselves like this, how could they not hurt others? ^6-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-38]]

A troubling emotion can make people do terrible things like killing themselves. If compassion does not even arise for them, what sense is there in anger? ^6-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-39]]

If hurting others is just the nature of a childish person, it is wrong to feel anger toward them. That would be like being mad at fire because its nature is to burn. ^6-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-40]]

Or what if this bad behavior is only temporary, and the nature of living beings is gentle? Even then, it is wrong to feel anger. That would be like being mad at the sky just because smoke rises in it. ^6-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-41]]

Someone might hit me with a stick. If I feel anger at the person swinging it, since he is driven by hatred too, it makes more sense to feel anger at hatred. ^6-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-42]]

In the past, I hurt living beings like this. Because I harmed living beings, it is only right that I get hurt now. ^6-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-43]]

His weapon and my body are both causes of suffering. He brought the weapon, and I brought my body. So who should get my anger? ^6-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-44]]

This body is like a painful sore shaped like a person. It is full of suffering and hurts to touch. Blinded by wanting, I held onto it. When it gets hurt, who should get my anger? ^6-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-45]]

A childish person does not want suffering. But they still want the causes of suffering. When they are hurt by their own mistakes, why should they blame others? ^6-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-46]]

Think of the hell guardians and the forest of sword leaves. My own actions made them appear. So who should get my anger? ^6-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-47]]

Pushed by my own actions, people appear and hurt me. If doing this makes them go to hell, haven't I ruined them? ^6-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-48]]

Because of them, I practice patience. This wipes away my many harmful deeds. But because of me, they go to the hells. They face long-lasting suffering there. ^6-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-49]]

I am the one who harms them. They are the ones who help me. O wicked mind, why do you act in such a mistaken way? Why do you create anger? ^6-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-50]]

If my intention has a good quality, I will not go to hell. If I protect myself, what will happen to them here? ^6-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-51]]

If I harm them back, they will not be safe. My good way of acting will get worse. Then my hard practice will be ruined. ^6-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-52]]

The mind is not a being with a body. Because of this, no one can destroy it anywhere. But by holding on tightly to the body, the body is hurt by sufferings. ^6-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-53]]

Disrespect, harsh words, and unpleasant talk do not harm the body. O mind, why do you feel such strong anger? ^6-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-54]]

If other people being upset with me will not destroy me in this life or another life, why do I dislike it? ^6-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-55]]

If I dislike this because it puts something in the way of my gain, my gain will just be left behind here. But my harmful deeds will stay firmly with me. ^6-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-56]]

It is better to die today than to live a long time through a wrong way to earn a living. Even if I stay alive for a long time, the suffering of death is exactly the same. ^6-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-57]]

Think of a person who wakes up after enjoying happiness in a dream for a hundred years. Think of another person who wakes up after enjoying happiness for just one moment. ^6-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-58]]

For both of the people who wake up, that happiness does not return. For both a long life and a short life, it is exactly the same at the time of death. ^6-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-59]]

Even if I get a lot of gain and enjoy happiness for a long time, I will leave naked and empty-handed. I will be just like someone robbed by a thief. ^6-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-60]]

I might say, "By staying alive through gain, I will wipe out harmful deeds and build a store of goodness." But if I feel anger to get that gain, won't my store of goodness run out? Won't more harmful deeds happen? ^6-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-61]]

If the very reason I live is destroyed, what is the point of living? What good is a life that only does harmful deeds? ^6-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-62]]

You might say, "I feel anger at people who say mean things, because they ruin living beings." But people say mean things about others too. Why do you not feel anger about that? ^6-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-63]]

A lack of faith depends on other people. If you have patience for people without faith, why not have patience for people who speak badly? Their bad words just depend on a troubling emotion. ^6-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-64]]

Some people insult and destroy statues, shrines, and the holy teachings. Even then, it is wrong for me to feel hatred. The Buddhas and others cannot be harmed by this. ^6-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-65]]

Some people harm my teachers, relatives, and friends. But just like before, I see that this harm only comes from circumstances. Seeing this, I should stop my anger. ^6-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-66]]

Both a being with a mind and a thing without a mind can hurt a being with a body. Why do we only blame the being with a mind? Therefore, practice patience when you are harmed. ^6-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-67]]

Some people do bad things because of confusion. Others react with anger because of confusion. Which of them is without fault? And which one is to blame? ^6-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-68]]

Why did I do the past action that makes others harm me? If everything comes from action, why should I be angry about this? ^6-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-69]]

Seeing this, I must try my best to build my store of goodness. I will do this so that everyone can have a loving mind for one another. ^6-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-70]]

Imagine a fire spreading from one burning house to another. If the fire catches on straw or things like that, people pull the straw out and throw it away. ^6-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-71]]

In the same way, the fire of hatred spreads to whatever the mind is clinging to. To stop my store of goodness from burning, I must throw away right now whatever my mind is clinging to. ^6-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-72]]

If a person about to be killed loses a hand but goes free, isn't that good? If human suffering frees someone from hell, isn't that a good thing? ^6-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-73]]

If I cannot bear even this much suffering right now, why do I not stop my anger? Anger is the cause of suffering in hell. ^6-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-74]]

Because of my desire for pleasure, I have suffered burning in hell thousands of times. Yet I have not achieved my own good or the good of others. ^6-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-75]]

This is not so harmful, and it will achieve great things. I should only be glad about suffering that takes away the harm from beings. ^6-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-76]]

If others find joy by praising those who have good qualities, O my mind, why don't you praise them and rejoice in that same way? ^6-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-77]]

The happiness you feel from rejoicing is a source of joy without any blame. A person with good qualities allows it. It is also the best way to bring others together. ^6-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-78]]

You might think, "Paying them will make them happy." If you do not want them to be happy, you might not pay their wages. That will ruin this life and the next. ^6-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-79]]

When people praise your own good qualities, you want others to be happy too. When they praise the good qualities of others, you do not even want happiness for yourself. ^6-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-80]]

You want happiness for all living beings. You showed this by giving rise to the awakening mind for awakening. If living beings find happiness themselves, why do you get angry at that? ^6-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-81]]

You want living beings to achieve becoming a Buddha. You want the three worlds to honor them. When you see them receive just a little respect, why does that upset you? ^6-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-82]]

Imagine someone you must take care of and give things to. If that relative finds a way to support themselves, do you get angry instead of being glad? ^6-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-83]]

If you do not want this for beings, who will want awakening for them? If someone gets angry at another person's success, where is their awakening mind? ^6-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-84]]

Whether someone gets a gift, or it stays in the giver's house, you do not have it either way. So what does it matter if it is given or not? ^6-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-85]]

Why do you throw away your store of goodness, your faith, or your own good qualities? You let go of what brings you gains. Tell me, why are you not angry at yourself? ^6-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-86]]

You feel no sorrow for the harmful deeds you yourself have done. On top of that, do you want to compete with others who have built up a store of goodness? This is the end of the second part of the book. ^6-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-87]]

Even if an enemy is unhappy, what is there for you to be glad about? Your wishing alone will not cause him any harm. ^6-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-88]]

Even if your wishes make that suffering happen, what is there for you to be glad about? If you say, 'I will be satisfied,' what could ruin you more than that? ^6-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-89]]

The fisherman of troubling emotions throws a terrible hook that is hard to bear. If I am caught by it, the hell guardians will certainly boil me in the cauldrons of hell. ^6-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-90]]

The honor of praise and fame does not build my store of goodness. It does not give me a long life. It does not become my strength or health. It does not even make my body comfortable. ^6-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-91]]

Even if I knew my own good, what own good is there in that? If I only want my mind to feel happy, I would also have to turn to gambling, alcohol, and things like that. ^6-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-92]]

If I waste my wealth for the sake of fame, and even get myself killed, what is the use of mere words? Once I am dead, who will be made happy by them? ^6-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-93]]

When a sandcastle falls down, children cry in despair. In the same way, when praise and fame fade away, my own mind acts just like a child. ^6-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-94]]

First of all, a sound has no mind. It cannot possibly mean to praise me. People might say that others are pleased with me. Is that a reason to feel joy? ^6-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-95]]

Whether someone is pleased with another person or with me, how does their joy help me? That joy and happiness belong only to them. I do not get a share of it. ^6-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-96]]

If I am happy because they are happy, I should feel this way toward everyone. Why am I not happy when they are happy for someone else? ^6-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-97]]

Feeling joy just because I think I am praised makes no sense. It is nothing but the behavior of a child. ^6-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-98]]

Praise and similar things distract me. They destroy my weariness with the cycle of rebirth. They cause jealousy toward those with good qualities. They also destroy excellence. ^6-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-99]]

Therefore, some people are around to destroy my praise and things like it. Are they not protecting me from falling into an unhappy realm? ^6-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-100]]

Since I seek freedom, I do not need the chains of gain and honor. These people give me freedom from being tied up. How could I be angry with them? ^6-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-101]]

When I wish to step into suffering, these people become a door that keeps me out. They act like a blessing of the Buddha. How could I be angry with them? ^6-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-102]]

I might think someone is blocking my store of goodness, but it is not right to be angry even at them. Since there is no hard practice equal to patience, should I not stay patient? ^6-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-103]]

If my own faults stop me from practicing patience with this, I am only making a block, even when the cause of a store of goodness is right in front of me. ^6-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-104]]

If one thing cannot happen without another, and it happens when the other is there, then that is its cause. How can it be called a block? ^6-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-105]]

A beggar who arrives at the right time is not a block to giving. Someone who gives you the vows of a monk or nun cannot be called a block to becoming a monk or nun. ^6-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-106]]

There are many beggars in the world, but people who cause harm are rare. If I do not hurt others, no one will hurt me. ^6-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-107]]

Therefore, an enemy is like a treasure that appears in the house without any hard work. Since they are a helper in the way of acting for awakening, I should be happy with my enemy. ^6-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-108]]

Patience is created by both my enemies and me. So, I should offer the result of patience to them first. This is because they are the cause of patience. ^6-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-109]]

Someone might say I should not honor my enemy, because they do not intend to help me practice patience. If so, why do we honor the holy teachings? They are also a good cause that helps us succeed. ^6-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-110]]

Someone might say I should not honor my enemy because they actually want to harm me. But if they tried to help me like a kind doctor, how could I ever learn patience? ^6-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-111]]

Therefore, patience arises by depending on someone's very hateful mind. Since they are the exact cause of patience, they deserve to be honored just like the holy teachings. ^6-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-112]]

Because of this, the Sage taught about the field of living beings and the field of the Victorious Ones. By making them happy, many have reached the perfection of excellence. ^6-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-113]]

Living beings and the Victorious Ones both help us achieve the qualities of a Buddha. Just as we respect the Victorious Ones, why do we not respect living beings too? ^6-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-114]]

They do not have the exact same intention. But they are alike because of the result. Just like the Buddhas, living beings also have good qualities. That is why they are equal. ^6-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-115]]

Making an offering to someone with a loving mind shows the greatness of living beings. The store of goodness that comes from having faith in the Buddhas shows the greatness of the Buddhas. ^6-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-116]]

Living beings have a share in bringing about the qualities of a Buddha. Because of this, living beings and the Buddhas are seen as equal. But no one is truly equal to the Buddhas. They have an endless ocean of good qualities. ^6-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-117]]

If even a tiny part of that special collection of supreme good qualities appears in someone, I should make offerings to them. Even giving them the three realms would be too little. ^6-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-118]]

A small part of the greatest qualities of a Buddha grows in living beings. Because they have this small part, it is right to treat living beings as worthy of gifts. ^6-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-119]]

There are true friends who never trick me and who help me in ways I cannot measure. How could I ever pay them back? The only way is by making living beings happy. ^6-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-120]]

They give up their bodies for these people. They even enter the Avichi hell for them. Helping these people is a way to pay them back. So, even if people cause me great harm, I should treat them all with complete goodness. ^6-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-121]]

Even my own lords do not care about their own bodies to help these people. How could a fool like me act with pride toward them and not act as a servant? ^6-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-122]]

The Sages are happy when these people are happy. They are upset when these people are hurt. By bringing them joy, I please all the Sages. By harming them, I harm the Sages. ^6-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-123]]

If someone's body is completely on fire, nothing nice can make them happy. In the same way, when living beings are hurt, there is no way to please those with great compassion. ^6-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-124]]

I have caused harm to beings. This has upset all those with great compassion. Today, I own up to each harmful deed. I pray the Sages forgive me for upsetting them. ^6-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-125]]

To please the Thus-Gone Ones, I will be a servant to the world from today onward. Many beings might kick my head. They might even kill me. I will not fight back. May the Protectors of the World be pleased! ^6-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-126]]

Those who have the nature of compassion have made all these beings into themselves. I have no doubt about this. The ones we see as living beings are those Protectors themselves. Why do I not respect them? ^6-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-127]]

This alone is what pleases the Thus-Gone Ones. This alone perfectly achieves my own good. This alone takes away the suffering of the world. Therefore, I will always practice this alone. ^6-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-128]]

For example, imagine one of a king's men hurts many people. People who look ahead do not hurt him back, even if they can. ^6-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-129]]

This is because he is not alone. The king's power is his power. In the same way, I should never look down on a weak person who causes harm. ^6-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-130]]

This is because the hell guardians and the compassionate ones are his forces. Therefore, just as subjects please a fierce king, I should please living beings. ^6-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-131]]

Even if a king gets angry, could he cause that harm of hell? That is what we will suffer if we displease living beings. ^6-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-132]]

Even if I make a king happy, he cannot give me the state of a Buddha. I can only get that by making living beings happy. ^6-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-133]]

Leave aside becoming a Buddha in the future by making living beings happy. Why do I not see that great glory, fame, and happiness will come to me in this very life? ^6-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-134]]

While in the cycle of rebirth, patience brings me beauty, health, and fame. I will also get a very long life and the great happiness of a king of the whole world. ^6-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^6-a]]

This is the sixth chapter from Entering the Way of the Awakening Hero. It is called Patience. ^6-a

## Chapter Seven: Teaching Joyful Effort ^7-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-1]]

Now that I have patience, I must start joyful effort. Awakening depends on this hard work. Just as nothing moves without the wind, a store of goodness does not grow without joyful effort. ^7-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-2]]

What is joyful effort? It is taking delight in a good deed. Now I will explain its opposite. The opposite is laziness, clinging to what is bad, and discouragement, which is looking down on oneself. ^7-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-3]]

Laziness comes from enjoying the pleasure of idleness. It grows from the wanting that comes with sleep. It also happens when you do not feel tired of the suffering in the cycle of rebirth. ^7-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-4]]

You are chased by the hunter of troubling emotions. You are caught in the net of birth. You have walked into the mouth of the Lord of Death. How do you still not know this? ^7-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-5]]

Have you not seen your own friends and family dying one by one? Yet you just keep sleeping. You are like a buffalo standing right next to its butcher. ^7-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-6]]

All the paths are blocked. The Lord of Death is watching you. How can you enjoy eating? How can you enjoy sleeping like this? ^7-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-7]]

You will die very quickly. Until then, you should gather stored-up goodness. Even if you give up laziness at the very end, what can you do when it is too late? ^7-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-8]]

I will think, "I haven't finished this. I just started that. This is only half done." Then the Lord of Death will suddenly arrive. I will think, "Oh no, I am ruined!" ^7-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-9]]

My relatives will lose all hope. Their eyes will be red and puffy from crying. Tears will run down their faces. Then I will look into the faces of the messengers of Yama. ^7-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-10]]

Remembering my own harmful deeds will torment me. I will hear the sounds of hell. I will be so terrified that I soil myself. When my mind gets confused, what will I do? ^7-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-11]]

In this life, you flip around in fear like a live fish out of water. Since you have done harmful deeds, there is no need to mention the unbearable sufferings of hell! ^7-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-12]]

When hot water touches you, your soft skin burns badly. You have done actions that lead to hell. Why are you just relaxing like this? ^7-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-13]]

You want the result without trying hard. You are fragile and face many harms. Death is grabbing you, yet you act like a god. Oh no, you are destroyed by sufferings! ^7-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-14]]

Use the boat of a human body to cross the great river of suffering! This boat will be hard to find later. So do not be foolish and sleep right now! ^7-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-15]]

The holy teachings are an endless cause of joy. Why have you left their great joy behind? Why do you enjoy distraction by the causes of suffering, along with restlessness and the like? ^7-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-16]]

Let go of discouragement. Gather your force with wholehearted commitment and mastery of oneself. Practice seeing self and others as equal! Practice swapping self and others! ^7-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-17]]

Do not fall into discouragement by thinking, "How could I ever reach awakening?" The Thus-Gone One is the Speaker of Truth, and he has spoken this truth: ^7-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-18]]

Think of flies, gadflies, bees, and worms. If even they build up the power of joyful effort, they will reach the highest awakening, which is very hard to achieve. ^7-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-19]]

I have been born as a human being. I know what is helpful and what is harmful. If I do not give up the way of acting for awakening, why should I not reach awakening? ^7-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-20]]

I might think, "I am afraid to give away my arms and legs." But this fear is just confusion. I am not weighing what matters more and what matters less. ^7-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-21]]

For countless millions of great ages, I will be cut many times. I will be pierced, burned, and split apart. Even so, I will not reach awakening. ^7-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-22]]

The suffering of reaching awakening has a limit. It is just like the pain of cutting the body open. You do it to pull out a splinter that is destroying you from the inside. ^7-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-23]]

All doctors cure sickness by causing some discomfort with their treatments. Therefore, to destroy many sufferings, I should put up with a little discomfort. ^7-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-24]]

The Greatest Doctor does not use normal treatments like these. He uses a very gentle method. With it, he cures countless great diseases. ^7-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-25]]

At first, the Guide starts us off with giving vegetables and things like that. Once we get used to that, we can slowly learn to give away even our own flesh. ^7-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-26]]

When I think of my own body as being like simple vegetables, giving away my flesh is no longer hard. What difficulty is there in that? ^7-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-27]]

If I give up every harmful deed, I will have no suffering. If I am a wise person, I will not be unhappy. It is wrong thinking and harmful deeds that hurt the mind and body. ^7-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-28]]

A store of goodness puts the body at ease. Being a wise person puts the mind at ease. Even while staying in the cycle of rebirth for the good of others, why would a compassionate one get tired? ^7-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-29]]

The power of the awakening mind uses up past harmful deeds. It also gathers an ocean of stored goodness. Because of this, the bodhisattva is greater than the Hearers. ^7-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-30]]

So climb onto the horse of the awakening mind! It takes away all tiredness. As you ride from happiness to happiness, what smart person would ever fall into discouragement? ^7-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-31]]

To help living beings, we need four kinds of strength. These are a strong wish, steadiness, joy, and letting go. A strong wish grows from the fear of suffering and from thinking about the benefits of that wish. ^7-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-32]]

I must leave the opposite behind. I should try hard to grow my joyful effort. I will do this through a strong wish, pride, joy, and letting go. I will also use the power of wholehearted commitment and mastery. ^7-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-33]]

I must destroy the countless faults of myself and others. It would take an ocean of great ages to end each and every fault. ^7-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-34]]

I do not see even a tiny bit of effort in myself to end those faults. I am a place of endless suffering. Why does my heart not burst? ^7-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-35]]

I must build up many good qualities in myself and others. If I must practice each and every good quality for an ocean of great ages, ^7-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-36]]

But I have never practiced even a tiny part of a good quality. I somehow found this life. It is amazing that I have made it meaningless. ^7-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-37]]

I have not made offerings to the Blessed One. I have not shared the joy of a great festival. I have not served the teachings. I have not fulfilled the wishes of the poor. ^7-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-38]]

If I have not given freedom from fear to frightened people, and I have not given happiness to sad people, then my birth only caused my mother pain and suffering. ^7-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-39]]

Because I did not have a strong wish for the teachings in the past, and still do not have it now, I have ended up so poor. Who would ever give up a strong wish for the teachings? ^7-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-40]]

The Sage has said that a strong wish is the root of every kind of good deed. The root of that strong wish is always meditating on the result that ripens. ^7-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-41]]

Suffering and unhappiness, many kinds of fears, and losing what we want all come from doing harmful deeds. ^7-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-42]]

By doing a good deed planned in your mind, wherever you go, your store of goodness will bring you honor and the gifts of its results. ^7-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-43]]

People who do harmful deeds might want happiness. But wherever they go, those harmful deeds will destroy them with the weapons of suffering. ^7-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-44]]

Because of my good deeds, I will become a child of the Well-Gone One. I will sit in front of the Victorious One. I will live inside a huge, cool, sweet-smelling lotus. The sweet words of the Victorious One will feed my bright glow. The light of the Sage will open my lotus, and I will have a perfect body. ^7-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-45]]

By doing many bad deeds, I will fall onto the intensely burning iron ground. The helpers of Yama will cause me great pain by tearing off all my skin. They will pour melted, hot copper over my body. Blazing swords and short spears will pierce me, cutting my flesh into a hundred pieces. ^7-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-46]]

Because of this, I should really want to do good deeds. I must practice them with great respect. Using the method of Vajradhvaja, once I begin, I should grow my pride. ^7-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-47]]

First, I should check my resources. Then I must decide to start or not to start. It is best not to begin at all. But once I begin, I should not turn back. ^7-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-48]]

If I get used to giving up, it will happen in another life too. My harmful deeds and suffering will increase. Any other action, and the time of its result, will become weak. Then that other action will not get finished either. ^7-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-49]]

I should build my pride in three things: my action, my troubling emotions, and my ability. Thinking, "I will do this alone," is called pride in action. ^7-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-50]]

This world has no power because of troubling emotions. It cannot achieve its own good. Other beings are not able to help themselves like I can. So, I will do this work. ^7-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-51]]

If others are doing lesser things, how can I just sit and do nothing? I do not act out of pride. For me, it is best to be without pride. ^7-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-52]]

When a crow finds a dead snake, it acts like a giant sky bird. If I am weak, even a small fall will hurt me. ^7-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-53]]

If someone feels sad and gives up their effort, can they ever escape their hardships? By building up pride and effort, you become hard to defeat even by great downfalls. ^7-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-54]]

Therefore, I must conquer falls with a strong mind. If a fall defeats me, my wish to conquer the three realms will just be a joke. ^7-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-55]]

I will conquer everything, and no one will conquer me. I am a child of the Victorious Lion. I will stay in this pride. ^7-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-56]]

Anyone who is beaten by pride has a troubling emotion. They do not have true pride. Someone with true pride does not fall under the enemy's power. The others fall under the power of the enemy called pride. ^7-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-57]]

People puffed up by the troubling emotion of pride are led by pride to an unhappy realm. Their human joy is ruined, and they become slaves who eat other people's food. ^7-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-58]]

They are foolish, ugly, and weak. People look down on them everywhere. If a person of hard practice is puffed up with pride and counted among the proud, tell me, what are the lowest people like? ^7-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-59]]

Whoever holds onto pride to beat the enemy called pride is truly proud. They are a winning hero. Whoever destroys the strong enemy called pride perfects the result of winning for wandering beings, just as they wish. ^7-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-60]]

If I live right in the middle of troubling emotions, I will stay strong in a thousand ways. I will be like a lion among foxes. I will not be hurt by the crowd of troubling emotions. ^7-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-61]]

When great danger comes, a person naturally protects their eyes. In the same way, even if danger comes, I will not fall under the power of troubling emotions. ^7-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-62]]

Just as I want the happiness that comes from playing a game, I should love whatever action I need to do. I should find joy in it and never get tired of it. ^7-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-63]]

Even if I do something just to get happiness, I am not sure if happiness will really come. But if the action itself becomes happiness, how could I have happiness without doing it? ^7-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-64]]

If we never get enough of our desires for pleasure, which are like honey smeared on a razor's edge, how could I ever get enough of a store of goodness? Its ripening is happiness and peace. ^7-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-65]]

Therefore, to finish my task, I should dive right into the action. I should be like a hot elephant in the midday sun that finds a cool lake and plunges right in. ^7-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-66]]

If my strength starts to fade, I should set the action aside so I can finish it later. Once a task is completely done, I should leave it behind, eager to start the next one. ^7-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-67]]

Just like an experienced warrior clashing swords with enemies on a battlefield, I must dodge the weapons of the troubling emotions. Then I must strike to destroy those enemy troubling emotions. ^7-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-68]]

If someone drops a sword in battle, they quickly pick it up out of fear. If I drop my weapon of keeping in mind, I must quickly pick it up too. I must remember the fears of hell. ^7-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-69]]

Poison uses the blood to spread all through the body. In the same way, if a fault finds a chance, it will spread all through the mind. ^7-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-70]]

Imagine carrying a bowl full of oil while someone with a sword stands nearby. You would fear death if you spilled it. A strict practitioner must be just as focused. ^7-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-71]]

If a snake crawled into your lap, you would jump up right away. In the same way, if sleep and sluggishness come, I must quickly push them away. ^7-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-72]]

Every time a fault happens, I will scold myself. I will think about it for a long time. I will say, "I must make sure this never happens to me again." ^7-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-73]]

I should ask myself, "How can keeping in mind become a habit in these situations?" For this reason, I should seek out the right company or do the right actions. ^7-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-74]]

Before I act, I want to be ready for anything. By remembering the teachings on carefulness, I should make myself jump up lightly. ^7-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-75]]

Just as the wind blows a piece of cotton around, I should let eagerness guide me. That is how I will succeed. ^7-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^7-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Awakening Hero, this is the seventh chapter, called the Teaching on Joyful Effort. ^7-a

## Chapter Eight: Calm Focus ^8-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-1]]

Now that I have grown my joyful effort, I should place my mind in deep focus. A person whose mind is totally distracted is caught between the fangs of troubling emotions. ^8-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-2]]

By keeping my body and mind in solitude, a wandering mind will not happen. Therefore, I should leave the world behind and completely drop every restless thought. ^8-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-3]]

We do not leave the world behind because of clinging. We are stuck wanting gain and other things. So, a wise person should completely let go of these things and think like this. ^8-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-4]]

Clear seeing joined with calm abiding completely destroys troubling emotions. Knowing this, I should first look for calm abiding. I can reach it by finding joy in freedom from clinging to the world. ^8-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-5]]

Why would a being who is not lasting feel true clinging for someone else who is not lasting? Because of this, they will not see their loved ones for thousands of lifetimes. ^8-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-6]]

If we do not see them, we feel no joy, and the mind does not rest as a settled mind. Even when we do see them, we are never satisfied. We are still hurt by wanting, just as before. ^8-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-7]]

If we become attached to living beings, what is truly real is completely hidden. The weary mind is also destroyed. In the end, we will be hurt by sorrow. ^8-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-8]]

By just thinking about them, this life will pass away with no meaning. Because of friends who are not lasting, even the eternal teachings will be destroyed. ^8-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-9]]

If I share the same fate as a childish person, I will certainly go to an unhappy realm. If they lead me to a fate unlike that of the noble ones, what is the use of relying on a childish person? ^8-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-10]]

They become friends in an instant. Then they become enemies just as fast. They get mad even at happy things. An ordinary person is very hard to please. ^8-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-11]]

If I say helpful things, they become angry. They also stop me from doing helpful things. If I do not listen to them, their anger will make them fall into an unhappy realm. ^8-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-12]]

They feel jealousy toward those above them. They compete with their equals. They show pride toward those below them, and act stuck-up when praised. If someone speaks to them unkindly, they feel anger. When will I ever get any benefit from a foolish person? ^8-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-13]]

If I spend time with a childish person, bad deeds will definitely happen. That childish person brings about praising oneself and putting others down. We will talk about enjoying the cycle of rebirth and things like that. ^8-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-14]]

If we rely on each other like this, we will only ruin ourselves. They will not do me any good. And I will not do them any good either. ^8-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-15]]

I will stay far away from a foolish person. If we meet, I will be friendly and make them happy. But I will not get too close. I will just act like an ordinary person. ^8-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-16]]

A bee just takes nectar from a flower. In the same way, I will only take what helps me practice the teachings. I will not become close to anyone, as if I had never seen them before. ^8-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-17]]

I might think that I have much gain and honor, and that many people like me. If I hold onto that puffed-up feeling, fear will come to me after I die. ^8-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-18]]

Therefore, my confused mind might hold tightly to many things. But all those things will add up together. They will grow a thousand times bigger and turn into suffering. ^8-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-19]]

Therefore, a wise person should avoid clinging, because fear comes from clinging. All these things will naturally be left behind anyway. I must stay strong and understand this completely. ^8-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-20]]

I might gather a lot of gain, fame, and a good name. But even with all that gain and fame, I have no idea where I will go after I die. ^8-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-21]]

If there are others who speak badly of me, why should praise make me glad? If there are others who praise me, why should I be sad when people speak badly of me? ^8-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-22]]

Living beings have many different strong wishes. If even the Victorious Ones cannot please them all, how could someone as lowly as me do it? Therefore, I will let go of my thoughts about the world. ^8-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-23]]

Living beings say bad things about people without gains. They also say mean things about people with gains. They are naturally hard to be around. How could they ever bring me joy? ^8-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-24]]

A childish person is never happy unless they get something for themselves. Because of this, the Thus-Gone Ones have said that no childish person is a friend. ^8-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-25]]

In the forest, deer, birds, and trees do not say mean things. When will I live with them? Their company is so pleasant. ^8-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-26]]

I will make my home in a cave, an empty temple, or at the foot of a tree. When will I stop looking back and become entirely free from clinging? ^8-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-27]]

When will I live in wide, open places that no one calls "mine"? When will I live there freely, without holding on to anything? ^8-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-28]]

When will I live without fear? I will own just an alms bowl and a few small things. I will wear clothes that no one wants, and I will not even hide this body. ^8-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-29]]

When will I go to a place of the dead? When will I treat my own body and the skeletons of others as exactly the same? Both are meant to pass away. ^8-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-30]]

One day, even this very body of mine will change. It will smell so bad that not even foxes will come near it. ^8-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-31]]

This body came to be as one whole thing. But the flesh and bones that were born together will break down and separate. If my own body falls apart, why even mention other friends? ^8-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-32]]

I was born alone, and I will die alone. No one else can take a share of my suffering. So what is the use of friends who only get in the way? ^8-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-33]]

People traveling on a road find a place to stay. In the same way, those on the path of life find a place to be born. ^8-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-34]]

While the world grieves all around, go to the forest! Do this before four men carry your body away. ^8-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-35]]

This body lives all alone, making no friends and no enemies. Since I act like I am already dead, no one will feel sorrow when I die. ^8-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-36]]

No one stays nearby to cause the harm of sorrow. Because of this, no one will distract me from remembering the Buddha and other things. ^8-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-37]]

Therefore, I will live all alone in beautiful, joyful forests that are free from hardship. These forests calm all distractions. ^8-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-38]]

I will let go of all other thoughts. I will focus only on my own mind. I will work hard at settling the mind and taming it. ^8-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-39]]

In this world and the next, desire for pleasure brings ruin. Here, it leads to being killed, tied up, and hurt. In the next life, it brings about the hells. ^8-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-40]]

In the past, you sent many messengers and made many requests for their sake. You did not hesitate to do harmful deeds or ruin your good name for them. ^8-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-41]]

You even put yourself in danger and spent all your wealth for them. You thought that hugging them would make you perfectly happy. ^8-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-42]]

But those very people are nothing but skeletons! They have no independence and no self. Why do you desire them and hold on so tightly? Why do you not go to freedom from sorrow? ^8-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-43]]

At first you worked hard to lift that face. Even when you lifted it up, it looked down shyly. Whether you had seen it before or not, that face was covered by a cloth. ^8-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-44]]

That face became a troubling emotion for you. Now it is right out in the open. Seeing it uncovered by vultures, why do you run away from it now? ^8-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-45]]

Even when others just looked at it with their eyes, you guarded it completely. While it is being eaten by them, why do you not guard it, you stingy person? ^8-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-46]]

When you see this heap of flesh, vultures and others will eat it. Why do you honor the food of others with flower garlands, sandalwood, and ornaments? ^8-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-47]]

When you see a simple skeleton, you are terrified even if it does not move. Why are you not afraid when something moves it, like a corpse that walks? ^8-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-48]]

Body waste and spit are both made from the exact same food. Out of these two, you dislike the body waste. So why do you like the spit? ^8-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-49]]

A person full of desire does not enjoy a soft cotton pillow. They say, "It does not leak a bad smell." They are confused about what is unclean. ^8-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-50]]

If you feel clinging to this even when it is covered, why do you not want it when it is uncovered? If you have no need for that, why do you hug it when it is covered? ^8-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-51]]

If you are not attached to filth, why do you hug another person? They are a cage of bones tied together. They are covered with the mud of flesh. ^8-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-52]]

You are full of filth yourself. You use it all the time. Yet you still want other bags of filth. You desire them out of greed for filth. ^8-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-53]]

You say, 'I love this flesh.' You want to touch and look at it. But how can you desire this flesh? It is naturally without a mind. ^8-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-54]]

The mind you want cannot be seen or touched. The part you can touch is not awareness. So what is the point of that useless hug? ^8-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-55]]

Another person's body has the nature of filth. It is not surprising if you do not realize this. But you yourself are filth itself. It is a great wonder that you do not realize that! ^8-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-56]]

You leave behind a fresh lotus opened by the bright sun. Your mind is attached to filth. Why do you find joy in a cage of filth? ^8-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-57]]

If you refuse to touch a patch of ground smeared with filth, how can you want to touch the body it came from? ^8-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-58]]

If you do not like filth, why do you hold someone in your lap who grew from an unclean field and was made by that seed? ^8-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-59]]

You do not want even a tiny worm born in filth. Yet you want a body that is full of filth and born from filth. ^8-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-60]]

You do not even look down on your own filth. But out of greed for filth, you also desire another bag of filth. ^8-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-61]]

Think of nice things like camphor, cooked rice, or vegetables. Once you put them in your mouth and spit them out, they make the ground foul. ^8-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-62]]

If you still doubt this is unclean, even when it is easy to see, look at the bodies left in the place of the dead. Look at those other unclean things, too. ^8-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-63]]

If the skin is peeled away from it, you will feel great fear. Since you know this, how can you feel joy for that same thing again? ^8-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-64]]

The smell rubbed on the body is just sandalwood and things like that. It is nothing else. Because of that smell of another, why do you have clinging to another? ^8-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-65]]

If it naturally smells bad, isn't it better to have no clinging to it? Why do people in the world who crave for no reason rub sweet smells on it? ^8-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-66]]

But if the sweet smell is sandalwood, what actually comes from the body here? Because of the smell of another, why do you have clinging to another? ^8-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-67]]

If it has long hair and nails, dirty yellow teeth, and the smell of mud, the nature of the body is truly scary when naked. ^8-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-68]]

Why do you work so hard to care for it, like a weapon that hurts you? Through the efforts of people confused about the self, this earth is completely stirred up by madmen. ^8-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-69]]

If you feel sick seeing only skeletons in the place of the dead, why do you like the village? The village is also a place of the dead. It is filled with moving skeletons. ^8-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-70]]

Even things that are impure cost a price to get. Working for that goal just makes you tired. It also brings harm in hell and other places. ^8-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-71]]

A child cannot gather wealth. When they grow up, what happiness is there in this? If you spend your whole life gathering wealth, you will get old. What can you do then with your desire for pleasure? ^8-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-72]]

An unhappy person full of desire works all day and gets completely tired out. They come home, and their tired bodies sleep like dead bodies. ^8-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-73]]

Some people travel to other places and experience the suffering of going far away. They long to see their wives and children. But they do not see them for years. ^8-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-74]]

Some people are completely confused. They want to help themselves, so they even sell themselves for a goal. But they do not get what they want. They are just blown around by the wind of other people's meaningless work. ^8-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-75]]

Some people sell their own bodies. They have no power and must work for others. When their wives have babies, it happens just anywhere. It might be at the foot of a tree or out in the wild. ^8-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-76]]

Fools are tricked by desire for pleasure. They want to survive and think, "I will make a living." They go into battle even though they fear losing their lives. They become servants just to make a profit. ^8-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-77]]

Sometimes a person full of desire gets their body cut apart. Some are stuck on the sharp points of stakes. Short spears pierce others. You can even see some being burned. ^8-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-78]]

Gathering, guarding, and losing wealth brings pain. You should know that wealth causes endless ruin. People get distracted by clinging to wealth. Then they have no chance to be free from the suffering of life. ^8-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-79]]

A person full of desire faces these and many other drawbacks. Their enjoyment is very small. They are just like animals pulling a cart. Those animals only eat a few mouthfuls of grass. ^8-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-80]]

People destroy this free and fortunate life, which is so hard to find. They do this just for a tiny bit of enjoyment. Even animals can easily get that small pleasure. These people are hurt by their own actions. ^8-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-81]]

A desire for pleasure will certainly fade away. It causes a person to fall into hell and other bad places. We go through so much tiring hardship all the time for something that is not even great. ^8-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-82]]

Even a ten-millionth part of that hardship could bring us to the state of a Buddha. A person full of desire goes through greater suffering than the way of acting for awakening. Yet they get no awakening at all. ^8-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-83]]

Think about the suffering in hell caused by a desire for pleasure. Weapons, poison, fire, steep cliffs, and enemies are not nearly as bad. They cannot even be used as examples. ^8-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-84]]

Once you grow tired of desire, you should feel joy for solitude. Go into the peaceful forests. They are completely empty of arguments and troubling emotions. ^8-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-85]]

Lucky people rest in lovely places made of wide stone slabs. They are cooled by moonlight and sandalwood. A quiet, peaceful forest breeze blows on them. They walk around and think about how to help others. ^8-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-86]]

They stay in empty houses, under trees, and in caves. They stay wherever they want for as long as they like. They leave behind the suffering of owning and guarding things. They live a carefree life without depending on anything. ^8-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-87]]

They act on their own without clinging and are not tied to anyone at all. The joy of being happy with enough is hard even for Indra to find. ^8-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-88]]

By thinking about these things, I see the good qualities of solitude. When my busy thinking is completely calm, I should meditate on the awakening mind. ^8-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-89]]

First, I should try hard to meditate on seeing self and others as equal. Since everyone is equal in happiness and suffering, I should protect everyone just like myself. ^8-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-90]]

A body has many parts, like hands, but it is protected as one whole body. In the same way, there are many different beings with their own happiness and suffering. But they are all one in wanting happiness just like me. ^8-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-91]]

My suffering does not hurt the bodies of others. Even so, that suffering is mine. It is unbearable because of holding on tightly to a self. ^8-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-92]]

In the same way, the suffering of others does not fall on me. Even so, that suffering is mine. It is hard to bear because of holding on tightly to a self. ^8-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-93]]

I should take away the suffering of others, because it is suffering, just like my own suffering. I should help others because they are living beings, just like my own body. ^8-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-94]]

Other people want happiness just as much as I do. We are exactly the same. So why do I only work for my own happiness? ^8-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-95]]

Other people hate suffering just as much as I do. We are exactly the same. So why do I protect myself and not others? ^8-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-96]]

If I do not protect others because their suffering does not hurt me, why do I protect myself from future suffering? That future suffering does not hurt me right now. ^8-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-97]]

Thinking that I will experience it is false. The person who dies is someone else, and the person who is born is also someone else. ^8-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-98]]

If everyone only has to protect themselves from their own suffering, think about the foot. The suffering of the foot is not the hand's suffering. So why does the hand protect the foot? ^8-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-99]]

You might say, "Even if it makes no sense, we act by holding on to a self." But anything that makes no sense about yourself and others should be given up as best as you can. ^8-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-100]]

A continuing stream and a collection of parts are not real in themselves. They are like a necklace or an army. Since no one truly owns suffering, who is there to control it? ^8-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-101]]

Suffering has no owner. Because of this, everyone is exactly the same. Because it is suffering, it must be cleared away. What is the point of making rules about who to help? ^8-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-102]]

Someone might ask, "Why should we stop the suffering of everyone?" There is no room to argue. If we stop it, we must stop it for all. If not, then my own suffering should not be stopped either, since I am a living being just like them. ^8-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-103]]

Someone might ask, "Since compassion brings so much suffering, why force yourself to feel it?" But think about the suffering of all beings. Compared to that, how is the suffering of compassion great? ^8-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-104]]

If facing one single suffering removes many other sufferings, a kind person should certainly take on that suffering for themselves and others. ^8-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-105]]

This is why Supushpachandra did not stop his own suffering. He knew the king would hurt him. He did this so the suffering of many would end. ^8-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-106]]

When my continuing stream gets used to this, I will love calming the suffering of others. I will dive right into the Avichi hell to help them. I will dive in like a swan landing in a lotus pond. ^8-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-107]]

When living beings are freed, there is an ocean of joy. Is that joy not enough? What is the use of wanting freedom? ^8-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-108]]

So even when I work for the good of others, I am not proud or amazed. I only find joy in the good of others. I do not hope for a result that ripens for me. ^8-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-109]]

I protect myself from even the smallest mean word. I should protect others in the exact same way. I must grow a mind of compassion like this. ^8-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-110]]

By getting used to it, I take the drops of seed and blood from others as "I". Even though they are without a thing, I still call them "I". ^8-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-111]]

In the same way, why don't I see other people's bodies as "me"? Thinking of my own body as "other" is not hard to do. ^8-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-112]]

I should know that I have faults and that others are oceans of good qualities. I must meditate on completely letting go of holding on to a self, and taking up others instead. ^8-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-113]]

We accept that hands and other parts belong to the body. In the same way, why don't we accept that beings with a body are parts of all beings? ^8-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-114]]

By getting used to it, the thinking mind of self has grown toward this body with no separate self. In the same way, why shouldn't the thinking mind of self grow toward other living beings by getting used to it? ^8-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-115]]

If it is like that, doing the good of others will not make me feel amazed or proud. This is just like eating food myself. I do not hope for a reward when I eat. ^8-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-116]]

Therefore, just as I protect myself from even tiny unpleasant things, I should get used to having a protective mind and a mind of compassion toward all beings. ^8-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-117]]

The Protector Avalokiteshvara felt great compassion. He wanted to take away the fear that beings feel in crowds. So, he put a blessing on his own name. ^8-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-118]]

I should not give up when things are hard. The power of getting used to things changes us. You might once be scared to even hear someone's name. But later, you might feel no joy without them. ^8-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-119]]

Do you want to quickly protect yourself and others? Then you should practice the exchange of self and others. This is the greatest secret. ^8-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-120]]

Through clinging to this body of mine, I feel afraid of even small things. Since this body creates so much fear, who would not hate it like an enemy? ^8-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-121]]

People want to cure their body's hunger and thirst. To do this, they kill birds, fish, and wild animals. They even wait in hiding along the roads. ^8-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-122]]

Some want money and respect so much that they even kill their own parents. They steal wealth from the Three Jewels. Because of this, they will burn in the Avichi hell. ^8-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-123]]

What wise person would want, protect, and honor that body? Who would not see it as an enemy and dislike it? ^8-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-124]]

"If I give this away, what will I use?" Thinking of my own good is the way of a demon. "If I use this, what will I give away?" Thinking of the good of others is the way of a god. ^8-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-125]]

If I harm others for my own sake, I will suffer in hell. If I harm myself for the sake of others, I will gain all excellence. ^8-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-126]]

If I wish to be great myself, I will be reborn in an unhappy realm as someone low and foolish. If I shift that wish to others, I will gain a happy realm and respect. ^8-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-127]]

If I use others for my own good, I will become a servant. If I use myself for the good of others, I will become a master. ^8-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-128]]

Whatever happiness there is in the world comes from wishing for the happiness of others. Whatever suffering there is in the world comes from wishing for my own happiness. ^8-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-129]]

Why say more? A childish person acts only for their own good. The Sage acts for the good of others. Just look at the difference between them! ^8-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-130]]

If I do not truly exchange my own happiness for the suffering of others, I will never reach the state of a Buddha. Even in the cycle of rebirth, I will find no happiness. ^8-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-131]]

Leave the next life aside for a moment. If a servant does not do their work and a master pays no wages, nothing gets done. They will not even reach their goals in this life. ^8-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-132]]

Out of confusion, I cause suffering to others. This brings me unbearable suffering, and I throw away the perfect joy that brings happiness I can see and cannot see. ^8-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-133]]

Think of all the harm, fear, and suffering in the world. If all of it comes from holding on to a self, what use is that great demon to me? ^8-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-134]]

If I do not completely let go of the self, I cannot leave suffering behind. It is just like fire. If I do not let go of the fire, I cannot stop being burned. ^8-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-135]]

So, I want to stop harm to myself. I want to stop the suffering of others. To do this, I should give myself to others. I should treat others as myself. ^8-135

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-136]]

My mind, you must know this for sure. I am under the power of others. Now you must think only about what helps every living being. Do not think of anything else. ^8-136

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-137]]

My eyes and other parts belong to others. It is wrong to use them for my own good. They are for the sake of others. It is wrong to use them to harm others. ^8-137

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-138]]

Therefore, put every living being first. Look at whatever is on my body. Take it away, and use it to help others. ^8-138

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-139]]

See a lower person and others as yourself. Then see yourself as them. Clear your mind of busy thinking. Practice feeling jealousy, rivalry, and pride. ^8-139

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-140]]

People honor that person, but not me. I did not get what they got. That person is praised, but people say bad things about me. They are happy, but I am in suffering. ^8-140

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-141]]

I do all the hard work, while this person just stays in happiness. This person is great in the world, but people say I am low and without good qualities. ^8-141

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-142]]

What is the use of having no good qualities? Everyone has good qualities. There are people who are better than this person. There are also people who I am better than. ^8-142

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-143]]

Losing my good conduct, my belief, and other things is caused by strong troubling emotions. It is out of my control. I must be healed as much as possible, and I will even accept harm. ^8-143

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-144]]

But if this person will not heal me, why does he look down on me? What use are his good qualities to me? He is the one who has those good qualities! ^8-144

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-145]]

Standing in the jaws of cruel unhappy realms, this person has no compassion for beings. He is proud of his good qualities. He even wants to challenge wise persons. ^8-145

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-146]]

When I look at someone equal to me, I want to make myself better than them. I will certainly get my gain and respect, even if I have to argue. ^8-146

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-147]]

I will make sure my good qualities are clear to the whole world. And whatever good qualities this person has, I will make sure no one hears about them. ^8-147

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-148]]

I will also hide my faults. I will be honored, but not this person. Today I will get great things. I will be respected, but not this person. ^8-148

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-149]]

When this person is ruined, I will gladly watch for a long time. I will make all beings laugh at them. I will make everyone make fun of them. ^8-149

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-150]]

People say that even this miserable person is competing with me. But is this person equal to me in learning or wisdom? Are they equal in looks, family, or wealth? ^8-150

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-151]]

Hearing my good qualities announced to everyone like this, I will feel a thrilling joy. I will fully enjoy this happiness. ^8-151

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-152]]

Even if this person gets something, if they do my work, I will give them just enough to live on. I will take what they got using my power. ^8-152

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-153]]

I will make this one lose his happiness. I will harm him all the time. He has hurt me for hundreds of lifetimes in the cycle of rebirth. ^8-153

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-154]]

O mind, you only wanted your own good. Countless great ages have passed. You worked until you were very tired, but you only got suffering. ^8-154

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-155]]

You must truly work hard to help others. The words of the Sage are never wrong. Later you will see the good qualities of doing this. ^8-155

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-156]]

If you had helped others in the past, you would not be stuck like this. You would surely have the perfect happiness of a Buddha. ^8-156

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-157]]

You are used to taking as "I" the drops of seed and blood from others. In the same way, get used to doing this for everyone else. ^8-157

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-158]]

Act like a spy for other people. Watch whatever appears on my body. Snatch those things away, and practice what is good for others. ^8-158

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-159]]

"I am happy and others are unhappy. I am high and others are low. I do good things and others do not." Why do you not feel jealousy toward yourself? ^8-159

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-160]]

Give up your own happiness. Take the suffering of others onto yourself. Always ask, "What is this self doing right now?" Look closely at your own faults. ^8-160

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-161]]

If others make a mistake, treat it as your own fault. But if you make even a small mistake, confess it to many people. ^8-161

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-162]]

Praise other people so much that you hide your own fame. Act like the lowest servant. Put yourself to work for everyone else. ^8-162

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-163]]

This self is full of faults. Do not praise it just for having a tiny, temporary good quality. Make sure that no one at all knows about your good qualities. ^8-163

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-164]]

In short, whatever harm you have done to others for your own sake, let that same harm fall upon yourself, for the sake of all living beings. ^8-164

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-165]]

Do not let it grow strong, or it will become bossy. Keep it held back with shame and fear, just like a new bride. ^8-165

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-166]]

Tell it, 'Do this! Stay like that! Do not act that way!' Bring it under control like this. If it breaks the rules, you must stop it. ^8-166

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-167]]

Even when I tell you what to do, if you, my mind, do not act that way, I must defeat you. All faults rely on you, so I have no other choice. ^8-167

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-168]]

That past time when you ruined me is over. Now I see you. Where will you go? I will destroy all of your feeling superior. ^8-168

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-169]]

Throw away the thought, 'I still have my own good!' I have sold you to others. Do not be sad. Just give them your help. ^8-169

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-170]]

If I fall into carelessness and do not give you to living beings, you will surely hand me over to the guards of hell. ^8-170

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-171]]

In that way, you have handed me over again and again. I have suffered for a long time. I remember those grudges. Now I will destroy your thoughts about your own good. ^8-171

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-172]]

If I wish to be happy, I should not delight in myself. If I wish to protect myself, I should always protect others. ^8-172

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-173]]

The more this body is completely cared for, the more fragile it becomes. ^8-173

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-174]]

Once it becomes weak like that, even the whole earth cannot fulfill its desires. Who else could give it what it wants? ^8-174

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-175]]

Wanting impossible things brings troubling emotions and ruins good intentions. But when someone does not depend on anything, their excellence will never run out. ^8-175

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-176]]

Therefore, I should not let the desires of the body grow. Not grabbing onto attractive things is an excellent thing. ^8-176

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-177]]

In the end, it will just turn to ashes. It cannot move unless something else moves it. This form is dirty and hard to bear. Why do I keep grabbing hold of a self in this? ^8-177

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-178]]

Whether it is alive or dead, what use is this machine to me? If it is no different from a lump of dirt, oh, why do I not get rid of my pride? ^8-178

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-179]]

By taking care of the body, I have only built up useless suffering. What is the point of clinging and anger toward this thing that is just like wood? ^8-179

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-180]]

It does not matter if I take good care of it, or if vultures eat it. Since it has no clinging and no hatred, why do I feel clinging toward it? ^8-180

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-181]]

If the thing that gets angry at blame and happy at praise actually knows nothing at all, then who have I worked so hard for? ^8-181

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-182]]

If I say that anyone who desires this body is my friend, then why do I not delight in everyone? After all, everyone desires their own body. ^8-182

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-183]]

So, without being attached, I will give up my body to help others. It has many faults. Still, I will keep it like a tool for getting things done. ^8-183

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-184]]

I have had enough of acting like a child! I will follow the wise. I will remember the teachings on carefulness. I will chase away sleep and dullness of mind. ^8-184

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-185]]

To clear away the veil, I will turn my mind away from wrong paths. I will always rest by settling the mind on the perfect thing to focus on. ^8-185

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^8-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Awakening Hero, the eighth chapter, called Calm Focus. ^8-a

## Chapter Nine: The Perfection of Wisdom ^9-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-1]]

The Sage taught all these branches for the sake of wisdom. So, anyone who wants to stop suffering should grow wisdom. ^9-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-2]]

People believe in two truths. They are everyday truth and deepest truth. Deepest truth is beyond the reach of the thinking mind. The thinking mind is just everyday truth. ^9-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-3]]

We see two kinds of worlds. There is the world of the meditation master and the world of ordinary people. The world of the meditation master proves the ordinary world wrong. ^9-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-4]]

Meditation masters are not all equal in their thinking minds. A higher master proves a lower one wrong. They use examples they both agree on. They do not question these examples because they just want a result. ^9-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-5]]

A person of the world sees a thing and believes it is what is truly real. They do not see that it is like an illusion. Because of this, the meditation master argues with the world. ^9-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-6]]

Seeing directly a shape and other things is just common usage. It is not a reliable way of knowing. It is false, just like the common usage that calls filth by the name of cleanness. ^9-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-7]]

To guide the world, the Protector taught about a thing. But in reality, things are not lasting only a moment. Someone might say, "This goes against even everyday truth!" ^9-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-8]]

There is no mistake in the everyday truth of a meditator. Compared to the world, they see the way things really are. Otherwise, when they see women as unclean, the world would prove them wrong. ^9-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-9]]

Someone might ask, "How do we get a store of goodness from a Victorious One who is like an illusion?" It is just like getting it from someone who is really existing. They might also ask, "If living beings are like illusions, how are they born again after they die?" ^9-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-10]]

As long as the circumstances come together, an illusion will appear for that long. Just because they have a long unbroken flow, how can a living being be truly existing? ^9-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-11]]

Killing a person made by magic is not a harmful deed, because they have no mind. But when acting toward someone who has the mind of an illusion, a store of goodness and a harmful deed can happen. ^9-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-12]]

Because things like sacred words do not have the power, the mind of an illusion does not appear. Because it comes from many different circumstances, that illusion itself comes in many different kinds. ^9-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-13]]

There is no single circumstance anywhere that has the power to do everything. Someone might say, "If the deepest truth is freedom from sorrow, and the cycle of rebirth is everyday truth, then..." ^9-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-14]]

Someone might say, "Since even a Buddha would keep going around in the cycle of rebirth, what is the use of the way of acting for awakening?" I reply: As long as the unbroken flow of circumstances is not cut, even an illusion will not stop. ^9-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-15]]

But when the unbroken flow of circumstances is cut, it does not happen even in everyday truth. Someone might ask, "When even the mistake does not exist, what will see the illusion?" ^9-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-16]]

I reply, "When you say the illusion itself does not exist, then what is seen?" Someone might answer, "If something else exists in the way things really are, that way it appears is the mind itself." ^9-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-17]]

If the mind itself is an illusion, then what is seen by what? Even the Protector of the world taught that the mind cannot see the mind. ^9-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-18]]

Just as a sword blade cannot cut itself, the mind cannot see itself. Someone might say, "But a lamp lights itself up perfectly!" ^9-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-19]]

A lamp does not need to be lit up, because darkness does not hide it. Someone might argue, "Unlike a clear crystal, a blue thing does not depend on anything else for its blueness." ^9-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-20]]

Someone might say, "We see that some things depend on other things, and some do not." But something that is not blue cannot make itself blue. ^9-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-21]]

When awareness knows a lamp, we can say, "The lamp is shining." But what knows the thinking mind so we can say, "The thinking mind itself is shining"? ^9-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-22]]

If no one sees it at all, it means nothing to say whether it is shining or not. It is like talking about the beautiful pose of the daughter of a woman with no children. ^9-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-23]]

Someone might ask, "If there is no awareness of itself, how can we have a memory of awareness?" A memory happens because it is connected to experiencing something else. It is like the poison of a rat. ^9-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-24]]

Someone might say, "Because the mind uses other circumstances to see, it shines to itself." Using magic eye ointment lets you see a vase. But it does not let you see the eye ointment itself. ^9-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-25]]

I am not denying the things you see, hear, and know. I only want to stop you from thinking things are truly real. That is the cause of suffering. ^9-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-26]]

Someone might say an illusion is not different from the mind. But they also say it is not exactly the same. If it is a real thing, how is it not different? If it is not different, it does not exist as a thing. ^9-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-27]]

Someone might say an illusion is not true, but we still see it. They say the mind is the one who sees. They might argue the cycle of rebirth must be based on a real thing. Otherwise, it would just be like empty space. ^9-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-28]]

If a non-existent thing depends on a thing, how could it do anything? Your mind would have no companion. It would be completely alone. ^9-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-29]]

When the mind is free of any thing the mind takes in, everyone becomes a Thus-Gone One. Even if this were true, what is the good of it? What is the point of making up the idea of Mind Only? ^9-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-30]]

Someone might ask: Even if you know things are like an illusion, how will that stop troubling emotions? Think of a magician who creates a woman made by illusion. Even the magician can still feel clinging toward her. ^9-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-31]]

That magician has not let go of his deep habits of troubling emotions toward things to know. Because of this, when he sees her, his deep habit of emptiness is very weak. ^9-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-32]]

If you keep getting used to the habit of emptiness, you will let go of the habit left in the mind by things. By getting used to thinking "there is nothing," you will later let go of that deep habit too. ^9-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-33]]

When you say, "This does not exist," and you look closely at the thing but cannot find it, what happens? How can a non-existent thing without a base stay in front of the thinking mind? ^9-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-34]]

When a thing and a non-existent thing do not stay in front of the thinking mind, there is no other way it appears. At that time, by having no object of focus, there is complete calm. ^9-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-35]]

A wish-fulfilling jewel and a wish-granting tree completely grant our hopes. In the same way, the form of the Victorious One appears to the one to be guided through the power of a wishing prayer. ^9-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-36]]

For example, a shrine for the great sky bird might be built. Even a long time after the builder passes away, it still clears away poison and other harms. ^9-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-37]]

In the same way, following the way of acting for awakening, the shrine of the Victorious One has been built. Even after the awakening hero passes into freedom from sorrow, it still achieves every goal. ^9-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-38]]

Someone might ask, "If we make offerings to someone without a mind, how will we get a result?" It is because the teachings say it is exactly the same whether the Buddha stays here or has passed into freedom from sorrow. ^9-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-39]]

Whether in everyday truth or in the way things really are, this makes sense. The sacred text says a result comes from it. It brings a result just like giving to a real Buddha. ^9-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-40]]

Someone might say, "Freedom comes from seeing the truths. Why do we need to see emptiness?" The sacred text says that without this path, there is no awakening. ^9-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-41]]

Someone might ask, "If the Great Vehicle is not proven, how is your sacred text proven?" The answer is, "Because it is proven for both of us." But at first, it was not proven for you. ^9-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-42]]

Whatever circumstance makes you believe in your text is the same for the Great Vehicle. If a text is true just because two groups accept it, then the Vedas and other books would also be true. ^9-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-43]]

Someone might say, "We reject the Great Vehicle because people argue about it." But you argue with a non-Buddhist about your sacred text. You also argue about other sacred texts, both with your own groups and with other groups. Since people argue about them, you would have to throw them all away. ^9-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-44]]

If being a monk is the root of the teaching, even being a monk is hard to prove. For people whose minds are holding on to things, even freedom from sorrow is hard to prove. ^9-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-45]]

If someone is set free just by giving up troubling emotion, they would be free right away. But even without troubling emotion, we still see the power of action in them. ^9-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-46]]

Someone might say it is certain that, for now, the wanting that causes holding on does not exist. Even if that wanting is not troubled by troubling emotion, why would it not exist, just as confusion still exists? ^9-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-47]]

Wanting happens due to the circumstance of feeling. And those people still have feeling. A mind that is fixing on something will remain stuck on something. ^9-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-48]]

A mind that does not have emptiness will start up again even after it stops. It is like a deep meditation with no perceiving. Because of this, you should meditate on emptiness. ^9-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-49]]

If you say that any speech found in a scripture was spoken by the Buddha, do you not agree that most of the Great Vehicle is equal to your teaching texts? ^9-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-50]]

You say that leaving out one text makes them all faulty. But if just one is like a teaching text, why are they not all spoken by the Victorious One? ^9-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-51]]

Even Mahakashyapa and others could not measure the depth of these words. Just because you do not understand them, who would make them unacceptable? ^9-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-52]]

For the sake of those who experience suffering from confusion, a person who is free from the extremes of clinging and fear can stay in the cycle of rebirth. This is the result of emptiness. ^9-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-53]]

You cannot succeed in proving wrong the position of emptiness. Therefore, without any doubt, you should meditate on emptiness. ^9-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-54]]

There is a dark veil of troubling emotions and of things to know. Emptiness is the remedy for this darkness. If you wish to quickly become all-knowing, how could you not meditate on it? ^9-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-55]]

If a thing produces suffering, fear will arise from it. But emptiness calms suffering. How could fear arise toward it? ^9-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-56]]

If any self existed, a person might be afraid of anything. But since no self exists at all, who is there to be afraid? ^9-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-57]]

Teeth, hair, and nails are not the self, and the self is not bones or blood. It is not mucus or phlegm, and it is not lymph or pus. ^9-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-58]]

The self is not fat or sweat, and the lungs and liver are not the self. Other inside organs are also not the self, and the self is not feces or urine. ^9-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-59]]

Flesh and skin are not the self, and warmth and wind are not the self. The openings of the body are not the self. In all ways, the six kinds of awareness are not the self either. ^9-59

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-60]]

If the awareness of sound were lasting forever, it would hear sound all the time. If there is no thing to know, what does it know? Why would it be called an awareness? ^9-60

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-61]]

If something that does not know anything were an awareness, then a piece of wood would be an awareness too. Therefore, it is certain that without a thing to know, there is no awareness. ^9-61

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-62]]

If that exact same thing knows a shape, why does it not hear at the same time? Someone might say it is because a sound is not close by. But if that is true, then its awareness of the sound is gone too. ^9-62

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-63]]

If something has the nature of noticing a sound, how could it become the thing that notices a shape? One person can be labelled as both a father and a son. But that is not what is truly real. ^9-63

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-64]]

So, brightness, restlessness, and darkness are neither a son nor a father. And the thing that notices a shape is not seen to have a nature that notices a sound. ^9-64

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-65]]

If it is like an actor who shows up in a new way, then we see it changing, so it is not lasting forever. If that new way is the exact same thing, then this kind of oneness has never been seen before. ^9-65

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-66]]

If that new part is not real, then tell me its own nature. If you say it is just awareness itself, then there is a problem. In that case, it would mean all people are exactly one person. ^9-66

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-67]]

Things that can think and things that cannot think would also become one. This is because they both exist in the same way. When the differences between things are false, what makes them similar? ^9-67

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-68]]

A thing without a mind is not the self, because it has no mind, just like a vase. But you might say it knows things because it gets a mind. Then the thing that did not know anything before would be destroyed. ^9-68

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-69]]

If the self never changes, what does the mind actually do to it? An unchanging self cannot know or do anything. That would make space into the self. ^9-69

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-70]]

Someone might say: If the self does not exist, the link between action and result makes no sense. A person disappears after doing an action. So whose action will it be? ^9-70

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-71]]

We both agree that the action and the result happen to different things. We also agree that the self does nothing here. So is this argument not pointless? ^9-71

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-72]]

It is impossible to see the exact same person holding the cause and the result. We talk about a doer and one who experiences only because they are part of a single continuing stream. ^9-72

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-73]]

A mind in the past or future is not the self, because it does not exist. But if the mind happening right now is the self, then when that mind disappears, there is no self either. ^9-73

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-74]]

Think of a banana tree trunk. If you pull it apart piece by piece, nothing is left. It is the same when you look closely for a self. A real self cannot be found. ^9-74

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-75]]

Someone might ask, "If there are no living beings, who should we show compassion to?" We show compassion to those imagined by our confusion. We accept them for the sake of the result. ^9-75

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-76]]

Someone might ask, "If there are no living beings, who gets the result?" That is true, but we only say someone gets it because of our confusion. Still, to calm suffering, we should not get rid of this confusion about the result. ^9-76

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-77]]

Pride causes suffering. This pride grows from the confusion of a self. Someone might say we cannot get rid of this confusion either. But meditating on no separate self is the best way to stop it. ^9-77

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-78]]

The body is not the feet or the lower legs. The thighs and the waist are not the body either. The belly and the back are not the body. The chest and the arms are not the body. ^9-78

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-79]]

The ribs and the hands are not the body. The armpits and the shoulders are not the body. The organs inside are not the body either. If the head and the neck are not the body, then what exactly is the body? ^9-79

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-80]]

If this body existed in all of these pieces through its different parts, the parts would just be in the parts. But where would the body itself be? ^9-80

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-81]]

If the whole body existed in the hands and other parts, then however many hands and parts there are, there would be that many bodies. ^9-81

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-82]]

If the body does not exist outside or inside, how does the body exist in the hands and other parts? If it is no different from the hands and other parts, how does it exist? ^9-82

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-83]]

Therefore, there is no body. Because of confusion, the idea of a body comes up when looking at the hands and other parts. This happens because of their shape. It is just like mistaking a tree stump for a person. ^9-83

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-84]]

As long as the gathering of circumstances lasts, the body appears like a person. In the same way, as long as those circumstances are present in the hands and other parts, the body appears to be there. ^9-84

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-85]]

In the same way, what is the hand? It is just a collection of fingers. The finger is also a collection of joints. And a joint can be divided into its own parts. ^9-85

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-86]]

Even a part is divided into tiny particles. That tiny particle is split into a part in one direction and another. But that split direction has no parts at all. It is just like space. So, even tiny particles do not really exist. ^9-86

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-87]]

So, what wise person would hold onto a shape? A shape is just like a dream. When the body does not really exist like this, who is a man? And who is a woman? ^9-87

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-88]]

If suffering is real, why does it not hurt people who are very joyful? If happiness is real, why do delicious foods not bring joy to people who are very sad? ^9-88

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-89]]

Someone might say you do not have that experience because a stronger force covers it up. But if something is not an experience, how can it be a feeling? ^9-89

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-90]]

Someone might say, "Suffering still exists in a faint form." Has its rough state not been removed? If you say, "It is just a different joy," then that faint form also belongs to that joy. ^9-90

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-91]]

If suffering does not happen because an opposing circumstance comes up, does it not prove something? It shows that what we call a feeling is really just holding on to an idea. ^9-91

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-92]]

For this very reason, we should use meditation on this careful thinking as a remedy. The calm focus that comes from looking closely is the food of the meditation master. ^9-92

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-93]]

If there is a gap between the sense organ and the object, where would they meet? Even if there is no gap, they are just one thing. What would meet with what? ^9-93

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-94]]

An atom does not enter another atom. It has no space inside, and they are equal. Without entering, there is no mixing. And without mixing, there is no meeting. ^9-94

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-95]]

How could it make sense to say that something without parts can meet? If you have ever seen meeting and being without parts happen together, show it to me! ^9-95

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-96]]

It does not make sense for awareness to meet anything, because it has no body. A gathering is also not a real thing, just as we saw before. ^9-96

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-97]]

So, if touch does not exist, where would feeling come from? What is the point of all this hard work? What could possibly harm what? ^9-97

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-98]]

There is no one who feels at all. Feeling does not really exist either. When we see this, why does our wanting not go away? ^9-98

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-99]]

Seeing and touching are just like a dream or an illusion. Feeling happens at the exact same time as the mind. So, the mind cannot see that feeling. ^9-99

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-100]]

A mind that comes before or after can only remember it, but cannot experience it. It cannot experience itself. Nothing else can experience it either. ^9-100

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-101]]

There is no one who feels at all. Because of this, feeling does not really exist. So how could this harm a collection of parts with no separate self? ^9-101

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-102]]

The mind does not live in a sense organ. It is not in a shape or anything like that, nor anywhere in between. The mind is not inside, and it is not outside. It cannot be found anywhere else either. ^9-102

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-103]]

It is not the body, and it is not anywhere else. It is not mixed with anything, and it is not separate. It is nothing at all. Because of this, every living being is in freedom from sorrow by nature. ^9-103

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-104]]

If awareness exists before the thing to know, what is it looking at? If awareness and the thing to know happen at the same time, what is it looking at? ^9-104

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-105]]

But if awareness comes after the thing to know, what causes it? Because of this, we cannot see the coming into being of any things that appear. ^9-105

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-106]]

Someone might say, "If everyday truth does not exist, how could there be the two truths? And if it relies on another everyday truth, how could living beings reach nirvana?" ^9-106

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-107]]

This is just the busy thinking of someone else's mind. It is not my own everyday truth. If it is figured out later, it exists. If not, everyday truth simply does not exist. ^9-107

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-108]]

Imagining and the thing imagined depend on each other. Every careful examination is spoken by relying on what people already know. ^9-108

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-109]]

Someone might say, "If you test things with careful thinking that must also be tested, then that careful thinking has no end." ^9-109

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-110]]

When you look closely at the thing being examined, there is no support left for careful thinking. Because there is no support, careful thinking does not happen. That is what we call freedom from sorrow. ^9-110

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-111]]

Some people say these two are true. That idea is very hard to defend. If an object exists through the power of awareness, what support is there for awareness itself? ^9-111

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-112]]

But if awareness exists because of the thing to know, what support is there for the thing to know? If they exist because of each other, neither of them would exist. ^9-112

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-113]]

If there is no father without a son, where does the son come from? Without a son, there is no father. In the same way, those two do not exist. ^9-113

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-114]]

Someone might say, "A sprout grows from a seed. That very sprout lets us know the seed exists. Awareness comes from the thing to know. So why doesn't that awareness let us know the thing to know exists?" ^9-114

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-115]]

If an awareness different from the sprout knows the seed exists, then an awareness is what knows the thing to know. But what lets us know that awareness itself exists? ^9-115

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-116]]

Right now, through everyday direct seeing, we can see all causes. Different things, like the parts of a lotus plant, are made by different causes. ^9-116

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-117]]

If you ask what made those different causes, they come from the different causes that happened before them. Why is a cause able to make a result? It gets its power from the cause that came before it. ^9-117

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-118]]

Someone might say a Creator is the cause of every being. First, tell me what this Creator is! If you say the Creator is just the elements, fine. But why tire yourself out arguing over a simple name? ^9-118

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-119]]

But things like earth are many. They are not lasting, they do nothing on their own, and they are not holy. Since they are things we walk on and they are unclean, they are not the Creator. ^9-119

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-120]]

The Creator is not space, because space does nothing. He is not the self, because I already proved that wrong. And if you say the maker of the world is beyond thought, what is the point of talking about something beyond thought? ^9-120

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-121]]

Also, what do you claim he makes? Don't you say that the self, earth, and the true nature of the Creator are lasting forever? Awareness comes from a thing to know, and... ^9-121

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-122]]

Happiness and suffering are without a beginning. They come from our actions. Tell me, what was made by him? If the cause is without a beginning, how can there be a beginning to the result? ^9-122

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-123]]

Why does he not create things all the time? He does not depend on anyone else. If there is nothing else that he did not make, what would he depend on? ^9-123

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-124]]

If he depends on things, then the gathering itself would be the cause. It would not be the Creator. When there is a gathering, there is no power to stop it from producing. Without it, there is no power to produce. ^9-124

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-125]]

If the Creator acts without wanting to, he is controlled by another. Even if he wants to act, he depends on his own desire. If he acts like this, how is he the Creator? ^9-125

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-126]]

People who believe in never-changing atoms have already been proven wrong. The Samkhya thinkers say that a never-changing Primal Matter is the cause of beings. ^9-126

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-127]]

They say there are good qualities known as brightness, restlessness, and darkness. Their perfect balance is called Primal Matter. When they are out of balance, they are said to be beings. ^9-127

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-128]]

It does not make sense for one thing to have three natures. So, it does not exist. In the same way, the good qualities do not exist. That is because they each have three parts too. ^9-128

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-129]]

If the good qualities do not exist, things like sound cannot exist either. A piece of clothing is a thing without a mind. So, it is impossible for happiness and other feelings to exist in it. ^9-129

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-130]]

If things have the nature of their cause, haven't we already looked closely at this? You say your cause is just happiness and other feelings. But cloth and other things do not come from happiness. ^9-130

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-131]]

Happiness and other feelings would come from things like cloth. Since those things do not exist, happiness does not exist either. Also, we never see happiness as something never-changing. ^9-131

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-132]]

If happiness is showing itself, why do you not notice the feeling? If that same thing becomes fine, how can it be both rough and fine? ^9-132

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-133]]

Because it stops being rough and becomes fine, both the fine and the rough are not lasting. In the same way, why do you not agree that all things are not lasting? ^9-133

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-134]]

If the rough thing is the same as happiness, then clear happiness is not lasting. Someone might say that a non-existent thing cannot arise because it is non-existent. ^9-134

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-135]]

But a clear thing still arises even if it was non-existent before. This is true even if you disagree. If the result already lives inside the cause, a person eating food would be eating filth. ^9-135

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-136]]

Use the money for a cotton cloth to buy cotton seeds and wear them! The world might not see this because of confusion. But people who know the way things really are say it is true. ^9-136

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-137]]

The world also has this knowledge. Since it exists, why is it not seen? If the world is not a reliable way of knowing, then seeing a clear thing is also false. ^9-137

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-138]]

Someone might ask: If a reliable way of knowing is actually wrong, is what it measures false? If so, meditation on emptiness would not make sense. ^9-138

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-139]]

Without touching a made-up thing, we cannot notice its non-existent thing. Therefore, if an object is false, its non-existent thing is also clearly false. ^9-139

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-140]]

Think of a son dying in a dream. The busy thinking that he does not exist blocks the idea that he exists. But that is also false. ^9-140

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-141]]

By looking closely, nothing at all exists without a cause. It also does not stay inside circumstances. This is true for one circumstance or many together. ^9-141

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-142]]

It does not come from somewhere else. It does not stay, and it does not go away. Confusion makes it seem true. How is it different from an illusion? ^9-142

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-143]]

Think about anything made by an illusion. Think about anything made by causes. Where did it come from? Where does it go? Look closely at this! ^9-143

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-144]]

Whatever is seen only because something else is near, and is unseen when that is gone, is made up. It is like a reflection. How could being truly real exist in that? ^9-144

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-145]]

If a thing already exists, why does it need a cause? But even if it does not exist, why does it need a cause? ^9-145

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-146]]

Even hundreds of millions of causes cannot change a non-existent thing. How could that state be a thing? And what else could become a thing? ^9-146

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-147]]

If a thing cannot exist when it does not exist, when will it ever become a thing? Until a thing is made, it cannot separate from being a non-existent thing. ^9-147

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-148]]

If it does not separate from being a non-existent thing, there is no chance for a thing to exist. A thing also cannot become non-existent. If it did, it would have two natures. ^9-148

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-149]]

So, there is no stopping, and there is no thing. Therefore, all these beings are always never born and never ending. ^9-149

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-150]]

Beings are like a dream. When we look closely, they are like a banana tree. In the way things really are, there is no difference between finding freedom from sorrow and not finding it. ^9-150

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-151]]

Since things are empty, what is there to gain and what is there to lose? What does it matter if someone honors you or treats you badly? ^9-151

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-152]]

Where do happiness and suffering come from? What is there to be upset or happy about? If I look for the way things really are, who is wanting? And what are they wanting? ^9-152

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-153]]

If I look closely at this world of living beings, who will die here? What will happen, and what has happened? Who is a relative, and who is a friend? ^9-153

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-154]]

May people like me completely understand that everything is like space. People who want happiness for themselves are stirred up by causes of fighting and excitement. ^9-154

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-155]]

They get very upset and very happy. With sadness, hard work, and arguing, they cut and pierce each other. Through harmful deeds, they live a very hard life. ^9-155

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-156]]

They come to happy realms again and again, enjoying a lot of happiness. But after they die, they fall into the sufferings of the unhappy realms. These sufferings are terrible and last a very long time. ^9-156

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-157]]

In life, there are many steep cliffs. There, what is not the way things really are is like this. Because things clash with each other there, in life there is no such thing as the way things really are. ^9-157

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-158]]

In that place, the ocean of suffering has nothing to compare with it. It is too hard to bear and has no edge. There, people have very little strength, and their lives are short. ^9-158

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-159]]

Even there, we spend our time just trying to stay alive and healthy. We get tired from hunger, sleep, and being hurt. We also waste time with foolish persons. ^9-159

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-160]]

Life passes by fast and has no meaning. Careful thinking is very hard to find. Since we are so used to distraction there, how can we ever stop it? ^9-160

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-161]]

Even there, Mara works hard to make us fall into the great unhappy realm. There are many wrong paths, and it is hard to overcome even doubt. ^9-161

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-162]]

Once again, the freedom to practice is hard to find. It is very hard to find a time when a Buddha appears. The river of troubling emotions is hard to leave behind. Sadly, suffering will just keep going on and on! ^9-162

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-163]]

Even though they suffer so much, some beings do not see their own pain. We should feel very sad for those who live in the river of suffering! ^9-163

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-164]]

For example, some people wash themselves again and again, but then walk right back into a fire. In the same way, some people live in terrible pain but still think they are happy. ^9-164

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-165]]

Some people act as if there is no growing old and dying. First they will be killed. Then they will take a terrible fall into an unhappy realm. ^9-165

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-166]]

When will I use a rain of my own happiness to bring peace to those burning in the fire of suffering? This rain will fall from clouds made of my store of goodness. ^9-166

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-167]]

When will I respectfully build up my gathered store of goodness while not fixing on anything? And when will I teach emptiness to those who are ruined by fixing on something? ^9-167

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^9-a]]

From Entering the Way of the Awakening Hero, this is the ninth chapter, called the Perfection of Wisdom. ^9-a

## Chapter Ten: Sharing the Goodness ^10-0

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-1]]

I have written this book about the way of acting for awakening. By this good deed, may every being begin the way of acting for awakening. ^10-1

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-2]]

There are beings everywhere sick with suffering in their bodies and minds. By my store of goodness, may they find an ocean of happiness and joy. ^10-2

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-3]]

As long as they remain in the cycle of rebirth, may they never lose their happiness. May every being reach the unbroken happiness of an awakening hero. ^10-3

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-4]]

There are many hells in the world realms. May every being with a body there rejoice with the happiness of Sukhavati. ^10-4

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-5]]

May those hurt by cold find warmth. Endless rivers flow from the great clouds of the awakening heroes. May those rivers cool everyone hurt by heat. ^10-5

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-6]]

May the forest of sword leaves become a thick sandalwood forest for them. May the trunks of the Shalmali trees also grow into wish-granting trees. ^10-6

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-7]]

May sweet songs of water birds like swans and geese make these places beautiful. May the regions of hell become wonderful. May they have lakes filled with sweet-smelling lotus flowers. ^10-7

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-8]]

May the heaps of hot coals become heaps of jewels. May the burned ground become a smooth floor of crystal. May the mountains of the Crushing Hell become heavenly palaces of offerings. May they be filled with Well-Gone Ones. ^10-8

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-9]]

May the rain of hot coals, burning stones, and weapons become a rain of flowers. Instead of striking each other with weapons, may they now toss flowers at each other in play. ^10-9

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-10]]

Some beings are trapped in a fiery river that cannot be crossed. Their flesh is gone and their bones are the color of kunda flowers. By the power of my good deeds, may they get the bodies of gods. May they live with goddesses in a gently flowing river. ^10-10

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-11]]

"Why are the men of Yama and the terrible crows and vultures so afraid? Whose great power chases away the dark and brings joy?" Thinking this, they look up and see blazing Vajrapani in the sky. May great joy free them from their harmful deeds. May they go with him. ^10-11

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-12]]

A rain of flowers and sweet-smelling water falls. The fires and hot coals of hell go out with a hiss. Wondering, "Where does this sudden happiness come from?" may the hell beings look up and see Padmapani. ^10-12

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-13]]

"Friends, throw away your fear and come quickly to us! His power frees us from all suffering and brings us joy. He protects all beings. He has the awakening mind and compassion. Who is this glowing boy with a topknot coming to make us fearless?" ^10-13

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-14]]

"Look at him! The crowns of a hundred gods bow to his feet. His eyes are wet with compassion, and a rain of flowers falls on his head. He has beautiful palaces and thousands of goddesses singing his praise!" When they see Manjushri like this, may the hell beings shout with joy. ^10-14

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-15]]

Through my roots of good deeds, may the living beings of hell see Samantabhadra and other awakening heroes. These heroes bring happy clouds that nothing blocks, and let a cool, sweet-smelling rain fall. When the living beings see this, may they be filled with joy. ^10-15

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-16]]

May animals be free from the fear of eating each other. Just like the people of Uttarakuru, may the hungry ghosts be happy. ^10-16

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-17]]

A stream of milk flows from the hands of the noble Avalokiteshvara. May this milk satisfy the hungry ghosts. After bathing in it, may they always stay cool. ^10-17

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-18]]

May the blind see with their eyes. May the deaf always hear sounds. Just like Mayadevi, may pregnant women give birth safely without any harm. ^10-18

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-19]]

May everyone without clothes find something to wear. May the hungry find food. May the thirsty find water and delicious drinks. ^10-19

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-20]]

May poor people find wealth. May very sad people find joy. May those who feel hopeless get their spirit back and become perfectly steady and firm. ^10-20

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-21]]

May all sick living beings get well quickly. May the sicknesses of all beings go away and never come back. ^10-21

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-22]]

May terrified people become brave. May trapped people be set free. May weak people become strong. May everyone be kind and gentle to each other. ^10-22

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-23]]

May all travelers be safe everywhere they go. Whatever they are traveling for, may they succeed without any trouble. ^10-23

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-24]]

May people on boats and big ships succeed in what they plan to do. May they reach the shore safely and celebrate with their families. ^10-24

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-25]]

May those who are lost on lonely paths meet fellow travelers. May they walk easily and not get tired. May they have no fear of robbers or tigers. ^10-25

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-26]]

May the gods protect children, old people, and anyone without help in scary, wild places. May the gods also protect those who are asleep or drunk. ^10-26

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-27]]

May everyone be free from any state without freedom. May they have trust, wisdom, and compassion. May they have perfect food and act well. May they always remember their past lifetimes. ^10-27

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-28]]

May everyone have endless things to enjoy, just like the treasure house of space. May they live freely, without any fighting or harm. ^10-28

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-29]]

May any living beings who lack brightness shine with great brightness. May any persons of hard practice who look plain get beautiful and perfect bodies. ^10-29

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-30]]

May all the women in the world become men. May lowly people reach high status, and may their pride be defeated. ^10-30

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-31]]

Through my store of goodness, may every living being stop doing harmful deeds. May they always do good deeds instead. ^10-31

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-32]]

May they never lose the awakening mind. May they always practice the way of acting for awakening. May the Buddhas hold them close, and may they give up the actions of Mara. ^10-32

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-33]]

May all those living beings live for a very long time. May they always be happy. May the sound of death never even be heard. ^10-33

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-34]]

May every place be filled with beautiful wish-granting trees. May these places be full of Buddhas and children of the Buddhas sharing the sweet teachings. ^10-34

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-35]]

Everywhere, may the ground be free of pebbles. May it be as flat as the palm of a hand. May it be made of soft lapis lazuli. ^10-35

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-36]]

In every circle of followers, may there be many awakening heroes. May their wonderful goodness make the earth beautiful, and may they stay there forever. ^10-36

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-37]]

May all beings with a body hear the sound of the teachings without ever stopping. May they hear it from birds, from trees, from rays of light, and even from the sky. ^10-37

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-38]]

May they always meet the Buddhas and the children of the Buddhas. May they bring endless clouds of offerings to the teacher of beings. ^10-38

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-39]]

May the gods send rain at the right time. May the harvests be plentiful. May kings act according to the teachings. May the world also flourish. ^10-39

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-40]]

May medicines be strong. May the recitation of secret sacred words be successful. May sky-goers, demons, and others like them have minds full of compassion. ^10-40

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-41]]

May no living being suffer. May they never be afraid or looked down upon. May no one ever be unhappy. ^10-41

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-42]]

May monasteries stand strong, beautiful with reading and reciting. May the community of monks and nuns always live in harmony. May the goals of the community of monks and nuns be achieved. ^10-42

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-43]]

May monks who want to train find solitude. May they leave every distraction behind and meditate with ready minds. ^10-43

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-44]]

May nuns have everything they need. May they stop arguing and causing harm. After becoming a monk or nun, may no one ever lose their good conduct. ^10-44

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-45]]

May those who have broken their good conduct get tired of it. May they wipe away all their harmful deeds. After they reach a happy realm, may their strict practice stay strong even there. ^10-45

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-46]]

May wise persons be honored and receive alms. May their continuing stream be completely pure. May they become famous everywhere. ^10-46

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-47]]

May they never feel the suffering of the unhappy realms. Even without hardship, may they have bodies better than the gods. May they quickly become Buddhas. ^10-47

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-48]]

May all living beings make offerings to all the Buddhas again and again. Through the wonderful happiness of the Buddhas, may they always be filled with happiness. ^10-48

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-49]]

May the awakening heroes do what they plan for the good of others. Whatever the Protectors wish for, may living beings receive it. ^10-49

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-50]]

In the same way, may the self-awakened ones and the Hearers also be happy. ^10-50

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-51]]

Because of Manjushri's kindness, may I always remember my past lifetimes. May I remember them until I reach the Ground of Perfect Joy, and may I succeed in becoming a monk or nun. ^10-51

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-52]]

Even with only simple food, may I be strong and full of life. In all my lifetimes, may I find the perfect chance to live in a quiet place. ^10-52

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-53]]

Whenever I want to see him or ask a small question, may I see the Protector Manjushri himself. May nothing ever stand in my way. ^10-53

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-54]]

I want to help every living being across space in the ten directions. Just as Manjushri acts, may my way of acting be exactly like his. ^10-54

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-55]]

As long as space remains, and as long as beings remain, may I also stay. May I stay to clear away the suffering of beings. ^10-55

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-56]]

Whatever the suffering of beings may be, let it all fall on me. Through the community of awakening heroes, may beings enjoy happiness. ^10-56

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-57]]

The Buddha's teaching is the only medicine for the suffering of beings. It is the source of all happiness. May the Buddha's teaching remain for a long time, along with gain and respect. ^10-57

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-58]]

Thanks to Manjushri's kindness, a good thinking mind comes to me. I bow down to Manjushri. Thanks to the good friend's kindness, I have grown. I also bow down to the good friend. ^10-58

![[bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།#^10-a]]

The tenth chapter, called sharing the goodness, from Entering the Way of the Awakening Hero. ^10-a
