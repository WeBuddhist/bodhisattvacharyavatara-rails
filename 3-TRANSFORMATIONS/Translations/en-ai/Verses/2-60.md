## Combined Commentary Summaries

The commentators read this as the first of several reflections on impermanence meant to loosen attachment. Since beginningless time, one has repeatedly experienced bodies and possessions of exactly this kind, all of which have perished, leaving nothing of substance behind.

Gyaltsab notes that one nevertheless still clings fervently to what has no essence, thereby acting against the guru's and the Buddhas' instructions — an act which is here explicitly named as cause for regret. Sazang and Thokme draw the same direct conclusion: since nothing of value remains from past enjoyments, one must now strive to accomplish the guru's and the Victors' instructions instead of clinging to what has already perished.
