## Traditional Interpretation

### BCAC19_KKP — Khenpo Kunpal, *tsig-'grel* word commentary (Tibetan)

Kunpal reads the verse as Śāntideva's threefold humility followed by his sole positive purpose. On the first three pādas: (1) there is no profound or previously unspoken content to be said here beyond what earlier masters — the buddhas, Nāgārjuna, and others — have already expressed; (2) even the poetic craft of superior composition (*sdebs sbyor*) is not Śāntideva's — Vibhūticandra's praise "none comparable to Śāntideva in realisations and intent" (quoted) shows what Śāntideva might have claimed, but he sets it aside; (3) therefore he had no thought of benefiting a broad audience. All three denials are a single act of abandoning pride in word and meaning (*ṭshig dang don la mkhas pa'i nga rgyal*). On the fourth pāda: the purpose is to cultivate (*bsgom*) in one's own mind the bodhicitta and the six perfections — the very content of a bodhisattva's training. Kunpal cites Candragomin: "of the three scholarly activities, composing is the one that does not err even over lifetimes" — hence writing is the superior method. The benefit: dispelling both pride and despondency (*sems nga rgyal* and *sems zhum pa*), the two obstacles to extraordinary virtue. (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md)

### BCAC14_NTS — Ngülchu Thogmé, *'grel pa* commentary (Tibetan)

Ngülchu treats verses 1-2 and 1-3 together. On 1-2: no content unspoken in the sūtras, no superior poetic skill — and for those two reasons no thought of benefiting others through this composition. Sole purpose: cultivating bodhicitta in one's own mind (*rang gi yid la byang chub kyi sems bsgom pa*). He then adds what would become verse 1-3's thought: incidentally, the author's own faith (*dad pa'i shugs*) will grow through the writing; and others on a comparable spiritual path (*skad pa mnyam pa*) who see this text may also benefit. Ngülchu notes that Khenpo Zhenga and Nāmsen read the qualifier "incidentally" (*re zhig*) as pointing toward a secondary other-benefit. The whole humility thus serves: showing the author free from pride, so that faults would not arise and readers could rely on him. (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md)

### BCAC14_SMPLG — Sa-bzang Ma-ti Paṇ-chen, *gzhung don rab gsal snang ba* commentary (Tibetan)

Sabzang splits verse 1-2 into two movements, each introduced by a heading. First movement (pādas 1–3): humility about content — no additional profound meaning beyond what the pure āgamas have already said — and about craft — no poetic mastery (*sdebs sbyor gzhan las mchog*) is his — and therefore no thought of benefiting a large audience. Heading: *"Showing the way of humility"*. He reads this as Śāntideva displaying non-pride in both word (*ṭshig*) and meaning (*don*) (*nga rgyal spangs nas dul par ston*). Second movement (pāda 4): composing to cultivate bodhicitta in one's own mind. Heading: *"(The second:) showing that one's own benefit is primary"* (*rang don gtso bor ston tshul*). The word *bsgroms/bsgom* denotes active, repeated mental cultivation — not mere study. (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md)

### BCAC19_KS — Gzhan-phan Byams-pa'i Go-cha, *mchan-'grel* annotation (Tibetan)

The interlinear annotation glosses each phrase directly on the verse line. "Profound content from scripture (*lung las*) not previously spoken" — the qualifier *lung las* makes explicit that the content is textually grounded, not that it is absolutely unprecedented. "Poetic skill superior to others" is straightforwardly denied. "Not for the benefit of others" follows as consequence of the two denials. "Composed to cultivate bodhicitta in one's own mind and make it habitual" (*rang gi yid la byang chub kyi sems bsgroms nas goms par bya ba*) is the sole positive purpose. The annotation closes: "what is shown as the past cause is matters settled in one's own mind" (*nang gi dngos po rdzogs par thugs la bzhag pa*). (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md)

### Divergences

No doctrinal divergences. The commentaries differ only in scope: Ngülchu and KS fold verse 1-2 into a single movement with verse 1-3; Sabzang divides 1-2 itself into two named sections distinguishing the humility (pādas 1–3) from the positive purpose (pāda 4). Kunpal reads the three denials as one unified act of pride-abandonment.

འདིར་ལུང་རྣམ་དག་གི་ཆེད་མ་གཏོགས་སྔར་མ་གསུངས་པའི་དོན་ཟབ་མོ་གཞན་འདིར་བརྗོད་ཀྱི་མེད་ (= ལུང་རྣམས་ལས་ལྷག་པ་མིན་) ཞིང་། ཚིག་གི་སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་གཞན་ལས་མཆོག་ཏུ་འབྱིན་པའི་མཁས་པ་ (= སྙན་ངག་གི་ནུས་མཐུ་) ཡང་བདག་ལ་ཡོད་མིན་ཏེ། རྒྱུ་མཚན་གཉིས་དེར་བརྟེན་གཞན་མང་པོར་ཕན་པའི་བསམ་པར་གཞུང་འདི་རྩོམ་པར་མ་བྱས་ཀྱི (= གཞན་དོན་གྱི་དམིགས་ཡུལ་མིན་) །རང་གི་རྒྱུད་ལ་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་དང་བྱང་སེམས་ཀྱི་སྦྱོར་བ་གོམས་པར་བྱ་བར་ (= བསྒོམ་ = ཡང་ཡང་རྒྱུད་ལ་གཞུར་བ་) ངས་གཞུང་འདི་བརྩམས་པ་ཡིན་ནོ། །
