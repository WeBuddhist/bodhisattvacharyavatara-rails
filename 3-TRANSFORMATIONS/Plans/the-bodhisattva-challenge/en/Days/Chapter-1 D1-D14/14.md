# Day 14 — Chapter One ends: honoring those who carry bodhicitta

## Opening
	
*Push notification:* Today: the people who answer harm with kindness.

We all know how rare it is to meet someone who answers cruelty with kindness. Today's verse, the last of Chapter One, bows to exactly such people: **those who carry bodhicitta**. It honors them, and takes refuge in them, as true sources of happiness.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

When someone wrongs us, we usually want them gone, or want them to pay for it.

Today's verse says a bodhisattva does the opposite. They bring happiness even to the people who harm them. But that seems to clash with what we read earlier, that harming a bodhisattva leads to terrible suffering.

**Ngulchu Thokme**, a fourteenth-century teacher, explains that **both can be true**. If you harm a bodhisattva, your own anger still brings suffering down on you. But the bodhisattva does not give up on you. Their compassion is so strong that they keep working to bring you happiness in the end.

He tells an old story of a king named Maitribala. Spirits drank his blood, yet instead of anger, he set those very spirits on the path to freedom.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

If someone irritates you today, a rude remark or a cold reply, see if you can **wish them well anyway**. Even a little is enough.
