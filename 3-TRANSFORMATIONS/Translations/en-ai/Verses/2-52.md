## Combined Commentary Summaries

The commentators agree that Vajrapāṇi's power is distinctive among the objects of refuge named here: the very sight of him is said to cause hostile spirits — the messengers of Yama (the Lord of Death), yakṣas, rākṣasas, and other malevolent beings — to flee in terror in every direction.

Sazang stresses that refuge is taken in him "with the utmost reverence," while Gyaltsab and Thokme both specify the class of beings that flee at his sight (Yama's messengers, hell-guardians, and other beings hostile to sentient beings). This verse closes the sequence of the "Refuge" section of the confession, having moved from Samantabhadra and Mañjughoṣa, to Avalokiteśvara, to Ākāśagarbha and the other bodhisattvas, and finally to the fierce protector Vajrapāṇi.
