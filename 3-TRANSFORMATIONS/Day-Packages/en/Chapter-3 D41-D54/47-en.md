# Day 47 — Turning others' anger or faith into their benefit, and praying that those who harm me awaken

**Date:** Aug 21  
**Chapter:** 3  
**Verses covered:** 3-15 to 3-16

<!-- sec:challenge -->
## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's daily practice is based on the two verses 15 and 16 from the third chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. These give a special instruction that turns even the person who harms us into a cause of awakening.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a passage of scripture that is easy to understand and encouraging. It runs: "May those who give me food obtain the happiness of complete peace," and so on.

Khenpo Kunzang Palden took this example and applied it to the prayer in verse sixteen. Its meaning is this. Whoever sees me, or hears me, or remembers me, or touches me, and whether the connection is a good one or a bad one: may all those beings accomplish everything they wish for. And may it become a cause for bringing about every benefit and happiness, both for now and in the end. That is the prayer.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today I will pray that those who harm me attain awakening.

**Explanation:** *(Patience Practice)* This practice works with all three kinds of harm: speaking harshly with the voice, harming with the body, and slandering behind one's back. Whichever of them is done, we do not get angry ourselves. Instead we pray that all of those people come to have the fortune of attaining awakening. This makes a cause of awakening for the beings who harm us. It also has the benefit of taming our own mind.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

In the phrase "a mind of anger or of faith," Khunu Lama Rinpoche distinguished two readings of the Sanskrit word *krodha*, according to whether or not it carries the subscript letter, and said that on one reading the sense is "anger" and on the other the sense is not "faith" but "displeasure"; and he said that some commentaries have it that way too. In brief: if any being at all, taking me as their object, generates anger toward me, or has something like a mind of displeasure arise, then since a connection of a bad kind has been made with me, may that itself always become the cause that accomplishes all their aims. This is explained just as it comes in the first chapter, in the line quoted above, "whoever does harm is linked to happiness" — the connection turns once again into a circumstance that brings about benefit and happiness. So: if anger or a mind of displeasure arises, may that very circumstance become, in the future, a cause that turns into a contributing condition for accomplishing that person's aims.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-15]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

In particular: if others, taking me as their object, have a mind of anger arise in them and wish to do me harm, or else have a mind of faith arise and wish to accomplish their own purpose through me — then may that very person always, by those means, come to accomplish everything they want, whether Dharma or material things. This shows that such an intention is never wasted.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-15]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

If any people, taking me as their object, come to have a mind of anger or a mind of faith, then in dependence on that very mind may all the aims of those people — both for now and in the end — always be accomplished.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-15]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

If any, taking me as their object, come to have a mind of anger or a mind of faith, may that itself always become the cause by which everything they wish for is accomplished. As for the words "a mind of faith," the Great Commentary explains them as "no faith."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-15]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Further: if others, taking me as their object, have a mind of anger arise in them, or if in some a mind of faith arises, may those very minds themselves always become the cause by which those beings accomplish, without exception, all their aims, both for now and in the end.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-15]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Taking my body and mind as their object, whatever beings — high, low or middling, enemy or friend — become angry with me, or have a mind of clear confidence, that is, faith, or a feeling of closeness: whichever such mind arises, may that itself always become the cause by which every aim those beings have in mind is accomplished. So one prays.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-15]]

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **kunpal / gyaltsab / sabzang / khenpo-kunga (and khenpo-zhengah's annotation commentary):** they take "a mind of faith" in its plain sense as faith, and explain it as a good state of mind, the opposite of anger (which is faulty by nature). ⚑
- **ngulchu-thogmed:** citing the Great Commentary as his source, he reports that "a mind of faith" here is explained as "no faith." ⚑
- **tenzin-gyatso:** citing Khunu Lama Rinpoche as his source, he explains that a difference of spelling in the word *krodha* gives two senses — one "anger," and the other not "faith" but "displeasure" — and says that some commentaries have it that way too. ⚑

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. This Verse Is a Prayer: Whatever Mind of Anger or of Faith Arises Toward Me, May That Itself Become the Cause That Accomplishes All Those Beings' Aims

Many commentaries agree in the end that it makes no difference whether the mind is one of anger or one of faith: may it become the cause by which those beings' aims are accomplished.

###### 2. The Aims Prayed For Are Both the Aims of the Present and the Final Aim

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Sabzang Mati Panchen both explain "all their aims" as all the aims of those people without exception, both for now and in the end.

###### 3. This Prayer Reaches Every Kind of Being — High, Low or Middling, Enemy or Friend Alike

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that the prayer covers whatever beings take me as their object — high, low or middling, enemy or friend — whether they become angry or a mind of faith arises in them.

###### 4. Whatever Mind of Anger or of Faith Arises Toward Me, It Is Not Wasted

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that the verse shows that another's mind of anger and their mind of faith alike are never wasted.

###### 5. Another Commentary Explains the Words "a Mind of Faith" as "No Faith" ⚑

Ngulchu Thogme cites the Great Commentary, where "a mind of faith" is explained as "no faith."

###### 6. On the Authority of Khunu Lama Rinpoche the Sanskrit Term Behind "a Mind of Anger or of Faith" Has Two Senses; and the Verse Is Linked to the First Chapter's "Whoever Does Harm Is Linked to Happiness"

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that if anger or a mind of displeasure arises, the prayer is that this circumstance become, in the future, a contributing condition for accomplishing that person's aims.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse is a prayer. Whether beings who take me as their object come to have a mind of anger or a mind of faith, it makes no difference: may that very mind always become the cause by which all the aims of those beings, both for now and in the end, are accomplished.

**Key points.**

- Whether a mind of anger or a mind of faith arises, may it equally become the cause by which those beings' aims are accomplished.
- The aims prayed for are all the aims without exception, both those of the present and the final one.
- This prayer reaches every being — high, low or middling, enemy or friend alike.
- The verse shows that another's mind of anger and their mind of faith alike are never wasted.
- ⚑ Most commentators take "a mind of faith" in its plain sense as faith; but Ngulchu Thogme records the Great Commentary's reading, "no faith," and His Holiness the Dalai Lama records, on the authority of Khunu Lama Rinpoche, the reading "displeasure" — a divergent sense of the word.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains this verse in accord with the confidence of the first chapter's line "whoever does harm is linked to happiness," showing that even a mind of anger or displeasure becomes a contributing condition for accomplishing that person's aims in the future.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Even if some beings speak ill of me, reviling and disparaging me to my face; or harm me with their bodies, striking and beating me; or do me the harm of mockery, speaking badly of me behind my back — still, for the sake of others I make this prayer: may everyone who has any connection with me come before long to have the fortune of being my own disciple, a fit vessel with a special connection to me.

And for my own sake this is a firm decision, a pledge: even if there is no advantage in it for me, may I become someone who does nothing but accomplish the welfare of beings, both for now and in the end.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-16]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Whatever beings speak ill of me, reviling me openly to my face; or whatever others do actual harm to my body or my possessions; or likewise, behind my back, send all kinds of mockery my way — jeering at me, disparaging me and so on — may all of them too come to have the fortune of attaining great awakening. This shows that what they do is never wasted.

In short, as it is said in the *Sūtra of the Meeting of Father and Son*: may all beings who have any connection with me — through seeing me, hearing me, remembering me or touching me, and whether the connection is a good one or a bad one — accomplish everything they wish for; and may this become the cause by which all their benefit and happiness, both for now and in the end, is brought about. That is the prayer.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-16]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Whatever people speak ill of me with their voices, or others harm me with their bodies, or likewise make fun of me behind my back — whatever it is they do, may it all come to have the fortune of attaining great awakening.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-16]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Whoever speaks harsh words to me openly, or others do me harm, or likewise make fun of me in front of other people — may all of them come to have the fortune of awakening.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-16]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Whatever beings revile me and speak ill of me, or others harm my body or my possessions, or likewise, though I am at no fault, invent things about me and send all kinds of mockery my way — may all of them too come to have the fortune of attaining great awakening.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-16]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Praying that everything others think and do with me as their object become meaningful has two parts: praying that their intention be meaningful, and praying that their action be meaningful.

First. If any beings, taking me as their object, generate anger in their minds with the wish to torment me, or have a mind of the clearest faith arise — or, following the Great Commentary, if a mind that has no faith arises — then in any case, whichever such intention arises, whether anger, faith or lack of faith, one trains the mind with the thought: "May that itself always become the cause by which those beings easily accomplish whatever they wish for — the higher realms, liberation and the rest, their aims both immediate and long-term." That is praying that their intention be meaningful.

And by way of action: if any beings revile me openly with their speech, disparaging me and speaking ill of me; or if others harm me with their bodies, striking and beating me; or if likewise, behind my back, they recount my faults to other people and send all kinds of mockery my way — then one trains the mind with the thought: "May whatever such action those people perform, reviling me and the rest, all become the cause of their coming to have the fortune of attaining, in the end, great awakening." That is praying that their action be meaningful.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-16]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

If some beings speak ill of me — jeering at me, laughing at me scornfully; or if some other beings harm my body and mind, doing me injury; or if yet others make fun of me openly or behind my back, insulting me or mocking me — whichever of these it may be, may they all in truth come to have the fortune of attaining the state of perfect awakening.

In short, whichever beings — enemy, friend or neither — abuse me, beat me, humiliate me, or in any way whatever go so far as to take my life, may all of them come to have the fortune of unsurpassed perfect awakening. One should train the mind in this way, he says, and pray it again and again.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-16]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Whatever Beings Speak Ill of Me, Harm Me With Their Bodies, or Make Fun of Me Behind My Back, May They All Come to Have the Fortune of Attaining Great Awakening

Many commentaries agree in the end that the meaning of this verse is a prayer that all the beings who do harm come to have the fortune of great awakening.

###### 2. The Commentaries Differ Over the Fruit of This Prayer ⚑

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden, Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sabzang Mati Panchen, Ngulchu Thogme and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explain it as praying that the very beings who do harm attain awakening. His Holiness the Dalai Lama, however, prays for the sake of others that everyone connected with him come to have the fortune of being his own disciples, and for his own sake explains it in the manner of a pledge to become someone who does nothing but accomplish the welfare of beings.

###### 3. Three Kinds of Harm Are Identified — Speaking Ill With the Voice, Harming With the Body, and Making Fun Behind One's Back

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden and many others make plain that the word "mockery" (ཕྱར་ཀ) means jeering at someone behind their back, disparaging them and the like.

###### 4. The Prayer Is Set Out in Two Parts — Praying That Their Intention Be Meaningful and Praying That Their Action Be Meaningful

Minyak Kunzang Sonam distinguishes the two. Praying that the mere arising of anger or faith in beings who take me as their object become the cause by which their aims are accomplished is praying that their intention be meaningful; and praying that what they actually do — reviling me with their speech and the rest — become the cause of attaining awakening is praying that their action be meaningful.

###### 5. The Reach of This Prayer Covers Every Kind of Being — Enemy, Friend or Neither Alike — and Extends Even to the Worst Case, the Taking of One's Life

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that whichever beings — enemy, friend or neither — abuse me, beat me, humiliate me, or in any way go so far as to take my life, one should pray that all of it become the fortune of unsurpassed awakening.

###### 6. Citing the *Sūtra of the Meeting of Father and Son* as the Illustration, the Reach of This Prayer Is Extended to All the Benefit and Happiness, Present and Ultimate, of Every Being Connected With Me by Sight, Hearing, Memory or Touch

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden cites the passage from that sutra and so extends the reach of this prayer to the accomplishing of everything wished for by all beings connected with him, whether for good or ill.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse is a prayer put into verse: whatever beings speak ill of me with their voices, harm me with their bodies, or make fun of me behind my back, may all of them come to have the fortune of attaining great awakening. So most of the commentaries agree in the end. ⚑ His Holiness the Dalai Lama, however, sets this verse out further, dividing it into a prayer for the sake of others and one for one's own sake.

**Key points.**

- May all the beings who do harm come to have the fortune of great awakening.
- The three kinds of harm are speaking ill with the voice, harming with the body, and making fun behind one's back.
- ⚑ There are divergent explanations of what the fruit means: most explain it as praying that the beings who do harm themselves attain awakening, but His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains it as praying, for the sake of others, that everyone connected with him become his own disciples, and, for his own sake, as a pledge to become someone who accomplishes the welfare of beings.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam divides this prayer into praying that their intention be meaningful and praying that their action be meaningful.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that the reach of this prayer extends to every being — enemy, friend or neither — and even to the taking of one's life.
- Khenpo Kunzang Pelden cites the *Sūtra of the Meeting of Father and Son* and so extends the reach of this prayer to all the benefit and happiness, present and ultimate, of every being connected with him, whether for good or ill.
