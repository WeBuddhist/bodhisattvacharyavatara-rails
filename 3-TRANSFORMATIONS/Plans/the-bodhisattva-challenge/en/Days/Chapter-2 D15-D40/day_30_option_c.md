# Day 30 — The one bag you get to pack

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Take three quiet minutes before sleep tonight
> **Detail:** Only the good you build travels with you. Tonight, look back over the day and tend it.

## Opening

Shantideva looks ahead to the moment of dying and asks a blunt question: who can actually help then? Not relatives, not friends, however much they love us. He pictures it without flinching. The one thing that goes with us, he says, is the good we have done, and he is sorry he gathered so little of it. It sounds grim, but it lands as encouragement: the merit is still ours to build, starting today.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, makes the deathbed concrete. You are lying ill, he says, surrounded on every side by relatives, friends, doctors, and nurses, looked after as well as anyone could be. None of it reaches the thing that is actually happening to you. When the end takes hold, all that care stays in the room, and you go on alone. So the real question becomes which things can travel with you. Possessions cannot. People cannot. The only thing that goes, Sazang says, is the merit you have already built, your refuge, your discipline, your good acts, and that becomes your one protector. His regret is simply that he left the packing so late. The encouragement hidden in that regret is that the bag is still open. What you put in it today, you keep.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Tonight, before you sleep, take three quiet minutes. First bring to mind the good you did today, however small, and let yourself be glad of it. Then admit anything you did wrong, plainly and without piling on blame. Last, wish that all of it, the good most of all, be for the benefit of everyone. This is the one bag you actually get to pack, so add a little to it each night.
