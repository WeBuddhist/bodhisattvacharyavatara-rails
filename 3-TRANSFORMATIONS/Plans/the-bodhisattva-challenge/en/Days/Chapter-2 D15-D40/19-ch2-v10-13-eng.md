## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice comes from verses 10 through 13 of the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. It shows us how to take our normal, everyday routines—like taking a shower, washing our face, or getting dressed—and turn them into deep spiritual practices. By mentally imagining that we are offering a sweet-smelling bath, clean robes, and beautiful ornaments to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas, we can wash away the negative energy and mental habits of ourselves and all living beings.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

These verses explain the practice of mental offering through bathing, drying, robing, and adorning holy beings. Master Shantideva pictures filling precious jeweled vases with sweet-scented water and bathing the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas while playing beautiful music and singing praises. After the bath, he imagines drying their bodies with clean, fragrant, peerless cloths. Next, he offers traditional monastic robes to those in monk form, and dresses great Bodhisattvas like Samantabhadra, Manjushri, and Avalokiteshvara in soft, heavenly silk garments and hundreds of shining jewel ornaments.

Spiritual teachers explain that the Buddhas are already pure; they do not get dirty, nor do they need our water, clothes, or jewels. We practice this visualization entirely to clean our own hearts. By imagining this pure, blessed offering, we imagine the falling water washing away all the anger, greed, sadness, and bad karma trapped inside our own minds and the minds of all beings. This simple act of imagination clears away mental fog and clothes our character in the inner beauty of patience, wisdom, and kindness.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today while washing or showering, I will mentally visualize bathing the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas to wash away the negative karma of all beings.

**Explanation:** _(Doing good)_ Usually, when we take a shower or wash our hands and face, we do it just out of habit to keep our physical bodies clean. Today, following the teachings of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_, I will turn this ordinary daily routine into a powerful positive action. While running the water, I will imagine that it is pure, scented nectar and that I am respectfully offering a bath to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas. I will strongly wish that the blessing of this clean water washes away all the mental pollution, bad habits, and suffering of myself and every living being, leaving our minds crystal clear and bright.
