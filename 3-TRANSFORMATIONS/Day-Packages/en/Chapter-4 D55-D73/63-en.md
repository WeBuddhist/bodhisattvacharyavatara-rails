## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the three verses 22 to 24 from the fourth chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. If we waste this good body of freedom and fortune we have found, great grief and regret will rise even more at the time of death.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a saying that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Whoever uses a bowl of gold set with jewels to wipe up filth and vomit is a great fool. But whoever is born human and does wrongdoing is far more foolish still.

The meaning of this saying is this: taking a gold bowl adorned with jewels and using it to wipe up dirty vomit is truly foolish. But more foolish still is the one who gains this precious human body, so hard to find, and then does wrongdoing. Our gaining this body of freedom and fortune is like gaining a precious bowl of gold. If we rely on this body and do wrongdoing instead of doing good, we become even more foolish than the one who wipes vomit with a gold bowl. So we should not spend this life of ours on pointless wrongdoing. We should always work at doing good.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, even if a discouraged feeling comes up while I practice Dharma, I will not give up. I will carry one small practice of doing good through to the end.

**Explanation:** *(Practice of diligence)* If we do not become discouraged today while doing good, no regret will come later at the time of death. Losing heart on the path of learning Dharma is a mistake. If we do one small good action each day, the strength of our diligence will grow.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains: if what remained were only the mass of faults already accumulated that propel us toward the lower realms — with nothing further being newly accumulated — then, however great that mass might be, one could hope that it would gradually be exhausted and would not have to be experienced again. But it is not like that. In the very abodes of the lower realms where immeasurable suffering is already being and has already been experienced, further new wrongdoing keeps arising. For as long as this clear and cognizant mind remains under the sway of delusion's grasping at true existence, then whenever it meets with a lingering condition, attachment or aversion arises, and, through the power of that, the accumulation of further bad karma can occur even in the abodes of the lower realms. For example — hells and hungry ghosts are not something we can observe directly in our present experience, but if we consider an animal existence such as a dog or a pig, the full range of attached-and-averse behavior is on display: fighting, sexual activity, mutual belittling, jockeying for dominance, and greedily fighting over food and drink. The arising of a virtuous thought, dependent as it is on the power of a field of merit, comes about only occasionally and by chance, so it is extremely difficult. Therefore, if we do not exercise great care in our present circumstance, there is a real danger that once dependent origination is set loose and, in the future, some unfavorable configuration of causes and conditions arises, there will be no chance left even to hope for a way out.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-22]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Even so, one might think: having experienced that suffering — the result of wrongdoing previously committed, the suffering of the lower realms — that suffering will be exhausted through having been experienced, and one will be freed. But even after experiencing just that much suffering of the lower realms, such as hell, this being of evil karma does not become free from that state of the lower realms. The reason is this: while experiencing that suffering, the result of previous evil karma, further wrongdoing is at the same time being newly generated and accumulated — the correspondingly similar results of ill will, anger, killing, and so forth — as illustrated, for example, by the stories of the hawk and the wolf, and of the two swift-runners, the crow and the vulture. Now, in general, manifest objects of knowledge are to be established by direct perception, hidden ones by inference, and extremely hidden matters such as cause and effect are to be measured by the authoritative scriptural word of the Victorious One — and it is taught that this functions as a proof through reasoning; if so, then, among the four kinds of reasoning — reasoning by function, reasoning by dependence, reasoning that proves through valid cognition, and reasoning of the nature of things — this belongs to the last, reasoning of the nature of things. However, since the hell-realm state of existence is the plane where karmic ripening occurs, if the accumulation of causes and the gradual experiencing of their results both continue there, it would seem that there would never be an occasion for release from it — and since I do not know how the author's actual intention here is to be reconciled with that, I ask that the learned examine this well.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-22]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

If one thinks: once the result of wrongdoing committed is exhausted, one is reborn in a fortunate realm, so it should not be so hard to become free of the lower realms — having experienced just that much, the result of previous karma, that person, having exhausted just that much, does not thereby become free of the lower realms. For while experiencing that former karmic result of suffering, further wrongdoing capable of generating powerful suffering keeps arising and accumulating from moment to moment — because, on the basis of a lower-realm existence, virtue is weak in force while wrongdoing accumulates continuously, one should strive to block the causes of the lower realms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-22]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

*(Ngulchu Thogme transcludes verses 4-21 and 4-22 together as one quoted block and comments on both at once; the explanation below covers both. In the current segmentation of this source, the quoted verse-pair is stamped as this file's own block ^4-42, and the explanation as ^4-43.)*

As for "wrongdoing committed in a single instant": according to what is said in the commentaries regarding the crimes of immediate retribution and the like, this refers to the very instant that completes the act. If such a hateful thought toward a special object is generated even for the shortest possible instant, or done at the very end of one's life, then one must remain in Avīci for an eon on that basis alone — so what need is there to mention beings who, through wrongdoing accumulated throughout beginningless saṃsāra, do not go to a fortunate realm but go to a lower one? Having experienced just that much — the result of previous wrongdoing — one does not thereby become free of suffering. For while experiencing it in just this way, further wrongdoing that is the cause of suffering keeps arising and accumulating.

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If one thinks: having experienced the karmic ripening, one is thereby freed — even having experienced just that much, such as the Avīci hell, that being does not thereby become free from the lower realms. The reason is this: while experiencing that suffering, the result of evil karma, one's malice remains unexhausted, so further wrongdoing — such as harming one another — keeps arising in abundance and accumulating.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-22]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

*(Minyak Kunzang Sonam's commentary sits after the 4-22 transclusion but continues the same discussion begun for 4-21; the explanation and quotations below draw on material relevant to both verses.)*

Even the wrongdoing of generating anger or ill will for the briefest possible instant toward an object such as a bodhisattva — an object that is itself an antidote — or of committing, in a single instant that completes an act, a deed such as one of the five crimes of immediate retribution, causes one to dwell in the Avīci hell for an intervening eon and experience suffering there — so what need is there even to mention that beings who, from beginningless time, circling through saṃsāra for a long duration, have accumulated wrongdoing that has not yet ripened and has not been destroyed even by an antidote, and who have many such deeds still lodged in their mindstream, will be obstructed by them from going to a fortunate realm and will have to remain continuously in the lower realms for many eons? Even so, if one definitely purifies the previously accumulated causes of the lower realms and closes the door to committing new ones, then, although being reborn in a fortunate realm is not in itself so rare, doing so under these conditions is extremely rare. If one does not act in this way, one goes to the lower realms, and once there, accomplishing virtue is extremely rare while wrongdoing is accumulated continuously, so one must pass through the lower realms alone for countless millions of eons — being reborn in a fortunate realm during that time is something one will not even hear the name of, let alone attain, so it is extremely hard to find. This is why the sūtras say: "Even should a hundred such beings be reborn among humans" — teaching that being reborn as a human is extremely difficult.

If one thinks: when experiencing the karmic ripening of wrongdoing in the lower realms, since the previous bad karma is thereby exhausted, one is freed from that — a being who has experienced the karmic result of previously accumulated wrongdoing, such as the suffering of hell, just once, and for whom that much is exhausted, does not thereby become immediately free of the lower realms and released from suffering. Why is this? Because, while experiencing that very suffering produced by previously accumulated evil karma, other kinds of wrongdoing that produce great suffering — such as anger arising among the inhabitants of the lower realms toward one another, and doing harm to one another — keep arising and accumulating from moment to moment on that same basis of a lower-realm existence; so, even after dying and transmigrating from one lower realm, one must pass again through another lower realm, making immediate liberation difficult. This is indeed so, since even though the capacity to accomplish virtue on the basis of a lower-realm existence is extremely weak, wrongdoing is accumulated continuously — as can be seen even now by observing scattered animals.

If one then wonders whether, having been born in the lower realms even once, one might never again obtain a fortunate rebirth — obtaining one is possible, but extremely difficult. Why? Because, even though it is conceivable that once the karmic forces propelling one back to the lower realms are exhausted, one might be born through karma that is certain to be experienced as rebirth in a fortunate realm, or through karma to be experienced on some other occasion, nevertheless, as just explained, since on the basis of a lower-realm existence the causes of the lower realms are accumulated for the most part, obtaining a fortunate rebirth is extremely difficult. For this reason, relative to the lower-realm destinies, this human body is extremely rare and scarce. Therefore one should strive by every means to abandon and purify the wrongdoing that is the cause of the lower realms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-22]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Once the ripened result of whatever wrongdoing and non-virtuous karma was accumulated in a previous birth and in this present life has been experienced in the lower realms such as hell exactly to that extent, this person who has accumulated wrongdoing does not, at the very end of that experience, thereby become free from the lower realms or free from saṃsāra in that manner. Rather, it is taught that while the suffering that is the result of the previously accumulated wrongdoing is being experienced, further wrongdoing that will produce suffering to be experienced in the future keeps arising in abundance. For example: when, as the ripened result of a previous act of killing, one takes birth in a hell-state for an intervening eon or so, and afterward transmigrates from there to another rebirth, there are two kinds of correspondingly similar result — the correspondingly similar result of experience, and the correspondingly similar result of action. The correspondingly similar result of experience is, for instance, that because of the fault of one's own previous killing, one must experience the suffering of being killed by others for five hundred lifetimes. The correspondingly similar result of action is that, through the correspondingly similar cause of having grown habituated to killing before, one becomes habituated, birth after birth and life after life, to killing again and again. Thus, the very delight one takes in killing is the correspondingly similar result-of-action of one's previous act of killing; and while experiencing that, further wrongdoing that will be the cause of future suffering — such as killing committed by another — keeps arising in abundance. This is the kind of meaning to be understood here.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-22]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Experiencing the Result of Previous Wrongdoing That Much Does Not by Itself Bring Freedom

All the commentaries agree in explaining that even after experiencing just that much suffering as the result of previously committed wrongdoing, a being does not thereby become free of the lower realms — because, even while experiencing the earlier suffering, further wrongdoing keeps arising and accumulating through ill will, anger, and the like.

###### 2. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk Opens the Gate to New Wrongdoing Through the Two Correspondingly Similar Results — of Experience and of Action

Using the example of killing, he explains that experiencing the suffering of being killed by others for five hundred lifetimes because of one's own earlier act of killing is the correspondingly similar result of experience, while, through the force of habituation, taking delight again and again in killing is the correspondingly similar result of action.

###### 3. Minyak Kunzang Sonam Cites Sūtra to Show How Extremely Rare It Is to Return from a Lower Realm to a Fortunate One

He explains that unless the previously accumulated causes of the lower realms are purified, being reborn in a fortunate realm is extremely difficult, and cites sūtra to the effect that even being reborn simply as a human is said to occur only once in a hundred.

###### 4. His Holiness the Dalai Lama Explains That, Through the Power of Delusion's Grasping at True Existence, New Wrongdoing Can Arise Even in the Lower Realms

He teaches, using the example of animals, that for as long as the mind remains under the sway of delusion's grasping at true existence, meeting with even a slight condition gives rise to attachment or aversion, so that new bad karma can be accumulated even in the abodes of the lower realms.

###### 5. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden Identifies a Difficult Point of Reasoning and Sets It Down for the Learned to Examine

He notes that, since the hell-realm state of existence is itself the plane of karmic ripening, it would seem to follow — if causes are accumulated there and their results experienced there in turn — that there would never be any occasion for release, and asks that the learned examine this difficult point well.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Even after experiencing just that much suffering of the lower realms as the ripened result of accumulated wrongdoing, a being does not thereby become free of the lower realms — for while that very suffering is being experienced, further wrongdoing, driven by ill will and anger, keeps newly arising and accumulating. All the commentators agree on this.

**Key points.**

- **Experiencing the result does not bring freedom.** While the result of previous evil karma is being experienced, further wrongdoing that will produce a future result keeps arising and accumulating in the meantime, so one does not become immediately free from the lower realms.
- **Explained through the correspondingly similar result.** Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk uses the example of killing: experiencing suffering oneself because of a previous act of killing is the correspondingly similar result of experience, while, through habituation, taking delight again and again in killing is the correspondingly similar result of action — this is how further wrongdoing arises.
- **New karma accumulated through delusion's grasping at true existence.** His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that because the mind remains under the sway of delusion's grasping at true existence, attachment or aversion arises whenever conditions are met, so that new bad karma can be accumulated even in the abodes of the lower realms — illustrated with the example of animal behavior.
- **How difficult it is to obtain a fortunate rebirth after falling to the lower realms.** Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that, as taught in sūtra, it is extremely rare to accomplish virtue on the basis of a lower-realm existence while wrongdoing accumulates continuously, so regaining a fortunate rebirth and a human body is extremely difficult.
- **A difficult point of reasoning set down for the learned.** Khenpo Kunzang Pelden notes that since the hell realms are themselves the plane of karmic ripening, this raises a difficult question about how release is ever possible, and asks that the learned examine it well.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

Again, considering that even obtaining such a perfect state of leisure is itself extremely rare, the teaching that one is utterly deluded if one does not then strive to increase the side of virtue means this: having obtained such a perfect state of leisure as this, if I do not generate the strength of the roots of virtue and grow accustomed to them, then there is no greater way to fool myself than this squandering of the wealth of virtue; and there is no greater delusion than this abandoning of the Dharma of virtue while it is still in hand.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He counsels: having obtained such leisure and endowment as this, if, instead of growing accustomed to virtue, I let myself fall under the sway of distraction and simply remain caught up in busyness, there is no greater way to fool myself and no greater delusion than this — so if I am to accomplish something truly meaningful for my own sake, something I myself hold dear, then I must exercise great care right now.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-23]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Third: accomplishing the holy Dharma is workable if one applies oneself, yet it is hard to find such an opportunity, and finding it is of great importance — having found such leisure as this, this precious human body, and having encountered the Dharma and a good teacher, I have, at best, secured this present life; at middling best, obtained a human body in a future life so as to awaken the potential of a bodhisattva's family; and at the very least, not grown accustomed at all to virtue that is free of regret even at death — if, instead, I do not accomplish any virtue at all and, under the sway of the eight worldly concerns, let my distracted mind squander this life to nothing, then there is no other self-deception greater than this being fooled by myself; and there is no other delusion greater than this being deceived by myself as to what should be adopted and what abandoned. Therefore one should not follow after the world's deceptive lures, however clever they seem; one should not, through petty-mindedness, fall under the sway of delusion, but should cultivate carefulness and accomplish the utterly pure holy Dharma.

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Having obtained such a perfect state of leisure as this — hard to find and of great benefit — if I do not grow accustomed to virtue with a pure motivation and application, then there is no greater way to fool myself than this, since I have thereby fallen away from something of great benefit. Being so utterly confused about what is to be adopted and abandoned, there is no greater delusion than this — as it is likewise said in the Suhṛllekha:

> "Whoever uses a bowl of gold set with jewels to wipe up filth and vomit is a great fool. But whoever is born human and does wrongdoing is far more foolish still."

Having said this, one should strive at virtue while one has obtained this state of leisure.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-23]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Extremely hard to find, and of such great benefit when found that it surpasses even a wish-fulfilling jewel a hundredfold — having now obtained such a perfect basis of leisure and endowment as this, if, at this very time when I have it, I do not grow accustomed and habituated, through both motivation and application, to the side of virtue that brings about a higher rebirth and liberation, and instead, through laziness and distraction, accomplish only the trivial business of this present life and let my whole life run to waste, then there is no greater way to fool myself than this — for I will have fallen away from the great benefit, for both myself and others, of accomplishing the holy Dharma — and there is no greater delusion regarding what is to be engaged in or turned away from than this, since I will not have known how to extract the essence from this leisure and endowment for the sake of myself and others, having instead employed this very precious human basis solely in wrongdoing and let it go entirely to waste.

Again, there is no greater self-deception than this, since I myself will have destroyed my own wealth of virtue; and there is no greater delusion as to my own good than this, since I will not have recognized the theft of the wealth of virtue that lay in my very own hand — this, according to the Great Commentary, is how it is explained.

The Suhṛllekha says:

> "Whoever uses a bowl of gold set with jewels to wipe up filth and vomit is greatly generous compared to one who, born human, does wrongdoing."

And Vīra likewise says:

> "One who, having obtained wealth in virtue across countless eons, through delusion here fails to gather even the slightest treasure of merit, will enter an unbearable hall of grief in the world beyond.
>
> One who is given passage to the Isle of Jewels but returns empty-handed to his own house, without the path of the ten virtuous actions, will not obtain it again afterward.
>
> Not to have attained happiness is itself suffering — there is no greater self-deception than that, and no greater delusion than that."

As this is said, at the time of having obtained leisure and endowment, one should strive to engage rightly, without error, in what is to be adopted and abandoned among virtuous and non-virtuous actions.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-23]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Having now obtained such a special basis as this, complete with the eight leisures and ten endowments, if I do not properly grow accustomed to or accomplish study, reflection, and meditation on the holy Dharma, or the three trainings of a bodhisattva and the like, then there is no other self-deception than this — I will have deceived myself — and no greater self-deception than this deception. Thus, on top of this basis of leisure and endowment now obtained, not knowing how to properly practice virtue, abandon wrongdoing, and be deluded about it — there is no delusion greater than this delusion.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-23]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Obtaining Leisure and Endowment Without Growing Accustomed to Virtue Is the Greatest Self-Deception and Delusion

All the commentaries agree in explaining that, having obtained such a perfect state of leisure as this, failing to grow accustomed to virtue means there is no greater way to fool oneself, and no greater delusion regarding what is to be adopted and abandoned.

###### 2. Sabzang and Minyak Kunzang Sonam Both Illustrate This with the Suhṛllekha's Image of the Golden Bowl

Both cite the *Suhṛllekha*'s teaching that doing wrongdoing after obtaining a human body is far more foolish than using a jewel-adorned golden bowl to wipe up filth and vomit, illustrating the fault of squandering the freedoms and endowments.

###### 3. Minyak Kunzang Sonam Cites Three Verses of Vīra, Illustrated by the Merchant Who Returns Empty-Handed from the Isle of Jewels

He explains that even a person made wealthy in virtue across countless eons who, through delusion, fails to gather even a little merit here will enter an unbearable hall of grief hereafter.

###### 4. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk Identifies This Special Basis Through the Eight Leisures and Ten Endowments

He explains that this present basis is distinguished by being complete with the eight leisures and ten endowments, so failing to properly practice virtue and abandon wrongdoing on it means there is no greater delusion than this.

###### 5. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden Explains This Through the Three Great Benefits of Leisure and Endowment

He explains that obtaining leisure and endowment secures, at best, this present life; at middling best, awakens the potential of a bodhisattva's family in a future life; and at the very least, provides virtue free of regret even at death — so failing to grow accustomed to it and letting one's life run to waste is the greatest self-deception.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Having obtained a basis of leisure and endowment that is hard to find and of great benefit, if one fails to grow accustomed to virtue, there is no greater self-deception and no greater delusion regarding what is to be adopted and abandoned than this — all the commentators agree on this.

- **The final outcome of self-deception and delusion.** Having obtained a perfect basis of leisure and endowment, if, through laziness and distraction, one fails to strive through both motivation and application at the virtue that is the cause of a higher rebirth and liberation, there is no greater way to fool oneself, and no greater delusion regarding what to engage in or turn away from.

- **The basis of the eight leisures and ten endowments.** Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk identifies the present basis as special in being complete with the eight leisures and ten endowments, and recognizes that being deluded about how to properly practice virtue and abandon wrongdoing on it is the greatest possible delusion.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains: if, even while understanding this state of affairs, I still become discouraged through the power of delusion, and, going along with the ordinary way most people live, fail to gather my capacities and apply diligent effort to the Dharma as best I can, but instead sit back complacently — then, in the end, at the time when the sun of death has risen, when signs such as the glow of hell-fire appear as a foretoken of being reborn in the lower realms, great sorrow is bound to arise in the mind, and there will be nothing whatsoever left to be done about it.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-24]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

If, having understood or realized — like a deer deceived by mirage-water — that having obtained leisure and endowment without accomplishing virtue means I will have fooled myself most gravely and fallen into the greatest delusion, I nevertheless, even now or later, still crave sense pleasures and pursue gain, honor, and fame, and, deceived and overcome by delusion, think "even now, or even later, someone like me cannot accomplish the Dharma," and so become discouraged and disheartened — cutting off my own share of the Dharma through poor discernment, thinking "I don't know how to study, my hatred is too great for compassion to arise, my distraction is too great for me to be capable of diligence," and the like — belittling myself in this way and letting my whole life run to waste, ruining myself by my own hand — then, at the very end of this life, at the time when I am dying, having been born in Jambudvīpa, so hard to be born in; having obtained this body of leisure and endowment, so hard to obtain; and having met with a teacher and the Dharma, so hard to meet — thinking, with regret, "I could have accomplished the holy Dharma with these, yet I did not accomplish it" — my chest and fingernails will fill with regret, great sorrow will arise or be born in my mind, and, having died, I will fall into hell — into the ordinary hells, the hells of intermediate duration, and the fire-hells seven times over.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-24]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

If I understand that I must strive at virtue and abandon wrongdoing, and yet, through delusion, I still become discouraged later on regarding bodhicitta, training in the conduct, and the like — knowing that I will thereby go to the lower realms — then at the time when I am dying, great sorrow will arise; therefore one should apply oneself with great diligence.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-24]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

*(Ngulchu Thogme transcludes verses 4-24 and 4-25 together as one quoted block and comments on both at once; the explanation below covers both. If, even having understood the faults of transgressing my training, I still, through delusion, become discouraged later on, and fail to apply myself to the training, then, at the time when I am dying, I will feel regret and great sorrow will arise; and after death, since the suffering of hell is unbearably difficult, my body will be burned for a long time — and it is certain that the unbearable fire of regret will blaze up and torment my own mind.

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If, having realized that I fooled myself by not doing virtue during my earlier time of leisure, I have fallen under the sway of confusion and delusion, and, later on too, become disheartened and discouraged and grow careless about virtue — then, at the end of this life, at the time when I am dying — or, according to the Sanskrit text and its commentary,

> "when urged on by the messengers of the Lord of Death"

— as this is said, at the moment surrounded and led by fearsome-looking figures, tormented by fierce regret over not having engaged in what should have been adopted and abandoned, great sorrow will arise.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-24]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

If, having thoroughly understood the very fact that failing to engage in what is to be adopted and abandoned among virtue and wrongdoing at the time of having obtained rare leisure and endowment means I have fooled myself, I nevertheless, under the sway of delusion, fail — not only previously, but even now, again — through carelessness and laziness, to be disheartened and discouraged, and have no enthusiasm for properly training in the general engagement and disengagement of virtue and non-virtue, and in particular in motivation, generating the mind of awakening, and the conduct of the six perfections — then, if I do not apply myself, I will end up going nowhere but to the lower realms at the time of death; and I will end up with nothing but great sorrow arising in my mind out of regret. This may also be explained as: "having understood the faults of transgressing the training."

According to the Sanskrit text and the Great Commentary, it is said: "sorrow arises again and again, greatly urged on by the messengers of the Lord of Death." That is: while the sorrow already arising from thinking that, having died before, one will long be tormented by suffering in hell and the like, keeps recurring, the messengers of the Lord of Death, at the actual moment of death, will signal to you: "You did not accomplish virtue while you were alive before, and committed this and that sinful deed of construction, so now you are falling to the lower realms — if you do not wish to go there, why did you commit such wrongdoing?" — and, with harsh words such as "Go on, get moving!" preceded by threats and blows, they urge you on with great force, so that great sorrow arises in the mind even more intensely than before.

The Smṛtyupasthāna says:

> "When one is overcome at the time of death, the mind is utterly shattered by suffering; I have seen no refuge whatsoever apart from virtue."

So it is said. Therefore, one should strive from this very moment onward at the means — the accomplishment of virtuous Dharma — that prevents regret at the time of death. The Śiṣyalekha says:

> "Having obtained this extremely rare human existence, accomplish exactly what you intend by way of it."

And so on; and Jowo Atiśa likewise says:

> "Having obtained this perfect body of leisure and endowment, so hard to find, and hard to find again hereafter, make it meaningful by striving to accomplish it."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-24]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

The meaning is this: if, having obtained a basis of leisure and endowment such as this, I ought to generate the supreme aspiration to enlightenment and train properly in its instructions, and yet, not having so trained, I fail to accomplish virtue — and if, having understood or realized that this state of affairs carries grave faults, my mind nevertheless remains, still now, in a state of carelessness, and, through delusion about how to accomplish virtue, I become disheartened or discouraged later on, generating the bad thought "how could someone like me possibly accomplish it?" — then, at some point, when this present life of mine comes to an end and I am dying, great suffering and sorrow born of regret will arise in my mind. This is the meaning.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Becoming Discouraged and Failing to Train Brings Great Sorrow at Death — All the Commentaries Agree

Under the sway of delusion, if one becomes disheartened and discouraged later on and fails to train in virtue, then at the time of dying one will feel regret and great sorrow will arise in the mind.

He explains that generating the bad thought "how could someone like me possibly accomplish it," through delusion about the proper way to accomplish virtue, leads to regret and great sorrow arising at the time of death.

If one fails to apply oneself with diligent effort and instead sits back complacently, then, when signs such as the glow of hell-fire appear at the time of dying, great sorrow is bound to arise in the mind with nothing left to be done about it.
