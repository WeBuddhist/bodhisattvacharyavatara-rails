# Day 15 — Chapter 2 opens: the whole world as an offering

## Opening

*Push notification:* Chapter 2 is called "Confessing Misdeeds" — but it begins with a gift.

Today we reach chapter 2. Before any confession, before any clearing of faults, Shantideva opens his hands and offers everything he can find to the Three Jewels. The first three verses list what he brings: flowers, fruit, medicine, clear water, jeweled mountains, quiet forests, trees bowed down with fruit.

It is a surprising way to begin.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

The first verse says the purpose of these offerings is to firmly take up bodhicitta. You build good conditions before you can truly hold it.

**Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen**, a fifteenth-century teacher, explains how this is possible without wealth or a temple. The flowers and mountains and streams in the verses have **no owner**. Because they belong to no one, you can offer them all in your mind. You simply picture them and present them to the Three Jewels.

Sazang Mati Panchen adds that offering things you do not grasp at brings merit without limit, and **the wider you give, the steadier bodhicitta becomes**.

## Aspiration

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

When you go outside today, find one beautiful thing that belongs to no one — a tree, the open sky, a flower, running water — and offer it in your mind to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.
