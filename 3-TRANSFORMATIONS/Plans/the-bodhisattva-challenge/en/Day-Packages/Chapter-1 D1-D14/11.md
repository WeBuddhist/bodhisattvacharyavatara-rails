# Day 11 — The friend who helps you see clearly

**Date:** Jul 16  
**Chapter:** 1  
**Verses covered:** 1-29 to 1-30

---

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

### Notification

**Title:** Thank someone who helped you see clearly  
**Detail:** The deepest help isn't comfort, but clarity. Today, remember someone who gave you that.

### Opening

Today's two verses name three things this mind does for people who are struggling. It brings them happiness. It takes away their pain. And it clears away their confusion. Then the verses ask: where is there anything, or anyone, equal to that?

### From the Tradition

These two verses show, in a way that is easy for ordinary people to understand, three wonderful qualities of the bodhisattva who removes the troubles of beings.

Beings want happiness but do not have it; with great love, the bodhisattva fills them with every kind of happiness, both for now and for the long run. Beings do not want suffering but have so much of it; with great compassion, the bodhisattva cuts away all the suffering of birth, ageing, sickness, and death, right at the root. And for people who do not know what to take up and what to give up when it comes to the causes of happiness and suffering, the bodhisattva, with great wisdom, clears away the darkness of their confusion.

What other good deed could equal the awakening mind, which helps beings through these three: love, compassion, and wisdom? There is no better friend, and no greater merit, anywhere.

### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when I feel angry or confused, I will immediately remember Bodhicitta to calm my mind.

**Explanation:** Today's verse asks a simple question: is there any friend or good action as powerful as Bodhicitta for clearing away our confusion? The answer is no, there is nothing else like it. By remembering this loving intention the exact moment you feel upset or lost, you are using the best possible way to bring peace back to your mind.

#### གཙོ་གནད། (Main teaching points)

1. **བྱམས་པ་ཆེན་པོས་གནས་སྐབས་དང་མཐར་ཐུག་གི་བདེ་བ་ཀུན་གྱིས་ཚིམ་པར་བྱེད་པའི་གནད།** — Verse 1-29 answers the first predicament named in 1-28: beings who want happiness cannot achieve it. The bodhisattva's great love satisfies beings lacking happiness (བདེ་བས་ཕོངས་པ་) with ALL happiness — both temporary (གནས་སྐབས་) and ultimate (མཐར་ཐུག་). KKP specifies: the happiness provided is not partial but complete, encompassing this life and future lives.
   ([[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md]])

2. **སྙིང་རྗེ་ཆེན་པོས་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ཐམས་ཅད་རྒྱུན་གཅོད་པར་བྱེད་པའི་གནད།** — The bodhisattva's great compassion does not merely reduce suffering but severs its continuum entirely (རྒྱུན་གཅོད་). NKW qualifies the compassion as "ཐུགས་དམིགས་པ་མེད་པའི་ཐུགས་རྗེ་ཆེན་པོ་" — compassion without any fixed referential object, operating on all beings of all three realms (ཁམས་གསུམ་).
   ([[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md]])

3. **བྱམས་སྙིང་རྗེ་ཤེས་རབ་གསུམ་ལས་གྲུབ་པའི་གནད། (SMPLG / NKW)** — SMPLG and NKW treat 1-29 as the opening of a two-verse unit (1-29/30). Within that unit, love addresses beings' lack of happiness; compassion severs their sufferings; and wisdom (addressed explicitly in 1-30) clears away the root delusion. All three qualities together constitute the bodhisattva's complete response to the triple predicament established in 1-28.
   ([[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md]]) ([[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md]])

#### གཙོ་གནད། (Main teaching points)

1. **ཤེས་རབ་ཆེན་པོས་གཏི་མུག་སེལ་བའི་གནད།** — Verse 1-30 completes the triad begun in 1-29 by naming the bodhisattva's third quality: great wisdom (ཤེས་རབ་ཆེན་པོ་). Whereas love and compassion address happiness and suffering, wisdom addresses the root: beings' ignorance (གཏི་མུག་) about what to adopt and abandon (བླང་དོར་). KKP specifies this as ignorance of cause and result (རྒྱུ་འབྲས་བླང་དོར་).
   ([[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md]])

2. **གྲོགས་མཆོག་གི་གནད། (བཤེས་ཀྱང་ག་ལ་ཡོད།)** — The verse's second hemistich asks three rhetorical questions: where is anyone equal in virtue? where is a friend who brings such benefit? where is such merit? KS and KKP make clear these questions function as proof of bodhicitta's incomparability — no other virtue, friend, or source of merit can parallel what bodhicitta provides.
   ([[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md]]) ([[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md]])

3. **བྱམས་བརྩེ་ཤེས་རབ་གསུམ་གྱི་མཐུད་མཚམས། (GDR / NTS / MKS)** — All three commentaries that group 1-28/29/30 together read 1-30 as the terminus of the triad: love (1-29a), compassion (1-29b), wisdom (1-30a) — each directly addressing one of the three predicaments of sentient beings described in 1-28: being deprived of happiness, having many sufferings, being deluded about their causes.
   ([[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md]]) ([[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md]]) ([[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md]])

4. **གཏན་གྱི་མཛའ་བཤེས་ཀྱི་གནད། (tenzin-gyatso)** — TG expands on the verse's "བཤེས་" (friend) by contrasting bodhicitta's changeless friendship — sustained as long as space endures — with ordinary worldly friendship, which even when genuinely helpful is unstable (མི་བརྟན་པ་). This sharpens the rhetorical question "དེ་འདྲའི་བཤེས་ཀྱང་ག་ལ་ཡོད།" into a substantive claim about impermanence.
   ([[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^1-30]])
