## བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ཀྱི་ཕན་ཡོན་བཤད་པ།

### Synthesis (original language)

མཚན་མོའི་མུན་ནག་གི་སྤྲིན་གསེབ་ཏུ་གློག་འགྱུ་བ་ལྟར་དགེ་བའི་བློ་གྲོས་ནི་བརྒྱ་ལམ་ན་ཐང་འགའ་ཙམ་འབྱུང་བས་ཤིན་ཏུ་ཉམས་ཆུང་ལ། སྡིག་པ་མི་དགེ་བ་ནི་སྟོབས་ཤིན་ཏུ་ཆེ་བས་དགེ་བ་ཕལ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་མི་ནུས་པ་རྣམས་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ཀྱིས་འགོག་ཅིང་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་པར་བྱེད་དོ།།

### Divergences

- **Precious Human Rebirth (དལ་འབྱོར་གྱི་ལུས་རྟེན་)** — དངུལ་ཆུ་ཐོགས་མེད། and རྒྱལ་ཚབ་དར་མ་རིན་ཆེན། read that the precious human rebirth is the essential physical basis for generating Bodhicitta and is extremely difficult to obtain ⚑; ས་བཟང་མ་ཏི་པཎ་ཆེན་བློ་གྲོས་རྒྱལ་མཚན། does not mention precious human rebirth in his raw summary ⚑.

### English translation

Just as lightning flashes momentarily amidst the dark clouds of a pitch-black night, the *virtuous mind* (དགེ་བའི་བློ་གྲོས་, *dge ba'i blo gros*) arises only occasionally and is extremely weak, whereas negativity and non-virtue are exceedingly powerful; however, *Bodhicitta* (བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་, *byang chub kyi sems*—the mind of enlightenment) prevents and overcomes those negative deeds that ordinary virtues cannot conquer.

The moment Bodhicitta is generated, even those wretched beings bound in the prison of *samsara* (འཁོར་བ་, *'khor ba*—cyclic existence) undergo a transformation of name and status, becoming known as the "Children of the Sugatas" (བདེ་གཤེགས་ཀྱི་སྲས་, *bde gshegs kyi sras*—Buddhas' heirs) and becoming worthy of homage and offerings by gods and humans alike.

Regarding the praise of its benefits through analogies: it is like an alchemical elixir (གསེར་འགྱུར་རྩི་, *gser 'gyur rtsi*) because it transforms this naturally impure body into the precious body of a Buddha; it is like a wish-fulfilling jewel (ཡིད་བཞིན་གྱི་ནོར་བུ་, *yid bzhin gyi nor bu*) because it dispels the destitution and suffering of beings; and while all other virtues are like a plantain tree (ཆུ་ཤིང་, *chu shing*) which is exhausted after bearing fruit once, the tree of Bodhicitta is like a wish-fulfilling tree (དཔག་བསམ་གྱི་ཤིང་, *dpag bsam gyi shing*) whose fruit is never exhausted and continuously increases.

When Bodhicitta is divided, there are the two aspects of *Aspiring Bodhicitta* (སྨོན་པའི་སེམས་, *smon pa'i sems*) and *Engaging Bodhicitta* (འཇུག་པའི་སེམས་, *'jug pa'i sems*), which are analogous to the difference between wishing to go to a destination and actually traveling there. Although great fruits arise in samsara from the aspiring mind, a continuous flow of merit does not occur; however, from the moment one formally adopts the engaging mind with an irreversible resolve to free the infinite realms of sentient beings, a continuous stream of merit equal to space arises uninterruptedly even when one is asleep or heedless, as taught in the *Subāhuparipṛcchā Sūtra* (ལག་བཟངས་ཀྱིས་ཞུས་པའི་མདོ་, *lag bzangs kyis zhus pa'i mdo*).

Such an altruistic attitude, which desires to dispel the immeasurable suffering of every single sentient being and establish them in the immeasurable qualities of Buddhahood, is an unprecedented and wondrous marvel that has never before been dreamed of—even in dreams or for their own self-interest—by parents, gods, sages, or Brahma.

Although sentient beings desire happiness, out of *ignorance* (གཏི་མུག་, *gti mug*) they destroy their own happiness as if it were an enemy and run directly into suffering; in contrast, Bodhicitta is the peerless spiritual friend and mass of merit that satisfies them with all happiness and cuts off all suffering.

If one generates a negative mind toward the Bodhisattva benefactor in whom precious Bodhicitta has been born, one will dwell in hell for aeons; however, if one generates a clear and faithful mind, its fruit will increase even further. Therefore, I pay homage and take refuge in the bodies of those in whom precious Bodhicitta is born.
