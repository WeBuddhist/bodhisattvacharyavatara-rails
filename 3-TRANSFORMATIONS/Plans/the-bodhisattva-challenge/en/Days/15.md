# Day 15 — Chapter 2 Opens: Offer Everything Beautiful

## Opening

You want to make an offering. But you look around and feel you have nothing.

No temple. No incense. No gift worthy enough.

Chapter 2 begins here. And Shantideva's first move surprises you: he does not start with confession. He starts with giving. Wide open hands. Everything he can find.

What he finds turns out to be the whole world.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.

> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.

> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

You have no physical offering to make. That is not a problem.

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen explains that these first verses have a purpose: you are preparing to take up bodhicitta. You need merit and good conditions first. So you begin by giving.

And what do you give? Everything that has no owner. Flowers in a field. Mountain streams. Forests. Jewels in the earth. Trees heavy with fruit.

These things belong to no one. So they are **yours to offer in your mind**.

Gyaltsab cites the *King of Meditative Stabilization Sutra*: the sutra says that flowers and jewels and everything else in all the world systems that no one has claimed — you can offer these. You hold them in your mind and present them to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.

This is not pretending. This is **the practice of mental offering**: your mind, open wide, gathering the beauty of the whole world and placing it before the Three Jewels.

Sazang Mati Panchen adds: this is how chapter 2 begins — not with confession first, but with giving. You build merit before you clear fault. Wide hands before empty hands.

## Aspiration

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.

> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice Challenge

**Your task today:**

At some point today, you will see something beautiful — a tree, water, open sky, a flower.

**Stop. Hold it in your mind for a moment. Offer it.**

Think: this belongs to no one. I give it to the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.

That is the practice from these verses.
