## Today's Verse

> If things happened by their own will,
> since no one desires suffering,
> no suffering would ever arise
> for any sentient being. ^6-34

> Through carelessness, I myself
> inflict harm on myself with thorns and such.
> To obtain women and such,
> I become greedy and fast. ^6-35

> Some hang themselves and jump into chasms.
> They eat poison and incompatible foods.
> By unwholesome actions,
> they inflict harm on themselves. ^6-36

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the thirty-fourth, thirty-fifth, and thirty-sixth verses of the sixth chapter. Some people, in the grip of anger, stop eating, jump from cliffs, drink poison, or hit their own heads. So they harm themselves too. When such a person harms us, was it really chosen with a clear mind?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable story, which is easy to understand and encouraging.

In Nalendra, in the land of Magadha, a householder did not let a guest from Kashmir, called Truth Awakening, sleep in his house. The guest became angry. He thought of driving the Sangha members out of the country and ruling alone. So he placed great hope in his own god. To receive a dream of the god, he lay on a great heap of thorns and suffered. In the dream, the god appeared before him. Then he got up from the thorns and went to the king. He said, "The Sangha members have already been driven out."

This story shows that people under the power of desire and anger lie on thorns, go hungry, and jump into chasms. In this way, they harm their own bodies too.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will not eat food that does not agree with my body, and I will not do anything that harms myself.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ People harm themselves by eating unsuitable food and by acting in ways that are not meritorious. So we must not be angry at a person who harms us. We must let compassion arise. This protects us, in this life and the next, from wrongdoing that harms us.
