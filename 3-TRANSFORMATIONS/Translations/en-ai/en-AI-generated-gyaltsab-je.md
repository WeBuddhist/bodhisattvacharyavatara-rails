# Chapter One: Explanation of the Benefits of Bodhicitta

### 1.1 Regarding the first, there are two parts: the explanation of the text of the chapter, and the title of the chapter.

### 1.2 Regarding the first, there are two parts: the advice that it is right to abandon negativity and accomplish virtue, and the detailed contemplation of the benefits of bodhicitta.

First: In this era of ours, where virtue is extremely weak and negativity is highly powerful, it is right to make effort in meditating on the remedy for negativity. For example, just as on a dark cloudy night, by the condition of a flash of lightning, forms are clearly shown for a mere instant; likewise, by the power of the Buddha, a virtuous mind that thinks to accomplish merit occasionally arises for just a moment among the worldly beings, but it does not arise continuously. For that reason, we always remain with extremely weak virtue, while the powerful negativity that produces rebirth in the bad migrations—resembling the darkness of the cloudy night—is extremely difficult to overcome, ^95

And since it produces great fear, it is extremely fierce. Knowing this, ^96

One must make effort to accomplish virtue and abandon negativity. ^97

The commentary explains "always remaining with weak virtue" in terms of time, ^98

And it is also explained for the purpose of understanding the occasion when virtue is of weak power and negativity is immense. ^99

Second [the detailed contemplation of the benefits of bodhicitta] has four parts: explaining the benefits of bodhicitta, identifying bodhicitta, the reasons why those benefits arise from it, and praising the individual who meditates on bodhicitta. ^100

### 1.3 First [explaining the benefits of bodhicitta] has three parts: being able to conquer all negativity and accomplish all virtue, obtaining the exceptional name and meaning, and explaining the benefits through analogies.

### 1.4 First [being able to conquer all negativity and accomplish all virtue] has three parts: conquering great negativity, being able to accomplish supreme bliss, and being able to accomplish whatever desired goals.

#### First: It is right to make effort in meditating on the two types of bodhicitta, because:

That fierce negativity which is the cause of rebirth in the hells and so forth cannot be overcome by any other virtue that is not the mind of perfect enlightenment, for nothing else has the power to overcome it. Although the ultimate generation of the mind has the capacity to abandon the seeds of obscuration, that is not the main subject of explanation here; rather, what is shown here is the benefit of the conventional generation of the mind, which purifies previously accumulated causes of bad migrations and cuts off future ones. ^104

#### Second: It is right to make effort in meditating on bodhicitta, because:

Having contemplated and thought about what is beneficial and the means for sentient beings over many countless eons, the Lords of Sages saw this very generation of bodhicitta as beneficial for sentient beings. This generation of the mind is like a seed that enables the infinite multitudes of sentient beings to easily obtain the supreme bliss through a joyful path, without depending on hardships such as plucking out one's hair. As it is said: "Going from happiness to happiness, ^106

What wise person would be discouraged?" ^107

Thus it is explained. Third: Since it is the supreme means to benefit oneself and all others, it is right to meditate on that very mind, because: ^108

Those who wish to overcome the hundreds of sufferings of their own existence by training the mind of a middling person, those who wish to clear all the unhappiness of all sentient beings by training the mind of a great person, and those who wish to enjoy hundreds of happinesses of high status and definite goodness, must generate this very bodhicitta, and must never abandon or let it degenerate. ^109

#### Second [obtaining the exceptional name and meaning]: It is right to make effort to generate bodhicitta, because:

If the aspiring and engaging bodhicitta is generated, from that very instant onward, one is called by the exceptional name "Sons of the Sugatas," and in meaning, one becomes an object of homage and devotion for worldly beings, including gods and humans. It is not only the Bodhisattvas dwelling on the great bhumis who possess those qualities; ^111

For even those wretched ones bound in the prison of cyclic existence by the force of karma and afflictions obtain those very benefits the moment they generate the mind. Third [explaining the benefits through analogies] has six parts: the analogy of transforming the low into the supreme, the analogy of being difficult to find and of great value, the analogy of the fruit being inexhaustible and increasing, the analogy of being able to protect from great fear, the analogy of easily conquering negativity, and how the benefits are taught in the Sutras. ^112

#### First [transforming the low into the supreme]: Having contemplated these benefits, one must firmly hold and not let degenerate that which is called bodhicitta, because:

Just as an ounce of the supreme gold-making elixir is able to transform a thousand ounces of iron into gold, likewise, this impure body, matching in cause and nature, is taken up and transformed by that very mind into the priceless, precious body of a Conqueror. ^114

This summarizes the benefits of generating the mind taught in the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra* using the analogy of a type of mercury called "gold-appearing" being able to transform a thousand ounces of iron into gold. ^115

#### Second [difficult to find and of great value]: Those who wish to be free from all the degradation of cyclic existence, the abode of wandering beings, must correctly generate the precious bodhicitta—which is like the king of wish-fulfilling jewels—and firmly hold it without letting it degenerate, because: ^116

Just as a wise merchant-captain guides merchants to the island of jewels, the peerless sole guide of wandering beings, the Buddha, who possesses immeasurable wisdom, thoroughly examined the supreme means to clear the poverty of wandering beings and saw this very bodhicitta as rare and of great benefit, hence of great value. Third [the fruit being inexhaustible and increasing]: Those who wish for virtue to be inexhaustible and to increase must also meditate on bodhicitta, because: ^117

All other virtues not embraced by bodhicitta are like a banana tree, which perishes after bearing fruit once, as its capacity to bear fruit again is completely exhausted; ^118

While the tree of bodhicitta, like a wish-granting tree, always bears fruit, is never exhausted, and increases more and more. As stated in the *Sūtra Requested by Inexhaustible Intelligence* (*Akṣayamatiparipṛcchā*): "Just as a drop of water fallen into the great ocean is not exhausted until the end of the eon, likewise, virtue dedicated to enlightenment is not exhausted until the attainment of the heart of enlightenment." ^119

#### Fourth [protecting from great fear]: Why should those conscientious ones who make effort in adopting virtue and avoiding negativity not rely on this meditation on bodhicitta? It is right to rely on it, because:

Even if one has committed extremely fierce negativity such as harming the Three Jewels or the immediate misdeeds, just as one is saved from the great fears of the road by relying on a brave, strong companion, likewise, by relying on bodhicitta, one is liberated from those fears of bad migrations in an instant. Fifth [easily conquering negativity]: Generating the mind possesses great capacity to conquer negativity, because: ^121

Just as the fire at the end of an eon that destroys the world completely burns the vessels below the first dhyāna, likewise, this generation of the mind completely burns great negativities that produce rebirth in the hells in a single instant. Since it is able to conquer even the capacity of karma that is definitely to be experienced, what need is there to mention indefinite karma? ^122

The great pioneers explain that even the ripening of karma that is definitely to be experienced is purified by confession endowed with the four opponent powers, and that what is taught as "definite to be experienced" is proved by many scriptures and reasonings to be so only if one does not meditate on the opponent class. ^123

#### Sixth [how the benefits are taught in the Sutras]: Those very benefits definitely arise from bodhicitta, because:

Those immeasurable benefits and praises of that mind were literally explained by the wise Maitreya, the Savior, to the young Bodhisattva Sudhana in the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*. From the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*: "Son of good family, bodhicitta is like the seed of all Buddhas' qualities; ^125

It is like a field because it makes all wholesome qualities of wandering beings grow; ^126

And so forth, up to: "It is like an excellent vase because it completely fulfills all intentions; ^127

It is like a short spear because it defeats the enemy of afflictions." ^128

Thus it is taught extensively. ^129

### 1.5 Second, identifying the essence of bodhicitta has three parts: the division of its essence, the explanation of the divisions by analogies, and the explanation of the distinction between the benefits of aspiring and engaging bodhicitta.

#### First [the division of its essence]: Generating the supreme mind of awakening, subject, should be known to be of two types when summarized from the viewpoint of its essence, because:

It is the aspiring mind of enlightenment focusing on enlightenment, and the engaging mind of enlightenment focusing on enlightenment. The definition of generating the Mahayana mind is: a mind associated with the aspiration to attain perfect enlightenment for the sake of others. ^132

When divided from the viewpoint of essence, there are two (aspiring and engaging); from the viewpoint of associates, twenty-two; and from the viewpoint of boundaries, four (aspiration, exceptional resolve, pure, etc.). ^133

The mere generation of the aspiring and engaging mind exists even before obtaining the exceptional path of accumulation, ^134

While the exceptional path of accumulation begins the path to Buddhahood over three countless eons, starting from when all classes of realizations of the one hundred and forty-four qualities taught in the *Abhisamayālaṅkāra* are resolved through study and contemplation, and one is first able to put them into practice. ^135

The assertion by some that there is no Mahayana generation of the mind in a Buddha is an extremely great misdeed, because: ^136

If there is no engaging generation of the mind, there would also be no engaging vows, and if so, one would have to assert that the Buddha lacks the Pratimoksha vows, Vidyadhara vows, and so forth, which would result in the complete cessation of vows. Furthermore, it also contradicts their own assertion that the ultimate mind of enlightenment is a generation of the mind. ^137

The assertion that the engaging vows and the engaging generation of the mind are contradictory, yet one must take the engaging vows separately from the ceremony of adopting the engaging generation of the mind, is a great misunderstanding. ^138

The conventional generation of the mind is established by focusing also on the conventional body of the Buddha, such as the Rupakaya, ^139

While the ultimate mind of enlightenment is that very mind which directly realizes the ultimate mode of existence of the Buddha. ^140

Many treatises explain this as the ultimate mind of enlightenment, but do not explain it as the Mahayana generation of the mind. ^141

The mere aspiring generation of the mind arises without depending on a ceremony, but the resolve never to abandon that mind until attaining Buddhahood—held by the secondary hook of commitment—is taught in the instructions of Lord Atiśa to be adopted through a ceremony, because: ^142

This is adopted by one who is able to train in the trainings of the aspiring mind, such as training in the causes for not letting the generation of the mind degenerate in this life, abandoning the four black deeds, and relying on the four white deeds to prevent separation from the generation of the mind in other lives. ^143

The method of obtaining that which has not been obtained and so forth should be known in detail from the *Great Stages of the Path of the Perfections* (*Lam rim chen mo*) composed by the venerable Lord Lozang Drakpa Palzangpo (Tsongkhapa). ^144

The method of obtaining the engaging generation of the mind and its trainings should be known in detail from the *Explanation of the Chapter on Ethics of the Bodhisattva Bhumi* (*Byang sa'i tshul khrims le'u'i rnam bshad*) composed by that very venerable Lord, and is not elaborated here for fear of being too wordy. ^145

#### Second [the explanation of the divisions by analogies]: Just as the mind that wishes to go to a certain place—even if remaining in bed and not associated with the physical action of going—is the thought that wants to go to that place,

While the thought that thinks of going to that place at the time when one is actually associated with the physical action of going, such as lifting and placing one's feet, is distinguished as the actual going; although both are equal in the thought of wanting to go, they are distinguished by whether or not they are associated with the physical action of going. Likewise, the wise should understand the distinction between the aspiring and engaging generation of the mind progressively, because: ^146

While both are equal in the thought of wanting to attain Buddhahood for the sake of others, they are distinguished by whether they do not depend on being associated with the actual practice of the perfections such as generosity, or whether they do depend on it. ^147

Since the assertions of the wise masters such as Kamalaśīla are similar, they are not elaborated here. ^148

### 1.6 Third [the distinction between the benefits of aspiring and engaging bodhicitta] has two parts: the benefits of the aspiring mind, and the benefits of the engaging mind.

#### First [the benefits of the aspiring mind]: As stated in the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra* regarding liberation through the life of Noble Maitreya: "Son of good family, it is like this: For example,

A diamond jewel, even if broken, outshines all exceptional golden ornaments, ^151

Does not lose the name of diamond jewel, and reverses all poverty. ^152

Son of good family, likewise, the diamond jewel of generating the mind of all-knowingness, even if devoid of effort, outshines all the golden ornaments of the qualities of Hearers and Solitary Realizers, ^153

Does not lose the name of a Bodhisattva, and reverses all the poverty of cyclic existence." ^154

Accordingly, although great fruits arise in cyclic existence from the aspiring mind of enlightenment, it does not produce a continuous, uninterrupted flow of merit in the way the engaging mind does. ^155

#### Second [the benefits of the engaging mind]: From whatever time onward, for the sake of liberating the infinite realms of sentient beings from cyclic existence and establishing them on the stage of Buddhahood, one correctly adopts the engaging mind with an irreversible attitude that does not turn back from the welfare of others, from that time onward, even if one is asleep or intoxicated, so that one is unmindful, a force of merit continuously arises, uninterrupted, in manifold ways, equal to space, because: ^156

The aspiring mind is merely the aspiration for the fruit, while the engaging mind is associated with accomplishing the complete causes of Buddhahood. ^157

### 1.7 Third, the reasons why those benefits arise from it has two parts: adducing scriptures, and proving by reasoning.

#### First [adducing scriptures]: That this engaging mind is reasonable and endowed with logic to possess those benefits was taught by the Tathāgata himself in the *Sūtra Requested by Subāhu* (*Subāhuparipṛcchā*), ^159

For the sake of those sentient beings of lesser aspiration who, having indefinite Hearer lineage, fear accumulating vast collections, turn their minds away from the Mahayana, and seek the enlightenment of Hearers; ^160

It was taught for the purpose of reversing their aspiration for the lesser and establishing them in the Mahayana. ^161

### 1.8 Second proof has two parts: the reason for obtaining the benefits from the aspiring mind, and the reason for obtaining them from the engaging mind.

First [the aspiring mind] has four parts: the greatness of the benefits due to the infinite function of accomplishing virtue, that there is no other beneficial mind superior to it, that such a beneficial mind has never arisen even for their own sake in other sentient beings, and summarizing with praise. ^163

#### First [the greatness of the benefits due to the infinite function of accomplishing virtue]: Even if one thinks to clear just a headache of sentient beings, that intention, laden with the wish to benefit, yields immeasurable merit.

What need is there to mention the infinite merit that arises when one wishes to clear the immeasurable unhappiness of each and every sentient being, and wishes to establish each and every sentient being in the immeasurable qualities of the Buddha? ^165

#### Second [no other beneficial mind superior to it]: Where in the world is there such a beneficial mind—whether it be a father or a mother wishing to benefit their child—or any other person wishing to accomplish the supreme bliss? Even the gods, the truth-speaking sages, or Brahmā who dwells in the four immeasurables do not possess such a beneficial mind. ^166

#### Third [never arisen even for their own sake in other sentient beings]: Since such a beneficial mind has never been dreamed of in the past even for their own sake by those sentient beings themselves, how could it actually arise for the sake of others? It is unprecedented. ^167

#### Fourth [summarizing with praise]: This precious, exceptional mind of the Bodhisattvas—the wish to attain Buddhahood for the purpose of accomplishing the happiness and discarding the suffering of all sentient beings, which has never arisen even for a single instant for their own sake in other sentient beings—has arisen as an unprecedented, marvelous wonder. Knowing this, others should also hold it as a wonder and make effort to generate it. ^168

### 1.9 Second proof [the engaging mind] has three parts: the actual benefits, its reasoning, and resolving doubts.

#### First [the actual benefits]: If the benefits of the aspiring mind are extremely great, the engaging mind associated with the actual practice of the path is far more exceptional, because:

Since it causes all wandering beings to obtain the happiness of high status and liberation, it is the cause of joy; and since it is the alchemical elixir that cures the sickness of suffering of all sentient beings, how can the merit of this mind—which is like a precious wish-fulfilling jewel—be measured? For it is infinite. As stated in the *Sūtra Requested by Jñānottara* (*Jñānottaraparipṛcchā*): "If the merit of bodhicitta ^171

Were to have a physical form, ^172

It would completely fill the sphere of space, and would exceed even that." ^173

Thus it is said. ^174

This should be known in detail as explained in the *Compendium of Trainings* (*Śikṣāsamuccaya*) by adducing the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*. ^175

#### Second [its reasoning]: Since even the mere beneficial thought of thinking "May I become a Buddha for the sake of saving all wandering beings" generates far more exceptional merit than making offerings to the Buddhas with whatever abundant offerings exist in tens of millions of Buddha-fields, as stated in the *King of Samādhi Sūtra* (*Samādhirāja*), what need is there to mention the infinite merit of striving with great effort in the practice of accomplishing the peerless happiness of each and every sentient beings without exception? ^176

#### Third [resolving doubts]: If one asks, "Since sentient beings themselves desire happiness and do not desire suffering, and by making effort they are able to obtain happiness and avoid suffering, are they not themselves skilled in adopting happiness and avoiding suffering? Why is it necessary for others to strive for their sake?" ^177

It is definitely necessary to strive, because: ^178

Although sentient beings have the wish to discard suffering, under the force of afflictions, they accomplish only the causes of suffering, thereby running directly towards the suffering of bad migrations and cyclic existence without end. Although they desire happiness, due to confusion and ignorance regarding its methods, they destroy their own happiness like an enemy, thereby entering the wrong path of adopting happiness and avoiding suffering. For that reason, for those sentient beings who are destitute of happiness and endowed with many sufferings due to being confused about the methods of happiness and suffering, satisfying them with all the happiness of high status and definite goodness, cutting off all suffering, and clearing the confusion of ignorance regarding adopting and discarding—how could there be any virtue equal to that mind which performs such functions? ^179

How could there be any other spiritual friend who brings such benefit, accomplishing all welfare and protecting from all harm? ^180

How could there be any other merit equal to that which points out all points of adopting and discarding without error? There is none. Therefore, one must make effort to generate the mind of enlightenment. ^181

Fourth [praising the individual who possesses it] has three parts: being worthy of praise because they act as friends unsolicited out of great compassion, that if one who accomplishes a minor benefit is praised, how much more is one who accomplishes all welfare and happiness worthy of praise, and being worthy of taking refuge in because they are the supreme field. ^182

#### First [being worthy of praise because they act as friends unsolicited]: It is right to praise and laud the Bodhisattvas, because:

Even one who merely repays a prior kindness in the world is praised as "one who is grateful." What need is there to mention the Bodhisattva who, unsolicited, out of great compassion, performs good deeds and accomplishes all welfare and happiness? They are indeed worthy of praise by gods and humans alike. ^184

#### Second [accomplishing all welfare and happiness is far more worthy of praise]: If the world honors a person as a great benefactor who gives a mere portion of ordinary food to a few wandering beings—such as a hundred—for a short time, in a disrespectful manner, satisfying them for only half a day, ^185

What need is there to describe how worthy of honor is the Bodhisattva who always and continuously, with a respectful manner, gives the peerless, supreme, uncontaminated bliss of the Sugatas to the infinite number of sentient beings, completely fulfilling all their mental hopes for a long duration? Regarding "always," the commentaries explain: "Even if space and wandering beings are exhausted, it is inexhaustible." Prajñākaramati (*Sher-'byung*) connects this with time, and Kṣemadeva (*Dge-lha*) connects this with the quality of the substance. Therefore, those who possess minds should be single-mindedly devoted to the Bodhisattvas. ^186

Third [being worthy of taking refuge in because they are the supreme field] has three parts: the reason why it is not right to disparage a Bodhisattva even slightly, the reason why it is right to generate faith, and therefore being worthy of paying homage and taking refuge in. ^187

#### First [not right to disparage even slightly]: Towards such a Son of the Conqueror who is the ultimate benefactor giving all welfare and happiness,

If anyone generates an evil mind wishing to disparage or harm them, that is the ultimate of misdeeds, because: ^189

If one generates an evil mind, as stated in the *Sūtra of the Utterly Peaceful and Certain Manifestation* (*Praśāntaviniścayaprātihāryasūtra*): "Mañjuśrī, for as many moments of anger and contempt as a Bodhisattva generates towards another Bodhisattva, for that many eons he must don the armor of remaining in the hells." ^190

Thus, as the Sage taught that one will remain in hell for as many eons as the number of moments of generating an evil mind, one must be extremely careful regarding this point, because: ^191

As stated in the *Sūtra of the Seal of Developing the Power of Faith* (*Śraddhābalādhānāvatāramudrāsūtra*), generating a harmful mind towards a Bodhisattva who is devoted to the Great Vehicle produces far more immeasurable negativity than destroying or burning as many stupas as the sands of the River Ganges. The reason for this is taught to be that the Buddhas are born from the Bodhisattvas; hence, harming the cause of the Buddhas is the ultimate of misdeeds, as taught in many scriptures and commentaries. ^192

#### Second [right to generate faith]: However, if any individual generates clear faith and devotion towards a Bodhisattva:

The fruit of that faith grows and increases far more than the fruit of the previously explained negativity, because: ^194

One will experience exceptional fruits for far more eons than the number of moments of generating that clear faith, ^195

As stated in the *Sūtra of the Seal of Entering Certainty and Uncertainty* (*Niyatāniyatāgatarājamudrāsūtra*). Third [worthy of paying homage and taking refuge in]: Even when great adversity or suffering is inflicted upon those great Sons of the Conquerors, sinful minds such as anger do not arise in them even at the cost of their lives; rather, their virtue naturally increases. According to the commentary, it is explained that because thoughts such as "there is no purpose for me to remain in anger" arise, anger and so forth do not arise in the continuum of the Bodhisattvas even under great pressure. ^196

The meaning is that even at the cost of their lives, the Bodhisattvas do not commit misdeeds, and their virtue naturally increases. ^197

For that reason, I bow down with body, speech, and mind to the body of the individual in whom that precious, supreme mind has arisen. And just as explained in the Chapter on Patience and in the *Jātaka* of King Maitrībala, where he established the five yakṣas on the noble stage of liberation even though they drank the blood of his body, I go for refuge to the Bodhisattva who, even when harmed, does not return the harm but solely produces happiness, being the source of bliss. In brief, since generating the mind is the sole gateway to enter the Mahayana, whether entering the path of Mantra or the Perfections, one must make effort to generate that mind through various means, because: ^198

To generate that mind, as explained before, one must first meditate on the benefits of generating the mind to develop a sincere enthusiasm for those benefits, and perform the seven-limb practice including refuge, as taught in this supreme treatise pointing out the stages of the Bodhisattva path and in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*. ^199

The benefits thus explained can be summarized into two: temporary and ultimate benefits. ^200

The first also has two: not falling into bad migrations, and being born in good migrations, because: ^201

If the generation of the mind is born, many previously accumulated causes of bad migrations are purified, future ones are prevented from being accumulated, previously accumulated causes of good migrations are embraced by it and vastly increase, and newly accumulated ones are motivated by it and become inexhaustible. ^202

Ultimate liberation and omniscience are also easily accomplished by relying on that mind. However, before that, if one lacks a genuine, uncontrived aspiration to obtain the temporary and ultimate benefits, even if one says "Since those benefits arise from generating the mind, I must make effort to generate it," it will remain mere words, as is extremely clear when examining one's own continuum. ^203

To generate the aspiration to obtain the two benefits of high status and definite goodness, one must train the minds of the small and middling persons, and then enter the meditation on the conventional bodhicitta which has love and compassion as its root. ^204

For those wise ones who have obtained the human body of leisure and fortune, diligently meditating on the two bodhicittas is the supreme way to take the essence of this support of leisure and fortune. Therefore, first diligently meditate on the benefits of that mind. ^205

This is the summarizing verse. ^206
