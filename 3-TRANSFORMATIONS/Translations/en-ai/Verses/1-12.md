## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three commentators agree that Verse 1-12 presents a powerful contrast between ordinary virtues and the virtue of the mind of enlightenment (bodhicitta) through the analogies of the banana tree (plantain tree) and the wish-fulfilling tree (wish-granting tree). This verse serves to encourage practitioners to cultivate bodhicitta so that their roots of virtue become inexhaustible and continuously grow.

### 1. The Analogy of the Banana Tree (Ordinary Virtues)
*   **Consensus on Exhaustion:** All three commentators explain that virtues not embraced or held by bodhicitta are like a banana tree. Just as a banana tree dies or decays immediately after bearing fruit once, ordinary virtues are completely exhausted once they yield their respective results.
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen's Detailed Breakdown:** Sazang Mati Panchen provides a precise scholastic distinction regarding the types of virtues that are exhausted. He notes that both "ordinary and transcendent virtues" that are not embraced by bodhicitta are subject to this limitation. Specifically:
    *   *Ordinary/Worldly Virtues:* These are exhausted after producing the maturation of worldly merit (temporary samsaric happiness).
    *   *Transcendent Virtues:* The untainted virtues of Hearers (Śrāvakas) and Solitary Realizers (Pratyekabuddhas) are eventually exhausted upon their entry into remainderless nirvana (*anupadhiśeṣa-nirvāṇa*).
*   **Gyaltsab Je and Ngulchu Thokme:** Gyaltsab Je and Ngulchu Thokme similarly emphasize that without being grasped or held by the mind of enlightenment, the capacity of any virtue to bear fruit again is completely spent upon its first maturation.

### 2. The Analogy of the Wish-Fulfilling Tree (Virtues Embraced by Bodhicitta)
*   **Consensus on Inexhaustibility:** In contrast to the banana tree, the tree of the mind of enlightenment acts like a wish-fulfilling (or wish-granting) tree. It continuously bears fruit, is never exhausted, and only increases more and more.
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen's Explanation of the Fruit:** Sazang Mati Panchen clarifies that virtue embraced by the mind of enlightenment and conjoined with the wisdom of dedicating virtuous roots yields continuous results from cyclic existence up to final nirvana. It constantly bears:
    *   *Temporary Fruit:* High rebirth and exceptional worldly happiness, such as that of a universal monarch (*cakravartin*).
    *   *Ultimate Fruit:* The ultimate qualities of omniscience (complete Buddhahood).
    These fruits are never exhausted; rather, they multiply and flourish.
*   **Gyaltsab Je and Ngulchu Thokme:** Both commentators echo this point, stating that the wish-granting tree of bodhicitta perpetually yields results and thrives inexhaustibly.

### 3. Scriptural Proofs and Supporting Analogies
The commentators cite different Mahayana sūtras to provide scriptural authority for this doctrine:
*   **The Ocean Drop Analogy (*Akṣayamati Sūtra*):** Gyaltsab Je cites the *Akṣayamati Sūtra* to illustrate the inexhaustible nature of dedicated virtue:
    > "Just as a drop of water that falls into the great ocean is not exhausted until the end of the eon, likewise, virtue dedicated to Enlightenment is not exhausted until one reaches the heart of Enlightenment."
*   **The Four Elements Analogy (*Jewel Casket Sūtra*):** Both Sazang Mati Panchen and Ngulchu Thokme cite the *Jewel Casket Sūtra* (*Sūtra of the Casket of Jewels*) to explain how bodhicitta supports and multiplies virtue:
    > "Mañjuśrī, for example, various [incense] trees grow and increase because they are supported by the four elements. Mañjuśrī, likewise, when virtuous roots are supported by the mind of perfect enlightenment and dedicated to omniscience, they grow and increase."
    This shared citation highlights the consensus between Sazang Mati Panchen and Ngulchu Thokme on the mechanism of bodhicitta as the ultimate supportive environment (like the four elements) that ensures the continuous growth of all virtuous roots.
