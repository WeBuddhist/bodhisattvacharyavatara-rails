## Today's Verse

> If one is unable to do so due to fear,
> celebrations, or similar circumstances, one may do as one pleases.
> For example, at times of giving,
> it is taught that one should be indifferent to discipline. ^5-42

> Whatever one has decided to do,
> one should not think of anything else.
> With a mind focused on that,
> one should accomplish that very thing for the time being. ^5-43

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the forty-second and forty-third verses of the fifth chapter. This teaching clearly shows that once we begin a task, we should not let our mind wander elsewhere. We should first bring that very task to completion.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

Based on the literal meaning of these two verses, we can explain as follows.

In times of danger or celebration, the verse tells us that it is permissible to relax a minor point of discipline for the sake of generosity. It also tells us that once we begin a task, we should not let our mind wander elsewhere before finishing it. We should complete that very task first.

So whatever task we do, we should match it to the situation, and we should bring the task we have begun to completion.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will focus my mind single-pointedly on one task. I will not take up any other task until I have finished it.

**Explanation:** _(Diligence Practice)_ Until we finish a task we have begun, we should focus on that task alone, without letting the mind wander. If we do this, that one task will be completed well, and we will be free from the distraction and mistakes that come from wanting to do many things at once.
