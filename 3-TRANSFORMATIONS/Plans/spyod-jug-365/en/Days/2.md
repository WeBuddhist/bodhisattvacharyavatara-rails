# Day 2 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: The Benefits of the Mind of Enlightenment — Verses 1-4 to 1-6

---

### 1. Refuge 

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Appreciating the Preciousness of Life (Extracting Essence):** Reflecting on the eighteen qualities of leisure and opportunity (*kṣaṇa-sampad*) and its extreme rarity inspires us to use our precious human birth for spiritual practice. It helps us overcome procrastination and laziness, motivating us to accomplish the high goals of high rebirth (*mngon mtho*) and definitive goodness (*nges legs*).
*   **Recognizing the Rarity of Virtuous Thoughts (Mindfulness of Grace):** Understanding that our fleeting virtuous thoughts are like a lightning flash in a dark night—rare and inspired by the Buddhas' blessings—helps us cherish every virtuous impulse. It instills deep gratitude and humility, prompting us to actively protect and grow these positive states.
*   **Acquiring the Ultimate Shield (Bodhicitta Cultivation):** Realizing that ordinary virtue is too weak to withstand the massive, dark force of negative karma and afflictions drives us to seek the only ultimate remedy: perfect Bodhicitta. This protective mind, endowed with emptiness and compassion, serves as our ultimate shield against falling into lower realms.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-4**

> དལ་འབྱོར་འདི་ནི་རྙེད་པར་ཤིན་ཏུ་དཀའ། །
> སྐྱེས་བུའི་དོན་སྒྲུབ་ཐོབ་པར་གྱུར་པ་ལ། །
> གལ་ཏེ་འདི་ལ་ཕན་པ་མ་བསྒྲུབས་ན། །
> ཕྱིས་འདི་ཡང་དག་འབྱོར་པར་ག་ལ་འགྱུར། ། ^1-4
> 
> *This precious human life of leisure and opportunity is extremely difficult to find. Now I have this chance to accomplish my goals. If I do not use it to do good, how will I ever find such an opportunity again?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-4**
This verse marks the beginning of the actual explanation of the stages of the path, urging the practitioner to extract the essence of a precious human life of leisure and opportunity (*kṣaṇa-sampad* / *དལ་འབྱོར།*). The commentators organize their explanations around three major thematic lines:
1.  **The Definition and Structure of Leisure and Opportunity:** All commentators agree that this precious support consists of eighteen distinct qualities: the eight freedoms (leisures / *དལ་བ།*) and the ten endowments (fortunes / *འབྱོར་པ།*). The eight freedoms refer to being free from the eight non-leisures (such as birth in lower realms, holding wrong views, or being born in an era without a Buddha). The ten endowments are divided into five personal fortunes (e.g., being human, having intact faculties) and five circumstantial fortunes (e.g., a Buddha has appeared, he teaches the Dharma), both sourced from the classical *Śrāvakabhūmi*.
2.  **The Extreme Difficulty of Obtainment:** The commentators emphasize that this eighteen-fold support is exceptionally rare because the causes required to secure it—ethical discipline as the foundation, generosity as a companion, and stainless aspiration as the linking connection—are incredibly difficult to cultivate. To illustrate this rarity, Gyaltsab Je and Ngulchu Thokme cite the famous analogy of the blind turtle rising from the vast ocean once every hundred years and putting its neck through a single drifting wooden yoke.
3.  **The Ultimate Purpose:** Having obtained this rare support, its primary purpose is to accomplish the goals of a person (*puruṣārtha* / *སྐྱེས་བུའི་དོན།*), namely temporary high rebirth (*mngon mtho*) and ultimate definitive goodness (*nges legs*). The commentators warn that because we mostly reside in states of non-leisure, failing to practice the Dharma now will make it virtually impossible to obtain such a perfect support again in future lifetimes.

#### **Verse 1-5**
> ཇི་ལྟར་མཚན་མོ་མུན་ནག་སྤྲིན་རུམ་ན། །
> གློག་འགྱུ་སྐད་ཅིག་རབ་སྣང་སྟོན་པ་ལྟར། །
> དེ་བཞིན་སངས་རྒྱས་མཐུ་ཡིས་བརྒྱ་ལམ་ན། །
> འཇིག་རྟེན་བསོད་ནམས་བློ་གྲོས་ཐང་འགའ་འབྱུང་། ། ^1-5
> 
> *A flash of lightning on a dark, cloudy night reveals everything for a single instant. Similarly, the power of the Buddha occasionally inspires a brief moment of virtue in this world.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-5**
The commentators use the lightning flash metaphor (*vidyut* / *གློག་འགྱུ།*) to illustrate the extreme rarity, brevity, and weakness of ordinary virtuous thoughts in comparison to the overwhelming force of negative actions in cyclic existence. They establish three major points:
1.  **The Metaphor of the Lightning Flash:** A flash of lightning on a pitch-black, cloudy night represents the momentary and non-continuous arising of a virtuous mind (*dge ba'i blo gros*). The dark, cloudy night represents the pervasive suffering and powerful negativity that traps worldly beings in cyclic existence, while the lightning flash represents a transient moment of virtuous intelligence (such as the thought, "I must engage in the holy Dharma").
2.  **The Power of the Buddha:** The commentators share the consensus that worldly beings, left to their own devices, are incapable of generating continuous virtue. The occasional arising of a virtuous mind is entirely inspired and illuminated by the compassion, blessing, and power of the Buddhas (*buddhānubhāva* / *སངས་རྒྱས་མཐུ།*).
3.  **The Need for Bodhicitta:** Because ordinary virtue is weak, transient, and easily exhausted, it is utterly insufficient to overcome the powerful, dark force of negativity. This serves as a call to action to cultivate and firmly maintain the ultimate remedy—the precious mind of enlightenment—and never let it decline.

#### **Verse 1-6**
> དེ་ལྟས་དགེ་བ་ཉམ་ཆུང་ཉིད་ལ་རཏག །
> སྡིག་པ་སྟོབས་ཆེན་ཤིན་ཏུ་མི་བཟད་པ། །
> དེ་ནི་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མིན་པ། །
> དགེ་གཞན་གང་གིས་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་པར་འགྱུར། ། ^1-6
> 
> *Therefore, ordinary virtue is always extremely weak. Negativity is immensely powerful and unbearable. Except for the perfect mind of enlightenment, what other virtue can overcome such great evil?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-6**
This verse contrasts the extreme weakness of ordinary worldly virtue with the immense, unbearable power of negativity, establishing the mind of enlightenment (Bodhicitta) as the only force capable of overcoming such negativity.
1.  **The Weakness of Virtue vs. the Power of Negativity:** Ordinary virtue is inherently feeble because it arises only occasionally and lacks continuity. In contrast, negative actions are like the thick, dense darkness of a cloudy night—pervasive, easily gathered, and possessing great power to produce the fierce, unendurable sufferings of the lower realms.
2.  **Bodhicitta as the Sole Remedy:** All three commentators reach the consensus that only perfect Bodhicitta can conquer and neutralize this massive negativity. Sazang Mati Panchen defines this perfect mind as that which is "endowed with the essence of emptiness and compassion."
3.  **Doctrinal and Scriptural Support:** Gyaltsab Je introduces a key distinction: while the ultimate mind of enlightenment directly realizes emptiness and abandons the seeds of obscuration, what is primarily praised in this verse is conventional Bodhicitta, which has the power to purify previously accumulated causes of bad migrations and completely cut off future ones. The commentators cite powerful scriptural analogies to prove this, such as the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra* comparing the eyes of the precious mind of perfect enlightenment to the eyes of a cat, which instantly overcome the "mice" of karma and afflictions.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Cherishing Leisure and Opportunity:** Today, take five minutes to reflect on your freedom from the "eight non-leisures" (such as living in a place of war, severe physical or mental incapacity, or lacking access to spiritual teachings). Generate a feeling of deep gratitude for your current circumstances, and resolve not to waste this day on trivial distractions.
*   **2. Cherishing Fleeting Virtuous Thoughts:** Pay close attention to your mind today. Whenever a positive impulse arises—such as a desire to help someone, a feeling of compassion, or a thought of generosity—recognize it as a precious, fragile spark (like a lightning flash). Act on it immediately before it is swept away by habitual negative or neutral thoughts.
*   **3. Recognizing Our Limitations and Seeking Refuge:** When facing difficult situations or negative emotions today, acknowledge that ordinary willpower is often too weak to overcome deep-seated habits. Instead of fighting them with raw effort, mentally take refuge in a higher source of wisdom (the Buddhas) or connect with the vast, compassionate motivation of Bodhicitta to elevate your mind.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Leisure and Opportunity (དལ་འབྱོར། / *kṣaṇa-sampad*):** The eighteen-fold precious human support consisting of eight freedoms (leisures) from unfavorable states and ten endowments (fortunes) that enable spiritual practice.
*   **The Purpose of a Person (སྐྱེས་བུའི་དོན། / *puruṣārtha*):** The ultimate goals of human existence, traditionally divided into temporary high rebirth (*mngon mtho*) and ultimate definitive goodness (*nges legs*), which is complete liberation and Buddhahood.
*   **The Power of the Buddha (སངས་རྒྱས་མཐུ། / *buddhānubhāva*):** The inspiring influence and blessings of the enlightened beings, which is the sole source of any virtuous thoughts arising in ordinary worldly beings.
*   **Great Negativity (སྡིག་པ་སྟོབས་ཆེན། / *mahā-pāpa*):** The heavy, powerful negative karma and afflictions that dominate cyclic existence, likened to the thick darkness of a cloudy night, which can only be vanquished by Bodhicitta.
