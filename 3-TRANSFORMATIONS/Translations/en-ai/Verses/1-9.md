## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three commentators agree on the core meaning of Verse 1-9: it demonstrates the immediate, dual transformation of name and status (meaning) that occurs the very instant a samsaric being generates the supreme mind of enlightenment (*bodhicitta*). Even the most wretched beings, bound in the prison of cyclic existence by karma and afflictions, are instantly elevated to become "heirs of the Blissful Ones" (Bodhisattvas) and objects of veneration for gods and humans alike.

### 1. The Dual Transformation: Name and Status (Meaning)
- **Consensus on Dual Shift:** Both Sazang Mati Panchen and Ngulchu Thokme explicitly structure their explanations around a dual transformation: a "change of name" (*ming gyur ba*) and a "change of meaning or status" (*don gyur ba*).
- **The Transformation of Name:** The commentators explain that upon generating this mind, the being is called by the exceptional name "Heir of the Blissful Ones" (*sugatasuta* / *bde gshegs sras*) or "Hero of Enlightenment" (*bodhisattva*).
- **The Transformation of Status (Meaning):** This is not a mere honorary title; it carries an actual change in spiritual status. The being immediately becomes an exceptionally distinguished field of merit, worthy of homage, offerings, prostration, and respect (*vandanīya*) by all worldly beings, including gods, humans, and heavenly beings (*sanamaraloka*). Gyaltsab Je describes this as obtaining the "exceptional meaning" of that name.

### 2. The Instantaneous Nature of the Elevation
- **Immediate Effect:** All three commentators emphasize the word "instant" (*kṣaṇena* / *skad cig gis*). There is no gradual transition or delay. 
- **Gyaltsab Je's Clarification:** Gyaltsab Je specifies that this elevation occurs "the moment the aspiring and engaging mind of enlightenment is generated, in that very instant." 
- **Universal Application:** Gyaltsab Je adds a crucial scholastic point: this immediate elevation is not exclusive to high-level Bodhisattvas who have attained the "great grounds" (the ten *bhūmis*, i.e., noble/Arya Bodhisattvas). Even ordinary, wretched beings who generate the conventional mind of enlightenment for the very first time receive this name and status.

### 3. The Paradox of the Samsaric Prison
- **Existential Boundness vs. Spiritual Nobility:** The commentators highlight the stark contrast between the being's current physical/existential reality and their newly acquired spiritual nobility.
- **Sazang's Description:** Sazang describes them as "previously tormented by various sufferings, bound by the fetters of karma and mental afflictions in the prison of cyclic existence."
- **Thokme's Description:** Thokme Zangpo similarly describes them as "wretched beings bound by the noose of karma and afflictions in the prison of cyclic existence."
- **Gyaltsab Je's Description:** Gyaltsab Je refers to them as "wretched beings bound in the prison of the cycle of existence under the power of karma and afflictions."
- **Consensus:** Despite being physically and karmically bound in the prison of samsara (*bhavacāraka*), their spiritual status is immediately elevated above the gods of the desire and form realms, making them worthy of veneration by those very gods.

### 4. Structural Classifications and Headings
- **Thokme's "Transforming Name and Meaning":** Thokme Zangpo places this verse under the structural heading of "Fourth, transforming name and meaning," treating it as the fourth of the five common benefits of the mind of enlightenment.
- **Sazang's "How One Becomes an Heir":** Sazang introduces this section under "Second, how one becomes an heir of the Victorious Ones," using it as a logical proof of how great benefits are obtained.
- **Gyaltsab Je's "Obtaining an Exceptional Name and Meaning":** Gyaltsab Je places this under the sub-heading of "obtaining an exceptional name and meaning" within his overall outline of the benefits of the mind of enlightenment.
