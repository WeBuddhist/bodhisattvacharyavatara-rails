## Traditional Interpretation

### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Palden (Tibetan)

Verse 1-10 closes the benefits-of-bodhicitta section with a vivid alchemical image and a direct instruction. The simile: just as the supreme alchemical elixir (*gser 'gyur rtsi yi rnam pa mchog*) — specifically the mercury-type elixir (*dngul chu'i rigs gser du snang ba*) that can transform a single measure of iron into a thousand measures of fine gold (*lcags srang stong gser du bsgyur*) — so too, bodhicitta transforms this impure body (*mi gtsang lus*, composed of filth, bile, pus, blood) into the priceless, inexhaustible form of the Victors (*rgyal ba'i sku rin chen rin thang med par bsgyur bar byed pa*). The conclusion and instruction: "Therefore, this bodhicitta — grasp it firmly and unwaveringly (*rab tu brtan par gzung shig*), without letting it waver." KKP cites the Gandavyuha (attributed to Maitreya's liberation chapter): the mercury-essence of bodhicitta dedicated to omniscience purifies all the iron of karma and klesha obscurations and transforms all dharmas into the gold of omniscience — and this mercury-essence alone, unlike ordinary mercury, can never be exhausted or defeated by all the karma and kleshas combined.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-10)

### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Tibetan)

GDR reads verse 1-10 as the third benefit of bodhicitta, presented through the priceless elixir metaphor (*gser 'gyur gyi dpe*). Just as the supreme grade of alchemical elixir (*dngul chu'i rigs gser du snang ba = mercury-type that transforms to gold*) transforms even base iron to priceless fine gold with a tiny amount, so bodhicitta (the elixir) transforms this inferior, impure body (*mi gtsang ba'i rang bzhin gyi lus dman pa*, which a śrāvaka would cast off and abandon) — grasped and held over many lifetimes without abandoning — into the priceless body of the Victors (*rgyal ba'i sku rin thang gzhal du med pa'i yon tan dang ldan pa = the wish-fulfilling jewel of the form-body with all marks and signs*). GDR's instruction: rather than abandoning this body as a śrāvaka would, dedicate it over lives to others, held by bodhicitta — then it becomes the buddhas' priceless body.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-10)

### ngulchu-thogmed — Ngulchu Thokme Sangpo (Tibetan)

NTS reads the verse concisely: like the supreme alchemical elixir (*gser 'gyur rtsi*) that transforms iron (*lcags*) to gold (*gser*), the precious bodhicitta transforms even this vile impure body — taken and held over many lifetimes — into the inexhaustible, priceless form of the Victors (*rgyal ba'i sku rin thang med pa*). The instruction is clear and decisive: grasp bodhicitta firmly and steadfastly, without wavering (*rab tu brtan par gzung*), now that one has seen its qualities and benefits. NTS also references the Gandavyuha's account of how the mercury-essence of bodhicitta, dedicated to omniscience, transforms all the iron of obscurations into gold.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-10)

### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Mi Nyag Kunzang Sönam (Tibetan)

MKS gives the fullest treatment. The alchemical elixir simile is unpacked in detail: "gser 'gyur rtsi" = the elixir that transforms to gold; "rnam pa mchog" = the supreme grade. MKS identifies the specific type: mercury-type (*dngul chu'i rigs gser du snang ba*), distinguished from other types of elixir. This supreme grade transforms even a single measure of iron into a thousand measures (*srang gcig lcags srang stong gser du bsgyur*) — i.e., one part elixir transforms iron one-thousandfold. By analogy, bodhicitta (the mercury-essence, *dngul chu'i bcud*) transforms this impure body (*mi gtsang ba'i rang bzhin gyi lus = the body composed of filth, phlegm, bile, pus, blood, etc.*) — rather than discarding it like the śrāvakas — into the priceless, inexhaustible body of the Victors (= fully enlightened buddha with two form-bodies, *rdzogs pa'i sangs rgyas kyi lha sku gnyis*). MKS quotes the Gandavyuha (Sdong po bkod pa) at length: "The mercury-essence dedicated to omniscience-mind, protected by the wisdom of dedicating virtue-roots to omniscience — it transforms all the iron of karma and klesha obscurations into the gold of omniscience — and this mercury-essence alone cannot be exhausted or defeated by all karma and kleshas combined." The verse's closing instruction: "Grasp this bodhicitta firmly, without wavering."
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-10)

### khenpo-kunga — Ngakwang Kunga Wangchuk (Tibetan)

NKW gives a clear, systematic reading: like the supreme alchemical elixir that transforms base iron to priceless gold (*srang gcig lcags srang stong gser du bsgyur*), bodhicitta transforms this impure body composed of filth into the priceless form of the Victors. The Sdong po bkod pa quotation NKW cites also notes: the light of sun and moon and the abundance of all material goods of gods and humans cannot match the value of the cintamaṇi jewel; similarly, all the virtue of gods, humans, śrāvakas, and pratyekabuddhas cannot match the value of bodhicitta. The verse closes with the instruction: having seen these qualities, grasp bodhicitta firmly and unwaveringly.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-10)

### Divergences

No substantive divergence on interpretation. The one notable difference: **what part of the Gandavyuha is cited**. KS, KKP, MKS, and NTS all cite the mercury-to-gold passage; NKW cites a different passage from the same sūtra comparing bodhicitta's value to the cintamaṇi surpassing all merit. ⚑ Both passages are from Sdong po bkod pa and are used to illustrate the same point; they are complementary, not contradictory.

## Word-by-word Disambiguation (ཚིག་དོན་གསལ་བཤད།)

- **གསེར་འགྱུར་རྩི་ཡི་རྣམ་པ་མཆོག་** — "the supreme type of gold-producing elixir": "gser 'gyur rtsi" = the elixir that transforms to gold (a classical Tibetan alchemical category). "rnam pa mchog" = the supreme grade/type. KS context: "shin tu cher sgyur bar byed pa'i mchog lta bu" — like the supreme [elixir] that transforms greatly. MKS identifies it as the mercury-type (*dngul chu'i rigs gser du snang ba*).
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-10) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-10)

- **མི་གཙང་བའི་རང་བཞིན་གྱི་ལུས་འདི་** — "this body of impure nature": "mi gtsang ba'i rang bzhin" = composed of impure substances (MKS: filth, phlegm, bile, pus, blood). The verse's point: even *this* body — not a special purified body but the very body composed of filth — is what bodhicitta transforms.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-10)

- **བླངས་ནས་** (blangs nas) — "having taken / having grasped": KS: "blangs te bzung nas" — taking and holding [the body] over many lifetimes for others' benefit, rather than discarding it like a śrāvaka. GDR specifies: "tshe rabs mang por gzhan don du dang du blangs nas" — joyfully accepting this body for many lifetimes for others' sake.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-10) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-10)

- **རྒྱལ་བའི་སྐུ་རིན་ཆེན་རིན་ཐང་མེད་པར་བསྒྱུར་བར་བྱེད་** — "transforms into the Victors' form, priceless": "rgyal ba'i sku" = the form of the Victors = the two form-bodies (*gzugs sku gnyis*) of a fully enlightened buddha. "rin thang med par" = priceless, immeasurable in value. The transformation: the worthless (base iron / impure body) → priceless (gold / Victors' form).
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-10)

- **རབ་བརྟན་གཟུང་** — "grasp firmly and steadfastly": the verse's final instruction. KS: "ji ltar blangs ba bzhin g.yo ba med par rab tu brtan par gzung shig" — just as [you have now] taken it, hold it without wavering, firmly and resolutely. This is the verse's practical conclusion: the 9-verse catalogue of bodhicitta's benefits terminates in an imperative.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-10)

- **གསེར་འགྱུར་རྩི་** (gser 'gyur rtsi = alchemical elixir / gold-producing elixir) — ལྕགས་ལ་སོགས་ཀི་རིགས་གསེར་དུ་བསྒྱུར་ནུས་པའི་རྩི་སྟེ། དངུལ་ཆུའི་རིགས་གསེར་དུ་སྣང་བ་མཆོག་སྲང་གཅིག་གིས་ལྕགས་སྲང་སྟོང་གསེར་བཟང་པོར་བསྒྱུར་ཐུབ་པའི་རྩི། དཔེར་བེད་སྤྱད་ལ་དཔེ་ཅན་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས།

- **རྒྱལ་བའི་སྐུ་** (rgyal ba'i sku = form of the Victors) — རྫོགས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གཟུགས་སྐུ་གཉིས་ (ལོངས་ལྡན་རྫོགས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་དང་སྤྲུལ་པའི་སྐུ). མཚན་དཔེ་ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པའི་ལུས། GDR: "yid bzhin gyi nor bu rin thang gzhal du med pa'i yon tan dang ldan pa" — possessing qualities immeasurable in value, like a wish-fulfilling jewel.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-10) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/རྒྱལ་བའི་སྐུ་_(victors-form).md]]

### འགྲེལ་པ་ནས་འབྱུང་བའི་གནད་ཚིག

- **དངུལ་ཆུའི་བཅུད་** (dngul chu'i bcud = mercury-essence) — MKS/KKP དངུལ་ཆུ་ = mercury; "bcud" = the essential/refined element. In the Gandavyuha analogy: bodhicitta = the mercury-essence dedicated to omniscience, which transforms the iron of obscurations into the gold of omniscience. The "essence" framing: not raw mercury but its refined quintessence — so too bodhicitta is not ordinary mental aspiration but the quintessence of the spiritual path.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-10)

- **ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ཉིད་དུ་བསྔོ་བ་** (dedication to omniscience) — KS/MKS: the alchemy only works when the mercury-bodhicitta is "dedicated to omniscience" (*thams cad mkhyen pa nyid du sems bskyed pa'i bcud kyis len*) and "protected by the wisdom of dedicating virtue-roots to omniscience" (*thams cad mkhyen pa nyid du bsngo ba'i ye shes kyis yongs su bzung ba*). Dedication (bsngo ba) is the mechanism that activates bodhicitta's transforming power; without it, the bodhicitta-mercury cannot transmute.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-10) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-10)

## Metaphors (དཔེ།)

- **གསེར་འགྱུར་རྩིའི་དཔེ་ (alchemical elixir)** → **མི་གཙང་ལུས་ = རྒྱལ་བའི་སྐུར་བསྒྱུར།** བཀོད་ལུགས། ལྕགས་ = མི་གཙང་བའི་ལུས; གསེར་འགྱུར་རྩི་ = བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས; གསེར་བཟང་པོ་ = རྒྱལ་བའི་སྐུ། མཚུངས་ཆོས་གཙོ་བོ། (①) ཆུང་ངུ་ཞིག་གིས་ (= སྲང་གཅིག་གིས་) ཆེ་ཆེན་པོར་བསྒྱུར་བར་བྱེད་; (②) དམན་པ་ (= ལྕགས་ / མི་གཙང་ལུས) ཆེས་མཐོན་པར་ (= གསེར་ / རྒྱལ་སྐུ) བསྒྱུར་བར་བྱེད།
