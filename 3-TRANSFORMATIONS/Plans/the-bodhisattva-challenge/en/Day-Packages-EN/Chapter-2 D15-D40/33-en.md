#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

"Therefore it is only right to take up the remedy at this very moment" — that is: therefore, to the victorious ones, who have become the protectors of limitless beings; who are always intent upon the purpose of protecting beings; whose capacity is unobstructed in every respect, and who therefore possess great power, the might that dispels every fear — to them I go for refuge from this very day.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Since in such a state there is no refuge able to protect, and regret arising then would come too late — for that reason: "buddha" means one in whom every fault is exhausted and every quality complete, one who is himself free of every fear; "victorious one" means one victorious over all that is adverse, the afflictive and the cognitive obscurations; and he is the protector of beings, one who has come under the sway of great loving compassion. As for "great power," he applies it to the Truth Body, as one's own purpose, giving "great power"; and to the Form Body, as the purpose of others, giving "dispels every fear," in the aspect of dispelling the fear of others' suffering. To such a buddha, "from this very day" — that is, from right now — "I shall go for refuge," he says. And as to the distinctive features of the object of refuge, he explains, following what the master Dignāga said, that a teacher who is free of fear himself, skilled in the means of liberating others from fear, who extends great compassion to all without near or far, and who acts for the benefit of all alike, whether they have benefited him or not — such a teacher is a teacher who does not deceive.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-47]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Second, the power of the support. Of the two senses of "the power of the support" — making something the object before which one confesses, and making it the support for purification — it is the latter here, that is, making it the support of refuge. Thus, since when one is dying and the welcoming party of the Lord of Death, the forms taken by one's own deluded bad karma, arrives, one is terrified and only then searches for a refuge and does not find one — therefore: the victorious one, the perfect buddha, is the protector of beings without exception; for the purpose of protecting all beings, as vast as space, from suffering entire, from the time he first generated the mind set on supreme awakening he strove for the welfare of others alone and brought it to completion; he is the great lord of the ten powers, the Buddha, the Blessed One. In particular, the actual power to protect all beings is this: possessing the ten powers of knowing, he perfectly teaches the sublime Dharma; and by accomplishing it oneself according to his word, every fear of the suffering of samsara and the lower realms and the rest is dispelled without exception. To him, not imitating those beings deluded about what to adopt and what to reject, who find it hard to be helped and put it off — "I, from this very day, until I reach the essence of awakening, go for refuge to the Buddha as my teacher": so one must think.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-47]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->
##### Zhenphen Jampé Gocha (annotation commentary)

In Zhenga's annotation commentary the underlying gloss is set out from the side of the annotation commentary itself, without any difference as to whether a full explanatory commentary is appended. It sets out in brief, in the underlying gloss, the manner in which — in that state where one has searched the four directions and found no refuge — it is therefore only right to go for refuge from this very moment to the victorious ones, who have become the protectors of limitless beings, who are always intent upon the purpose of protecting beings, and who possess unobstructed great power, the might that dispels every fear.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^2-47]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Since, when one is born in the lower realms, one searches for a refuge and does not find one — therefore, to the victorious one, the protector of beings, who through his application strives for the purpose of protecting all beings, and who, possessing a supreme might that protects from fear, has the great power able to dispel every fear: to him one should go for refuge quickly, from this very day.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^2-47]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Since at that time there is no refuge — therefore, to the victorious one, the protector of beings, who has brought to completion his intense striving for the purpose of protecting all beings, and whose capacity to protect is supreme, that is, great power: possessing those two, he dispels every fear; and to him I go for refuge from this very day.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^2-47]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

For the reason just explained, therefore: the victorious one, the perfect buddha, protector of beings without exception, possessed of the utmost diligence for the purpose of protecting beings entire, who by a great power of unobstructed capacity dispels every fear of suffering —

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-47]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

For the reason just explained — that at the time of actually being born in the lower realms, and once born, there is no refuge and no means whatever of averting it — therefore: the victorious one, victorious over the enemy of the four māras, who has become the single protector who is the trustworthy resort of the whole host of beings without partiality of near or far; who has brought to completion the diligence he undertook for the purpose of protecting from all harm those refugeless beings, whether they have benefited him or not; who, being himself free of every fear and possessing an unobstructed might able to protect others from fear, therefore has great power; and who, unasked, dispels the fear and suffering of others without exception — to that very unsurpassed refuge, the Jewel of the Buddha, without deferring to a later time, from this very day onward and until I reach the essence of awakening, I go for refuge. That such a supreme three are the unsurpassed refuge of those who wish for liberation is stated in many scriptures; and going for refuge to the Buddha and the rest is supreme among what liberates from every fear — and, applying it to the Dharma and the Sangha as well, it is said that one is liberated from the fear of the lower realms and set upon a support in the higher realms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-47]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Since, when the fear of the lower realms — the result of wrongdoing — falls upon one, there is no other refuge, for that very reason: to the victorious one, victorious in the battle with māra, the perfect buddha, the Blessed One, who has become the protector of beings, of sentient beings; who is intent upon the purpose of protecting beings from suffering both for the time being and ultimately; who possesses the great strength and capacity able to protect from suffering; and who is able to dispel every fear of the harms of samsara and of birth, aging, sickness and death — to that Jewel of the Buddha, from this very day, I go for refuge. Thus he requests refuge in the Jewel of the Buddha.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-47]]

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **kunpal:** he divides "the power of the support" into two — making something the object before which one confesses, and making it the support for purification — and explains that it is the latter here, expounding it by way of those two headings.
- **gyaltsab:** he divides "the power of the support" into three — relying on the Three Jewels; relying on the buddhas' heirs, who have the power of aspiration-prayer; and, having gone for refuge, accomplishing what has been enjoined — and explains it by way of those three.
- **tenzin-gyatso:** he divides "great power, dispelling every fear" into two — "great power" from the side of the Truth Body, as one's own purpose, and "dispelling every fear" from the side of the Form Body, as the purpose of others — which is unlike the manner of Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and others, who state "great power, dispelling every fear" as a single quality.

The meaning does not conflict, but since there are differences in how the headings of "the power of the support" are divided and in the sense given to "great power, dispelling every fear," it is noted separately.

#### Stories and Illustrations

<!-- story:BCAC19_KKP -->
##### BCAC19_KKP — The Account of Bodily Strength Told in the Sutra of Hurling the Stone

At the point of the phrase "great power, dispelling every fear," an example is given from among the categories of the Tathāgata's powers, namely the strength derived from father and mother. When the Tathāgata came to the city of Kuśi, the five hundred champions of that land, each with a retinue of five hundred, were sweeping and cleaning the road; and there was a great boulder which they could not lift to throw aside. The Tathāgata dislodged it with the big toe of his right foot and hurled it into the sky with his hand — that is the strength derived from father and mother. Then by miraculous power he reduced it to fine particles and scattered them in all directions; and when the champions became unhappy at this, he gathered the particles back together, restored the boulder as it had been, and set it down in an isolated place — this, it is said, is called the strength of meditation, or the strength of wisdom.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

1. **Since at the point of death there is no other refuge, one must go for refuge to the Buddha.** — All the commentaries agree in the end that, in a state where one has searched the four directions and found no refuge, it is only right to go for refuge from this very moment.
2. **The three qualities of the Buddha as object of refuge — protector of beings; intent upon the purpose of protecting; great power that dispels every fear.** — It is explained that the victorious one, the perfect buddha, is victorious in the battle with māra; that he strives to completion for the purpose of protecting all beings from suffering; and that, his might to protect being unobstructed, he possesses all three qualities, able to dispel every fear.
3. **The capacity to protect rests on the Buddha's ten powers of knowing.** — Kunzang Pelden cites the categories of the Tathāgata's powers (the strength derived from father and mother, the strength of miraculous power, the strength of wisdom and so on) and the scriptural passage on the ten powers of knowing, showing this through the account of his bodily strength.
4. **The reason why the object of refuge, the Buddha, has become an authoritative person.** — ⚑ Tenzin Gyatso cites the master Dignāga to explain that the Teacher first generated the compassion wishing to benefit beings and so gradually became an authoritative person; and he also divides "great power, dispelling every fear" into two, from the side of the Truth Body as one's own purpose and the Form Body as the purpose of others.
5. **Scripture shows that the Three Jewels are the unsurpassed refuge of those who wish for liberation.** — Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the *Questions of Vimaladattā* and the *Sūryagarbha Sutra* and others to show that going for refuge to the Buddha liberates one from the fear of the lower realms.
6. **Going for refuge is a commitment undertaken until one reaches the essence of awakening.** — Minyak Kunzang Sonam says that, without deferring to a later time, one goes for refuge to the Jewel of the Buddha from this very day onward until one reaches the essence of awakening; and Kunzang Pelden likewise says one must think, "from now until I reach the essence of awakening, I go for refuge to him as my teacher."

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse begins the going for refuge to the Buddha for the sake of confessing wrongdoing, by way of "the power of the support"; and, resting on that state in which one has searched the four directions and found no refuge, it states the commitment: "To the victorious one, the buddha, endowed with the three qualities — protector of beings, intent upon the purpose of protecting, and dispelling every fear by his great power — I go for refuge from this very moment."

**Key points.**

- All the commentaries explain in agreement that, knowing there is no other refuge at the time of death, it is only right to go for refuge from this very moment.
- The Buddha as object of refuge is explained as endowed with three qualities: protector of beings, intent upon the purpose of protecting, and great power that dispels every fear.
- Kunzang Pelden cites the categories of the Tathāgata's powers and the ten powers of knowing, showing this through the account of his bodily strength at the city of Kuśi.
- ⚑ Although Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and others state "great power, dispelling every fear" as a single quality, Tenzin Gyatso divides it in two, from the side of the Truth Body as one's own purpose and the Form Body as the purpose of others.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the *Questions of Vimaladattā* and the *Sūryagarbha Sutra* to show that the Three Jewels are the unsurpassed refuge of those who wish for liberation, and explains that going for refuge is a commitment to be maintained until one reaches the essence of awakening.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

The Jewel of the Dharma — the Dharma that the Buddha, in whom every fault is exhausted and every quality complete, has realized in his mind, or that he possesses in his mind, and which dispels the fears of samsara — and likewise the assemblies of bodhisattvas who are training in such a Dharma of scripture and realization: to these too, he says, I truly go for refuge, by way of having seen the reasons.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-48]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

When that Buddha, having gathered the two accumulations of merit and wisdom over countless aeons on the path of training, at last attained the wisdom that knows all aspects at the foot of the lord of trees, the bodhi tree — as it is said, "A Dharma like nectar have I attained: profound, peaceful, free of elaboration, luminous, uncompounded" — then all the profound and vast Dharma was realized in his mind, or known by him. Whichever of those Dharmas one puts into practice dispels, or pacifies, every fear of samsara without exception: to that Dharma of scripture and realization, in the manner of the path, I go for refuge from this very day. And to the assembly of bodhisattvas, the noble Sangha who do not turn back, likewise, from this very day, in the manner of companions, I go for refuge truly — that is, without pretence and without doubt.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-48]]

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^2-48]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->

Furthermore, to the Jewel of the Dharma that the victorious one realized in his mind — which, if relied upon, dispels the fears of samsara — and to the assembly of bodhisattvas who have attained the station of the noble ones as well, likewise I truly go for refuge; that is, one should undertake them as companions in accomplishing the path.

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

To him I go for refuge, from this very day onward until I reach the essence of awakening. And to whichever sublime Dharma that victorious one realized in his mind, which, when put into practice, dispels all the fears of samsara; and to the assembly of the Sangha of bodhisattvas, the noble ones who do not turn back, as well — just as one went for refuge to the Buddha, so likewise I go for refuge truly, that is, without pretence and without doubt, with supreme faith: and this is because they liberate one fully from fear.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-48]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

*(Part of a commentary that treats verses 47 and 48 together.)* To the Jewel of the Dharma — whichever sublime Dharma that victorious one realized in his mind and made manifest, which, when a disciple relies on it within his own being and puts it into practice, acts as the remedy for attachment and the other afflictions and dispels all the fears of samsara — and to the assembly of bodhisattvas who have made manifest the station of the noble ones, that is, the Jewel of the Sangha, as well: just as was said before, from this very day, with a truly correct faith of trust that has no pretence and no doubt, I go for refuge. That such a supreme three are the unsurpassed refuge of those who wish for liberation: thus going for refuge to the Buddha and the rest is supreme among what liberates from every fear, and it is said that one is liberated from the fear of the lower realms and set upon a support in the higher realms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-48]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

To whichever Jewel of the Dharma of scripture and realization that perfect buddha, the Blessed One, realized in his mind — that Dharma whose very nature is to dispel, without exception, every fear of samsara and the lower realms for beings within samsara — and, further, to the Jewel of the Sangha, the assembly of bodhisattvas, as well: just as, from this very day, one goes for refuge to the Jewel of the Buddha, so likewise to the Jewel of the Dharma and the Jewel of the Sangha I truly go for refuge. Thus he explains that going for refuge in reliance upon the Three Jewels is the power of the support.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-48]]

#### Stories and Illustrations

<!-- story:BCAC13_KTB -->
##### BCAC13_KTB — The Power of Refuge

At the point of the phrase "so likewise I truly go for refuge," a story is told as an example of how very great the benefit is if one goes for refuge from the depths of the heart. A young god of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three, his lifespan spent, saw the five signs of death appear; looking with clairvoyance, he understood that he would be born in Jambudvīpa, in Rājagṛha, as a piglet. Tormented in mind by fierce suffering, he cried out in lamentation. Indra, lord of the gods, saw him and asked what was wrong; and when he explained his situation, Indra had him take hold of going for refuge. Then, when he transferred from that life, he was born as a god in the realm of Tuṣita. Indra, not seeing him born anywhere at all — not in the three lower realms, nor in the human world or elsewhere — asked the Blessed One, who said that he had been born as a god in the realm of Tuṣita. The lord of the gods, marvelling, said: "Those who have gone to the Buddha for refuge / do not go to the lower realms; / having cast off the human body, / they obtain the body of a god." And it is explained in the same way, applying it to the Dharma and the Sangha as well.

#### Main Teaching Points

1. **This verse shows the going for refuge to the Jewel of the Dharma and the Jewel of the Sangha.** — All the commentaries explain in final agreement that, following the going for refuge to the Jewel of the Buddha in the preceding verse, here one likewise goes for refuge truly to both the Jewel of the Dharma and the Jewel of the Sangha.
2. **The sublime Dharma is the Dharma of scripture and realization realized in the victorious one's mind, which, being the remedy for the afflictions, dispels the fears of samsara.** — The Jewel of the Dharma is the Dharma of scripture and realization directly realized by the Buddha; and it is explained that when it is put into practice it acts as the remedy for attachment and the other afflictions and dispels all the fears of samsara.
3. **The Jewel of the Sangha is the assembly of bodhisattvas who have attained the station of the noble ones and do not turn back.** — The third object of refuge is settled as the noble assembly of bodhisattvas who have attained the grounds, the Sangha who do not turn back.
4. **The heart of going for refuge is to go truly, with a faith of trust free of pretence and doubt.** — It is explained that the sense of "truly" is going for refuge with a faith of trust that has no pretence and no doubt.
5. **Going for refuge to the Three Jewels is the unsurpassed refuge of those who wish for liberation, supreme among what liberates from every fear.** — Scripture is adduced as witness that if one goes for refuge to the Three Jewels one is liberated from the fear of the lower realms and set upon a support in the higher realms.
6. **If refuge is taken from the depths of the heart, the benefit is very great.** — The story of a young god, certain to be born as a piglet, who by the power of going for refuge was instead born as a god in Tuṣita, shows directly the great power of going for refuge.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Following the going for refuge to the Jewel of the Buddha in the preceding verse, this verse states that one likewise goes for refuge truly, without pretence and without doubt, to both the Jewel of the Dharma — the Dharma of scripture and realization realized in the victorious one's mind, which dispels the fears of samsara — and the Jewel of the Sangha, the assembly of bodhisattvas who have attained the station of the noble ones.

**Key points.**

- All the commentaries agree in the end that the main point here is going for refuge, from among the Three Jewels, to the Jewel of the Dharma and the Jewel of the Sangha.
- The Jewel of the Dharma is explained as the Dharma of scripture and realization realized in the Buddha's mind, which, being the remedy for the afflictions, dispels the fears of samsara.
- The Jewel of the Sangha is the assembly of bodhisattvas who have attained the station of the noble ones and do not turn back.
- Scripture such as the *Sūryagarbha* is adduced as witness that the benefit of going for refuge to the Three Jewels is liberation from the fear of the lower realms.
- The benefit of going for refuge from the depths of the heart is shown directly by the story of the young god who, certain to be born as a piglet, was instead born in Tuṣita by the power of going for refuge.
