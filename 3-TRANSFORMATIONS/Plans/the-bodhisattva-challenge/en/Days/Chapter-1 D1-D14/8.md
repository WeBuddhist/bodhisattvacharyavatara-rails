# Day 8 — Even wishing to cure a headache yields immeasurable merit

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Someone you know is struggling
> **Detail:** Even a small wish to help counts. Today, wish once, clearly, that they be free of it.

## Introduction

These two verses are a logical argument in two steps. Verse 21 takes the smallest imaginable act of goodwill — wishing to cure a headache — and says even that yields immeasurable merit. Verse 22 draws the conclusion: if that's true, what about the wish to free every being from all suffering and bring them every good quality?

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

*MIND TRAINING (recite 3 times)*

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in immeasurable equanimity, free from attachment and aversion.

*REFUGE (recite 3 times)*

> Until I reach the heart of awakening, I take refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha.

*TAKING THE BODHISATTVA VOW (recite 3 times)*

> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And trained step by step in the bodhisattva conduct,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And train step by step in that same conduct.

## From the Tradition

Sazang Mati Panchen illustrates verse 1.21 with a story. A woman named Maitrakanya had done something harmful to her mother and was reborn in a temporary hell, where a burning iron wheel revolved on her head. In the middle of that torment, a single thought arose: may all beings who would experience this kind of pain have it ripen on me instead. By the power of that thought alone, the wheel vanished. She died in that instant and was reborn in a celestial realm. Sazang cites this story to show that even one localized thought of wishing to take on another's pain — arising in the worst of circumstances — carries the power verse 1.21 is pointing at.

## Aspiration

> May the precious and supreme bodhicitta —
> Where it has not yet arisen, may it arise;

> But grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of this practice,
> May all wandering beings, without exception,
> Enter the path of the bodhisattva.

## Today's Practice Challenge

Sazang's story turns on a single genuine thought in a moment of real pain. Think of one specific person you know who is dealing with something hard — an illness, a loss, a situation they can't get out of. Just once, form the wish as clearly as you can: may this person be free from what they're going through. Don't evaluate whether you did it right. Verse 1.21 says the intention itself is what carries the weight.
