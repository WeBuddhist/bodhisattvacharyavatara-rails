## Today's Verse

> Wherever fish and other creatures
> are driven away so they are not killed,
> when the intention to desist is attained,
> it is taught as the perfection of discipline. ^5-11

> Unruly beings are like space;
> they cannot be overcome.
> If this single angry mind is conquered,
> it is like conquering all those enemies. ^5-12

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the eleventh and twelfth verses of the fifth chapter. Let us look together at this point: we do not need to fight enemies as numerous as space. By defeating only our own angry mind, we win a great victory.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a valuable teaching that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago, King Manicuda gave his finest elephant to a brahmin. That brahmin sold the elephant to a lesser rival of the king. The rival king then led an army, together with that same elephant, to attack King Manicuda. Even so, King Manicuda did not commit the wrongdoing of striking back in anger, for the sake of this one life. He never retaliated at all. He even gave his own kingdom to the one who had attacked him.

This shows us how to give up anger. We can practice patience without striking back at those who harm us.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, the moment anger arises in my mind, I will catch it with mindfulness.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ The unruly beings and enemies in this world are as vast and endless as space. We can never defeat and tame every outer enemy all at once. But if we defeat and calm only the anger in our own mind, we gain the same benefit as defeating every outer enemy. So instead of feeling anger toward difficult outer conditions, we should recognize anger the moment it arises in the mind, and work to tame it, starting today.
