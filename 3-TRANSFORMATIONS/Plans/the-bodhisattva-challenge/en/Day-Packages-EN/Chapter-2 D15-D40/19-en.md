#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Thus: the field where the bathing of the bodies is offered — the Thus-Gone Ones, and their heirs, the great bodhisattvas exemplified by the eight close heirs, Maitreya, Mañjughoṣa and the rest, who abide on the great grounds; the vases with which the bathing is offered, formed of precious substances, and of many kinds; scented waters mixed with sweet fragrance, perfect in scent and touch and pleasing, beautifully filled — with these I offer the bathing. And at the time of bathing, too, I offer the bathing of the bodies with endless kinds of offering, together with song, dance, and music.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-11]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

[After describing the arrangement of the bathing hall, the actual bathing is explained.] The Thus-Gone Ones — those who have come in accord with suchness, the true nature — while their Truth-Body minds never stir from the intent of the dharmakāya, appear in Form Bodies, the magical display of wisdom, in whatever form tames each being; and their heirs, the bodhisattvas, come with their ocean-like retinue. Laying their robes over the pillar-rail, oneself or emanated offering-deities present white bathing-cloths that do not obscure the body, which they put on; then, with many large and beautiful jeweled vases of sapphire and the like, filled two-thirds full with pleasing scented water of cleansing substances and various sweet fragrances, one offers the bathing in the proper manner. In one stream, upon the light-terrace outside the bathing hall, beautiful, lovely offering-goddesses — some singing sweet, captivating songs of praise, such as "When, chief of the two-legged...," and some with music such as round-drums and flutes — offer the bathing of the bodies.

Then, when one lifts the threshold of the bathing hall, one imagines that the bathing-water falls from the sky and purifies, without exception, all the wrongdoing and obscurations of oneself and all other beings placed below; that in particular all the cruel, harmful intentions and deeds of local deities, earth-lords, spirits, demons, disease-lords and the like are pacified; that human sickness and cattle-sickness are cut off; and that the precious awakening mind arises in their beings.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-11]]

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

To whom: the holy field, the Thus-Gone Ones, the perfectly complete buddhas, and their heirs, the great bodhisattvas. With what: vases formed of many precious substances such as sapphire, beautifully filled with pleasing scented water mixed with various sweet fragrances; and, together with captivating, sweet songs and music such as round-drums, one offers the bathing of their bodies.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-11]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

To what field and how the bathing is offered: to the field where the bathing is offered — the Thus-Gone Ones and their heirs, the great bodhisattvas — with vases of great value formed of precious substances such as sapphire and gold, or of many kinds, beautifully filled with pleasing water mixed with various utterly sweet fragrances and together with flowers; and, together with utterly captivating song and dance and many millions of kinds of music that make sweet and beautiful sounds, such as clay-drums and round-drums — beautifully and adorningly — "this bathing of the bodies I offer."

The purpose of offering the bathing: it is explained that when most of us today, and those like us, engage in listening to, reflecting on, meditating on, and practicing the holy Dharma, our mental capacity is so slight that the points of this or that occasion do not appear clearly to the mind but go dark — and that this comes about by the force of the great mass of wrongdoing and downfalls gathered from beginningless time until now, and in particular of being overwhelmed by such things as the obscuration of misusing offerings. So it is said that offering the bathing is of very great benefit for such a condition.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-11]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Moreover, the person making the offering sends out from his own heart rays of light of various colors; and, roused by those very rays, from their natural abode he invites the Thus-Gone Ones, the perfectly complete buddhas, the Blessed Ones, together with their inconceivable host of heart-heirs, the bodhisattvas, and requests them to be seated within the bathing hall. Gently, all their old robes and garments are received back; upon their bodies white bathing-cloths, of a nature that does not obscure the body, are offered and put on, each in turn; and from the tiers of the outer bathing-basin, the offering-goddesses — flower-goddesses, incense-goddesses, lamp-goddesses, scented-water-goddesses and the rest, all utterly beautiful and pleasing — with many gold and sapphire vases beautifully filled with pleasing, sweet-scented water of aloeswood, camphor, saffron and the like, and together with various kinds of sweet song and the sounds of various kinds of music, offer the bathing of the bodies of those buddhas and bodhisattvas.

All the bathing-water swirls within the bathing-basin; and, just as the bathing is completed, the small openings and holes of the lower floor open all at once by themselves, and — just as a heavy rain falls from the sky — it pours down from the crown of the heads of oneself and all other beings placed below the bathing hall, washing the whole outside and inside of the body and destroying every stain. And the old robes and garments, the bathing-cloths, the drying-cloths and the rest all melt into the form of red light and dissolve down through the point between the brows of oneself and all beings; one should imagine with conviction that thereby all the blessings and attainments of the buddhas and bodhisattvas are gained by oneself and all others, so that in a very short time one has become fortunate to attain manifest, perfect buddhahood.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-11]]

#### Main Teaching Points

1. **The actual bathing-offering, within the mind-emanated offering.** — After the arrangement of the bathing hall in verse 2-10, this verse explains the actual bathing-offering: bathing the bodies with jeweled vases beautifully filled with scented water.
2. **The field of offering — the Thus-Gone Ones and their heirs, the bodhisattvas.** — All the commentaries agree that the field is the Thus-Gone Ones and their heirs, the great bodhisattvas; Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso specifies that "their heirs" is exemplified by the eight close heirs, Maitreya, Mañjughoṣa and the rest, who abide on the great grounds.
3. **The offering-substance — sweet-scented water beautifully filling jeweled vases.** — Minyak Kunzang Sonam and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk describe in detail the vases formed of precious substances such as sapphire and gold, beautifully filled with pleasing water scented with aloeswood, camphor, saffron and the like.
4. **The manner of offering — the bathing offered together with song and music.** — All the commentaries explain in one voice that, while bathing the bodies of the field of offering, one offers together with sweet songs and many kinds of music such as round-drums.
5. **The direct result of the bathing — purifying the wrongdoing and obscurations of oneself and others, and becoming a cause for the awakening mind to arise.** — Kunzang Pelden explains imagining that the bathing-water falling from the sky purifies the wrongdoing and obscurations of oneself and all beings, pacifies harmful intentions, and gives rise to the precious awakening mind; and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk likewise explains imagining that the drops of bathing-water wash away the stains of all beings and that one gains the blessings and attainments of the buddhas and bodhisattvas.
6. **The purpose of offering the bathing — the benefit of making the meaning of the Dharma appear clearly to those overwhelmed by wrongdoing and downfalls.** — Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that, for people in whom the meaning of the Dharma does not appear clearly to the mind by the force of the wrongdoing, downfalls, and obscuration of misused offerings gathered from beginningless time, offering the bathing is of very great benefit.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse explains the actual bathing-offering within the mind-emanated offering: to the Thus-Gone Ones and their heirs, the bodhisattvas, one offers the bathing of their bodies with jeweled vases beautifully filled with sweet-scented water, marked by many songs and music. All the commentaries explain it in one voice.

**Key points.**

- This is the actual bathing-offering, within the mind-emanated offering, following the description of the bathing hall in verse 2-10.
- The field of offering is the Thus-Gone Ones and their heirs, the bodhisattvas; Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso specifies that "their heirs" is exemplified by the eight close heirs.
- The offering-substance is sweet-scented water beautifully filling jeweled vases, and the manner is to offer together with song and music.
- Kunzang Pelden and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk both explain the direct result: purifying the wrongdoing and obscurations of oneself and others, and giving rise to the precious awakening mind.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam sets out clearly that offering the bathing is of great benefit for those in whom the meaning of the Dharma does not appear clearly, being overwhelmed by wrongdoing, downfalls, and the obscuration of misused offerings.

Thus, after the bathing, to the Thus-Gone Ones and their supreme heirs, upon whose bodies there is a little moisture of water, [I dry their bodies] with fine, clean garments, incomparable and wondrous, perfumed with sweet fragrance — that is, I wipe and dry their bodies. ##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

To the bodies of the victors together with their heirs whose bathing has been offered, with incomparable cloths — such as divine cloth and Kāśika cloth — that are stainless and clean, thoroughly perfumed with dry, utterly sweet-scented medicinal powder, I wipe and duly dry their bodies. One should imagine that all the garments, bathing-cloths and drying-cloths, in the form of red light, dissolve into the point between the brows of oneself and all beings as blessing and attainment, so that all their wisdom and qualities are gained. After the drying, then, to those holy fields who appear as Emanation Bodies in the form of ones free of attachment, with the aspect of the ordained — as in "blue, red-ochre and saffron dye" — I should offer holy garments, the thirteen requisites of livelihood such as the three Dharma-robes whose color is beautifully dyed with the three permissible dyes, perfumed with utterly sweet fragrance.

##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **kunpal:** at the point of drying the body with the cloth, he sets out in particular a way of visualizing — that the garments, bathing-cloths and drying-cloths, in the form of red light, dissolve as blessing and attainment into the point between the brows of oneself and all beings, so that wisdom and qualities are gained; this is a portion not explained by the other commentaries.

The meaning does not conflict, but since Kunzang Pelden clearly sets out a particular way of visualizing, it is noted separately.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

1. **After the bathing, drying the body with incomparable cloths.** — All the commentaries explain in one voice that, after the bathing is offered, one dries the bodies of the victors together with their heirs with incomparable cloths, stainless and clean and thoroughly perfumed with incense.
2. **Offering holy garments utterly sweet in fragrance, beautifully dyed with the three dyes.** — After the drying, it is explained that one offers robes utterly sweet in fragrance — garments like the three kinds of Dharma-robe whose color is beautifully dyed with the three dyes, blue, red-ochre, and saffron.
3. **The distinction of the recipient — garments offered to those in the aspect of the ordained.** — Kunzang Pelden and Ngulchu Thogme both make clear that these garments are offered to the Emanation Bodies, or to the fields of offering who appear in the aspect of the ordained.
4. **The structure of the chapter — from the second part of this verse begins the offering of other items of enjoyment.** — Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk makes clear that from the second part of this verse (the words on offering utterly sweet-fragranced holy garments) begins the section called "offering of other items of enjoyment."
5. **The purpose of the offering — the aspiration to purify the karmic and afflictive stains of all beings.** — Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk teaches that one should imagine, by offering the bathing-offering, that all the karmic and afflictive stains in the beings of oneself and all others are purified.
6. **The distinction concerning robes and fragrance permitted on occasions of discipline.** — Minyak Kunzang Sonam makes clear that, although anointing robes with fragrance and the like is generally prohibited for the ordained, it is permitted on occasions such as when others of faith are gathering merit.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse, at the close of the bathing-offering, describes drying the bodies of the victors together with their heirs with incomparable cloths, and then begins the offering of items of enjoyment by presenting holy garments utterly sweet in fragrance, beautifully dyed with the three dyes.

**Key points.**

- After the bathing is offered, one dries the bodies of the victors together with their heirs with incomparable cloths, stainless and clean and perfumed with incense; all the commentaries explain this in one voice.
- Then one offers garments utterly sweet in fragrance — like the three kinds of Dharma-robe, beautifully dyed with the three dyes — to those in the aspect of the ordained.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk makes clear that from the second part of this verse begins the offering of other items of enjoyment; and this offering aspires to purify the karmic and afflictive stains of all beings.
- ⚑ Kunzang Pelden sets out in particular a way of visualizing — the garments, bathing-cloths and drying-cloths dissolving as red light and blessing into the brow-point of oneself and all beings — which the other commentaries do not explain.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam makes clear that, although anointing robes with fragrance is generally prohibited for the ordained, it is permitted on occasions such as when others of faith are gathering merit.

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Thus, after the bathing and drying of the body, to the holy fields — the Thus-Gone Ones together with their heirs in the aspect of the ordained — I offer permissible garments of saffron color, beautifully dyed, complete in measure and utterly sweet in fragrance, holy ones. Then, to the bodhisattvas who appear in the aspect of householders, [I offer] fine, thin, soft garments of various kinds, of good material and shape; and, further, hundreds of the finest ornaments — precious crowns, ear-ornaments and the rest, in many forms — with which I adorn the noble Samantabhadra, the noble Mañjuśrī, the noble Avalokiteśvara, the Lord of the World, and — included under "and the rest" — the noble Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin, the noble Ākāśagarbha and the other bodhisattvas who appear god-like: these too I adorn with distinguished ornaments.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-13]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

To those in the aspect of a universal monarch, such as Enjoyment Bodies who possess attachment, [I offer] fine garments formed of divine substances, beyond price, utterly thin and light, soft to the touch, of various colors and shapes — garments able to give rise to the bliss of concentration in the body: the dancer's long sleeves; the upper garment of white silk patterned with golden geese; the sash of fine cotton; the diadem of various silks; the streamer — the five silken garments; and, in place of the dancer's long sleeves, there is also the counting of a hanging silk. Offering thus, one should think: "may all beings, oneself and others, thereby gain the garments of shame and conscientiousness." As for the forms of ornament, upon gold and the like, set with fine mosaic of sapphire and the rest: the crown-ornament, ear-ornament, neck-ornament, necklace, breast-pendant, bracelet, anklet, and belt — the eight kinds of precious ornament, made supremely fine — with those many hundreds, I adorn and offer to the supreme, holy noble ones: Samantabhadra, Mañjughoṣa, and — by the word "and" — Avalokiteśvara, the Lord of the World, and also Vajrapāṇi, Kṣitigarbha, Maitreya, Ākāśagarbha, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin and the rest. And one should think: "may all beings, oneself and others, thereby gain the fine ornament of the marks and signs."

To the bodhisattvas in the manner or marks of householders, [I offer] fine garments arising from divine substances and fit for gods, light and thin, soft to the touch, of various shapes and colors; and with the finest ornaments — precious diadems, ear-ornaments, neck-ornaments, bracelets, belts, necklaces and the rest, becoming hundreds of supreme ornaments — with those various ones I adorn the noble Samantabhadra, Mañjughoṣa, Avalokiteśvara, and — by the word "and" — the taught ones: Maitreya, Vajrapāṇi, Ākāśagarbha, Kṣitigarbha, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin, and the other buddhas and bodhisattvas as well. <!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

After the bathing of the bodies is done, then to the field — the buddhas and bodhisattvas abiding in the manner of Emanation Bodies free of attachment, or in the aspect of the ordained — one offers garments whose color is beautifully dyed with the three permissible dyes (blue and pale-blue, red and pale-red, yellow and pale-yellow): the ordained garments and requisites, such as the patched Dharma-robe, the upper Dharma-robe, the lower Dharma-robe, and the sitting-cloth; and all that is holy or fine, endowed with the sweet scent of sandalwood and the like, one offers — praying, "may all beings, oneself and others, in the future attain fine garments of shame and conscientiousness." Again, "fine, thin garments": divine garments such as pañcālika cloth and precious woolens, fine and thin, soft to the touch, of various natures; of various colors too — blue, yellow, white, red; and the finest ornaments of gold, silver, pearl, sapphire and the like — crown-ornament, ear-ornament, neck-ornament, bracelet, anklet and the rest — in many hundreds. With those various ones, [I adorn] the buddhas and chief bodhisattvas who abide in the manner of Enjoyment Bodies, or with a householder's support — those with long hair who wear ornaments, like the victors of the five families — and the noble Samantabhadra, the venerable Mañjughoṣa, Avalokiteśvara the Lord of the World, and — "and the rest" — Vajrapāṇi, and the bodhisattvas Ākāśagarbha, Kṣitigarbha and the rest: on the bodies of all of them too I duly place ornaments. Relying on offering such supreme ornaments, one prays: "may there arise in the beings of oneself and all others the perfect ornament of the three trainings."

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **minyak-kunzang-sonam:** he notes that there also exists another textual reading of this line, "Ajita, Samantabhadra, and Mañjughoṣa."
- **gyaltsab / sabzang:** within the phrase "and the rest" they set out directly "Ajita" or "Ajita the Protector" [Maitreya], adding him to the count of those to be adorned.
- **kunpal / ngulchu-thogmed / minyak-kunzang-sonam / khenpo-kunga / tenzin-gyatso:** within "and the rest" they do not place Ajita, but set out — with differing fixed numbers — Vajrapāṇi, Kṣitigarbha, Ākāśagarbha, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin, and Maitreya.

The meaning does not conflict, but since the fixed number and the names within "and the rest" differ, it is noted separately. The textual variant that Minyak Kunzang Sonam sets out may be the reason Gyaltsab and Sabzang include "Ajita."

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

1. **After the garment-offering, adorning the noble ones with hundreds of supreme ornaments.** — All the commentaries agree that, after the garment-offering of the earlier verse, one offers to Samantabhadra, Mañjughoṣa, Avalokiteśvara and the rest by way of fine, thin, soft divine garments of various kinds and many hundreds of the finest precious ornaments.
2. **There are two aspects of the field of offering — the aspect of the ordained and the aspect of householders.** — As Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso set out clearly, to the fields who abide in the manner of Emanation Bodies free of attachment, or in the aspect of the ordained, one offers the three Dharma-robes; and to the fields in the guise, or aspect, of householders one offers fine, thin, soft garments and hundreds of supreme ornaments. Ngulchu Thogme too explains "those who bear the marks of householders" as showing the householder's manner by the power of those to be trained.
3. **The fixed number of the supreme ornaments — the eight precious ornaments.** — Kunzang Pelden explains that within "hundreds of supreme ornaments" are the eight precious ornaments: the crown-ornament, ear-ornament, neck-ornament, necklace, breast-pendant, bracelet, anklet, and belt.
4. **Other bodhisattvas added within "and the rest."** — Many commentaries explain that Vajrapāṇi, Kṣitigarbha, Ākāśagarbha, Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin, Maitreya and the rest are added, together with Samantabhadra, Mañjughoṣa, and Avalokiteśvara, among those to be adorned.
5. **Making aspiration-prayers together with the offering.** — Kunzang Pelden explains that after offering the supreme ornaments one should think, "may all beings, oneself and others, gain the fine ornament of the marks and signs"; and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains it as an aspiration-prayer, "may there arise in the beings of oneself and all others the perfect ornament of the three trainings."
6. **Some commentaries mark this verse's place within the fixed count of the mind-emanated offering.** — Gyaltsab gives the heading "the third" for the section on ornaments, and Sabzang, at the boundary of the following verse, gives "[the fourth,] the offering of sweet fragrance" — indicating which position within the third [item], the garment-and-ornament offering, this occupies.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse, after the garment-offering, describes adorning the noble ones who abide in the guise of householders — Samantabhadra, Mañjughoṣa, Avalokiteśvara and the rest — with fine, thin, soft divine garments of various kinds and many hundreds of the finest precious ornaments.

**Key points.**

- This is the occasion, after the garment-offering verse, of adorning the noble ones with hundreds of supreme ornaments.
- The field of offering has two aspects — the aspect of the ordained (offered the three Dharma-robes) and the aspect of householders (offered fine garments and ornaments) — as Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso distinguish.
- Kunzang Pelden explains "hundreds of supreme ornaments" as belonging to the eight precious ornaments (the crown-ornament and the rest).
- ⚑ Because the commentaries differ in the fixed number and names within "and the rest" — some placing Vajrapāṇi and the rest, others "Ajita" [Maitreya] — this seems to arise from the textual variant that Minyak Kunzang Sonam sets out.
- After offering the ornaments, one makes the aspiration-prayer that all beings, oneself and others, may attain the ornament of the marks and signs, or the ornament of the three trainings.
