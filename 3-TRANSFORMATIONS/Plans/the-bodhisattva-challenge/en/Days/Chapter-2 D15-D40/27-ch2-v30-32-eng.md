## Today's Verse

> I have, because of the afflictions, Caused harm with body, speech, and mind To the three jewels and to my parents And to the gurus, among others. ^2-30

> I, who am wicked and am stained By numerous failings, have committed Misdeeds that are most terrible. Before the guides, I confess them all. ^2-31

> I am going to perish quickly, Before I’m cleansed of my misdeeds. How can I be rescued from them? I beg you, please deliver me. ^2-32

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on verses 30 through 32 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Over a lifetime we do a great deal of harm, some of it deliberate and much of it careless, to the very people we owe the most: our parents, our teachers, and the Three Jewels. Today's verses set that beside a second fact, that this life is short and the hour of death is not fixed. Together they push us to stop postponing and to confess our mistakes now, with real regret.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

These verses stop saying "I have done wrong" and start saying who it was done to. Driven by the afflictions, Shantideva harmed the Three Jewels, his own parents, and his teachers, with body, speech, and mind. He lays the worst of it before the guides. Then he says the thing that makes it urgent: he may die before he is cleansed, so he asks to be rescued quickly.

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains why quickly is the whole point. That we die is settled. When we die is not. A road accident ends a life in a moment, and no doctor anywhere will guarantee that a particular person is still alive tomorrow. So the stretch between now and death is not a period we can make plans inside.

The commentaries give an old image for what waiting costs us. Rust does not attack iron from outside. It rises out of the iron, and then it eats the iron. Our own misdeeds work like that. They sit in us and consume us, and if death arrives while they are still sitting there, there is nowhere to go but downward.

Which is why this is something to do today rather than something to schedule.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will not speak a harsh word to my parents or my teachers. I will speak to them with respect and warmth.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ The people who have been kindest to us are often the ones we are sharpest with. Anger comes out most easily at home, and we let ourselves say things to a parent or a teacher that we would never say to a stranger. Today's verses count this as a serious wrong, not a small domestic slip, because these are the people whose kindness we are standing on. So today I will keep watch on my tone with my parents and my teachers. Before I answer them, I will bring to mind one specific thing they have done for me. This clears something of what has already been done, and it stops the next round of it from starting.
