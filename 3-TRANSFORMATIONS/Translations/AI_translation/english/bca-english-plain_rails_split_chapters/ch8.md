## 8. Chapter Eight: Teaching on Meditation ^8-0

When diligence has thus been generated,
the mind should be settled in meditation.
A person whose mind is distracted
remains in the grip of troubling emotions. ^8-1

By being in solitude, in body and mind,
distraction will not arise.
Therefore, abandon worldly things
and completely give up discursive thoughts. ^8-2

Due to attachment and craving for gain and so forth,
one does not abandon worldly things.
Therefore, completely give up these things,
and the wise should examine them in this way. ^8-3

Knowing that insight, perfectly endowed with calm,
completely destroys troubling emotions,
one should first seek calm, and that too
is accomplished by delight in non-attachment to worldly things. ^8-4

Who among the impermanent
would truly become attached to the impermanent?
For a thousand eons,
one will never see what is pleasant. ^8-5

If one does not see it, one will not rejoice.
The mind will not be settled in equipoise.
Even if one sees it, one will not be satisfied,
and one will be tormented by craving as before. ^8-6

If one becomes attached to beings,
one is completely obscured from the truth.
One also destroys the mind of weariness,
and ultimately one will be tormented by sorrow. ^8-7

Merely by thinking of them,
this life will pass meaninglessly.
By impermanent friends,
the eternal Dharma will also be destroyed. ^8-8

If one associates with foolish people,
one will certainly go to the lower realms.
If one leads them to what is not equal,
what is the use of relying on foolish people? ^8-9

In a single instant, one becomes a friend,
and in a moment, one also becomes an enemy.
Becoming angry at the object of joy,
it is hard to please ordinary people. ^8-10

If one speaks beneficently, they become angry.
One also turns away from benefit.
If one does not listen to their words,
one will go to the lower realms due to anger. ^8-11

They envy the high, compete with equals,
are arrogant when praised by the low,
and become angry when unpleasant things are said.
When will one receive benefit from foolish people? ^8-12

If one associates with foolish people,
one will certainly generate unwholesome things,
such as praising oneself and criticizing others,
and talk that delights in cyclic existence. ^8-13

Thus, relying on oneself and others
will only lead to ruin.
One's own purpose has not been accomplished by them,
and one will not accomplish their purpose. ^8-14

One should keep far away from foolish people.
If one meets them, one should please them with joy.
One should act well, merely as a stranger,
without becoming too intimate. ^8-15

Like a bee taking honey from a flower,
one should take only the essence of the Dharma.
One should dwell without intimacy with everyone,
as if one had never seen them before. ^8-16

If one harbors such pride as,
"I have much gain and respect,
and many people are fond of me,"
fear will arise after death. ^8-17

Therefore, whatever the deluded mind
is attached to,
that, multiplied a thousandfold,
will become suffering and arise. ^8-18

Therefore, the wise should not be attached.
Fear arises from attachment.
Since these things are by nature impermanent,
be steadfast and truly understand. ^8-19

Even if much gain arises,
and fame and renown arise,
where will one go
with the collection of gain and fame, which is uncertain? ^8-20

If others criticize me,
what joy is there in praise for me?
If others praise me,
what unhappiness is there in criticism? ^8-21

If the buddhas themselves
cannot satisfy the various inclinations of beings,
what need is there to speak of a lowly person like me?
Therefore, abandon worldly concerns. ^8-22

Beings criticize those who have no gain.
They speak unpleasant things to those who have gain.
How can joy arise
from those whose nature is difficult-to-please? ^8-23

Because foolish people's own purposes
do not arise without joy,
the buddhas taught
that no foolish person is a friend. ^8-24

The deer and birds in the forest
do not speak unpleasant things.
When will I dwell together
with those with whom association is pleasant? ^8-25

When will I dwell in a cave,
a deserted temple, or at the foot of a tree,
without looking back,
and without attachment? ^8-26

When will I dwell freely
in naturally spacious places,
without claiming any place as my own,
and without attachment? ^8-27

When will I dwell fearlessly,
holding only the bare necessities, such as an alms bowl,
and wearing clothes that no one wants,
even if this body is not hidden? ^8-28

When will I go to a charnel ground
and treat the bones of others
and my own body
as equally subject to decay? ^8-29

When will this very body of mine
become such that even foxes
will not approach it
because of its smell? ^8-30

Even this body, which arose alone,
and the flesh and bones born with it,
will decay and separate.
What need is there to speak of other friends? ^8-31

One is born alone,
and one dies alone.
If one does not take on the suffering of others,
what is the use of friends who cause hindrance? ^8-32

Just as travelers
take up lodging,
likewise, those who have set out on the path of cyclic existence
take up a place of rebirth. ^8-33

As long as this body
is not carried away by four men,
while all the world is tormented,
one should go to the forest. ^8-34

Having no friends and no enemies,
one should dwell alone in solitude.
Having considered oneself as already dead,
there will be no grief even if one dies. ^8-35

No one nearby
will cause grief or harm.
Therefore, no one will distract one
from recollecting the Buddha and so forth. ^8-36

Therefore, one should dwell alone
in a very delightful forest,
which is easy, brings happiness,
and calms all distractions. ^8-37

Having abandoned all other intentions,
with a single thought for oneself,
one should strive to settle the mind
and to tame it. ^8-38

In this world and in the next,
desires generate vexation.
Here, they cause killing, binding, and cutting.
In the next, they lead to hell and so forth. ^8-39

For the sake of those
for whom one has repeatedly pleaded with messengers,
and for whom one has not hesitated
to commit misdeeds or dishonorable acts, ^8-40

one has also entered into danger
and exhausted one's resources.
Those very ones,
by embracing whom one experiences supreme joy, ^8-41

are merely skeletons, nothing else.
They are not independent and not oneself.
Why, then, does one not go to nirvana,
craving and clinging to them? ^8-42

That which one first lifts with effort,
even when pulled, looks down in shame.
Whether seen before or not,
its face is covered with clothes. ^8-43

That face of troubling emotions,
which is now directly present,
why does one flee now,
having seen it removed by vultures? ^8-44

That which one completely protects
even when others look at it with their eyes,
as long as it is eaten by them,
why do you, miserly one, not protect it? ^8-45

Having seen this heap of flesh
eaten by vultures and other creatures,
why do you offer flowers,
sandalwood, and ornaments to the food of others? ^8-46

Having seen it as merely bones,
if you are afraid even when it is unmoving,
why would you not be afraid
if it were moved by someone like a zombie? ^8-47

If feces and saliva
arise from the same food,
why do you dislike feces
but like saliva? ^8-48

Not delighting in a soft pillow
made of cotton,
the lustful, deluded by impurity,
say it is not oozing with foul smell. ^8-49

If one is attached to it even when it is covered,
why not desire it when it is uncovered?
If there is no need for it,
why embrace what is covered? ^8-50

If there is no attachment to impurity,
why do you embrace in your lap
a cage of bones bound by sinews,
plastered with the mud of flesh? ^8-51

You yourself possess much impurity,
and you constantly use it.
You also desire other bags of impurity
with craving for impurity. ^8-52

If you desire to touch and see
this flesh, saying, "I like this flesh,"
how can you desire
flesh that is by nature insentient? ^8-53

The mind that desires
cannot touch or see.
That which can do so is not cognition.
What is the use of embracing it meaninglessly? ^8-54

It is not amazing that one does not understand
the impure nature of another's body.
It is very amazing that one does not understand
that one's own body is impure. ^8-55

Having abandoned the young lotus
opened by the sun's rays, free of clouds,
why does one delight in a cage of impurity
with a mind attached to impurity? ^8-56

If you do not want to touch
a place soiled with impurity,
how can you desire to touch
the body from which it came? ^8-57

If there is no attachment to impurity,
and it arose from a field of impurity,
and its seed was generated by it,
why do you embrace another in your lap? ^8-58

You do not desire even a tiny worm
that arose from impurity,
yet you desire a body that is the nature of much impurity,
and you desire an impure person. ^8-59

Not only do you not criticize
your own impurity,
but you also desire others
with craving for the impure bag of impurity. ^8-60

Even delightful camphor and so forth,
or cooked rice or vegetables,
when put into the mouth, spit out, and discarded,
will soil the ground with impurity. ^8-61

If there is doubt even when such impurity
is directly perceived,
then look at other impurity
in bodies discarded in charnel grounds. ^8-62

Even knowing that great fear arises
when the skin is peeled off,
how can joy arise again
for that very thing? ^8-63

The fragrance applied to the body
is sandalwood and other things, not something else.
Why does one become attached to others
by that external fragrance? ^8-64

If it were not attached to this
due to its naturally foul smell, would it not be better?
Why do worldly people, who burn meaninglessly,
apply sweet fragrance to it? ^8-65

What has happened to this body
with sweet-smelling sandalwood?
Why does one become attached to others
by the fragrance of others? ^8-66

If the body's nature is terrifying
when naked, with long hair and nails,
yellow teeth, and a foul smell,
and smeared with the smell of mud, ^8-67

why does one strive to clean it
like a weapon that harms oneself?
By the deluded efforts of oneself,
this whole world is disturbed by madness. ^8-68

If one is disgusted in a charnel ground
merely by seeing bones,
does one delight in the charnel ground of a city
filled with moving bones? ^8-69

That which has become impure
is not found without a price.
By striving for it, one experiences lavish
and hellish harm. ^8-70

Foolish people, unable to increase wealth,
what happiness is there in this when one is mature?
If one's life is spent on wealth and so forth,
what is the use of desires when one is old? ^8-71

Some base people, full of craving,
are completely exhausted by work all day long.
Having come home, their worn-out bodies
lie down like corpses. ^8-72

Some are tormented by the hardships of travel
and by being far away.
Though desiring children and wives,
they will not even see them. ^8-73

Deluded by wanting self-benefit,
one sells oneself for the sake of what.
Without obtaining it, one is driven by the wind
of others' meaningless actions. ^8-74

Some sell their own bodies
and are used by others without control.
Even if wives give birth to children,
they appear at the foot of trees in deserted places. ^8-75

Foolish people, deceived by desires,
enter battle, fearing for their lives,
thinking, "I will live, I will live."
They become servants for profit. ^8-76

Some lustful people have their bodies cut.
Some are impaled on stakes.
Some are pierced by spears.
Some are also seen being burned. ^8-77

One should know that wealth is endless vexation
due to the torment of accumulating, protecting, and losing it.
Those who are distracted by attachment to wealth
have no opportunity to be liberated from the suffering of cyclic existence. ^8-78

For lustful people, there are many such
faults, and the benefit is small.
It is like cattle pulling a cart
eating a few mouthfuls of grass. ^8-79

That small benefit, which is not hard to obtain
even by cattle,
is destroyed by those tormented by karma,
who have obtained this precious opportunity, so hard to find. ^8-80

Desires will certainly be destroyed
and cause one to fall into hell and so forth.
Since it is not great, whatever toil
arises constantly, ^8-81

if by a mere fraction of that toil
buddhahood is accomplished,
then lustful bodhisattvas
have great suffering and no awakening. ^8-82

If one considers the suffering of hell and so forth,
desires cannot be compared to weapons,
poison, fire, precipices,
or enemies. ^8-83

Having thus grown weary of desires,
one should generate delight in solitude.
In a peaceful forest,
free of quarrels and troubling emotions, ^8-84

one should wander, thinking of others' benefit,
in a delightful mansion of spacious flat stones,
cooled by the moonlight and sandalwood,
with the gentle wind of the peaceful forest blowing. ^8-85

One should dwell in an empty house,
at the foot of a tree, or in a cave,
for as long as one wishes,
abandoning the suffering of clinging and protecting, and acting freely without dependence. ^8-86

That happiness of contentment,
which is acting freely, without attachment,
and not connected to anyone,
is hard to find even by the mighty. ^8-87

In this and similar ways,
having considered the benefits of solitude,
one should meditate on the pacification of discursive thoughts
and on the Mind of Awakening. ^8-88

One should first strive to cultivate
the equality of oneself and others.
Since all beings equally desire happiness and suffering,
one should protect all as oneself. ^8-89

Just as the body is one object to be protected,
even though it has many parts, such as hands,
likewise, all beings, though distinct in their happiness and suffering,
are one in equally desiring happiness, like oneself. ^8-90

If my suffering
does not harm another's body,
even so, that suffering of theirs
is unbearable due to clinging to a self. ^8-91

Similarly, the suffering of others
will not fall upon me,
even so, that suffering of theirs
is hard to bear due to clinging to a self. ^8-92

I should remove the suffering of others,
because it is suffering, like my own suffering.
I should benefit others,
because they are beings, like my own body. ^8-93

When both myself and others
are alike in desiring happiness,
what difference is there between me and others,
that I should strive for happiness for myself alone? ^8-94

When both myself and others
are alike in not desiring suffering,
what difference is there between me and others,
that I should protect myself and not others? ^8-95

If, because that suffering
does not harm me, I do not protect it,
then if future suffering
does not harm me, why should I protect it? ^8-96

The notion that "I will experience it"
is mistaken.
For in this way, the dead person is another,
and the one who is born is also another. ^8-97

If the suffering of someone
is to be protected by that very person,
then the suffering of the foot is not protected by the hand.
Why, then, should it be protected by it? ^8-98

If, even though it is illogical,
one engages in this due to self-clinging,
then whatever is illogical, whether self or other,
should certainly be abandoned as much as possible. ^8-99

The continuum and collection
are false, like a garland or an army.
Since there is no suffering person,
who will have power over this? ^8-100

Since suffering has no owner,
all suffering is without distinction.
Since it is suffering, it should be removed.
What is to be done with certainty? ^8-101

Why is it indisputable that the suffering of all
should be averted?
If it is averted, then all is averted.
Otherwise, I am also like other beings. ^8-102

If one asks, "Why generate
much suffering compassion?"
If one considers the suffering of beings,
how can compassion bring much suffering? ^8-103

If by one suffering
many sufferings are eliminated,
then the compassionate should generate
that suffering for themselves and others. ^8-104

Therefore, just as Moon-Beautiful,
knowing the king's harm,
did not remove her own suffering,
for the sake of exhausting the suffering of many, ^8-105

likewise, when the continuum is cultivated,
one delights in the pacification of others' suffering.
One will enter even the lowest hell,
like a swan entering a lotus lake. ^8-106

When beings are liberated,
is that ocean of joy not enough?
What is the use
of desiring liberation? ^8-107

Therefore, even if one acts for the sake of others,
there is no pride or wonder.
Delighting solely in the benefit of others,
there is no hope for karmic results. ^8-108

Therefore, just as one protects oneself
from the slightest unpleasantness,
likewise, one should cultivate the mind of protection
and compassion for beings. ^8-109

By habit, one knows
the semen and drops of blood of others
as "mine,"
even though they are non-existent. ^8-110

Similarly, why not grasp
the bodies of others as "I"?
It is not difficult to place
one's own body among others. ^8-111

Knowing oneself to be flawed
and others to be an ocean of good qualities,
one should meditate on completely abandoning self-clinging
and taking on others. ^8-112

Just as one considers hands and so forth
to be parts of the body,
why not consider beings
to be parts of the world? ^8-113

Just as the notion of "mine" arose
for this selfless body through habit,
similarly, why should the notion of "I" not arise
for other beings through habit? ^8-114

If that is the case, then when one acts for the benefit of others,
no wonder or pride will arise,
just as one does not hope for a reward
after eating one's own food. ^8-115

Therefore, just as one protects oneself
from the slightest unpleasantness,
likewise, one should cultivate the mind of protection
and compassion for beings. ^8-116

Therefore, Protector Avalokiteshvara,
with great compassion,
blessed his own name
to remove the fear of cyclic existence for beings. ^8-117

One should not turn back from hardship,
for by the power of habit,
even the fear of hearing a name
will become displeasure if it is not there. ^8-118

Whoever wishes to quickly protect
themselves and others,
should practice the supreme secret
of exchanging self and others. ^8-119

Whoever is attached to this body,
fear arises from minor dangers.
Who would not hate
that body, like an enemy, which causes fear? ^8-120

Whoever wishes to perform the ritual
of healing this body, which is afflicted by hunger, thirst, and other illnesses,
kills birds, fish, and wild animals,
and waits on the road. ^8-121

Whoever kills their own father and mother
for profit and respect,
and steals the property of the Three Jewels,
will be burned in the lowest hell. ^8-122

What wise person would desire,
protect, and honor that body?
Who would not look at it like an enemy
and despise it? ^8-123

If one asks, "What is used if given?"
the self-interested mind is demonic.
If one asks, "What is given if used?"
the other-interested mind is divine. ^8-124

If one harms others for oneself,
one will be tormented in hell and so forth.
If one harms oneself for others,
one attains all excellent qualities. ^8-125

Whoever desires to be exalted
will go to the lower realms, become bad, and foolish.
If one transfers that to others,
one will attain happy realms and respect. ^8-126

If one uses others for oneself,
one will experience being a servant and so forth.
If one uses oneself for others,
one will experience being a lord and so forth. ^8-127

All the happiness in the world
arises from the desire for others' happiness.
All the suffering in the world
arises from the desire for one's own happiness. ^8-128

What need is there to speak much?
Look at the difference
between a foolish person acting for self
and a buddha acting for the benefit of others. ^8-129

If one does not truly exchange
one's own happiness for the suffering of others,
one will not attain buddhahood,
and there will be no happiness in cyclic existence. ^8-130

Let alone the next world,
even in this life, the purpose of a servant not working
and a lord not giving rewards
will not be accomplished. ^8-131

Abandoning all excellent happiness,
both visible and invisible,
and due to causing suffering to others,
the deluded take on unbearable suffering. ^8-132

Whatever harm,
fear, and suffering exist in the world,
all of that arises from self-clinging.
What will that great demon do to me? ^8-133

If one does not abandon oneself,
one cannot abandon suffering,
just as if one does not abandon fire,
one cannot abandon burning. ^8-134

Therefore, to pacify one's own harm
and to pacify the suffering of others,
one should give oneself to others
and take on others as oneself. ^8-135

"I have become dependent on others,"
you, mind, should certainly know.
Now, you should not think of anything else
except for the benefit of all beings. ^8-136

It is not appropriate for the dependent eyes and so forth
to accomplish their own purpose.
It is not appropriate for the eyes and so forth
to act wrongly towards their purpose. ^8-137

Therefore, prioritize beings.
Whatever one sees in one's own body,
one should take that
and use it to benefit others. ^8-138

Having made the lowly and so forth into oneself,
and having made oneself into others,
one should cultivate envy, competition, and pride
with a mind free of discursive thoughts. ^8-139

"This one is honored, but I am not.
I have not found this, but this one has.
This one is praised, but I am criticized.
This one is happy, but I am suffering." ^8-140

"I am doing the work,
but this one dwells in happiness.
This one is great in the world,
but I am known as lowly and without good qualities." ^8-141

"What is the use of being without good qualities?
I am completely endowed with good qualities.
There are those who are inferior to this one,
and there are also those who are superior to me." ^8-142

Ethical discipline, views, and other things
are not under my control due to the power of troubling emotions.
I must heal myself as much as possible,
and I must accept harm. ^8-143

If I am not to be healed,
why do I despise myself?
What is the use of their good qualities to me?
I am endowed with good qualities. ^8-144

This one, who dwells in the mouth of the cruel lower realms,
without compassion for beings,
wishes to overcome the wise
with pride in external good qualities. ^8-145

Looking at those who are equal to me,
I will certainly accomplish, even through conflict,
my gain and respect,
so that I may be superior to them. ^8-146

My good qualities
should be evident to all the world,
and no one should hear
of their good qualities. ^8-147

My faults should be hidden,
and I should be honored, not this one.
I have found good gain now,
and I should be respected, not this one. ^8-148

I will look with delight for a long time
at the wrongdoings committed by this one.
I will make all beings laugh
and criticize each other. ^8-149

"This troubling emotion also
is said to compete with me.
Are they equal in hearing, wisdom,
form, lineage, or wealth?" ^8-150

Having heard of my good qualities,
thus proclaimed to all,
one should fully enjoy the happiness
of joy that makes the hair stand on end. ^8-151

If this one has gain,
and if they work for me,
I will give them only enough to live,
and I will take it by my own strength. ^8-152

I will deprive this one of happiness
and constantly inflict harm upon them.
This one has harmed me
in cyclic existence through all hundreds of lifetimes. ^8-153

You, mind, desiring your own purpose,
even though countless eons have passed,
you have only accumulated suffering
through such great toil. ^8-154

Therefore, you should certainly
engage in the benefit of others.
The Buddha's words are unfailing,
and its benefits will be seen later. ^8-155

If you had done this work
in earlier times,
it would not be possible for you to be in such a state
of not being the perfect Buddha. ^8-156

Therefore, just as you clung to "I"
in the semen and drops of blood
of others,
similarly, cultivate that for others. ^8-157

Having greatly considered others,
whatever appears in one's own body,
one should take that
and use it to benefit others. ^8-158

"I am happy, but others are not.
I am high, but others are low.
I am beneficial, but others are not."
Why do you not envy yourself? ^8-159

"I will be free of happiness,
and the suffering of others will be inflicted upon me."
When this happens, "What is this doing?"
one should examine one's own faults. ^8-160

Even the wrongdoings committed by others
should be transformed into one's own faults.
Even if one's own wrongdoing is small,
it should be proclaimed to many people. ^8-161

By speaking of the superior fame of others,
eclipse one's own fame.
I should be used for all purposes,
like the lowest servant. ^8-162

"This one is by nature flawed,
so do not praise them for temporary good qualities.
Ensure that no one
ever knows their good qualities." ^8-163

In short, whatever harm you have done to others
for your own sake,
let that harm
fall upon yourself for the sake of beings. ^8-164

This one should not be strengthened
in a stubborn way.
It should be established with shame and fear,
like a new bride, restrained. ^8-165

"Do this, dwell in this way,
and do not do that."
This one should be controlled in this way.
If it transgresses, it should be punished. ^8-166

If, even though thus advised,
you, mind, do not act in that way,
then, by relying on all faults,
you yourself will certainly be punished. ^8-167

The former time when you ruined me
was different.
I see you now; where will you go?
All your arrogance will be destroyed. ^8-168

Now, abandon the thought
that "I still have my own purpose."
I have sold you to others.
Do not be sad; offer your milk and flesh. ^8-169

If, having become careless, you
do not give yourself to beings,
you will certainly be given by me
to the guardians of hell. ^8-170

Thus, for a while,
you will suffer for a long time because I have given you.
I will remember my grievances
and destroy your self-centered thoughts. ^8-171

If I desire happiness,
I should not delight in myself.
If I desire to protect myself,
I should always protect others. ^8-172

The more this body is
Protected and cared for,
The more it falls into the trap
Of being demanding. ^8-173

If the desires of such a demanding body
Cannot be fulfilled
by all the things on this earth,
Then who can fulfill its desires? ^8-174

Unfulfilled desires cause troubling emotions
And disappointment.
But for one who is free of all dependence,
Their happiness is endless. ^8-175

Therefore, do not give an opening
For the body's desires to grow.
That which does not cling to pleasant things
Is truly good. ^8-176

This unbearable, impure form
Ends up as ashes in the end,
Unmoving and moved by others.
Why do I cling to it as a self? ^8-177

Whether I live or die, it is better.
What use is this machine to me?
What difference is there between this and a log?
Alas, it does not remove pride! ^8-178

By caring for the body,
I accumulate needless suffering.
What use is this body, which is like a piece of wood,
To attachment and anger? ^8-179

If I protect this body,
Or if vultures and the like eat it,
If there is no attachment or hatred,
Why should I be attached to it? ^8-180

If I become angry when insulted,
And pleased when praised,
If I do not understand that very thing,
Then what effort have I made? ^8-181

If I consider that this body is what I desire,
And that I am its friend,
Since everyone desires their own body,
Why should I not rejoice in that? ^8-182

Therefore, I will abandon this body without attachment
For the benefit of beings.
Although it has many faults,
I will accept it as a tool for actions. ^8-183

Therefore, childish behavior is enough.
I will follow the wise,
And remembering the teaching on carefulness,
I will ward off sleep and dullness. ^8-184

Therefore, to remove obscurations,
I will turn my mind from wrong paths,
And always, with proper focus,
I will settle myself in meditation. ^8-185

This concludes the eighth chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "Teaching on Meditation." ^8-a
