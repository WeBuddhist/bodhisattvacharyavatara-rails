# Day 2 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: The Benefits of the Mind of Enlightenment — Verses 1-4 to 1-6

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Precious Support Realization (Leisure and Opportunity):** Reflecting on the extreme difficulty of obtaining a precious human life inspires profound gratitude and urgency. This awareness helps one value their current circumstances and avoid wasting time on trivial pursuits. It shifts the mind toward meaningful spiritual practice.
*   **Inspiration of Grace (Divine Blessings):** Recognizing that virtuous thoughts arise through the power and blessings of the Buddhas cultivates deep humility and devotion. Rather than relying on personal pride, one learns to open their heart to spiritual guidance. This receptive attitude allows positive qualities to grow naturally.
*   **Conquering Afflictions (Bodhicitta's Power):** Understanding that ordinary virtue is too weak to resist powerful negative patterns reveals the necessity of a supreme remedy. Cultivating the precious mind of enlightenment provides the only force strong enough to overcome deep-seated afflictions. It offers a reliable shield against spiritual decline.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-4**
> དལ་འབྱོར་འདི་ནི་རྙེད་པར་ཤིན་ཏུ་དཀའ། །སྐྱེས་བུའི་དོན་སྒྲུབ་ཐོབ་པར་གྱུར་པ་ལ། །
> གལ་ཏེ་འདི་ལ་ཕན་པ་མ་བསྒྲུབས་ན། །ཕྱིས་འདི་ཡང་དག་འབྱོར་པར་ག་ལ་འགྱུར། ། ^1-4
> 
> *This precious human life of leisure and endowment is extremely difficult to obtain. Now that one has the chance to accomplish the purpose of a being, if one does not make use of it for benefit, how will such a perfect opportunity arise again?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-4**
The great teacher Śāntideva urges practitioners to realize the immense value of this precious human life. This support is defined by eight freedoms and ten endowments of fortune. The commentators emphasize that this support is exceptionally rare to obtain. Its causes, like ethical discipline and generosity, are extremely difficult to compile. They warn that failing to use this opportunity makes future human birth virtually impossible.

#### **Verse 1-5**
> ཇི་ལྟར་མཚན་མོ་མུན་ནག་སྤྲིན་རུམ་ན། །གློག་འགྱུ་སྐད་ཅིག་རབ་སྣང་སྟོན་པ་ལྟར། །
> དེ་བཞིན་སངས་རྒྱས་མཐུ་ཡིས་བརྒྱ་ལམ་ན། །འཇིག་རྟེན་བསོད་ནམས་བློ་གྲོས་ཐང་འགའ་འབྱུང་། ། ^1-5
> 
> *Just as a flash of lightning on a dark, cloud-covered night illuminates all for one brilliant instant, so too, by the power of the Buddhas, the mind of virtue and wisdom arises but rarely in the world.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-5**
The great teacher Śāntideva uses the metaphor of a sudden lightning flash to illustrate the brevity of virtuous thoughts. The thick darkness represents the heavy cloud of afflictions that traps worldly beings. The lightning flash represents a rare, momentary impulse toward virtue. The commentators agree that such virtuous impulses do not arise from a person's own strength. They are inspired solely by the compassion and blessings of the Buddhas.

#### **Verse 1-6**
> དེ་ལྟས་དགེ་བ་ཉམ་ཆུང་ཉིད་ལ་རྟག །སྡིག་པ་སྟོབས་ཆེན་ཤིན་ཏུ་མི་བཟད་པ། །
> དེ་ནི་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མིན་པ། །དགེ་གཞན་གང་གིས་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་པར་འགྱུར། ། ^1-6
> 
> *Therefore, virtue is always meager, while the power of negativity is immense and unbearable. Except for perfect bodhicitta, what other virtue can overcome it?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-6**
The great teacher Śāntideva contrasts weak ordinary virtue with the overwhelming power of negativity. The commentators explain that ordinary virtue is fleeting and easily exhausted. In contrast, negative actions are massive and continuously generated. They conclude that while ordinary good deeds are insufficient, conventional Bodhicitta possesses supreme power. It can purify past misdeeds and completely prevent future bad rebirths.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Valuing My Time and Opportunities:** Today, I will reflect on the extreme rarity of my human life by pausing during a busy moment to appreciate my freedom and ability to practice. I will consciously avoid wasting my precious time on trivial gossip or idle distractions, dedicating my energy instead to meaningful actions.
*   **2. Invoking Spiritual Support with Humility:** Whenever I notice a positive impulse or a kind thought arising in my mind today, I will acknowledge that this is a blessing rather than my own achievement. I will cultivate humility by silently thanking my teachers and the Buddhas for inspiring virtue within me.
*   **3. Applying Bodhicitta to Overcome Negative Habits:** When I encounter irritating situations or feel anger arising today, I will recognize that my ordinary patience is too weak to handle these emotions on its own. I will immediately invoke the wish to benefit all beings as a powerful shield to neutralize my negative reactions.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Leisure and Opportunity (དལ་འབྱོར།):** This term refers to the eighteen unique qualities of a precious human life that make spiritual practice possible. It consists of the eight freedoms from non-leisure states and the ten endowments of personal and circumstantial fortune. Commentators explain that this support is extremely rare and must be used to achieve both temporary happiness and ultimate liberation.
*   **Power of the Buddha (སངས་རྒྱས་མཐུ།):** This term represents the spiritual influence and blessings of enlightened beings that inspire worldly minds. Commentators explain that worldly beings are naturally trapped in negative habits and cannot generate virtue on their own. Therefore, any momentary impulse toward goodness is entirely the result of the Buddhas' compassion and light.
*   **Goodness or Virtue (དགེ་བ།):** This term refers to positive mental states and deeds that lead to happiness and spiritual progress. In the context of today's teachings, ordinary worldly virtue is described as extremely weak and short-lived, like a flash of lightning. It is contrastingly compared to the supreme, continuous virtue of the mind of enlightenment.
