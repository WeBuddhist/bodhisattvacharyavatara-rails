## Traditional Interpretation

### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Palden (Tibetan)

The verse performs the third of four opening functions: *deflating pride* (ཁེངས་པ་བསྐྱུང་བ). Śāntideva had every reason for pride — Vibhūticandra (Vibhūticandra, an Indian paṇḍita who encountered the text) praised him as incomparable among those who composed on the Buddha-dharma, one whose intention and spiritual experience matched Śāntideva's inner realization. The bodhisattva story collections (the 34th birth story of the fearless hero, the Wishing Tree of King Gewaí Wangpo, etc.) show that the content of the text is actually Śāntideva's own previous-life realisations recorded in the sūtras, even if he does not claim originality. Yet he gives two reasons for disclaiming this is for others: (1) there is no *previously unheard content* here that goes beyond the Buddha's word and the sūtras; (2) he has no *skill in verse-craft* that would produce a superior literary text. Therefore he has no *intention for others' benefit* of the great scope that producing an unprecedented composition would imply. The real purpose: to *cultivate bodhicitta and the six pāramitās in his own mind* and to familiarise himself with them through composition — the highest of the three scholarly activities (explanation, debate, composition), because composition embeds the content in mind through repeated articulation. Two benefits of the disclaimer: avoiding pride (the water of merit does not gather on the mound of pride) and protecting the text from misleading attachment to novelty. The citing of Candragomin on the three scholarly activities is here explicit.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-2)

### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Tibetan)

Gyaltsab reads this as a statement in two parts. First, the author disclaims benefit-for-others across content and form: in terms of *content* (བརྗོད་བྱ), not a single point is here that has not already appeared in the Buddha's word or the śāstras; in terms of *form* (རྗོད་བྱེད), the author has no special literary skill with weighty syllables, meters, or rhetorical ornaments that would produce a text superior to what has been written before. Since both reasons preclude benefiting others by bringing a fresh teaching in a new form, the author has no such intention. *Second*, the actual reason for composing: to cultivate bodhicitta in his own mind. Gyaltsab notes that to say "I composed it for my own cultivation" while not having realized it would be wrong; to say so when one has realized it but is only seeking further familiarization is fine. Gyaltsab explicitly makes the point that one whose stream of virtue is already active benefits from composing, because composition is the mode of practice that generates the most continuous familiarization.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-2)

### ngulchu-thogmed — Ngulchu Thokme Sangpo (Tibetan)

NTS addresses verses 1-2 and 1-3 together (combined block). He reads verse 1-2 as a twofold disclaimer and a statement of purpose. In terms of content, nothing appears in this śāstra that was not already given in the Buddha's sūtras, the intent-explications of Nāgārjuna and his sons, or Asaṅga and his brothers. In terms of form, Śāntideva denies having the skill in meter and rhetoric that could exceed the existing texts and please the learned. For both reasons, he has no intention to benefit others by producing something novel. The real purpose: to cultivate bodhicitta in his own mind. He makes clear that for bodhisattvas who have already entered the great path, composing a text is not a distraction but rather the means of keeping the stream of practice unbroken and growing.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-3)

### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Mi Nyag Kunzang Sönam (Tibetan)

MKS opens with the question: even if you expound scripture faithfully, is there anything here that surpasses what has already been taught? On the level of *content*, nothing here exceeds what exists in the Buddha's word and the commentaries. On the level of *literary form*, the existing texts — such as those of King Gewaí Wangpo — were composed with poetic skill, producing eloquent texts that delight scholars; Śāntideva disclaims equal poetic skill. Because both conditions for benefiting others are absent, the intention of benefiting others was not the purpose. The purpose is to cultivate bodhicitta in the author's own mind. MKS then raises an objection: if you have not internalised the text, you shouldn't compose it; if you have, what is the need to compose separately? He answers: for those who have already entered the path, composition is how cultivation is deepened and the stream maintained, beyond what silent reflection alone accomplishes.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-2)

### khenpo-kunga — Ngakwang Kunga Wangchuk (Tibetan)

NKW reads the verse as containing a self-deflation before the declaration of purpose. The two disclaimers match content and form: (1) the text contains nothing that first appeared here, since the existing sūtras and śāstras contain all the subject matter; (2) Śāntideva does not have the literary skill of, say, King Dharmarāja Gewaí Wangpo, who composed the Wishing Tree text with great poetic flourish. Because both conditions for benefiting others fail, the intention for others is denied. The real purpose stated: to cultivate and familiarize in his own mind. NKW notes the verse deflates pride in two directions — the pride of having something new to say, and the pride of being a skilled wordsmith — and that these two modes of pride are the main obstacles to genuine dharma-practice.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-2)

### Divergences

Whether verse 1-2 carries a genuine third purpose (for others' benefit) beyond Śāntideva's stated denial is debated. KKP notes that Vibhūticandra's praise shows the content is extraordinary and actually deeply beneficial to others — so the disclaimer of other-benefit is a formal deflation of pride rather than a literal truth. GDR and MKS concur. NKW takes the disclaimer more at face value as emphasising the primacy of self-cultivation. ⚑ No commentator reads it as meaning the text fails to benefit others; the disagreement is about whether the text also has an implicit intention to benefit others that the author understates.

- **རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་** — "རང་གི་ཡིད་" = རང་རྒྱུད། "བསྒོམ་" = གཞིར་བཟུང་ལ་ཡིད་བྱེད་ཅིང་འདྲིས་ཤིང་གོམས་བར་བྱ་བ། KS མཆན་གྱིས་"བསྒོམས་ནས་གོམས་པར་བྱ་བ" ཞེས་འགྲེལ་ཏེ་གོམས་པར་བྱ་བ་གཙོ་ཆེར་བསྟན།
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-2)

## Key Concepts (ཆོས་ཀྱི་གནད་ཚིག)

### ཚིགས་བཅད་ནང་གི་གནད་ཚིག

- **སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་** (verse-craft / poetic composition) — ལེགས་སྦྱར་གྱི་སྙན་ངག་གི་སྡེབ་སྦྱོར། གཞུང་གི་ཁྱད་ཆོས་ (རྗོད་བྱེད་) ཀྱི་ངོས་ནས་གཞན་ལ་ཕན་པའི་ཐ་སྙད།
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-2) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་_(verse-craft).md]]

- **བསྒོམ་/གོམས་** (cultivation / familiarization) — "བསྒོམ་" གཞིར་བཟུང་ལ་ལ་ཡིད་ལ་གཞག་ཅིང་རང་རྒྱུད་ལ་སྦྱངས་པ། "གོམས་" = ཡིད་ལ་བྱེད་ཚུལ་ཐོར་མ་མ་ཡིན་པར་ཐབས་ཤིང་བྱར་གཏོགས་ཏེ་གཞི་ལ་གཟུང་བར་བྱ་བ། KKP ལྟར་རྩོམ་པས་བསྒོམ་པ་ལས་ཀྱང་གོམས་པ་ལེགས་ཤེས་མར་འཆར།
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-2) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/བསྒོམ་གོམས་_(cultivation-familiarization).md]]

### འགྲེལ་པ་ནས་འབྱུང་བའི་གནད་ཚིག

- **འཆད་རྩོད་རྩོམ་གསུམ་** (explanation, debate, composition) — མཁས་པའི་བྱ་བ་གསུམ། གཏན་ལ་མི་འཁྲུལ་བར་གོམས་ཤིང་བློ་ལ་ཐིམ་བར་བྱ་བའི་ཚུལ་དུ་རྩོམ་པ་མཆོག་ཏུ་འགྱུར་བར་གསུངས་ (ཙནྡྲ་གོ་མིའི་ལུང་གིས།)
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-2) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/འཆད་རྩོད་རྩོམ་གསུམ་_(three-scholarly-activities).md]]

## Disambiguated Restatement (Tibetan)

ཞི་བ་ལྷ་ཞལ་ནས། རྒྱལ་བའི་བཀའ་དང་གཞུང་ལུགས་ཀྱི་རྒྱ་མཚོ་ལས་ (བརྗོད་བྱའི་ངོས་ནས་) སྔར་མ་གྲགས་པའི་དོན་ཅིའང་འདིར་བརྗོད་མི་ནུས། (རྗོད་བྱེད་ཀྱི་ངོས་ནས་) མཁས་མཛད་ཀྱི་སྙན་ངག་གི་སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་ལ་མཁས་པ་ཡང་ཡིན་མིན། དེ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་ཕྱིར་གཞན་ལ་གསར་དུ་ཕན་འདོགས་པར་བྱ་བའི་བསམ་པ་གཙོ་བོ་མ་ཡིན་ཞིང་། འོ་ན་གཞུང་འདི་ཅིའི་ཕྱིར་བྲིས་ཤེ་ན། རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་སྤྱོད་པ་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་ལྡན་བསྒོམ་ཞིང་གོམས་པར་བྱ་བའི་ཕྱིར་གཞུང་འདི་བྲིས་པ་ཡིན་ཏེ། ཁེངས་པའི་རྒུ་ཟུར་ལ་ཡོན་ཏན་གྱི་ཆུ་མི་འཛིན་ཞིང་བུད་ཀྱིས་མི་བསླུའི་ཕྱིར་ང་རྒྱལ་མི་བསྐྱེད་དེ། ཡང་ཞུམ་པས་ལེ་ལོར་མི་ལྟུང་བར་ཐབས་ཤེས་ཟུང་འཇུག་གི་ལམ་ལ་ཉམས་ལེན་བྱ་བར་གཞུང་ལ་འཇུག་གོ
