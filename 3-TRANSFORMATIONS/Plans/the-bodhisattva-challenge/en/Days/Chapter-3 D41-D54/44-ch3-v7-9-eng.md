## Today's Verse

> For as long as there are sick beings,
> until their illnesses are cured,
> may I be medicine, a doctor,
> and their nurse. ^3-7

> May I rain down food and drink,
> removing the suffering of hunger and thirst.
> During the eons of famine,
> may I become food and drink. ^3-8

> For beings who are destitute and poor,
> may I become an inexhaustible treasure.
> May all kinds of necessities
> appear before them. ^3-9

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 7 to 9 from the third chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. We turn this body of ours into medicine for the sick, food for the hungry, and a treasury for the poor. Then together we reflect on that vast loving mind which removes the suffering of beings.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a passage of scripture that is easy to understand and encouraging. The three verses of that passage match today's three verses exactly, in order: illness, famine, and poverty.

> In an intermediate eon of illness
> he becomes the supreme healing medicine.
> By that beings are set free,
> and become free of illness and happy.
>
> In an intermediate eon of famine,
> turning into food and drink,
> having taken away hunger and thirst,
> he teaches the Dharma to living creatures.
>
> For beings who are poor
> he becomes an inexhaustible treasure.
> And whoever he gives a gift to,
> he sets them to generating bodhicitta.

So Vimalakirti taught.

This clearly shows how the illness, the hunger and thirst, and the poverty of beings are removed. It also shows how they are set in bodhicitta.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will take medicine or food to someone who is ill, and help them.

**Explanation:** _(Doing good)_ The _Bodhicaryāvatāra_ says: for beings who are sick, until their illness is healed, may I myself become the medicine and the nurse. If today we visit someone who is ill and help them, it takes away another's suffering. It also increases the good heart of love and compassion in us.
