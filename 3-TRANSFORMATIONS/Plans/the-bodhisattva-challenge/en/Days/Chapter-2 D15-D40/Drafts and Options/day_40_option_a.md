# Day 40 — Let the body say it too

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Make one real bow today as you let something go
> **Detail:** The confession chapter closes. Today, let your body carry what the words point to.

## Opening

Chapter Two closes here, and it ends not with a thought but with a gesture. Shantideva stands before the buddhas and bodhisattvas, joins his palms, and bows again and again as he confesses. After all the reflection of these days, the chapter finishes with the whole body taking part. Today is a fitting place to let yours join in too.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*

> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>

> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

> The second chapter, “Confessing Misdeeds,” from Entering the Way of the Bodhisattva.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, keeps the verse concrete: standing right before the protectors, palms pressed together, a heart moved by the fear of suffering, bowing over and over, and confessing all of it. Notice that the mind is not left to do this alone. The body is enlisted, and there is real wisdom in that. A resolve held only in the head can stay vague and slip away by evening. When you actually lower your body, press your palms together, bow, something in you registers the turn that words alone cannot quite deliver. This is why the tradition says the physical act of prostration clears away obscurations of the body itself, not just the mind. The gesture is not empty ritual; it is the inner change made visible, and the making-visible is part of how the change takes hold.

## Today's Practice

Once today, let your body take part in the letting-go. Bring your palms together and make one slow, sincere bow to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and as you bow, quietly release one thing you have been carrying, a regret, a habit, a hardness. You do not need many. One real bow, with your whole self behind it, is how this chapter draws to a close.
