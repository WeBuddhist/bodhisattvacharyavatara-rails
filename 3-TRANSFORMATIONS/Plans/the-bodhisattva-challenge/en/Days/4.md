# Day 4 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: The Benefits of the Mind of Enlightenment — Verses 1-10 to 1-12

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Transformation of the Self (Inner Change):** The wish for enlightenment can change you deeply, like an elixir that turns iron into gold. Even a body that feels ordinary or impure can become the basis for a beautiful, awakened life. Holding this wish strongly helps you move forward on the spiritual path.

*   **Seeing True Value (Wisdom):** The Buddha looked carefully at all the ways to help beings, and found this wish for enlightenment to be the most valuable. When you know how rare and precious this wish is, it is easier to protect and grow it. This understanding helps you make better choices in daily life.

*   **Endless Growth (Lasting Virtue):** Ordinary good actions can fade, just like a banana tree that dies after it gives fruit once. But the wish for enlightenment is like a tree that never stops growing and giving fruit. Every good thing you do, when held by this wish, becomes lasting and keeps increasing.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-10**

> གསེར་འགྱུར་རྩི་ཡི་རྣམ་པ་མཆོག་ལྟ་བུ། །
> མི་གཙང་ལུས་འདི་བླངས་ནས་རྒྱལ་བའི་སྐུ། །
> རིན་ཆེན་རིང་ཐང་མེད་པར་བསྒྱུར་བས་ན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་རབ་བརྟན་ཟུང་། །
>
> *Like a supreme alchemical elixir, this mind takes our impure body and transforms it into the priceless body of a Buddha. Therefore, hold firmly to this precious mind of enlightenment.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-10**

The great teacher Śāntideva uses the image of a special elixir to explain the power of the wish for enlightenment. Just as one small drop of this elixir can change a thousand pieces of iron into pure gold, this wish can take an ordinary body filled with faults and transform it into something priceless and beautiful. Because this transformation is so amazing and powerful, it is important to hold onto this wish with great care and never let it go.

---

#### **Verse 1-11**

> འགྲོ་བའི་དེད་དཔོན་གཅིག་པུ་ཚད་མེད་བློས། །
> ལེགས་པར་ཡོངས་སུ་བརྟགས་ན་རིན་ཆེ་བས། །
> འགྲོ་བའི་གནས་དང་བྲལ་བར་འདོད་པ་རྣམས། །
> རིན་ཆེན་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ལེགས་བརྟན་པར་ཟུང་། །
>
> *The sole guide of wandering beings thoroughly examined this mind with immeasurable wisdom. He saw its immense value. Therefore, those who wish to escape worldly suffering must hold firmly to this precious jewel of enlightenment.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-11**

The Buddha, like a wise captain who guides travelers safely across the sea, looked deeply at all the ways to free beings from suffering. With great wisdom, he found that the wish for enlightenment is like a precious jewel — rare, hard to find, and of enormous value. Because this jewel is so powerful and special, anyone who truly wants to be free from the pain of ordinary life should hold it carefully and not let it slip away.

---

#### **Verse 1-12**

> དགེ་བ་གཞན་ཀུན་ཆུ་ཤིང་བཞིན་དུ་ནི། །
> འབྲས་བུ་བསྐྱེད་ནས་ཟད་པར་འགྱུར་བ་ཉིད། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ཀྱི་ལྗོན་ཤིང་རྟག་པར་ཡང་། །
> འབྲས་བུ་འབྱིན་པས་མི་ཟད་འཕེལ་བར་འགྱུར། །
>
> *All other virtues are like a banana tree that dies after bearing fruit once. In contrast, the tree of the mind of enlightenment continuously bears fruit and always grows.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-12**

The great teacher Śāntideva teaches that ordinary good actions — even very positive ones — are limited. They are like a banana tree, which grows, gives fruit once, and then dies. But the wish for enlightenment is like a wish-granting tree that never stops giving fruit. Every good action done with this wish becomes stronger and never runs out. Instead of finishing, it keeps growing and brings benefit again and again, both now and in the future.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Remembering the Value of My Inner Change:** Today, when I feel low or unworthy, I will remind myself that my wish to help others is like a powerful elixir. I will tell myself that this wish can change my mind and actions into something good and valuable.

*   **2. Protecting My Good Wish:** During the day, when I am faced with a choice, I will ask myself if my actions protect or weaken my wish to be a better person. I will try to make decisions that keep my wish for helping others strong and clear.

*   **3. Connecting My Good Actions to Others:** When I do something kind or helpful today, I will take a moment to connect it to a bigger purpose — the benefit of all beings. This way, even a small action becomes part of something that grows and does not run out.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམར་བར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Mind of Enlightenment (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས།):** This is the wish to reach full awakening in order to help all living beings. It is the central teaching of today's verses. The commentaries describe it as the most powerful and valuable thing a person can develop in their heart.

*   **Alchemical Elixir (གསེར་འགྱུར་རྩི།):** In the traditional texts, this refers to a special substance that can turn ordinary iron into pure gold. Here, it is used as a comparison to show how the wish for enlightenment can completely change and improve a person's body, speech, and mind.

*   **Sole Guide (དེད་དཔོན།):** This term means a captain or guide who leads travelers safely. In this verse, it refers to the Buddha, who is the only one with enough wisdom to lead all beings out of suffering. He found the wish for enlightenment to be the best tool for this work.

*   **Wish-Granting Tree (ལྗོན་ཤིང།):** This is a tree from Buddhist tradition that is said to give whatever a person needs without ever running out. Here, it is used to describe the wish for enlightenment, which always grows and produces good results, unlike ordinary virtues that eventually run out.

*   **Banana Tree (ཆུ་ཤིང།):** A banana tree is used in the teachings as a symbol of something that gives fruit only once and then dies. Ordinary good actions are compared to this tree — they produce some result but eventually come to an end. This contrast helps show why the wish for enlightenment is so much more valuable.
