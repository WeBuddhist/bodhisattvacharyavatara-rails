# Day 60 — Four snakes in one basket: why this borrowed body cannot wait for tomorrow

**Date:** Sep 3  
**Chapter:** 4  
**Verses covered:** 4-14 to 4-16

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the three verses 14 to 16 from the fourth chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. Carelessness makes us fall into the lower realms. Even when we get a human body, we are worn down by illness, imprisonment and the like. And four things are hard to bring together at once. They are a buddha appearing, faith, a human body, and the chance to grow used to doing good. So let us know that this body does not last, that it is like something borrowed, and hurry to do good.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

As Master Nagarjuna says in the *Ratnavali* and Master Aryadeva in the *Chatuhshataka*, this body of ours is a gathering of the four elements: earth, water, fire and air, and they sit together like four poisonous snakes put into one basket, each against the others, so the body is never steady for long.

Today's medical scans and tests show the same thing. There is no solid core in the body that stays firm. There are seventy-two thousand fine channels in our body, and if even one of them is cut it can become a cause of death.

So we should not put things off until tomorrow, resting on a body that may die at any time. From today itself we should work at the Dharma with great heart.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, knowing that this body does not last and is like something borrowed, let us work at the holy Dharma without putting it off.

**Explanation:** *(Practice of remembering that nothing lasts)* Today we may be free of illness and free of harm, yet life runs down moment by moment and deceives us. This body we hold so dear is also something borrowed for a short while. So while we have this rare human life, we should not put things off. We should work at the practice.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

If one asks what reason there is for the claim that what has never once occurred will not occur — if I go on acting just as before, then, again and again, just as before, even if I go to the lower realms or obtain a higher rebirth, I will experience being tormented by illness, being bound by a king's authority, having my limbs and ears and so on cut off, being cut in half, and so forth.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He counsels: even now we count ourselves among the followers of the Teacher and have some small acquaintance with, and understanding of, his teaching — if we fail to take this kind of opportunity to heart, and instead let an opportunity for Dharma practice slip by unmet even though it is available, then even should countless buddhas of the present and future come, it will be difficult to come within their healing sphere just as before. Although the Tathāgata's compassion is, on his own side, like an astonishing iron hook, if we ourselves do not have the complete condition of a "loop," so to speak, in our own minds for that hook to catch, we will have to experience limitless suffering in the lower realms even in the future as well.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-14]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

If, even now, I go on committing wrongdoing and unwholesome acts instead of accomplishing virtue, just as with my former inferior conduct, then, not just once but again and again, the same thing will happen: I will be born in the lower realms, and even if I become free of that and obtain a higher rebirth, the remainder of that karma will still ripen so that I experience the sufferings of being bound by many illnesses, chains, and the like, of being cut by swords, and of being slashed by weapons.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-14]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

As it is said. If one thinks there is no fault since one will be protected later — if I go on being careless just as before, then again and again I will fail, just the same, to come within the healing sphere, and, just the same, I will have to experience suffering after suffering in a continuous stream: remaining in the lower realms, or, even if born in a higher realm, being tormented by illness and bondage, or, in the lower realms, by illness and the cutting off of limbs and the cutting of the body, and boundless other sufferings.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-14]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If, even now, I am careless about the trainings just as with my former inferior conduct, then again and again, just the same, I will go on failing to come within the healing sphere: for the most part I will experience intense suffering in the lower realms, and even when I am occasionally born in a higher realm, I will experience many sufferings such as severe illness, untimely death, the cutting of limbs, being wounded by weapons, being bound, and being beaten.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-14]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

If one thinks: though I fall to the lower realms, since many buddhas will come, will they not, out of compassion for those who suffer from wrongdoing, protect me from my faults and rescue me from the lower realms? — countless and immeasurable buddhas who accomplish only the benefit and happiness of all beings have already come before and passed away, yet I, through the power of having become careless, allowed the trainings of my own mindstream to degenerate and committed many wrong deeds, and so was not within the healing sphere of those great physicians — the countless buddhas — who heal the illness of beings' bodies and minds; it is like a physician being unable to cure an incurable patient, or like rain falling from the gods onto a fire-scorched seed being unable to make it sprout.

Apart from that, it is of great benefit to meet a buddha when one's own mindstream is pure. If one thinks: even though this happened before, will I not be protected later? — not only was I previously outside the healing sphere, but even now, just as before, if I do not strive at the trainings I am to accomplish, and instead remain careless in my training, loosening my vows and failing to turn away from non-virtue, then, again and again, just the same, I will fail to come within the healing sphere, and, having fallen from one lower realm to a lower realm still, I will have to experience suffering following one wave after another like ripples on water; for the most part, having been born in the lower realms, I will experience immeasurable suffering to a great degree, and even when born in a higher realm because of that, I will experience many sufferings — many kinds of illness, untimely death, or being bound by ropes and the like, having limbs cut off by swords, having the body cut open by weapons, being killed, being beaten, and so on — or, alternatively, this may be read as applying to experiencing illness, bondage, cutting, and the like within the lower realms themselves.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-14]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

If, even now, just as before, I commit non-virtuous deeds such as killing driven entirely by afflictions such as attachment, then, again and again, just as before, as the result of that non-virtuous karma I will go to states such as the hell realms, the realm of hungry ghosts, and the animal realm; and even if, on occasion, I obtain a higher rebirth such as a human body, then, based on that, I will experience being tormented by illnesses of wind, bile, and phlegm and the like; or by a king's punishment — being bound by chains and ropes, being imprisoned; or by having limbs cut off, having the body cut in half, and other such intense sufferings, as is fitting.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-14]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Relying Again on Inferior Conduct Leads to Repeated Rebirth in the Lower Realms and, Even in a Higher Rebirth, to Illness, Bondage, and Mutilation

If I go on relying on inferior conduct just as before, then, not just once but again and again, I am born in the lower realms; and even when I obtain a higher rebirth, I experience many sufferings such as being tormented by illness, being bound in chains, having limbs cut off, and having the body cut open. All the commentaries agree on this.

###### 2. If One Does Not Strive at the Trainings, One Continually Fails to Come Within the Healing Sphere of Countless Buddhas

Minyak Kunzang Sonam illustrates this with the image of rain falling on a fire-scorched seed, unable to make it sprout: if one loosens one's vows, one must experience suffering following one wave after another like ripples on water.

###### 3. A Buddha's Ability to Ripen a Disciple Depends on That Disciple's Mindstream Being Fully Ripened

Minyak Kunzang Sonam illustrates this with the sūtra's image of the sun opening only an already-ripened lotus.

###### 4. The Merit of Diligence in the Trainings Exceeds Even Vast Offerings of Jewels to Countless Buddhas

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites both the *Sūtra on the Miraculous Display of Perfect Tranquility* and the *Candrapradīpa Sūtra* to show that the mere intention to train in the Dharma, or upholding even a single precept as the teaching declines, generates merit exceeding vast offerings to countless buddhas.

###### 5. Failing to Take the Present Opportunity to Heart Makes It Difficult to Come Within the Healing Sphere of Buddhas Even in the Future

His Holiness the Dalai Lama counsels that even if countless future buddhas were to come, it would be difficult to come within their healing sphere unless one's own mind has the complete condition — like a "loop" — for the Tathāgata's compassionate "iron hook" to catch.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that if I rely again on inferior conduct, then, again and again, I am born in the lower realms, and even when I obtain a higher rebirth, I experience many sufferings such as illness, bondage, and mutilation. All the commentaries agree on this basic ground.

**Key points.**

- Relying on the same inferior conduct as before leads not to a one-time consequence but to being born repeatedly in the lower realms, with residual suffering — illness, bondage, mutilation — persisting even into a higher rebirth.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam shows, through the image of rain on a fire-scorched seed and the sun opening a ripened lotus, that a buddha's power to help depends on the disciple's own mindstream being ready.
- The merit of even a moment's genuine intention to train, or upholding a single precept as the teaching declines, is said in scripture to exceed vast offerings of jewels to countless buddhas.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama counsels that failing to take the present opportunity to heart makes it difficult to come within a buddha's healing sphere even in the future, since compassion alone cannot act without the disciple's own readiness.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

A Tathāgata's arising in the world; one's own possession of faith; and, having abandoned the states of no leisure, obtaining a human body, together with the completeness of one's faculties and so forth, making one fit to grow accustomed to virtue — when such conditions become extremely rare, will they ever be obtained again? <!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Even granting that one could obtain such an excellent basis, complete with the eight leisures and ten endowments, again and again, and that even if one squandered the opportunity once or twice there might still be room to hope for another opportunity later on — that is not actually the case. In general, even a Tathāgata's arising is extremely rare; and even when one has arisen, it is difficult to generate faith on one's own part. Likewise, obtaining a human body complete with the faculties indispensable for accomplishing the Dharma, and being fit to grow accustomed to virtue, are rare — so he says one will obtain them again only after a long time, if at all; they are hard to obtain.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-15]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Second, regarding reflecting on the basis, the freedoms and endowments, in order to cultivate carefulness: this has three parts — reflecting on how difficult the freedoms and endowments are to find, in order to cultivate carefulness; reflecting on how difficult it is to be freed from the lower realms, in order to cultivate carefulness; and teaching that once the freedoms and endowments are obtained, one must strive at virtue. As to the first: like the udumbara flower, a Tathāgata's coming or arising in the world; having faith in his teaching; and obtaining the precious human body as one's basis — through these three in sequence, one is shown to be endowed, respectively, with the endowments of others and with one's own endowment, and with leisure through having abandoned the states of no leisure. Since favorable conditions are complete and adverse conditions absent, one is fit to grow accustomed to and accomplish virtue — and such a state is extremely rare to find; if it is not obtained just this once, will there be some other time when it is found and obtained? There will not. Therefore one must strive to make this obtaining of the freedoms and endowments meaningful.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-15]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

If one thinks one will strive when one obtains the freedoms and endowments later on: like the udumbara flower, a Tathāgata's arising in the world, together with faith in the Buddha's teaching — the three baskets and so forth — and obtaining a human body; and, having obtained it, being born in a central land, having complete faculties, and not having one's karma inverted at its very end, and the like — becoming one endowed with the basis fit for growing accustomed to virtue, complete with one's own endowments and the endowments of others, is rare. So the freedom and endowment for training the mind in two ways will be obtained — if at all — only at some future time, since it is hard to find.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-15]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

*(This span contains only the verse transclusion with no independent prose; Ngulchu Thogme's commentary treats 4-15 and 4-16 together after their consecutive transclusions — see the block cited under 4-16.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^4-15]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Like the udumbara flower, a Tathāgata's arising in the world, together with faith in his teaching, obtaining a flawless human body, and one's body and mind being fit to grow accustomed to virtue — such a state, when extremely rare, will be obtained, if at all, only at some time in the future; it is fitting to strive at the time one has obtained this supreme and hard-to-find opportunity.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-15]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Among the five endowments dependent on others, a Tathāgata's arising in the world is, as the *Compendium of Sūtras* explains at length, as rare as the udumbara flower; and by that same token the other four endowments dependent on others are also hard to find, and are shown here summarized by the word "and." Likewise, among the five endowments dependent on oneself: having faith in the taught holy Dharma, the three baskets; having abandoned the eight states of no leisure and obtained a human body in a central land; and, through having complete faculties and one's karma not inverted at its very end, having body and mind fit to be applied to and grown accustomed to the side of virtue — these five endowments dependent on oneself. Such a coming-together of the perfect qualities of the freedoms and endowments is rare, that is, extremely hard to find; will it, then, be obtained at some time or when? It will not. Therefore, at this present time when one has obtained this supreme and hard-to-find opportunity, one must strive on the side of virtue.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-15]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Moreover, the freedoms and endowments are difficult to obtain; and even at the time of obtaining them, a Tathāgata's arising, one's own faith and good intention obtained through faith in the Buddha's qualities, obtaining the thoroughly pure human body complete with the eight leisures and ten endowments, having complete faculties and limbs and meeting with conditions favorable to accomplishing the Dharma such as a spiritual friend — in short, being of a kind fit to grow accustomed to virtue in the proper way — is, by way of causes, examples, and numbers, extremely rare. #### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Coming-Together of a Buddha's Arising, Faith, a Human Body, and Fitness for Virtue Is Extremely Rare

All the commentaries agree in explaining that the coming-together of a Tathāgata's arising in the world, faith in his teaching, obtaining the precious human body, and being complete in one's faculties and fit to grow accustomed to virtue, is extremely rare by way of causes, examples, and numbers.

###### 2. One Must Strive to Make This Present Opportunity Meaningful

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden and Sabzang Mati Panchen both counsel that since it will not be obtained again at some other time, one must strive to make this obtaining of the freedoms and endowments meaningful.

###### 3. The Freedoms and Endowments, Examined in Detail as Five Endowments Dependent on Others and Five Dependent on Oneself, Are Extremely Rare

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains the rarity of the freedoms and endowments in detail by enumerating the five endowments dependent on others and the five dependent on oneself.

###### 4. The Sūtra on Entering Maitreya's Way Clearly Sets Out the Rarity of the Four States and the Counsel of Heedfulness

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains, from this sūtra, that even a human body, a buddha's arising, faith, and bodhicitta are each hard to find even across a hundred million kalpas, and that the great sages attained enlightenment through heedfulness.

###### 5. Even Generating Faith Is Difficult for Each Individual Being

His Holiness the Dalai Lama adds that even when a Tathāgata has arisen, generating faith on one's own part is difficult, further underscoring the rarity of the freedoms and endowments.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

In this way, even on a day such as today when the body is free of illness, with food, and free from the harm of thieves, snakes, wild beasts, and the like, life deceives us in an instant, suddenly, like a great bolt of lightning falling; and the body, not being within one's own control, is like a borrowed reflection for a moment — the Lord of Death separates one from it suddenly.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Even though we may have this kind of leisure on a day like today, free of illness and with favorable conditions gathered, there is no room to relax, thinking we may take our time — because life is a deceiver in an instant, and there is a real danger of its being cut off, moment by moment, deceptively. In the context of the *Ratnāvalī*, it is said that the body itself is also subject to the conditions of death; and likewise, in the context of the *Catuḥśataka*, an example is given: the four elements gathered within the body are, toward one another, in the nature of mutual enemies, like four extremely fierce and venomous snakes poured into a single vessel — if, at most, they simply refrain from fighting with one another for a while, that much alone counts as peace and harmony; there is no possibility whatsoever of anything beyond that, of one becoming a friend to another. So, as doctors explain regarding our own bodies, if one looks inside with an X-ray, it is clear there is nothing stable or firm. Even the slightest damage to one fine channel brings immediate discomfort; it is said that the seventy-two thousand fine channels are the seat of our life-force, in the limbs and vital organs. The more complex a mechanism is — like a wristwatch — the more easily it breaks down. The body is, for a while, for a short span of time, a kind of borrowed reflection or borrowed substance; it is not something that remains in an ever-reliable way.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-16]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Moreover, even when one has a day such as today, free from the adverse condition of illness, and possessed of the favorable conditions of food and the like, without being harmed by enemies or spirits, this present life, while one is alive, cannot be relied upon even for a single instant — it is a deceiver, being exhausted moment by moment; and since this body does not remain for long, it is, like a reflection for one moment or a borrowed ornament, not something one has permanent license to use — the Lord of Death, like its true owner, has no fixed time for taking it back.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-16]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

If one thinks: since this body one has obtained is free of adverse conditions and possessed of favorable ones, one will strive later — there is no room to relax. Since a human body free of the adverse condition of illness is like the sun, "such a day" may mean either this, or: even on a day such as today, free of illness, when favorable conditions such as food are present and adverse conditions such as harm are absent, life does not remain even for an instant but perishes swiftly, and so deceives; this body, like a reflection for a single moment, is not within one's own control — so one should strive right now, without relaxing.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-16]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

A Tathāgata's arising, having faith in the abode of his teaching, obtaining a human body, and being fit to grow accustomed to virtue — in short, when such a rare state, complete with the eighteen qualities of freedom and endowment, is obtained, will it ever be found again elsewhere? It will not. Even at the time of having obtained it, on a day such as today, free of illness, and with favorable conditions such as food present and adverse conditions such as harm absent, life cannot be relied upon even for an instant and so deceives; this body, like a reflection for a single moment, is not within one's own control. *(Note: this block, ^4-29, groups the explanation for 4-15 and 4-16 together after their consecutive transclusions.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^4-29]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Again, even while one has, for the time being, a day such as today, free of illness, and possessed also of gains such as food and clothing, without being harmed by humans or non-humans, this present life, while one is alive, cannot be relied upon even for a single instant — it is of the nature of a deceptive falsehood; since this body does not remain for long, it is like an ornament borrowed for one moment, and the Lord of Death, like its true owner, has no fixed time for taking it back.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-16]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

If one thinks: well then, is what one has found now, hard as it was to find, enough? — although one has found it, if one does not strive from this very moment, without wasting this opportunity, then on a day such as today, free of illness — which itself can become a condition for death to the body — when favorable conditions such as food and clothing sustaining one's life are present and adverse conditions such as harm from humans and non-humans are absent, life does not remain even for an instant, like a torrent rushing down a steep mountainside, and cannot be relied upon even for that brief span; it perishes swiftly and deceives — it is of the nature of deception. The body does not remain for long and is never, in a lasting way, within one's own power; like clothing or ornaments borrowed for a single moment that their true owner takes back whenever they wish, so too the Lord of Death, like that true owner, will separate body and mind from one another at an uncertain time — so one should not waste this opportunity, but strive at virtuous practice from this very moment.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-16]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Having generated the supreme aspiration to enlightenment, the reason it is fitting to accomplish its trainings with great swiftness is that this basis of the freedoms and endowments is not stable: even on a day such as today, whose very nature is that death may come at any time yet is free of illness, when there is no harm whatsoever, from food or otherwise, to body and life, one's present life is, by nature, deceptive, betraying one suddenly within an instant of time; at such a time, this very body is like a reflection for a single moment — like another person's possession borrowed for a while — and so it is taught to be of the nature that the Lord of Death may snatch it away without warning.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Even Today, Free of Adverse Conditions and Rich in Favorable Ones, Life Cannot Be Relied Upon Even for an Instant

All the commentaries agree in explaining that even when free of illness, with food and other favorable conditions present, and without harm from enemies or spirits, life is exhausted moment by moment and the body is never within one's own lasting control.

###### 2. One Must Strive Right Now, Without Waiting for a More Convenient Time

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both counsel that since the body is not within one's own control, there is no room to relax, thinking one will strive later — one must strive right now.

###### 3. The Deep Reason for the Body's Instability: The Four Elements Exist as Mutual Enemies Within It

His Holiness the Dalai Lama, citing the *Ratnāvalī* and the *Catuḥśataka*, clarifies through subtle reasoning that the body is impermanent, since its four internal elements exist as mutual enemies to one another.

###### 4. The Instability of the Body Can Even Be Seen Through Modern Medical Perspectives

His Holiness the Dalai Lama shows, through the examples of X-rays and the seventy-two thousand fine channels, that the body's lack of any stable core can be recognized even through present-day knowledge.

###### 5. Because the Basis of the Freedoms and Endowments Is Unstable, the Trainings Must Be Accomplished with Great Swiftness

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that because this basis of the freedoms and endowments is not stable, the trainings of bodhicitta must be accomplished with great swiftness.
