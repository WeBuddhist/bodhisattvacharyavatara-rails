# Day 1 — Shantideva wrote this only to train his own mind

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Catch yourself before your next sharp word
> **Detail:** Shantideva wrote this to train his own mind. Today, pause before a sharp word or a small lie.

## Opening

Chapter One praises the awakening mind. But Shantideva opens quietly. He bows, admits he has nothing new to say, and tells us he wrote this only to train his own mind. That honesty is where we begin.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

In the first three verses, Śāntideva bows with respect to the Buddhas, the bodhisattvas, and everyone else who deserves respect, and he makes his promise to write this text. Why should we bow to everyone worthy of respect? There is an old story that answers this. Long ago, the daughter of King Ajātaśatru was in truth an emanation of a bodhisattva. The great arhats were proud, and they would not bow to her — they treated her as no more important than a young fox. But in the end she overcame their pride and led them to the Great Vehicle, and they bowed to her. This story teaches us clearly that we should show respect to everyone, everywhere.

Śāntideva was not proud about how skilled he was with words and meaning. He said he wrote this text not so much to help others, but "to train his own mind" — to make his own heart familiar with the awakening mind (bodhicitta). This is like the saying, "the water of good qualities cannot stay on the ball of pride." It shows how important humility is, even for someone who is very learned. When we calm our own mind in this way and let our faith grow, little by little we also start to have a good effect on others like us, and something great is achieved. This is the deep meaning he is pointing to.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

The first kind of faith is simply trusting that what you do has real effects. Most of us believe that in theory. Today, just before a sharp word or a small lie, ask yourself whether you truly believe it in that moment.
