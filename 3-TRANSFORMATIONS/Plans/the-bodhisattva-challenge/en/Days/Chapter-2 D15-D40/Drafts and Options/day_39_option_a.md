# Day 39 — One question worth keeping

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Swap one worry-loop today for a useful question
> **Detail:** Today's verse offers a single question to hold: what feeds this, and how do I get free of it?

## Opening

The mind loves to circle a difficulty, and its loops usually go nowhere: why me, if only, whose fault is this. Shantideva offers one question to hold instead, day and night: much of my suffering grows from my own harmful reactions, so how can I get free of that root? It is not a question of blame. It is a question with a way out built into it.

## From the Tradition

Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, says to hold only this one thought, continuously. That word only is doing real work. The mind, left to itself, runs a dozen unhelpful loops around any pain, replaying, blaming, wishing it were otherwise. The verse asks us to trade all of that for a single question: suffering grows from harmful action, so how do I get free of the harm? Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen puts the object of the thought even more plainly, it is simply cause and effect, how our actions and our suffering are linked. Notice that this is not self-blame; it is the opposite. Blame ends in a dead feeling of being at fault. This question ends in agency: if there is a cause, there is something to work with. The whole point of tracing the pain to its root is that a root can be pulled.

## Today's Practice

The next time you catch your mind circling a hurt today, replaying it or hunting for who to blame, gently swap the loop for one question: what reaction of mine feeds this, and how might I loosen it? You do not need an answer on the spot. Just point the mind at the root rather than the wound, and notice that this direction has an exit in it.
