# Requirements — Bodhicaryāvatāra, Thai, word-by-word

## Target language
Thai, in Thai script. Modern standard written Thai; spaces only between phrases and sentences, as Thai is written.

## Audience and purpose
Readers who study the Tibetan text: students of Classical Tibetan, translators, practitioners who recite the Tibetan and want to know what each word means (see `audience.md`). Each segment becomes a table: every Tibetan word, in order, with its gloss in Thai.

## Word split
- Split each segment into its words, in order, so that the entries together reproduce the whole Tibetan segment: every syllable once, nothing skipped, repeated or changed.
- One entry per word: a noun, verb, adjective or adverb together with its own syllables; compounds and names stay together; a locked term whose words stand together stays one entry. Grammatical particles (case markers, connectives, the topic marker, quotation and final particles) get their own entries.
- The word split is fixed by the English word-by-word track (../en-wbw/): use exactly its `bo` entries, in order, and write only the glosses.

## Glosses
- Each gloss gives the meaning of the word in this verse, as the academic translation reads it, in a few words of Thai: nouns in their base form, verbs in a neutral form.
- A particle gets a short gloss of its function in Thai (the word Thai would use for "of", "by", "to", "and", "as for", or the like).
- For a word that is a term in `termbase.md`, the gloss is the locked academic rendering.
- Established Thai Buddhist vocabulary (Pāli and Sanskrit loans as Thai readers know them), as the termbase sets it.
- Proper names: the customary Thai Buddhist forms (ศานติเทวะ, มัญชุศรี, สมันตภัทร).
- No notes, brackets or explanations in a gloss.
