## 9. Chapter Nine: The Perfection of Wisdom ^9-0

All these limbs of practice,
The Buddha taught for the sake of wisdom.
Therefore, whoever wishes to calm
Suffering should generate wisdom. ^9-1

Conventional reality and ultimate,
These are considered the two truths.
Ultimate reality is not an object of the mind;
The mind is said to be conventional. ^9-2

In this regard, two kinds of people are seen:
Practitioners and ordinary people.
Among these, ordinary people
Are refuted by practitioners. ^9-3

Practitioners, too, by differences in their minds,
Are refuted by those of higher stages.
Both, however, rely on examples
And do not analyze for the sake of results. ^9-4

Ordinary people perceive things
And conceive them as truly existent.
Since they are not like illusions,
Practitioners and ordinary people dispute here. ^9-5

Direct perception of forms and so forth
Is by convention, not by valid cognition.
It is false, like the convention of purity
In impure things and so forth. ^9-6

For the purpose of guiding ordinary people,
The Buddha taught that things exist,
But they are not momentary in ultimate reality.
If you say this contradicts conventional reality: ^9-7

The conventional reality of practitioners is faultless;
Compared to ordinary people, they perceive ultimate reality.
Otherwise, if a woman is conceived as impure,
This would be refuted by ordinary people. ^9-8

How can merit from an illusory Buddha
Be like that from a truly existing one?
If beings are illusory,
How can they be reborn after death? ^9-9

As long as conditions are assembled,
So long will an illusion also arise.
How can beings be truly existent
Because they last for a long time? ^9-10

Killing an illusory person and so forth
Does not create misdeeds, because there is no mind.
But for an illusory being endowed with a mind,
Merit and misdeeds arise. ^9-11

Because mantras and so forth lack power,
An illusory mind does not arise.
The illusion that arises from various conditions
Is also of various kinds. ^9-12

A single condition is not capable of everything;
Such a thing does not exist anywhere.
If ultimate reality is nirvana,
And cyclic existence is conventional, then ^9-13

Even a buddha would return to cyclic existence,
So what would bodhisattva conduct accomplish?
If the stream of conditions is unbroken,
An illusion would also not cease. ^9-14

But when the stream of conditions ceases,
It does not arise even conventionally.
When delusion itself does not exist,
By what would an illusion be perceived? ^9-15

When you have no illusion,
What then is perceived?
If something else truly exists,
That form is the mind itself. ^9-16

When the mind itself is an illusion,
What sees what, and by what?
Even the Buddha, the protector of the world,
Taught that the mind does not see the mind. ^9-17

Just as the blade of a sword
Cannot cut itself, so too the mind.
Just as a lamp truly
Illuminates its own nature, ^9-18

A lamp does not illuminate itself,
Because it is not obscured by darkness.
Like a crystal that is blue
Does not depend on anything else for its blueness. ^9-19

Likewise, some things are seen
To depend on others, and some do not.
It is not blue, but it is blue;
It does not make itself blue. ^9-20

If one says that a lamp illuminates itself,
Knowing it by knowledge,
By what knowledge is it known
That the mind is inherently clear? ^9-21

When nothing is seen by anyone,
Whether clear or unclear,
Like the gait of a barren woman's daughter,
Speaking of it is meaningless. ^9-22

If there is no self-awareness,
How can consciousness be remembered?
When something else is experienced, it is remembered
Through connection, like the poison of a mouse. ^9-23

Since it is seen to be endowed with other conditions,
If it is self-illuminating,
From applying magical eye-salve,
Seeing a pot does not make it the eye-salve itself. ^9-24

Seeing, hearing, and knowing
Are not to be refuted here.
Here, what is to be averted
Is the true conception that causes suffering. ^9-25

An illusion is not other than the mind,
And if it is not other, it is impermanent.
How can it not be other if it truly exists?
If it is not other, it does not truly exist. ^9-26

Just as an illusion is not true,
So too the mind that perceives it.
If cyclic existence has a truly existing basis,
Then it would be like space elsewhere. ^9-27

Since non-existence relies on existence,
How can it be an agent and be endowed with it?
Your mind, without a companion,
Would be utterly alone. ^9-28

When the mind is free from grasping,
Then all are buddhas.
Even so, what benefit is there
In conceiving of mere mind? ^9-29

Even if one knows something to be like an illusion,
How can defilements cease?
When it comes to an illusory woman,
Even the creator himself generates attachment. ^9-30

The creator has not abandoned
The latent patterns of defilements regarding the object of knowledge.
Therefore, when he sees it,
The latent patterns of emptiness are weak. ^9-31

Through cultivating the latent patterns of emptiness,
The latent patterns of truly existing things will be abandoned.
And through cultivating "nothing exists,"
That too will later be abandoned. ^9-32

When the object to be investigated
Is not perceived as "nothing exists,"
How can non-existence, devoid of a basis,
Remain before the mind? ^9-33

When both existence and non-existence
Do not remain before the mind,
Then, with no other aspect,
It utterly calms into non-perception. ^9-34

Just as a wish-granting jewel and wish-granting tree
Completely fulfill hopes,
So too, by the power of aspirations,
Buddhas appear in forms to tame beings. ^9-35

For example, a shrine to a garuda,
Once built, even after it has passed away
For a long time, still pacifies
Poisons and so forth. ^9-36

Likewise, a buddha shrine built
In accordance with bodhisattva conduct,
Even after the bodhisattva has passed into nirvana,
Accomplishes all purposes. ^9-37

How can making offerings to something inanimate
Be fruitful?
Because it is taught that
Those who remain and those in nirvana are equally worthy. ^9-38

Whether conventionally or ultimately,
It is appropriate, and the result is from scripture.
For example, just as
There is a result from an existing Buddha. ^9-39

One is liberated by seeing the truth;
What is accomplished by seeing emptiness?
Because scripture teaches that
Without this path, there is no awakening. ^9-40

If the Mahayana is not established,
How is your scripture established?
Because this is established for both,
But for you, it is not established at first. ^9-41

The condition by which one believes in it
Is the same for the Mahayana.
If it is true because others desire it,
Then the Vedas and so forth would also be true. ^9-42

If you say the Mahayana is disputed,
Then your scriptures, like those of non-Buddhists,
And other scriptures, being disputed
By yourself and others, should be abandoned. ^9-43

If the root of the teaching is the state of being a monk,
Then the state of being a monk is also difficult to maintain.
And for those whose minds are focused on objects,
Nirvana is also difficult to attain. ^9-44

If one is liberated by abandoning defilements,
Then one would immediately attain it.
Even without defilements,
The power of karma is seen in them. ^9-45

If you say that grasping craving
Does not exist, as is certainly the case,
Why would craving not exist
Even if it is not a defilement, like general delusion? ^9-46

Craving arises from feelings,
And feelings exist even in them.
A mind focused on objects
Will remain in some state. ^9-47

A mind devoid of emptiness,
Having ceased, will arise again,
Like the absorption of non-perception.
Therefore, one should cultivate emptiness. ^9-48

If you consider any statement
That enters the sutras to be taught by the Buddha,
Why do you not consider most of the Mahayana
To be similar to your sutras? ^9-49

If, by the exception of one,
Everything becomes flawed,
Why would not everything
Be taught by the Buddha by the exception of one sutra? ^9-50

Any statement that
Great Kashyapa and others could not fathom,
Who would make it unacceptable
Because you have not understood it? ^9-51

For the sake of those suffering from ignorance,
Liberated from the extremes of attachment and fear,
Remaining in cyclic existence is accomplished.
This is the fruit of emptiness. ^9-52

Therefore, refuting
The aspect of emptiness is inappropriate.
Therefore, without doubt,
One should cultivate emptiness. ^9-53

Emptiness is the antidote to the darkness
Of the veils of defilements and objects of knowledge.
Whoever wishes to quickly attain omniscience,
How can they not cultivate it? ^9-54

If something that causes suffering
Gives rise to fear,
How can fear arise
From emptiness, which pacifies suffering? ^9-55

If there were some self,
Then fear would arise from anything.
But since no self exists,
Who is it that would be afraid? ^9-56

Teeth, hair, and nails are not the self.
Bones and blood are not the self.
Mucus is not the self, phlegm is not the self,
And lymph and pus are also not the self. ^9-57

Fat and sweat are not the self.
Lungs and liver are also not the self.
Other internal organs are also not the self.
Feces and urine are not the self. ^9-58

Flesh and skin are not the self.
Heat and wind are also not the self.
Pores are not the self. In every respect,
The six consciousnesses are also not the self. ^9-59

If the cognition of sound were permanent,
Then one would always perceive sound.
If there is no object of knowledge, what does it know?
By what is it called knowledge? ^9-60

If non-knowing is knowledge,
Then wood would also be knowledge.
Therefore, it is certain that
Without an object of knowledge present, there is no knowledge. ^9-61

If it knows form by itself,
Why then is it not also hearing?
If it is because sound is not close,
Then there is no knowledge of it either. ^9-62

How can the nature of the perceiver of sound
Be the perceiver of form?
One is conceived as father and son,
But it is not truly so. ^9-63

Thus, clarity, activity, and
Inertia are not sons, nor are they fathers.
It is not seen to be
Of the nature of the perceiver of sound. ^9-64

If it is seen in another aspect, like an actor,
Then it is not permanent.
If that other aspect is itself,
Then it is one, and that one did not exist before. ^9-65

If that other aspect is not true,
Then tell me its own nature.
If you say it is knowledge itself, then
All beings would be one. ^9-66

Sentient and insentient things
Would also be one, because their existence is similar.
When the distinction is contradictory,
What is the basis of similarity? ^9-67

Insentient things are also not the self,
Because they are insentient, like pots and so forth.
If you know it is sentient,
Then non-knowing would be destroyed. ^9-68

If the self is unchanging,
What has the mind done to it?
Thus, non-knowing and actionless,
Space would be made the self. ^9-69

If there is no self,
The connection of karma and its results is illogical.
Since karma is done and then ceases,
Whose karma would it be? ^9-70

The agent and experiencer are established for both
As having distinct actions and bases for results,
And also as having no self.
Is it not meaningless to dispute this here? ^9-71

This seeing that a cause
Is endowed with a result is not possible.
It is taught that the agent and experiencer
Are based on a single continuum. ^9-72

Past and future minds
Are not the self, because they do not exist.
If the present mind is the self,
Then when it ceases, the self also ceases. ^9-73

For example, if a plantain tree trunk
Is separated into parts, nothing is found.
Likewise, when analyzed
By thorough analysis, the self is also not truly existent. ^9-74

If there are no beings,
For whom should compassion be cultivated?
It is what is conceived by ignorance,
Accepted for the sake of results. ^9-75

If there are no beings, whose is the result?
True, but it is desired from ignorance.
For the sake of pacifying suffering,
The ignorance regarding results should not be averted. ^9-76

The pride that is the cause of suffering
Increases through the ignorance of a self.
If you say that it is not averted even from that,
Then cultivating selflessness is supreme. ^9-77

The body is not the feet or calves.
The thighs and waist are also not the body.
The belly and back are also not the body,
And the chest and shoulders are also not the body. ^9-78

The ribs and hands are also not the body.
The armpits and shoulders are also not the body.
The internal organs are also not it,
And the head and throat are also not the body.
What then is this body? ^9-79

If this body exists
In all its individual parts,
Then the parts would exist in the parts,
But where does it itself exist? ^9-80

If the self, which is the body of all,
Exists in the hands and so forth,
Then there would be as many bodies
As there are hands and so forth. ^9-81

If the body does not exist externally or internally,
How can the body exist in the hands and so forth?
If there is nothing other than the hands and so forth,
How can it exist? ^9-82

Therefore, the mind conceives a body
In the hands and so forth, which are bodiless, through ignorance.
Like the conception of a person in a scarecrow post
Due to a particular configuration of shape. ^9-83

As long as conditions are assembled,
So long does the body appear like a person.
Likewise, as long as it exists in the hands and so forth,
So long does the body appear there. ^9-84

Likewise, since it is a collection of fingers,
What then is the hand?
And since it is a collection of joints,
The joints are also separated into their own parts. ^9-85

The parts are also separated into particles,
And the particle is also divided by its directional parts.
Since directional parts are free from parts,
Like space, therefore, even particles do not exist. ^9-86

Thus, who with discernment
Would be attached to a form like a dream?
When the body is thus non-existent,
What person is there, and what woman? ^9-87

If suffering truly exists,
Why does it not harm intense gladness?
If there is happiness, why does it not
Cause gladness in grief and torment, like delicious tastes? ^9-88

If it is not experienced because it is overwhelmed
By something stronger,
Then what is not the experiencer itself?
How can that be feeling? ^9-89

Subtle suffering truly exists.
Is not its gross aspect removed?
If you say that is merely other happiness,
Then that subtle aspect is also its. ^9-90

If, due to the arising of conflicting conditions,
Suffering does not arise,
Is it not established that the conception of feeling
Is clinging to an object? ^9-91

Therefore, this analysis,
The antidote to this, should be cultivated.
The food of practitioners in meditation
Arises from the field of analysis. ^9-92

If there is space between the sense faculties and their objects,
Where do they meet?
If there is no space, they are one,
So what meets what? ^9-93

Particles do not enter particles;
There is no gap, they are equal.
There is no mixing in what has not entered,
And no meeting in what is not mixed. ^9-94

How can meeting
Be appropriate for something without parts?
If you see meeting
And something without parts, then demonstrate it. ^9-95

The meeting of consciousness with a bodiless thing
Is not appropriate.
Since a collection is non-existent,
As was analyzed before. ^9-96

Thus, if contact does not exist,
From what does feeling arise?
What is the purpose of this toil?
What harms what? ^9-97

When no experiencer exists,
And feeling also does not exist,
Then, seeing this situation,
Why would craving not cease? ^9-98

Seeing or touching
Is of the nature of a dream or illusion.
Since it arises simultaneously with the mind,
Feeling is not seen by it. ^9-99

What arises before and after
Is remembered, not experienced.
One does not experience oneself,
Nor do others experience it. ^9-100

No experiencer exists;
Therefore, feeling is not itself.
Thus, in this collection of selflessness,
Why would this harm it? ^9-101

The mind does not dwell in the sense faculties.
It is not in forms and so forth, nor in between.
It is not the mind internally or externally,
Nor is it found elsewhere. ^9-102

What is not the body, nor elsewhere,
Nor mixed, nor separate, nor anywhere,
That is nothing at all. Therefore,
The nature of beings is nirvana. ^9-103

If knowledge exists before the object of knowledge,
On what does it focus to arise?
If knowledge and the object of knowledge are simultaneous,
On what does it focus to arise? ^9-104

If it exists after the object of knowledge,
Then from what does knowledge arise?
Thus, the arising of all phenomena
Will not be understood. ^9-105

If conventional reality does not exist in that way,
Where are the two truths?
And if it is by another conventional reality,
How can beings pass beyond sorrow? ^9-106

This is the notion of another's mind;
It is not one's own conventional reality.
If it is later ascertained, then it exists;
Otherwise, conventional reality itself is non-existent. ^9-107

Conception and the object to be conceived
Are mutually dependent.
Just as all analysis
Is spoken of based on convention. ^9-108

When analysis
Analyzes by thorough analysis,
Then that thorough analysis
Is also without end, because it is analysis. ^9-109

When the object of analysis is analyzed,
There is no basis for thorough analysis.
Since there is no basis, it does not arise,
And that too is said to be nirvana. ^9-110

According to whom these two are true,
That is very difficult to maintain.
If things are established by the power of knowledge,
What is the basis for knowledge existing? ^9-111

If knowledge is established from the object of knowledge,
What is the basis for the object of knowledge existing?
If they exist by mutual dependence,
Then both would be non-existent. ^9-112

If there is no son, there is no father,
From what does sonship arise?
Without a son, there is no father;
Likewise, both are non-existent. ^9-113

Just as a sprout arises from a seed,
And the seed is understood by it,
Why is it not understood that
Knowledge, born from the object of knowledge, exists? ^9-114

If a seed is understood by knowledge
Other than the sprout,
From what is the existence of knowledge
That understands that object of knowledge understood? ^9-115

For the time being, all causes
Are seen by direct perception in the world.
The distinctions of lotus stems and so forth
Are produced by the distinctions of causes. ^9-116

If you ask what made the distinctions of causes,
It is from the distinctions of previous causes.
Why are causes able to produce results?
It is from the power of previous causes. ^9-117

If Ishvara is the cause of beings,
First, tell me who Ishvara is.
If you say it is the elements, that is true,
But what is the toil in merely naming? ^9-118

However, earth and other elements are many,
Impermanent, unmoving, not divine,
Trampled, and impure.
Therefore, that is not Ishvara. ^9-119

Ishvara is not space, because it is unmoving.
It is not the self, because it was refuted before.
And what is the use of speaking
Of an inconceivable creator by saying it is inconceivable? ^9-120

What is it that is desired to be created by it?
Is not the self, and earth and so forth,
And the essence of Ishvara also permanent?
Knowledge arises from the object of knowledge, and ^9-121

Beginningless pleasure and pain are from karma.
Tell me what is created by that.
If the cause has no beginning,
Where is the beginning of the result? ^9-122

Why does it not always create?
It does not depend on anything else.
If it is not created by it, and nothing else exists,
On what does it depend? ^9-123

If it depends, then a collection
Would be the cause, not Ishvara.
If there is a collection, it does not arise, because it is powerless,
And without it, there is no power to arise. ^9-124

If Ishvara creates without desiring,
Then it would be dependent on others.
If it desires, it would depend on desire,
So how can it be Ishvara if it creates? ^9-125

Those who assert that particles are permanent
Were also refuted before.
The Samkhyas assert that the permanent Primal Substance
Is the cause of beings. ^9-126

The equilibrium of the qualities called
Clarity, activity, and inertia
Is called the Primal Substance.
Disequilibrium is said to be beings. ^9-127

It is illogical for one to have three natures;
Therefore, it does not exist.
Likewise, qualities do not exist,
Because they are also threefold individually. ^9-128

If qualities do not exist, then sounds and so forth
Would also be very far from existing.
And happiness and so forth cannot exist
In insentient things like cloth. ^9-129

If phenomena are of the nature of their causes,
Are not phenomena already analyzed?
Your cause is also happiness and so forth,
And cloth and so forth do not arise from that. ^9-130

Happiness and so forth are from cloth and so forth.
Since that does not exist, happiness and so forth do not exist.
And happiness and so forth, as permanent,
Are never perceived. ^9-131

If clear happiness and so forth exist,
Why is experience not grasped?
If it is subtle,
How can it be gross and subtle? ^9-132

By abandoning the gross and becoming subtle,
Those subtle and gross things are impermanent.
Likewise, why do you not assert
That all phenomena are impermanent? ^9-133

If the gross is not other than happiness,
Then clear happiness is impermanent.
If you assert that nothing
Arises because it does not exist, ^9-134

You do not desire it to arise without being clear,
But it exists.
If the result exists in the cause,
Then eating food would be eating impurity. ^9-135

Buy cotton seeds with the price of cloth
And wear them.
If the world does not see it through ignorance,
Then know that it is established by itself. ^9-136

Since that knowledge also exists in the world,
Why is it not seen?
If the world is not valid cognition,
Then seeing what is clear is also not true. ^9-137

If valid cognition is not valid,
Will not what is measured by it be false?
Therefore, in ultimate reality, emptiness
Would be inappropriate to cultivate. ^9-138

Without touching a conceived thing,
Its non-existence is not grasped.
Therefore, whatever is a false thing,
Its non-existence is clearly false. ^9-139

Therefore, the notion that
A child in a dream has died, and that it does not exist,
Is an obstacle to the notion
That it exists, and that too is false. ^9-140

Thus, by such analysis,
Nothing exists without a cause.
It does not exist in conditions
That are individual or collected. ^9-141

It does not come from elsewhere,
It does not remain, it does not go.
What difference is there between this
That ignorance makes true, and an illusion? ^9-142

Whatever is created by an illusion,
And whatever is created by causes,
Investigate from where it comes
And where it goes. ^9-143

Whatever is seen
By being close, and is not absent if it is not,
Is like a fabricated reflection.
How can it have true existence? ^9-144

If a thing exists,
What need is there for a cause?
And if it does not exist,
What need is there for a cause? ^9-145

Even hundreds of millions of causes
Cannot transform a non-existent thing.
How can that state be a thing?
And what else would be a thing? ^9-146

If existence is not possible when non-existent,
When will a thing ever exist?
Until a thing arises,
Its non-existence will not cease. ^9-147

If it is not free from non-existence,
There is no possibility of a thing existing.
And a thing will also not be non-existent,
Because it would entail two natures. ^9-148

Thus, cessation does not exist,
And things also do not exist. Therefore,
All these beings
Are always unborn and unceasing. ^9-149

Beings are like a dream;
When analyzed, they are like a plantain tree.
Nirvana and non-nirvana
Are ultimately no different. ^9-150

Thus, in empty things,
What is gained, what is lost?
What is honored
Or completely disdained? ^9-151

From what is happiness or suffering?
What is there to dislike, what is there to like?
When investigated in ultimate reality,
Who craves, and for what? ^9-152

When analyzed, in this living world,
Who dies here?
What will arise, what has arisen?
And who are relatives and friends? ^9-153

Let me embrace all
As being like space.
Those who desire their own happiness
Are driven by causes of conflict and eagerness. ^9-154

They are greatly agitated and rejoice,
Grieve, strive, dispute,
Mutually cut and pierce,
And live with great difficulty through misdeeds. ^9-155

Repeatedly coming to happy realms,
And repeatedly enjoying many pleasures,
After death, they fall into
Long, unbearable sufferings in the lower realms. ^9-156

There are many chasms in cyclic existence.
There is nothing like this that is not itself.
There, too, by mutual contradiction,
There is nothing like this truly existing. ^9-157

That ocean of unbearable suffering,
Without example, is boundless.
There, one is so weak,
And life there is also short. ^9-158

There, too, by being alive and free from illness,
By acting, by the toil of hunger and thirst,
By sleep, by harm, and likewise,
By childishness and meaningless association, ^9-159

Life passes quickly and meaninglessly,
And discernment is very hard to find.
There, too, where is the means
To avert the habit of distraction? ^9-160

There, too, Mara strives
To make one fall into the great lower realms.
There are many wrong paths,
And it is hard to overcome doubt. ^9-161

Again, leisure is hard to find,
And buddhas are very hard to find.
The river of defilements is hard to abandon.
Alas, suffering will persist! ^9-162

Thus, though greatly suffering,
Those who do not see their own suffering,
These who dwell in the river of suffering,
Alas, they are worthy of lamentation! ^9-163

For example, someone repeatedly bathes,
And repeatedly enters fire.
Likewise, though dwelling in great suffering,
They imagine themselves to be happy. ^9-164

Thus, acting as if
There is no aging and death,
Having first killed themselves,
Unbearable downfalls in the lower realms will come. ^9-165

When will I pacify
Those tormented by the fire of suffering,
With showers of my own happiness,
Born from the clouds of merit? ^9-166

When will I accumulate
Merit with reverence in a non-objectifying way,
And teach emptiness
To those who are ruined by objects? ^9-167

This concludes the ninth chapter of Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct, "The Perfection of Wisdom." ^9-a
