# Day 62 — A blind turtle's neck through the ocean's yoke: why one moment of anger costs an eon in hell

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the two verses 20 and 21 from the fourth chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. Like a blind turtle's neck slipping through the hole of a yoke drifting on a great ocean, let us think closely about the good fortune of gaining this rare human body.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

The Buddha spoke to the monks like this: "Imagine the whole world turns into a great ocean. In it, a yoke with a single hole drifts about, moved by the wind in every direction. Deep in the ocean there is a blind turtle that comes up to the surface only once every hundred years. For that blind turtle's neck to slip through the hole in the drifting yoke is extremely hard." The Buddha said that gaining a human body is even harder than that. So, when we gain this precious human body, so hard to find, we should not fall under the power of carelessness and laziness. We should take the essence from the holy Dharma.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when anger toward others arises in me, I will catch my own mind right away and hold back from acting on hatred.

**Explanation:** *(Taming the mind)* Even a single moment of anger can bring great suffering in the lower realms. So we must always watch our own mind with care. If we give up hatred, we are freed from great wrongdoing. This is the best way to protect ourselves from the lower realms.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

The Teacher, the Blessed One, gave an example from the *Saṃyuktāgama*: suppose there were a golden yoke with a single hole, drifting aimlessly on a great ocean, and a blind turtle that came up to the surface of the water only once every hundred years. For that turtle's neck to happen to pass through the hole of that yoke on such waters would, in reality, be possible only at the outer limit of chance — an example of something exceedingly hard to encounter. In just the same way, he said, though it is so in reality, because we fall under the power of our own afflictions, there is not a single kind of birth in this cycle of existence in the three realms that we have not already taken; and apart from simply following after our karma, we have no freedom of our own as to where we are born. As Āryadeva says in the *Catuḥśataka*: "Ordinary people, for the most part, cling entirely to what is not virtuous; therefore individual beings, for the most part, are bound to go to the lower realms." Aside from falling, over and over, into the boundless lower realms, he said, obtaining this excellent basis on which the Dharma can be accomplished is exceedingly rare.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-20]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

It is for this very reason that the Blessed One, in the Sūtra of Genuine Instruction, gave an extreme example to consider: this entire ground becomes a great ocean, and on it drifts a yoke with a single hole, carried and tossed about in every direction by the wind. That a blind turtle, coming to the surface only once every hundred years, should happen to raise its neck through the hole of that yoke is possible only at the very outer limit of chance — because the ocean is vast, the yoke has no mind and cannot aim for the turtle, and the turtle has no eyes and cannot aim for the hole, and so on. Just as that is exceedingly rare, he said, obtaining a human body is harder still to attain — it is exceedingly difficult to obtain.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-20]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

It is precisely because it is so hard to obtain a higher rebirth once one has fallen to the lower realms that the Blessed One taught, in the *Saṃyuktāgama*: "Monks, suppose this great earth became a great ocean, and on it there floated a yoke with a single hole, moved and driven along by the wind; and suppose there were a blind turtle, and that turtle raised its neck to the surface only once every hundred years. Monks, what do you think — would it be easy for that turtle's neck to happen to pass through the hole of that yoke on the great ocean?" "Blessed One, it would not." The Blessed One said: "Monks, obtaining a human body is just as hard to attain as that." As he taught in this way, it is precisely because, if one does no virtue and accumulates wrongdoing, one will not even hear so much as the word for a happy rebirth, that the Blessed One gave the example of the great ocean: just as it is hard for a turtle's neck, coming up only once every hundred years, to happen to pass through the hole of a yoke that drifts back and forth across the great ocean, driven this way and that, so obtaining a human body is harder still to attain — so he taught, in order that we should strive at virtue while we have this opportunity of leisure and endowment.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-20]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

It is precisely for this reason that the Blessed One taught that obtaining a human body is extremely hard, like a turtle's neck happening to pass through the hole of a yoke drifting on a great ocean. From the *Saṃyuktāgama*: "Monks, suppose this great earth became a great ocean, and there floated on it a yoke with a single hole, moved by the wind and drifting about; and suppose there were a single blind turtle, and that turtle raised its neck to the surface only once every hundred years — monks, what do you think? Would it be easy for that turtle's neck to happen to pass through the hole of the yoke on the great ocean?" "Blessed One, it would not." The Blessed One said: "Monks, obtaining a human body is just as hard to attain as that."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^4-38]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

It is precisely for this reason that the Buddha, the Blessed One, said in the *Sūtra on Nanda's Going Forth*: "For example, consider this: this entire ground becomes a great ocean, and on it there is a yoke with a single hole, driven about by the wind; and within that ocean there is a wandering turtle, which comes to the surface only once every hundred years. That its neck should happen to pass through the hole is possible only at the outer limit of chance — because the ocean is vast and wide, because the yoke has only a single hole, because it is driven by the wind, and because the turtle has only one eye and rarely comes to the surface. Just as that is exceedingly rare, so too is it extremely hard, even among animals, to be born specifically as a human being."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-20]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Precisely because, once one falls to the lower realms, one only accumulates further wrongdoing there and accomplishes no virtue, so that one cannot even hear so much as the word for a happy rebirth for a hundred million eons, the Buddha, the Blessed One, taught that obtaining a human body is harder still to attain than a turtle's neck happening to pass through the hole of a single-holed yoke that drifts and is driven by the wind in every direction on a great ocean, its neck coming up to the ocean's surface only once every hundred years — this is explained at length by citing the *Saṃyuktāgama* in the *Compendium of Sūtras*. And the reason for this is that the ocean is vast and wide, the yoke has only one hole, it is driven by the wind in every direction, the turtle is blind, and it comes to the surface only once every hundred years, and so on. The *Sūtra on Nanda's Going Forth* also teaches this example together with the further example of a mustard seed scattered from the sky happening to pass through the eye of a needle set up in the sky. The *Bodhisattvapiṭaka* likewise says: "Just as a turtle's neck happening to pass through the eye of a piece of wood, so it is heard that a human body is extremely hard to attain." And Āryaśūra also says: "A turtle's neck happening to pass through the hole of a yoke drifting on a great ocean — just so, the Blessed One said, is a human body extremely hard to find." As for why it is so hard to find, the *Catuḥśataka* says: "Ordinary people, for the most part, cling entirely to what is not virtuous; therefore individual beings, for the most part, are certain to go to the lower realms" — because most happy rebirths, such as the human, also cling entirely to the unwholesome, such as the ten non-virtues, a human body is hard to find even apart from going to the lower realms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-20]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

It is precisely for this reason — that once one is born in the lower realms, one does not even hear so much as the word for a happy rebirth while abiding in a higher state — that the fully awakened Buddha, the Blessed One, made use of an example. For instance: suppose there is a great ocean equal in extent to a trichiliocosm of a billion worlds, greatly tossed about by the wind, and within it, as at the time of plowing a field, there is a yoke of about a fathom's length, of the kind used to bind an ox's neck, with a hole at its center; and suppose that deep within that ocean there is a single blind, wandering turtle, which comes up so that its head barely breaks the ocean's surface only once every hundred years. At such a time, for that very turtle's neck to happen to pass into the hole at the center of that yoke, which is being driven in every direction by the wind, is extremely difficult — and just as that example goes, so too, he said, is this very human basis extremely hard to attain once one has passed on from a lower rebirth. This same point is taught in the *Sūtra on Nanda's Going Forth*.

To explain the example and its meaning a little further: the vastness of the great ocean represents the abodes of the six classes of beings in cyclic existence in the three realms, which are vast indeed, like an ocean. The nature of the yoke's single hole represents the rarity or scarcity of the gateway to birth as a god or human in the higher realms. As for the blind, wandering turtle — though it is possible that some beings have karma similar to that needed to obtain a body in a higher realm, they are, in that respect, exactly like the eyeless, wandering turtle searching for the gateway to a higher birth; and as for the yoke that does not remain in one place but is driven by the wind in every direction — though it is possible that some beings occasionally have a karmic connection of the kind needed to obtain a body in a higher realm, the obstructing conditions to that are extremely numerous, and in that respect it is exactly like the yoke driven by the wind. It is by means of such reasons and this example that he taught this point.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Obtaining a Human Body Is as Rare as a Blind Turtle's Neck Happening to Pass Through the Single Hole of a Yoke Drifting on the Ocean

It is for this very reason that the Blessed One taught, through the example of a turtle's neck happening to pass through the hole of a yoke drifting on the great ocean, that obtaining a human body is extremely hard to attain. All the commentaries explain this in agreement.

###### 2. A Full Accounting of the Example's Reasoning Requires Three Factors Together: a Vast Ocean, a Yoke with Only One Hole, and a Blind Turtle

Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both spell out in detail why all three factors must come together, clarifying the point of just how rare this is.

###### 3. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk Correlates Example and Meaning Point by Point: Ocean, Yoke, and Turtle Each Explained Separately

The ocean represents the abodes of cyclic existence in the three realms; the yoke's single hole represents the rarity of the gateway to a higher rebirth; the eyeless turtle represents a being for whom obstructing conditions are so numerous that even an existing karmic connection is hard to bring to fruition.

###### 4. The Reason a Human Body Is Hard to Obtain Is That Most Beings Overwhelmingly Cling to Unwholesome Ways and So Fall to the Lower Realms

Minyak Kunzang Sonam and His Holiness the Dalai Lama both cite the *Catuḥśataka* to explain that the main reason a human body is hard to find is that individual beings, for the most part, engage in wrongdoing.

###### 5. The Commentaries Diverge on Which Scripture Is the Source of the Parable, Naming Two Different Sūtras

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Ngulchu Thogme, among others, name the *Saṃyuktāgama*, while Sabzang Mati Panchen and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk name the *Sūtra on Nanda's Going Forth*; Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites both. ⚑

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that obtaining a human body is comparable to, and just as rare as, a blind turtle's neck happening to pass through the single hole of a yoke drifting on the ocean — a rarity the Blessed One himself taught. All the commentaries agree on this basic ground, though there is also a difference over which scripture is named as the source of the parable.

**Key points.**

- Obtaining a human body is extremely rare because three factors must come together at once — a vast ocean, a yoke with only a single hole, and a blind turtle — as many commentaries explain in detail.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk correlates example and meaning point by point, explaining the ocean, the yoke, and the turtle each separately.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam adduces the further, distinctive example of a mustard seed scattered from the sky happening to pass through a needle's eye, adding to the point.
- The main reason a human body is hard to find is that most beings cling to unwholesome ways and so overwhelmingly fall to the lower realms — clarified by Minyak Kunzang Sonam and His Holiness the Dalai Lama through the *Catuḥśataka*.
- There is a divergence over the parable's scriptural source — named as the *Saṃyuktāgama* by some and the *Sūtra on Nanda's Going Forth* by others — though Minyak Kunzang Sonam draws on both. ⚑

### Verse 4-21

> **Rail source:** `2-RAILS/Verses/4-21-summary.md` &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Rail status:** `draft`

<!-- sub:root-verse -->
#### Root Verse

*Plain English translation (source: `3-TRANSFORMATIONS/Translations/en-translate/BCA-Full-Plain-English.md#^4-21`):*

> If even one moment of wrongdoing can lead to an entire age spent in the deepest hell, imagine what all the wrongdoing built up since beginningless time could do. How could that possibly lead anywhere good?

---

<!-- sub:interlinear -->
#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

One might ask, why should this be so? If even a single moment's wrongdoing — such as generating an evil thought toward a bodhisattva, an heir of the victorious ones — is enough to cause one to dwell in Avīci for an eon, then there is no need even to mention that the many wrongdoings accumulated in cyclic existence since beginningless time will prevent one from going to a happy rebirth.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Here the lifespan in Avīci is explained as one intermediate aeon, in accordance with the root verses of the *Abhidharmakośa*, which say: "That which is extremely hot, Avīci, lasts an intermediate aeon." If even accumulating a single moment's act of great and powerful wrongdoing requires one to abide in a place like Avīci for the length of a full intermediate aeon, then, given that we must still experience the ripening of that entire mass of faults accumulated throughout beginningless cyclic existence and not yet brought to ripen, there is no need even to mention how extremely difficult it would be to easily obtain the excellent basis of a happy rebirth.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-21]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Second: it is said that even the wrongdoing of generating an evil thought toward a bodhisattva — an act completed in a single instant, whether at the very end of one's life or simply the instant of completing the deed, comparable to the ānantarya crimes — will, for that reason alone, cause one to abide for a long time, for an intermediate aeon or more, in Avīci and to experience suffering there. If that is so, then, given the power of the non-virtuous wrongdoing we have accumulated in cyclic existence from beginningless time until now, it goes without saying — needs no further explanation — that this alone will cause us to experience unending suffering for a long time in the lower realms, let alone go to a happy rebirth. In general, when the cause is small but the result great, as with taking life, even though the time taken is short, the result is that one must experience the suffering of hell for a long intervening aeon; this being so, there is not the slightest amount of wrongdoing accumulated by any of us from beginningless time that has already been fully experienced and exhausted, or purified through confession, remaining in our own mindstream. Moreover, at this very moment, in this very body, is there even a single instant in which we have not, through evil thoughts, accumulated the wrongdoing that leads to rebirth in hell? For this reason it is difficult even to find a time, from the moment we were born until we die, when we might be freed from hell, given the wrongdoing we have accumulated. As for how karmic results are experienced, the *Abhidharmakośa* says: "Among those that revolve in the round of karma — the weighty, the proximate, the habitual, and that done first — of these, whichever comes first will ripen first." In accordance with this, of the earlier or later ripening of the results of virtue and non-virtue, whichever karma is to be experienced is that which is weighty, or, lacking that, close to the time of death, or, lacking that, habitual, or, lacking even that, whichever was done earliest — that is what is experienced first. Therefore there is no certainty that a person given to wrongdoing will, through some earlier virtue, obtain a happy rebirth at death, nor that a person endowed with the Dharma will, because of some earlier non-virtue, fail to be born in hell — because it is not certain that having once experienced confession, all one's earlier evil karma has thereby been purified and exhausted.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-21]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

If even the wrongdoing of a single moment's anger toward a bodhisattva is enough to cause one to abide in Avīci for an eon, then there is no need even to mention that the wrongdoing accumulated in beginningless cyclic existence, not yet destroyed by its antidote and still present in one's own mindstream, will prevent one from going to a happy rebirth.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-21]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

*(Ngulchu Thokme transcludes verses 4-21 and 4-22 together as one quoted block and comments on both at once; the explanation below covers both.)*

As for the phrase "wrongdoing done in a single moment" — according to the commentaries, which explain it in connection with the ānantarya crimes and the like, this refers to the very instant in which the deed is completed; and if it is done toward a special object, such as generating an evil thought, it may even be at the very end of one's life. If even that is enough to cause one to abide in Avīci for an eon, then how much more so, needless to say, will the wrongdoing accumulated in beginningless cyclic existence prevent one from going to a happy rebirth, let alone from going to a lower realm at all. Nor does merely experiencing that much result of one's earlier wrongdoing bring complete liberation from suffering — for even while experiencing it, one goes on generating and accumulating further wrongdoing, the causes of further suffering.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^4-41]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If it is said that even the wrongdoing of generating anger toward a holy object, or an ānantarya-type deed done in a single instant of completing the act, is enough to cause one to abide in Avīci for a long intermediate aeon, then there is no need even to mention that, bound by the wrongdoing and obscurations accumulated throughout beginningless cyclic existence, one will fail to go to a happy rebirth and will instead abide for a long time in the lower realms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-21]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

*(Minyak Kunzang Sönam transcludes verses 4-21 and 4-22 together and comments on both at once; the explanation and quotations below cover both.)*

If even the wrongdoing of an act toward an antidote-like object such as a bodhisattva — anger or malice arising for the mere duration of an instant, or an ānantarya-type deed such as one of the five heinous acts, completed within a single instant — is enough to cause one to dwell and suffer in the Avīci hell for a long intervening aeon, then there is no need even to mention that the many wrongdoings accumulated over the long duration of one's revolving through beginningless cyclic existence, whose ripening has not yet occurred and which remain, unconquered by any antidote, in great number in one's mindstream, will obstruct one from going to a happy rebirth and will instead require one to abide continuously in the lower realms for many eons. Even so, if one thoroughly purifies the causes of the lower realms already accumulated, and closes the door to accumulating new ones, then, while being born in a happy rebirth is not so very rare, being born as one described above is exceedingly rare. If one does not do so, and instead goes to the lower realms, then, once there, accomplishing virtue is exceedingly rare while wrongdoing accumulates continuously, so that one must pass through the lower realms alone for many hundreds of millions of eons — being born in a happy rebirth during that time is something one would not even hear the name of, and so it is exceedingly hard to obtain.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-21]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Even the wrongdoing of generating, or committing, a single instant of an evil thought of anger toward a holy object such as a bodhisattva causes one to dwell — or be born — in the Avīci hell realm for one eon for each instant of anger generated; this being so, there is no need even to mention that, through the wrongdoing and non-virtuous karma accumulated over beginningless lifetimes in cyclic existence, one is simply unable to go to, or take rebirth in, a happy state such as the realms of gods and humans, he taught.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-21]]

#### Scriptural Quotations

> "Among those that revolve in the round of karma — the weighty, the proximate, the habitual, and that done first — of these, whichever comes first will ripen first."
> — the *Abhidharmakośa* (Kunpal's naming; adduced to explain the four factors — weight, proximity, habituation, priority — that determine which of many accumulated karmas ripens first, grounding why a single grave act can send one to Avīci ahead of a lifetime's lesser sins.)

> "That which is extremely hot, Avīci, lasts an intermediate aeon."
> — the root verses of the *Abhidharmakośa* (Tenzin Gyatso's naming; adduced to fix the literal duration of Avīci at one intermediate aeon, grounding the verse's "aeon" in Abhidharma cosmology.)

> "Having committed wrongdoing for a single instant, one dwells in Avīci for an eon; having committed wrongdoing across many lifetimes, why even speak of how hard a happy rebirth would be to attain?"
> — Āryaśūra (Minyak Kunzang Sonam's naming; a verse restatement of the root verse's own a fortiori logic, in the commentator's own words.)

> "Though there may be many instants of preparatory activity leading up to an ānantarya-type act, the actual deed itself is accomplished in a single instant; or alternatively, though there may be many instants, because they are of such short duration they are spoken of as 'a single instant' — just as one might say that even a multitude of actions performed in the blink of an eye are done, so to speak, in the space of a single blink."
> — the Great Commentary (Minyak Kunzang Sonam's naming — Prajñākaramati's *Pañjikā*; adduced to clarify that "a single instant" can denote either the literal instant in which an ānantarya-type act is completed, or a short span of many instants loosely called "one instant" because it passes as quickly as a single blink.)

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Even a Single Instant's Evil Thought Requires Dwelling in Avīci for an Eon — Needless to Say What Beginningless Accumulated Wrongdoing Will Do

If even an act as brief as a single instant — generating an evil thought toward a bodhisattva, an heir of the victorious ones, or an ānantarya-type deed — is enough to require dwelling in Avīci for an eon, then there is no need even to mention that the many wrongdoings accumulated in cyclic existence since beginningless time will prevent one from going to a happy rebirth. All the commentaries explain this in agreement.

###### 2. The Abhidharmakośa Explains the Order in Which Karma Ripens through Four Factors: Weight, Proximity, Habituation, and Priority

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden cites the *Abhidharmakośa* and, having explained that even among the four factors it is the weighty one that may ripen first, connects this to the reasoning behind the verse's own logic.

###### 3. The Lifespan of Avīci Is Fixed at One Intermediate Aeon by the Root Verses of the Abhidharmakośa

His Holiness the Dalai Lama cites the root verses of the *Abhidharmakośa* to explain in depth the meaning of the verse's word "aeon."

###### 4. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk Correlates the Duration in Avīci Directly with the Number of Instants of Anger

He sets out the distinctive explanation that one must dwell in Avīci for one eon for each instant of anger generated.

###### 5. There Are Two Ways of Understanding the Phrase "a Single Instant"

Minyak Kunzang Sonam, citing the Great Commentary, sets out both understandings: that "a single instant" refers either to the very instant in which an ānantarya-type deed is actually completed, or to a span of many instants that, because it is so brief, is spoken of as a single instant. Ngulchu Thogme likewise explains it as either the instant in which such a deed is completed, or the very end of one's life when an evil thought arises toward a special object, in agreement.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that even generating a single instant's evil thought toward an object such as a bodhisattva, an heir of the victorious ones, or committing an ānantarya-type deed, is enough to cause one to dwell in Avīci for an eon; there is then no need even to mention that the many wrongdoings accumulated in cyclic existence since beginningless time will prevent one from going to a happy rebirth and will instead keep one abiding for a long time in the lower realms. All the commentators explain this in agreement.

**Key points.**

- "A single instant," according to the Great Commentary, refers either to the very instant in which an ānantarya-type deed is completed, or to a span of many instants that, because it is so brief, is spoken of as a single instant.
- Khenpo Kunzang Pelden cites the *Abhidharmakośa* to explain that the order in which karmic results ripen follows the weighty, the proximate, the habitual, and that done first, in that sequence.
- ⚑ On the duration of dwelling in Avīci, His Holiness the Dalai Lama holds, following the root verses of the *Abhidharmakośa*, that it is one intermediate aeon, while Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk holds that one must dwell in Avīci for a number of eons equal to the number of instants of anger generated.
- Because wrongdoing accumulated since beginningless time — not yet destroyed by an antidote and not yet brought to ripen — obstructs one, one is unable to be born in a happy rebirth and must instead abide in the lower realms for many eons.
