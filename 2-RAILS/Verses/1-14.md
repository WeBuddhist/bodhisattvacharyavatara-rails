## Traditional Interpretation

### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Palden (Tibetan)

KKP reads verse 1-14 as the fifth benefit. The first couplet: bodhicitta (*byang chub kyi sems*) is like the end-of-era fire (*dus mthai me*) — the conflagration that consumes everything up to the first dhyāna heaven (*bsam gtan dang po man chad kyi lha gnas thams cad*) without leaving even ash — in that it incinerates in a single instant (*skad cig gcig gis myong bya lhag med du nges par sreg pa'am dag par byed pas*) the great sins (*mi brgya bsad pa lta bu'i sdig pa chen po*) that have not already been addressed, leaving nothing of the karmic potential to ripen. The second couplet (*de yi phan yon dpag tu med pa dag / byams mgon blo dang ldan pas nor bzang bshad*) refers to the Gaṇḍavyūha sūtra, in which Maitreya the Protector, possessed of great wisdom (*blo gros chen po dang ldan pa*), taught Sudhana (*nor bzang*), the son of the merchant Nor-bzan, the immeasurable benefits of bodhicitta — 210 similes in the *Sdong po bkod pa* chapter. KKP explicitly defers the elaboration: "understand this from the Gaṇḍavyūha sūtra chapter."
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-14)

### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Tibetan)

GDR labels this the fifth benefit (*lnga ba ni*): "bodhicitta eliminates great sins with the fire simile." GDR draws a precise distinction from verse 1-13: verse 1-13 showed that bodhicitta *overrides* (*zil gyis gnon*) definitely-experienced karma (*myon nges kyi las kyi nus pa 'joms par byed nus*); verse 1-14 goes further — "if it can overcome definitely-experienced karma, *a fortiori* indefinitely-matured karma (*ma nges pa*) — so the latter can be eliminated from the root (*rtsad nas 'joms pa lta ci smos*). The fire simile: the end-of-era fire (*bskal ba 'jig pa'i dus mthai me*) incinerates everything from the first dhyāna downward without remainder. Bodhicitta similarly incinerates great sins (*dmyal pa skyed pa'i sdig pa chen po rnams*) in a single instant, definitively and from the root. The Gaṇḍavyūha quotation on bodhicitta's 210 benefits is adduced in support.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-14)

### ngulchu-thogmed — Ngulchu Thokme Sangpo (Tibetan)

NTS reads the verse in two parts. First couplet: the end-of-era fire (*dus kyi mthai mes bsam gtan dang po man chad kyi snod ma lus par sreg pa bzhin du*) — as this fire incinerates all vessels of existence up to the first dhyāna without remainder — so too bodhicitta (*de des ni mtsams med pa lnga la sogs pa'i sdig pa chen po rnams skad cig gis nges par sreg pa ste rtsad nas 'joms par byed de*) incinerates the five anantarya sins and the like in a single instant, eradicating them from the root. NTS then notes a possible objection: doesn't this contradict the preceding verse (1-13), which also said sins are purified? — and answers: bodhicitta's power increases progressively; the earlier verse showed *suppression* (zil gyis gnon pa), this verse shows *root-eradication* (*rtsad nas 'joms pa*). Second couplet: Maitreya the wise (*byams mgon blo dang ldan pas*) taught Sudhana (*nor bzang*) the immeasurable praises (*phan yon dpag tu med pa dag*) in the Gaṇḍavyūha, and NTS cites the Gaṇḍavyūha passage on Sudhana's arrival and Maitreya's extended praise of bodhicitta.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-14)

### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Mi Nyag Kunzang Sönam (Tibetan)

MKS gives the most extensive analysis. First couplet: the end-of-era fire is specifically the seven-sun conflagration (*nyi ma bdun shar ba'i mes*) that reduces everything from the first dhyāna downward to ash without leaving even a particle (*thal ba rdul phran tsam yang ma lus par sreg par byed pa de bzhin du*). Similarly, bodhicitta (*byang chub kyi sems des ni*) incinerates great sins that are capable of producing the unbearable suffering of hells and the like (*dmyal sogs kyi sdug bsngal mi bzad pa skyed nus pa'i sdig pa chen po rnams kyang dus skad cig gcig gis nges par te gdon mi za bar sreg par byed cing rnam smin rtsad nas 'joms par byed nus pas na*). MKS also records an alternative reading attributed to *dge lha*: "burning definitively in a single instant (*skad cig gcig gis nges par sreg par byed*) requires the attainment of great patience (*bzod chen*)" — i.e. the purificatory capacity increases with the quality of bodhicitta. Second part: MKS devotes extensive space to the Sudhana-Maitreya narrative in the Gaṇḍavyūha, citing multiple verses in which Maitreya extols Sudhana and then enumerates bodhicitta's benefits across a full enumeration of 210 similes.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-14)

### khenpo-kunga — Ngakwang Kunga Wangchuk (Tibetan)

NKW reads the first couplet as the fifth benefit. Bodhicitta, like the end-of-era fire that incinerates everything from the base of the wind-maṇḍala up to the first dhyāna (*'og gzhi rlung rdo rje rgya gram gyi dkyil 'khor yan chad nas bsam gtan dang po'i lha gnas man chad bya sgro me la sreg pa'am rtswa sbur mes 'tshig pa bzhin du thal ba rdul phran tsam du mi lus par sreg par byed pa'i dpe de bzhin du*) — in that way, great sins (*sdig pa chen po srog gcod pa dang rku ma brku ba la sogs pa ste ngan song du myong ba cung zad ma nges pa'i las ngan pa de dag thams cad*) are eliminated in a single instant, incinerated to the root so that the karmic ripening need not be experienced (*'bras bu rnam par smin pa myong mi dgos par sbyong bar byed pa'i rang bzhin can*). The second couplet hands off to the Gaṇḍavyūha: Maitreya the Protector, endowed with wisdom, taught Sudhana the son of merchant Nor-bzan these immeasurable benefits using 210 similes.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-14)

### Divergences

⚑ **Scope of "sins eliminated"**: GDR is the most explicit in differentiating 1-13 from 1-14: 1-13 = *myon nges kyi las* suppressed (*zil gyis gnon*); 1-14 = *ma nges pa* eradicated from the root (*rtsad nas 'joms*). MKS discusses an alternative framing (some say the reverse) but ultimately endorses GDR's distinction. NTS reaches the same conclusion. NKW conflates the two verses' scopes somewhat, calling the sins of 1-14 "those with uncertain ripening in the lower realms (*ngan song du myong ba cung zad ma nges pa*)."
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-14) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-14)

⚑ **Quality of bodhicitta required**: MKS records a position (*dge lha*) that root-eradication "in a single instant" requires the attainment of great patience (*bzod chen thob pa'i skad cig gcig gis*) — i.e. a high level of bodhicitta is needed for the full fire-simile power. Other commentaries do not specify this qualification; they speak in terms of bodhicitta generally. This divergence concerns the *applicability* of the simile to ordinary practitioners.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-14)

## Word-by-word Disambiguation (ཚིག་དོན་གསལ་བཤད།)

- **དུས་མཐའི་མེ་** — "the fire at the end of the era / end-of-era fire": MKS specifies this as the conflagration of the seven suns (*nyi ma bdun shar ba'i mes*) that arises at the time of universal destruction (*bskal pa 'jig pa'i dus*), incinerating everything from the base of the wind-maṇḍala to the first dhyāna. NKW also specifies: "from the base of the wind-maṇḍala through the first dhyāna heaven, like dry grass set alight, leaving not even dust."
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-14) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-14)

- **ངེས་པར་སྲེག་པར་བྱེད་** — "incinerate definitively": *nges par* = beyond doubt, completely; GDR and NTS: "rtsad nas 'joms pa" (eradication from the root) — the sin's karmic capacity is eliminated entirely, not merely suppressed or temporarily overridden as in 1-13.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-14) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-14)

- **བྱམས་མགོན་བློ་དང་ལྡན་པས་** — "Maitreya the Protector, endowed with wisdom": བྱམས་མགོན་ = Maitreyanātha / Maitreya the Protector (not the buddha Maitreya, but Asaṅga's teacher, the bodhisattva Maitreya). All commentaries identify this as the Maitreya who appears in the Gaṇḍavyūha / Sdong po bkod pa. བློ་དང་ལྡན་པས་ = "endowed with wisdom" (mkhyen rab / blo gros chen po), attributed to the bodhisattva's great discernment.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-14) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-14)

- **ནོར་བཟང་** — "Sudhana" (Skt. *Sudhana*): MKS and NTS give the full identification: the youth Sudhana (*gzhon nu nor bzang*), son of the merchant Nor-bztan (*tshong dpon nor brtan gyi bu*), who searched for the bodhisattva's practices (*byang chub sems pa'i spyod pa tshol*) and met 110 spiritual friends, culminating in the encounter with Maitreya in the palace Vairocana-vyūha at the shore of the southern sea.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-14) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-14)

## Key Concepts (ཆོས་ཀྱི་གནད་ཚིག)

- **བྱམས་མགོན་** (Maitreya the Protector) — In this verse = the bodhisattva Maitreya who appears in the Gaṇḍavyūha. All commentaries agree. Distinguished from the future buddha Maitreya in Tusita heaven.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-14) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/བྱམས་མགོན་_(maitreya-bodhisattva).md]]

> རིགས་ཀྱི་བུ་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ནི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་ཆོས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ས་བོན་ལྟ་བུའོ། །འགྲོ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་དཀར་པོའི་ཆོས་རྣམ་པར་འཕེལ་བར་བྱེད་པས་ཞིང་ལྟ་བུའོ། །འཇིག་རྟེན་ཐམས་ཅད་བརྟེན་པས་ས་ལྟ་བུའོ། །བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཡོངས་སུ་སྲུང་བས་ཕ་ལྟ་བུའོ། །དབུལ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཡང་དག་པར་གཅོད་པས་རྣམ་ཐོས་ཀྱི་བུ་ལྟ་བུའོ། །དོན་ཐམས་ཅད་ཡང་དག་པར་སྒྲུབ་པས་ཡིད་བཞིན་གྱི་ནོར་བུའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ལྟ་བུའོ། །བསམ་པ་བསམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཐམས་ཅད་ཡོངས་སུ་རྫོགས་པར་བྱེད་པས་བུམ་པ་བཟང་པོ་ལྟ་བུའོ། །ཉོན་མོངས་པའི་དགྲ་ཕམ་པར་བྱ་བ་ལ་མདུང་ཐུང་ལྟ་བུའོ།
> — སྡོང་པོ་བཀོད་པ་ལས། བྱམས་མགོན་གྱིས་ནོར་བཟང་ལ་གསུངས་པ་ལས། (Gaṇḍavyūha, Maitreya to Sudhana)

## Disambiguated Restatement (Tibetan)

བྱང་ཆུབ་མཆོག་ཏུ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་དེས་ནི་བསྐལ་པ་འཇིག་པའི་དུས་མཐའི་མེས་བསམ་གཏན་དང་པོའི་ལྷ་གནས་མན་ཆད་ཐལ་བའི་ལྷག་མ་ཙམ་ཡང་མི་ལུས་པར་སྲེག་པ་དེ་བཞིན་དུ། གོང་གི་ཚིགས་བཅད་ལས་ཁྱད་པར་དང་ (= ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་པ་མ་ཡིན་པར་) མ་ངེས་པའི་སྡིག་པ་ཆེན་པོ་ (= ངན་སོང་དུ་འཁྲིད་ཀྱི་འབྲས་བུ་རྣམ་སྨིན་མ་ངེས་པའི་ལས་ངན་པ་) རྣམས་དུས་སྐད་ཅིག་གཅིག་གིས་རྩད་ནས་ངེས་པར་འཇོམས་ཤིང་སྲེག་པར་བྱེད། བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ཀྱི་ཕན་ཡོན་དཔག་མེད་པ་དེ་དག་རྗེ་བཙུན་བྱམས་མགོན་བློ་གྲོས་ཆེན་པོ་དང་ལྡན་པས་གཞོན་ནུ་ནོར་བཟང་ (= ཚོང་དཔོན་ནོར་བརྟན་གྱི་བུ་) ལ་སྡོང་པོ་བཀོད་པའི་མདོ་ལས་དཔེ་ཉིས་བརྒྱ་སུམ་བཅུའི་སྒོ་ནས་རྒྱ་ཆེར་གསུངས་པ་ཡིན་པས། འདིར་བཤད་མ་ཟིན་པའི་ཕན་ཡོན་གཞན་རྣམས་དེ་ལས་ཤེས་པར་བྱའོ།
