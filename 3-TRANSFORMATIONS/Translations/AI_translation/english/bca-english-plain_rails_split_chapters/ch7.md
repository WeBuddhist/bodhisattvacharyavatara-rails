## 7. Chapter Seven: Practice of Diligence ^7-0

Having cultivated patience in that way, cultivate diligence.
Awakening dwells in diligence like this:
Like an unmoving place free of wind,
Merit does not arise without diligence. ^7-1

Diligence is enthusiasm for good deeds.
Its opposite will be explained:
Indolence, attachment to unwholesome things,
Despondency, and self-contempt. ^7-2

From tasting the flavor of ease and indolence,
And from craving that depends on sleep,
Weariness with the suffering of cyclic existence does not arise,
And indolence comes about. ^7-3

Once caught in the net of troubling emotions,
And fallen into the net of rebirth,
Having gone into the mouth of the Lord of Death,
Why do you still not know? ^7-4

Did you not see your own kind being gradually killed?
Yet, what is this dependence on sleep,
Like an outcaste and a buffalo? ^7-5

With all paths blocked,
And the Lord of Death watching,
How can you enjoy eating?
How can you enjoy sleeping like this? ^7-6

You will die soon,
So accumulate good deeds for as long as you can.
At that time, even if you abandon indolence,
What will you do when it is too late? ^7-7

"This was not done, this was begun,
This was left half-finished."
When the Lord of Death suddenly arrives,
You will think, "Alas, I am ruined!" ^7-8

With eyes red and tears streaming down your swollen face
From intense grief,
And your relatives having lost hope,
You will look at the face of Yama's messengers. ^7-9

When you are tormented by the memory of your misdeeds,
And hear the sounds of hell,
And your body is soiled with impurity from fear,
What will you do when you are overwhelmed? ^7-10

If you are afraid in this life,
Like a living fish writhing,
Then needless to speak of the unbearable suffering
Of hell, which arises from misdeeds. ^7-11

When touched by hot water,
A young person feels intense heat.
Having committed the actions that lead to hell,
Why do you remain so happily? ^7-12

One who desires results without diligence,
And is delicate, experiences much harm.
Seized by death, yet like a god,
Alas, overcome by sufferings! ^7-13

Relying on the human boat,
Cross the great river of suffering.
This boat is hard to find again,
So, foolish person, do not sleep at this time. ^7-14

Having abandoned the supreme joy of the sacred Dharma,
Which is the endless cause of joy,
Why do you delight in distraction,
Frivolity, and the like, which are causes of suffering? ^7-15

Non-despondency, strength,
Taking up, self-mastery,
Equality of self and others,
And exchanging self and others. ^7-16

Do not be despondent, thinking, "How could I attain awakening?"
For the buddhas, who speak the truth,
Declared this truth: ^7-17

A fly, a flesh-fly, a bee,
Or a worm, whatever kind of being,
If it cultivates the strength of diligence,
Will attain unsurpassed, hard-to-reach awakening. ^7-18

I, born as a human of good family,
Knowing what is beneficial and harmful,
If I do not abandon bodhisattva conduct,
Why would I not attain awakening? ^7-19

But if you are afraid that you must give up
Your limbs and so forth,
You are merely afraid out of ignorance,
Without weighing what is heavy and what is light. ^7-20

For countless eons,
You have been cut,
Pierced, burned, and torn many times,
But you will not attain awakening. ^7-21

The suffering I endure to accomplish awakening
Is limited,
Like the pain of lancing a wound
To remove a deeply embedded thorn. ^7-22

All doctors, through their treatments,
Eliminate the discomfort of illness.
Therefore, to destroy many sufferings,
Endure minor discomfort. ^7-23

Such ordinary medical treatments
Were not performed by the Supreme Sage.
With extremely gentle methods,
He performed countless great cures. ^7-24

The guide first taught giving
Vegetables and the like.
Having grown accustomed to that, later on,
One also gives one's own flesh. ^7-25

When the thought arises in one's body
That it is like vegetables and the like,
What difficulty is there then
In giving one's flesh and so forth? ^7-26

There is no suffering because misdeeds are abandoned.
There is no unhappiness because of wisdom.
For wrong thoughts
And misdeeds harm the mind and body. ^7-27

If the body is happy through good deeds,
And the mind is happy through wisdom,
Then even dwelling in cyclic existence for the benefit of others,
Why would compassionate beings be weary? ^7-28

This is said to be supreme by the hearers
Because the power of the Mind of Awakening
Exhausts former misdeeds
And gathers an ocean of merit. ^7-29

Therefore, mounting the horse of the Mind of Awakening,
Which dispels all weariness,
Who, being wise, would be despondent
When going from happiness to happiness? ^7-30

To accomplish the benefit of beings, strength,
Aspiration, confidence, joy, and relinquishment are needed.
Aspiration is generated by contemplating
The fear of suffering and its benefits. ^7-31

Having abandoned the opposing factors in that way,
One should strive to increase diligence
Through aspiration, confidence, joy, relinquishment,
Taking up, self-mastery, and strength. ^7-32

I must destroy my own and others' faults,
And for each fault,
An ocean of eons will be exhausted. ^7-33

If I have not seen even a fraction
Of the effort to exhaust faults,
Then I am the source of immeasurable suffering.
Why does my heart not break? ^7-34

I must accomplish my own and others' many good qualities.
If each good quality
Is cultivated for an ocean of eons, ^7-35

Yet I have never cultivated
Even a fraction of good qualities,
Then it is astonishing how I have wasted
This life, which is somehow obtained. ^7-36

I have not honored the buddhas,
Nor given the joy of great celebrations.
I have not performed actions for the teachings,
Nor fulfilled the wishes of the poor. ^7-37

I have not given fearlessness to the fearful,
Nor happiness to the wretched.
I have merely caused the pain of my mother's womb
And nothing but suffering. ^7-38

Because I lacked aspiration for the Dharma
In the past and now,
Such destitution has arisen.
Who would abandon aspiration for the Dharma? ^7-39

The Sage taught that aspiration
Is the root of all wholesome factors.
Its root is always
Cultivated by contemplating karmic results. ^7-40

Suffering, unhappiness,
Various kinds of fear,
And separation from desired things
Arise from committing misdeeds. ^7-41

By performing good deeds with the mind,
Wherever one goes,
There, by that merit,
One is honored with the fruit of good qualities. ^7-42

Misdeed-doers, though wanting happiness,
Wherever they go,
There, by those misdeeds,
They are utterly destroyed by the weapon of suffering. ^7-43

Dwelling in the heart of vast, fragrant, cool lotuses,
Nourished by the sweet speech of the victorious ones,
With supreme bodies born from lotuses, expanded by the light of the Sage,
One becomes a spiritual child before the victorious ones through virtue. ^7-44

By Yama's henchmen, utterly tormented, with all skin flayed,
Molten copper poured onto the body by intense fire,
Torn into a hundred pieces of flesh by blazing swords and spears,
And falling onto an intensely burning iron ground—this happens through many non-virtues. ^7-45

Therefore, aspire to virtue,
And cultivate it with reverence.
Having begun with the ritual of the Vajra Victory Banner,
One should cultivate confidence. ^7-46

First, having assessed one's capacity,
One should either begin or not.
Not beginning is best,
But once begun, one should not turn back. ^7-47

Otherwise, in other births, one becomes accustomed to it,
And misdeeds and suffering will persist.
At the time of the result, one will be inferior,
And it will not be accomplished. ^7-48

Confidence should be cultivated in three things:
Actions, troubling emotions, and capacity.
"I alone will do it!"
This is the confidence of action. ^7-49

This world, powerless over troubling emotions,
Cannot accomplish its own purpose.
Beings cannot do it as I can,
Therefore, I will do this. ^7-50

If others perform inferior actions,
How can I remain idle?
I will not act out of arrogance;
It is best for me to be free of arrogance. ^7-51

If a crow encounters a dead snake,
It acts like a garuda.
If I am weak,
Even a minor downfall will inflict harm. ^7-52

If one abandons effort with despondency,
Is there any escape from destitution?
By cultivating the effort of confidence,
Even a great one is hard to conquer. ^7-53

Therefore, with steadfast mind,
One must destroy downfalls.
If I am defeated by downfalls,
I will be a laughingstock to those who wish to conquer the three realms. ^7-54

I will conquer everyone,
And no one will conquer me.
I, a lion's child,
Will abide in this confidence. ^7-55

Those beings conquered by arrogance
Are not arrogant due to troubling emotions.
The arrogant do not fall under the sway of enemies;
Those others fall under the sway of the enemy of arrogance. ^7-56

One filled with the arrogance of troubling emotions
Is led to the lower realms by arrogance,
Deprived of human joys,
And becomes a servant who eats others' food. ^7-57

Foolish, unattractive, weak,
And always scorned.
If an ascetic filled with arrogance
Is counted among the arrogant,
Tell me, what kind of inferior person is that? ^7-58

Whoever cultivates confidence to conquer the enemy of arrogance,
That one is truly confident and a victorious hero.
Whoever utterly destroys the rising enemy of arrogance
Fulfills the desired results for beings, like a victorious one. ^7-59

Dwelling amidst the factors of troubling emotions,
One must stand firm in a thousand ways.
Like lions among jackals and the like,
One must not be overcome by the collection of troubling emotions. ^7-60

Even if great danger arises,
Just as one guards one's eyes,
Likewise, even if danger arises,
One must not fall under the sway of troubling emotions. ^7-61

Whatever action one undertakes,
Like desiring the delight of play,
One should be devoted to that action,
And rejoice, never sated with it. ^7-62

Even if one acts for the sake of happiness,
Whether happiness arises or not is uncertain.
If one does not perform the action itself, which becomes happiness,
How can one be happy? ^7-63

If one is not sated with desires,
Which are like honey on the edge of a razor,
Then why would one be sated with the peaceful
Merit that is the result of happiness? ^7-64

Therefore, to complete the action,
Like an elephant tormented by the midday sun,
Entering a lake when it finds one,
One should also engage in that action. ^7-65

If one is exhausted and connected with weariness,
One should abandon it for the sake of resuming it later.
If it is well-completed, one should give it up,
Desiring further and further. ^7-66

Like an old enemy encountered in battle,
One should avoid the weapon of troubling emotions,
And move to destroy the enemies of troubling emotions. ^7-67

If one's sword falls in battle,
One quickly picks it up out of fear.
Likewise, if the weapon of mindfulness is lost,
One should quickly recall the fear of hell. ^7-68

Just as poison, relying on blood,
Spreads through the body,
Likewise, if an opportunity is found,
Faults spread through the mind. ^7-69

Like someone carrying a pot filled with mustard oil,
With a sword-wielder standing nearby,
Fearing death if it spills,
A practitioner should likewise be vigilant. ^7-70

Therefore, if a snake comes into one's lap,
Just as one quickly stands up,
Likewise, if sleep and indolence come,
One should quickly ward them off. ^7-71

For each fault that arises,
Reproaching oneself, one should resolve
That this will never happen to me again,
And reflect on it for a long time. ^7-72

"How will mindfulness become a habit
In these circumstances?"
For this reason, one should desire
Appropriate actions, whether encountered or not. ^7-73

Before performing any action,
With the power that is in everything,
Remembering the teaching on carefulness,
One should make oneself light and ready to rise. ^7-74

Just as the wind controls cotton,
Making it go and come,
Likewise, controlling oneself with enthusiasm,
One will succeed in that way. ^7-75

This concludes the seventh chapter, "Practice of Diligence," from Engaging in Bodhisattva Conduct. ^7-a
