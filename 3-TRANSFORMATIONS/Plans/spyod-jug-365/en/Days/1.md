# Day 1 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: Benefits of Bodhicitta — Verses 1-1 to 1-3

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> 
> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Obstacle Pacification (Spiritual Progress):** Paying homage and taking refuge in the Three Jewels aligns our mind with the enlightened lineage, pacifying obstacles in our daily practice. This devotion generates vast merit, establishing a firm foundation for both temporary happiness and ultimate awakening.
*   **Humility and Pride Reduction (Mind Cultivation):** Contemplating Śāntideva's profound humility helps us overcome scholastic pride and intellectual arrogance. By acknowledging our own limitations and seeking forgiveness for faults, we cultivate a receptive, gentle mind that is ready to absorb the teachings.
*   **Self-Familiarization (Bodhicitta Habituation):** Reflecting on the author's motivation reminds us that true study is for self-cultivation rather than worldly fame. Setting down these realizations helps habituate our own minds with bodhicitta, while opening a path to benefit other fortunate practitioners.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-1**
> བདེ་གཤེགས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་མངའ་སྲས་བཅས་དང་། །
> ཕྱག་འོས་ཀུན་ལའང་གུས་པར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏེ། །
> བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་ནི། །
> ལུང་བཞིན་མདོར་བསྡུས་ནས་ནི་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ། །
> 
> *Paying homage with reverence to the Sugatas — the Dharmakāya and their heirs — and to all who are worthy of veneration, I shall now briefly describe, in accordance with scripture, the practice of entering the discipline of the Sugatas' heirs.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-1**
The three commentators agree that this verse represents the opening homage to the Three Jewels—the Sugatas (Buddhas), the Dharmakāya (Dharma), and their Bodhisattva heirs (Sangha)—and preceptors. This worship serves to eliminate obstacles, generate merit, and introduce the text's purpose of summarizing canonical scriptures on the Bodhisattva vows.

#### **Verse 1-2**
> སྔོན་ཆད་མ་བྱུང་བ་ཡང་འདིར་བརྗོད་མེད། །
> སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་མཁས་པའང་བདག་ལ་ཡོད་མིན་ཏེ། །
> དེ་ཕྱིར་གཞན་དོན་བསམ་པ་བདག་ལ་མེད། །
> རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་ངས་འདི་བརྩམས། །
> 
> *What is written here has not been said before, nor do I possess any skill in composition. Therefore I have no thought of benefiting others — I composed this solely to cultivate it in my own mind.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-2**
The commentators agree that Śāntideva expresses deep humility by denying any claim to originality or poetic genius, which serves to abandon pride and preemptively seek forgiveness. His primary motivation is self-benefit—specifically, to familiarize and habituate his own mind with the qualities of bodhicitta and the perfections to prevent spiritual decline.

#### **Verse 1-3**
> དགེ་བ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་བདག་གི་དད་པའི་ཤུགས། །
> འདི་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་རེ་ཞིག་འཕེལ་འགྱུར་ལ། །
> བདག་དང་སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཀྱང་། །
> ཅི་སྟེ་འདི་དག་མཐོང་ན་དོན་ཡོད་འགྱུར། །
> 
> *Through these writings, my faith in virtue may grow stronger for a while, and should another of equal fortune encounter this, perhaps it may also be of benefit to them.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-3**
The commentators' consensus is that while self-cultivation remains the immediate goal, the treatise holds a secondary, altruistic purpose of benefiting other Mahayana practitioners of equal fortune. Expressing this potential for mutual benefit inspires trainees to engage with the text with joy, while completing the author's demonstration of absolute freedom from pride.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Commencing Actions with Devotion and Homage:** Before starting any major task, study session, or meditation today, pause to pay mental or physical homage to your teachers, the lineage, or the values you hold highest. This establishes a respectful, focused mindset and aligns your intentions with positive outcomes, just as Śāntideva did.
*   **2. Practicing Intellectual Humility:** When sharing knowledge or working with others today, actively monitor and set aside any urge to show off or act superior. Remind yourself of your own limitations and state your ideas with genuine humility, acknowledging that your wisdom is built upon the works and kindness of others.
*   **3. Studying for Self-Transformation:** Focus today's study or practice entirely on your own mental habituation and inner growth rather than trying to impress others or gain social status. Let the primary aim be the familiarization of your own mind with virtue, trusting that any benefit to others will naturally flow from your genuine transformation.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**
> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །
> 
> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །
> 
> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **བདེ་གཤེགས། (Sugata / Gone to Bliss):** In Verse 1-1, this refers to the Buddhas. Commentators explain it in terms of perfect abandonment (leaving afflictions beautifully, without returning, and completely) and perfect realization (knowing reality directly, firmly, and without exception), distinguishing the Buddha from non-Buddhist ascetics and lesser vehicle Arhats.
*   **སྡོམ། (Vow / Discipline / Restraint):** In the phrase `བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་` (entering the discipline of the Sugatas' heirs) in Verse 1-1, this refers to the ethical vows and commitments of a Bodhisattva. It encompasses the training in the six perfections and the three types of moral discipline, representing the active engaging aspect of the path.
*   **གཞན་དོན། (Benefit of Others):** In Verse 1-2, Śāntideva states he has no primary intention of "other-benefit" (`གཞན་དོན་བསམ་པ་བདག་ལ་མེད།`). Commentators explain that while other-benefit is the ultimate outcome, he deliberately renounces any prideful claim of teaching others, framing his text as a tool for self-cultivation.
*   **སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ། (Equal Fortune / Similar Disposition):** Used in Verse 1-3 to describe other practitioners who share the same spiritual capacity, family, and aspirations as the author. If such fortunate trainees encounter and practice these formulations, they will find them highly meaningful and beneficial.
