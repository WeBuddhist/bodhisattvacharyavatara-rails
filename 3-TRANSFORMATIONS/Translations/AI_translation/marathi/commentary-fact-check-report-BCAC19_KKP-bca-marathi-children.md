# Fact-Check Report: KKP (Khenpo Kunpal) × Marathi Children's Translation

Prepared by: commentary-fact-check skill  
Commentary: `1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md`

Severity key:
- **ERROR** — translation contradicts or inverts the commentarial meaning
- **SOFTENING** — translation reduces intensity, scope, or doctrinal precision in a way that could mislead
- **NOTE** — editorial observation; no error

## Chapter 1 — Introduction / Bodhicitta Aspiration

### ERROR — verse 1-1
**Marathi:** "तू श्रद्धाळू आहेस, तर बोधिसत्त्वाच्या आचरणात प्रवेश घे."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP opens by explaining the title and the author's dedicatory purpose — Śāntideva is presenting a brief summation of entry into bodhisattva conduct *following scripture* (*lung bzhin*). The verse is a first-person authorial statement of intent.  
**Issue:** Same as NTS: Marathi converts authorial dedication into second-person exhortation, misrepresenting the verse's function.

### SOFTENING — verse 1-5
**Marathi:** "जसा आनंद आणखी वाढत राहतो."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP emphasises that bodhicitta is like a wish-fulfilling jewel (*yid bzhin nor bu*) in that it spontaneously produces all positive qualities — this is the precise basis of the comparison.  
**Issue:** Wish-fulfilling-jewel imagery dropped in Marathi, weakening the doctrinal force of why bodhicitta "always grows."

### SOFTENING — verse 1-28
**Marathi:** "पापांची माफी मागणे सुद्धा कठीण आहे."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP specifies the verse is saying that harming a bodhisattva creates an impediment to liberation lasting *for eons* because bodhisattvas are in the stream of the Buddha's activity.  
**Issue:** Temporal scale (eons) and specific object (bodhisattvas) absent from Marathi.

## Chapter 2 — Confession of Misdeeds

### SOFTENING — verse 2-1
**Marathi:** "मी मनापासून चांगली पूजा अर्पण करतो."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP cites the commentary (*rnam bshad*) listing the six qualities of perfect offering (same as NTS; see NTS report). KKP adds that the outer objects of offering are the Three Jewels and the inner attitude must be free from the three-sphere conception (*'khor gsum rnam dag*).  
**Issue:** All six qualifications and the inner/outer distinction are omitted. Appropriate for children; not for scholarly reference.

### SOFTENING — verse 2-24
**Marathi:** "माझे आई, वडील, गुरू यांना दुखावले."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP explains the verse enumerates the specific objects of harmful conduct in order of severity: the Buddha, the Dharma, the Sangha, then parents, teachers. Each has specific karmic consequences.  
**Issue:** Three Jewels entirely omitted from Marathi. This is a significant omission — KKP treats harm to the Three Jewels as distinctly graver than harm to parents/teachers and devotes more space to it.

### NOTE — verse 2-21
**Marathi:** "सतत सुंदर फुलांचा वर्षाव होत राहो."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP specifies the rain as *precious substances* (*rin chen*) — jewels, flowers, and fragrant offerings raining simultaneously on the Three Jewels. "सुंदर फुलांचा" (beautiful flowers) captures only one item.  
**Issue:** Minor simplification consistent with children's register; no doctrinal error.

## Chapter 3 — Taking Bodhicitta

### SOFTENING — verse 3-6 / 3-7
**Marathi (3-6):** "मी त्यांना औषध आणि डॉक्टर बनेन."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP expands: the aspiration is to become inanimate *and* animate sources of benefit — earth, water, fire, wind as inanimate; then medicine, physician, nurse (*snye ma*) as animate/personal. The inanimate category (becoming like the earth, etc.) immediately precedes this verse.  
**Issue:** Same as NTS report: "nurse/grass-tender" omitted, and the inanimate-benefactor context is absent.

### SOFTENING — verse 3-22
**Marathi:** "जे मला नाही माहीत, ते समजून घेण्याची शक्ती मिळो."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP explains this verse is asking for *discernment of all phenomena* (*chos thams cad mkhyen pa*) — i.e., omniscience, not merely personal understanding. This is a bodhisattva aspiration for omniscient knowledge of all phenomena for the benefit of beings.  
**Issue:** Marathi renders this as a personal request for understanding ("what I don't know"), reducing an omniscience-aspiration to an individual learning request. For children acceptable; for practitioners significant.

## Chapter 4 — Heedfulness

### SOFTENING — verse 4-11
**Marathi:** "तर बोधिसत्त्वाच्या भूमी मिळायला खूप उशीर होतो."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP gives a specific example to illustrate the delay: like a hermit who could attain the highest siddhi in seven days but causes a seven-month delay by secretly taking a sesame seed — a precise calibration of cause-and-effect in spiritual attainment.  
**Issue:** The teaching example is dropped. The Marathi's paraphrase is accurate but loses the precision of karmic cause-and-effect KKP uses here.

### SOFTENING — verse 4-14
**Marathi:** "बंधने, कापले जाणे, तुकडे होणे असे भोगावे लागेल."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP lists: in lower realms — disease (*nad*), bondage (*lcags sgrog*), being cut (*bcad pa*), being hewn apart (*bshags pa*) and similar sufferings.  
**Issue:** Disease (*nad*) omitted from first position in Marathi.

## Chapter 5 — Vigilance

### NOTE — verse 5-17 (guarding mental factors)
**Marathi:** "फुकटचे विचार करीत राहणे."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP distinguishes "pointless thoughts" (*rnam rtog*) as specifically including attachment (*'dod pa*), hatred, and delusion arising internally — not just idle thinking in general.  
**Issue:** Minor; Marathi's "pointless thoughts" is a permissible simplification.

## Chapter 6 — Patience (Kṣānti)

### SOFTENING — verse 6-1
**Marathi:** "एका रागानेच नष्ट होते."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP cites the *Mañjuśrīvilāsa-sūtra* to establish that a single flash of anger destroys merit accumulated over **a hundred eons** (*bskal pa brgya*). The root text says *a thousand eons* (*bskal pa stong*); KKP's sūtra citation uses a hundred eons for scriptural backing.  
**Issue:** The scale (hundred or thousand eons) is doctrinally central — both commentaries emphasise this magnitude to motivate patience practice. Marathi drops all quantification.

### SOFTENING — verse 6-5 (anger alienates friends)
**Marathi:** "असा कोणीही नाही."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP enumerates: an angry person (1) saddens all friends and relatives, (2) cannot gather retinue even through generosity, (3) lacks any comfort — "even a single happy person cannot be found among the angry" (*bde bar gnas pa de ni 'ga' yang ngam gcig kyang yod pa ma yin*).  
**Issue:** The enumeration is collapsed to a single line ("there is no such person"). Acceptable for children; substantive omission for practitioners.

### NOTE — verse 6-10 / 6-11 (fixable/unfixable)
Both KKP and NTS closely follow the root text's two-part logical structure. Marathi renders only one horn. See NTS report for detail.

## Chapter 7 — Diligence (Vīrya)

### SOFTENING — verse 7-1
**Marathi:** "तसेच उत्साहाशिवाय पुण्यही निर्माण होत नाही."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP states explicitly that diligence produces *both* the merit accumulation (*bsod nams kyi tshogs*) and the wisdom accumulation (*ye shes kyi tshogs*) — the "two accumulations" (*tshogs gnyis*). This pair is the explicit focus.  
**Issue:** Wisdom accumulation dropped. Same finding as NTS.

### SOFTENING — verse 7-2
**Marathi:** "आणि स्वतःला कमी लेखून खचून जाणे."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP names all three: laziness of procrastination (*snyom las*), laziness of attachment to base activities (*bya ba ngan pa la zhen pa*), laziness of self-deprecation (*sgyid lugs bdag nyid brnyas pa*). The Marathi verse captures only the third.  
**Issue:** Same as NTS finding.

### SOFTENING — verse 7-6 (fish and net / buffalo and butcher)
**Marathi:** "आणि कसे शांत झोपू शकतोस?"  
**KKP commentary:** KKP unpacks two vivid similes: (1) fish driven by fishermen into a net (*nya ba dang nya rgya*), analogizing afflictions driving beings into the net of rebirth; (2) buffalo (*ma he*) killed one by one while remaining unperturbed. These similes justify the exhortation "how can you sleep peacefully?"  
**Issue:** Both similes are dropped from the Marathi. The exhortation remains but its grounding is lost. For children, acceptable; the similes are striking teaching devices worth preserving where possible.

## Chapter 8 — Meditation (Samādhi)

### SOFTENING — verse 8-4
**Marathi:** "जी जगाच्या आसक्तीचा त्याग करून, मनापासून आनंदाने साधता येते."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP explicitly names *śamatha* (*zhi gnas*) and *vipaśyanā* (*lhag mthong*) as the paired method: śamatha as the basis (*rten*), vipaśyanā as the corrective force (*gnyen po*). The verse states that vipaśyanā overcomes all afflictions but requires śamatha; seeking śamatha first is the sequence.  
**Issue:** Same as NTS. The śamatha/vipaśyanā structure is absent from Marathi.

### SOFTENING — verse 8-5/6/7 (attachment to companions)
**Marathi (8-5):** "आपली प्रिय व्यक्ती पुन्हा दिसणार नाही."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP uses the *Lalitavistara* sūtra: "Just as drinking salt water, craving objects of desire never becomes satisfied" — to explain that attachment to companions not only causes present suffering (8-5/6) but creates craving that lasts for *a thousand lifetimes* (*tshes rabs stong*) without ever seeing the beloved again.  
**Issue:** The thousand-lifetimes scope and the sūtra simile are both absent. The Marathi correctly conveys "you won't see them again" but loses the temporal extent that makes the teaching urgent.

## Chapter 9 — Wisdom (Prajñā)

### SOFTENING — verse 9-2
**Marathi:** "कारण बुद्धी स्वतःच व्यावहारिक सत्याचाच भाग आहे."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP carefully distinguishes: *kun rdzob* (conventional truth) is the appearing aspect of phenomena (*snang tshul*); *don dam* (ultimate truth) is the actual mode of being (*gnas tshul*). The discursive mind (*blo*) apprehends only *kun rdzob*; the ultimate is not its object.  
**Issue:** Marathi's *buddhi* ("wisdom/intelligence") is an ambiguous rendering of *blo* (discursive cognition). The verse's intent is specifically that *ordinary discursive mind* — not wisdom/prajñā — is limited to the conventional. Using *buddhi* conflates ordinary mind with wisdom. The error is not gross but the doctrinal distinction (ordinary mind vs. prajñā) is precisely what Chapter 9 seeks to motivate.

### NOTE — verse 9-3 (two types of persons)
**Marathi:** "साधकांच्या समजुतीने खोडले जाते."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP identifies two types: (1) *rnal 'byor 'jig rten pa* — yogic practitioners (with śamatha/vipaśyanā); (2) *'jig rten phal pa* — ordinary worldlings. The Marathi "साधक" (practitioners) adequately captures this.  
**Issue:** None.

### NOTE — Chapter 9 overall
Chapter 9's philosophical density (two truths, refutation of Sāṃkhya/Vaiśeṣika/Mīmāṃsā, dependent origination, the Madhyamaka dialectic) is handled by condensation throughout. No additional ERRORs beyond the *blo* vs. *buddhi* issue at 9-2.

## Chapter 10 — Dedication

### NOTE — verse 10-1
**Marathi:** "बोधिसत्त्वाच्या मार्गावर चालू लागोत."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP explains that the power of aspiration prayers (*smon lam*) is what enables bodhisattvas to benefit beings across vast numbers of beings and lifetimes — and that the merit of this text must be dedicated to that aspiration.  
**Issue:** None. Marathi faithfully conveys the aspiration.

### NOTE — verse 10-4 (liberation of hell beings)
**Marathi:** "अत्यंत सुखी अशा आनंदाने आनंद मिळो."  
**KKP commentary:** KKP describes the aspiration in extraordinary detail: the hell beings' environment transforming into a pure land (*dag pa*) like the Sukhāvatī realm, with jeweled landscapes replacing torment; a lengthy passage describing the specific transformation of each hell.  
**Issue:** The specific Sukhāvatī / pure land transformation imagery is absent from Marathi. Acceptable simplification.

### NOTE — Chapter 10 overall
No ERRORs detected against KKP in Chapter 10.
