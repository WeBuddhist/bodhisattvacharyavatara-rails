## Today's Verse

> If that self is permanent,
> it is clearly inactive, like space.
> Even if it meets other conditions,
> it does not change, so what can be done to it? ^6-29

> If it remains as before when acting,
> what has the agent done to it?
> What connection is there
> to say that this is its action? ^6-30

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the twenty-ninth and thirtieth verses of the sixth chapter. We may think the person who harms us is bad by nature and never changes. But what could a thing that never changes at all, like space, do?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging.

Some people hold that a permanent self creates anger. But if that self is permanent, it cannot do anything, like space. Other conditions cannot give a permanent thing any new change. If change enters it, or if it depends on conditions, then it becomes something that does not last. So anger is not produced by a self that never changes. Anger arises from conditions that do not last.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will not label other people as bad by nature. I will give up anger.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ Harm comes from conditions that change, not from a permanent doer. So it is a mistake to see the person who harms us as an enemy by their own nature. The wisdom that looks at conditions calms anger.
