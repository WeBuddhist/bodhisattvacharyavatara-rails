## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three classical commentators agree that Verse 1-25 represents the logical and emotional climax of the preceding analysis, specifically building upon the a fortiori argument of Verse 1-24. Because ordinary sentient beings have never generated this supreme, altruistic aspiration even for their own self-interest, the actual birth of such a mind in a Bodhisattva is a wonder of the highest order—truly unprecedented (*apūrva* / *snga na med pa*) and a wondrous jewel of the mind (*cittaratna* / *sems kyi rin chen*).

### 1. The Unprecedented and Wondrous Nature of Bodhicitta
*   **Consensus:** The commentators emphasize that Bodhicitta is not merely an ordinary virtuous thought, but a completely transcendent, jewel-like transformation of the mind. Its arising in the mental continuum of a practitioner is an unprecedented event that deserves the highest praise.
*   **Gyaltsab Je** frames this explanation as the fourth and final part of his division on the benefits of the aspiring mind, which concludes with praise. He defines this mind as the wish to attain the state of a Buddha to accomplish the happiness and eliminate the suffering of all sentient beings. He highlights that because this mind does not arise in ordinary beings even for a single instant for their own sake, it is an "unprecedented, marvelous, and wondrous jewel of the mind." Based on this exceptional quality, Gyaltsab Je exhorts others to recognize its wonder and make diligent, active efforts to cultivate it.
*   **Ngulchu Thokme** presents a closely aligned view, linking this verse directly to the conclusion of his section on showing how the Bodhisattva's attitude is exceptionally distinguished from the world. He asserts that since this mind focuses solely on the benefit of others and does not arise in other sentient beings even for their own sake, it is indeed an "unprecedented, marvelous, and wondrous jewel of the mind."

### 2. The Analogy of the Wish-Fulfilling Jewel and Sovereign Benefits
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen's Unique Analogy:** Sazang Mati Panchen organizes his commentary under the thematic heading of "how it is obtained due to being a marvelous virtue" (the third part of his explanation). He introduces a powerful and unique analogy: in whichever sentient being's stream of being this mind arises, they become equal to a "supreme, precious wish-fulfilling jewel" (*yid bzhin nor bu*) that grants all needs and aspirations. Sazang Mati Panchen explains that because this mind does not arise even for one's own self-interest in ordinary beings, its birth in a Bodhisattva is an "unprecedented, marvelous distinction" that guarantees they will obtain immense, irreversible benefits.

### 3. Doctrinal and Structural Integration
*   **Consensus:** The commentators demonstrate how this verse bridges the preceding logical refutations of worldly selfishness with the subsequent praise of Bodhicitta's active qualities. By establishing that Bodhicitta is completely unique and absent in ordinary beings, they ground the necessity of the Mahayana path and the exceptional status of the Bodhisattvas as the ultimate refuge and source of joy for all wandering beings.
