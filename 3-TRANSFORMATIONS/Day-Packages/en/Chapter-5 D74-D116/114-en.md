## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and second and one hundred and third verses of the fifth chapter. Together, we listen to this life story of the young man Paljung relying on spiritual friends.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable point, which is easy to understand and encouraging. First, we must carefully examine who has the required qualifications and who does not. Then we must look for a good spiritual friend who has the qualifications, and rely on that friend. If we do not rely on a spiritual friend, our mind has no way to grow. We must rely on such a spiritual friend properly, with pure intention and pure action. The friend must be skilled in the Mahayana scriptures, but that is not all. It is taught that the main quality is pure ethics, staying in vows and keeping the trainings properly. We must not give up such a good spiritual friend, even for the sake of our life.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, I will practice according to the teachings of my own good spiritual friend, and serve my friend with respect.

**Explanation:** *(Taming the mind)* We must always rely on a good spiritual friend who is skilled in the meaning of the Mahayana and keeps the conduct of a bodhisattva. If we rely on a good spiritual friend and do not give them up even for the sake of life, our mind will grow. This brings great benefit to taming our own mind.

##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

First, having generated the supreme awakening mind, until one attains the state of great awakening, one must rely with respect, through the three doors, on the holy lama, the good spiritual friend, in whose company, at all times, all good qualities arise of themselves. As for the vehicle — of the three, what travels, where one travels, and by what one travels — it is the last of these. As the *Ornament of the Sutras* says: "A great focus, / likewise the two accomplishments, / wisdom, and being diligent in effort, / becoming skilled in method, / great perfect accomplishment, / and the great enlightened activity of the buddhas: / because it has these seven greatnesses, / it is definitely called the Great Vehicle." Thus the Dharma that has the seven greatnesses is the Great Vehicle; and good spiritual friends who are skilled in teaching the words and meaning of the profound and vast Great Vehicle, and whose conduct is the ethics of a bodhisattva, the supreme discipline, that is, who abide in the vows, one does not give up even for the sake of one's own body and life. Even if one's life is at stake, one must not do anything such as going against the lama's instructions or upsetting his mind, and one must not abandon reliance on the lama.

##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Always, the good spiritual friend — since one goes by this, it is a vehicle, that is, it is the two, method and wisdom. And as the *Ornament of the Sutras* says: "A great focus, / likewise the two accomplishments, / wisdom, and the undertaking of effort, / becoming skilled in method, / great perfect accomplishment, / and the great enlightened activity of the buddhas: / because it has these great ones, / it is definitely called the Great Vehicle." Because it has the seven greatnesses as stated there, it is the Great Vehicle; one who is skilled in its meaning and abides in the supreme discipline of a bodhisattva, that is, in the vows, one does not give up even for the sake of one's own life.

One must keep company with the holy good spiritual friend, relying on whom, at all times, all qualities, both temporary and ultimate, arise of themselves in one's own mind-stream; such a friend is their basis. Since one goes by this, it is a vehicle — the path that has the two, the profound and the vast, or method and wisdom. ##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

So it is said, and here the qualifications of a good spiritual friend are taught. A lama, a good spiritual friend, who has such qualifications — who himself has received the bodhisattva vows and not let them degenerate, and who abides in the bodhisattva trainings; who has the compassion to embrace disciples to be trained, and the ability to embrace them; and who has the fitness of expression to be able to confer the vows directly, through words — it is taught that one must take the vows mainly from someone like that. If one searches for such a lama and cannot find him — for instance, when it would endanger one's life or obstruct pure conduct, and no lama who is a good spiritual friend is available — then, if one knows how to take the vows oneself, one has faith that the buddhas and bodhisattvas are actually present in the space in front of one, and performs the ritual of the three parts, preparation, main part, and conclusion, properly in their presence. If one takes the vows in this way, one may take them; both the Mind-Only and the Madhyamaka traditions have accepted this.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Having generated the supreme awakening mind, one relies — not abandoning him even for the sake of one's own life — on a good spiritual friend who is skilled in the meaning of the Great Vehicle and abides in the supreme discipline of a bodhisattva, in order to accomplish the practice. By relying on such friends one gains benefit and happiness, in the short term the happiness of higher rebirth and in the end definite goodness; if one does not rely on a spiritual friend, the mind cannot grow.

**Key points.**

- The qualifications of a good spiritual friend are these: not having lost the bodhisattva vows once received; having the compassion and the ability to embrace the disciples to be trained; and being able to confer the vows through words.
- One should rely on a friend who is not merely skilled in the Great Vehicle scriptures but also has the quality of pure ethics: the discipline of a bodhisattva, the vows.
- As for the way of relying on a lama, following the biography of the youth Paljung in the *Array of Stalks*, one needs a mind like the earth, a vajra, a servant, a sweeper, a vehicle, a dog, a boat, and a wise son, and one must generate toward the spiritual friend the idea of a physician.
- If one searches for such a spiritual friend and cannot find one, one meditates on the buddhas and bodhisattvas in the space in front of one and takes the vows oneself.

##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

As for how to rely: in the *Array of Stalks* chapter of the *Avataṃsaka*, the bodhisattva Norzang relied on a very great number of good spiritual friends — a hundred and ten, and so on. When he came before the youth Palgyi Jungné and the maiden Palgyi Lodrö, the way of relying on a lama, a good spiritual friend, and praise of this, were taught in the biography of relying on spiritual friends, with respect, and these included: "Son of good family, you should generate toward yourself the idea of a sick person, toward the good spiritual friend the idea of a physician, toward the Dharma the idea of medicine, and toward diligent practice the idea of nursing the sick back to health." By this and so on, the way of relying was taught at length, and one must train and rely accordingly. By reading this biography of the youth Paljung and the other sutras that the Buddha spoke, one must come to know the dos and don'ts of how to rely on a good spiritual friend, and the other trainings of the bodhisattvas.

##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

As for how to rely: as in the way of relying on a lama in the biography of the youth Paljung in the *Array of Stalks* — a mind like the earth, since one does not weary of bearing every burden; like a vajra, since one's intention is not broken; like the Encircling Range, since one is not stirred by any suffering; like a servant, since one does not complain about undertaking any task; like a sweeper, since one gives up pride and great pride; like a vehicle, since one honors heavy burdens; like a dog, since one does not become angry; like a boat, since one does not weary of going and coming; and, by looking to the face of the good spiritual friend, with a mind like a wise son, one should serve and honor the good spiritual friend. "Son of good family, you should generate toward me the idea of a sick person. Toward the good spiritual friend, the idea of a physician; toward the instruction, the idea of medicine; and toward diligent practice, the idea of nursing the sick back to health." ##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

From the *Array of Stalks* chapter, which shows the manner of the biographies of the two, the youth Palgyi Jungné and the maiden Palgyi Lodrö: as those two taught at length to that very bodhisattva, the young man named Norzang, the way of relying on the holy lama, the good spiritual friend, so one must train. And the manner of it is, from that same sutra:

> "Son of good family, in order to accomplish great virtue, you should not grow weary of seeking a good spiritual friend. You should never be satisfied with seeing him. You should never think 'enough' in questioning him. One who is embraced by a good spiritual friend does not fall into the lower realms. One does not turn back from the Great Vehicle. One does not act against the trainings." — and from that passage on:
> "Son of good family, therefore, with unceasing mindfulness of this, you should go into the presence of the good spiritual friend. Because one does not weary of bearing every burden, with a mind like [text as given; see the note], you should serve and honor the good spiritual friend. Likewise, joining them: with a mind like a vajra, because the intention is not broken; with a mind like the mountains of the Encircling Range, because one is not shaken by any suffering; with a mind like a worldly servant, because one does not complain about undertaking any task; with a mind like a sweeper, because one gives up pride and great pride; with a mind like a vehicle, because one honors heavy burdens; with a mind like a dog, because one does not become angry; with a mind like a boat, because one does not weary of going and coming.
> Son of good family, you should generate toward yourself the idea of a sick person. Toward the good spiritual friend, the idea of a physician; toward the instruction, the idea of medicine; and toward diligent practice, the idea of nursing the sick back to health. Son of good family, you should generate toward yourself the idea of one who is afraid. Toward the good spiritual friend, the idea of a hero; toward the instruction, the idea of a weapon; and toward diligent practice, the idea of dispelling the enemy." — and so on; and:
> "Therefore, do not become attached to a single root of virtue. Do not devote yourself to a single light of illumination of the doors of the Dharma. Do not think it is enough to fully complete a single conduct of the victors' heirs. Do not take up the accomplishment of a single aspiration prayer with zeal. Do not generate the idea of 'enough' regarding the entry into the three kinds of patience. Do not ever let the full perfection of the six perfections be interrupted."

And so on — it was taught at length. Moreover, [the words ཡང་རྒྱ་དཔེར་དང་སྒྲ་ཡོད་ལ་དེས — unclear in the rail, left untranslated] it is said that what occurs in the biographies of the laywoman Immovable and the monk Ocean Banner, and so on, should also be gathered in; and in the *Compendium of Trainings* the biography of the heir Always Weeping and so on is also taught. These show the reasons why one must rely on a good spiritual friend, the benefits of relying, how to rely, and so on. As for another reason one must rely, from the *Jātaka*:

> "The holy are not kept at a distance by anyone; / through disciplined conduct, rely on the virtuous. / If one is near them, the dust of their qualities / will cling to one even without deliberately meditating on it."

So it says. This accords with the *Compendium of Trainings*, "not abandoning the good spiritual friend"; one should understand it in detail from that itself. These two, this verse and the following one on looking to the scriptures, show the special causes for training in the trainings and for the growth of wisdom; and it is said that these two also show the holding fast to the holy Dharma.

In the first blockquote the rail spells "does not fall" as མིལྷུང་, and in the second it spells "sweeper" as ཕུག་དར་བ (Ngulchu Thogme has ཕྱག་དར་བ); both are kept as given in the Tibetan companion file. The rail's own frontmatter note says this block quotes the *Gaṇḍavyūha* passages on devotion to the teacher.)*

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

"Learn according to the way of relying on a lama; / this and the other teachings the Buddha gave — / by reading the sutras, one should come to know them." So it is said. That is: as it appears in the biography of the bodhisattva, the youth Palgyi Jungné, and the maiden Palgyi Lodrö, "Son of good family, you should generate toward yourself the idea of a sick person, toward the good spiritual friend the idea of a physician, toward the Dharma the idea of medicine, and toward diligent practice the idea of nursing the sick back to health" — and so on, one should train just as the way of relying on a lama is taught at length. Moreover, this biography of Paljung and the way of relying on a lama taught by the perfect Buddha, and everything else, such as what shravakas are to train in, must be understood by reading the sutras of the greater and lesser vehicles — this is the meaning.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** It is taught that one should learn the way of relying on a lama or good spiritual friend according to the biography of Paljung. The many things to be trained in must be understood by reading those sutras.

**Key points.**

- In the biographies of the youth Palgyi Jungné and the maiden Palgyi Lodrö and so on, and in the *Avataṃsaka* sutra and the *Compendium of Trainings*, the way of relying on a spiritual friend is taught at length.
- In the *Array of Stalks*, in the biography of the youth Paljung, the way of relying on a spiritual friend is taught through the minds of the earth, the vajra, the sweeper, the vehicle, the dog, the boat, the wise son, and the idea of a physician, and so on.
- The maiden Palgyi Lodrö taught the youth Paljung that one must generate toward oneself the idea of a sick person, toward the spiritual friend the idea of a physician, toward the Dharma the idea of medicine, and toward diligent practice the idea of nursing the sick back to health.
