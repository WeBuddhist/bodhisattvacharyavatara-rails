# Day 9 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: Benefits of Bodhicitta — Verses 1-25 to 1-27

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **A Truly Rare and Precious Mind (Wonder of the Heart):** The wish to awaken for the benefit of all beings is very rare. Ordinary people do not produce this wish even for their own benefit. When this wish arises in a practitioner, it is a wonderful and precious event worth deep appreciation.

*   **A Source of Joy and Healing for All (Cosmic Benefit):** Today's verses teach that the wish to awaken is the cause of happiness for every living being. It acts like a powerful medicine that removes suffering. By reflecting on this, the practitioner begins to see how their own spiritual growth helps others.

*   **A Merit That Cannot Be Measured (Boundless Goodness):** Even a simple wish to help others has more value than making great offerings to the Buddhas. Actively working for the happiness of all beings creates merit that is beyond any limit. This understanding gives the practitioner great energy and confidence in the practice.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-25**

> གཞན་དག་རང་གི་དོན་དུ་ཡང་། །
> མི་འབྱུང་སེམས་ཅན་དོན་སེམས་གང་། །
> སེམས་ཀྱི་རིན་ཆེན་ཁྱད་པར་འདི། །
> སྔ་ན་མེད་པའི་རྨད་ཅིག་འཁྲུངས། །
>
> *This precious jewel of the mind arises in no other being even for their own sake. It is a wondrous birth that is truly unprecedented.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-25**

The wish to awaken for the sake of all beings does not arise naturally in ordinary people, not even for their own benefit. Because of this, when such a wish actually arises in a practitioner, it is like the appearance of a rare and priceless jewel. This precious mind is something completely new in the world, and the great teacher Śāntideva encourages every practitioner to recognize this wonder and work hard to keep it alive.

---

#### **Verse 1-26**

> འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན་གྱི་དགའ་བའི་རྒྱུ། །
> སེམས་ཅན་སྡུག་བསྔལ་རྩིར་གྱུར་པ། །
> རིན་ཆེན་སེམས་ཀྱི་བསོད་ནམས་གང་། །
> དེ་ལ་ཇི་ལྟར་གཞལ་གྱིས་ལང་། །
>
> *This jewel of the mind is the cause of joy for all wandering beings, and the medicine that cures their suffering. How can its immeasurable merit ever be measured?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-26**

The wish to awaken serves two great purposes for every living being: it is the cause of their happiness and the remedy for their pain. Just as a powerful medicine heals disease, this precious mind removes the suffering of all beings in the cycle of existence. Its merit cannot be counted or compared, because it reaches every being without exception, and that is why the great teacher Śāntideva asks: how could such goodness ever be measured?

---

#### **Verse 1-27**

> ཕན་པར་བསམས་པ་ཙམ་གྱིས་ཀྱང་། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་མཆོད་ལས་ཁྱད་འཕགས་ན། །
> སེམས་ཅན་མ་ལུས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི། །
> བདེ་དོན་བརྩོན་པ་སྨོས་ཅི་དགོས། །
>
> *Merely wishing to benefit others is far superior to making offerings to the Buddhas. What need is there to speak of striving for the happiness of all sentient beings without exception?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-27**

Even the simple thought "May I benefit others" is worth more than filling countless worlds with precious offerings for the Buddhas. If a single kind wish is already that powerful, then actively working to bring happiness to every single being creates merit that is completely beyond any limit. The great teacher Śāntideva uses this argument to show that there is no greater spiritual action than dedicating oneself fully to the well-being of all living beings.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Recognizing the Rare Gift:** Today, I will take a moment in the morning to remember how rare the wish to help others truly is. I will think: "This wish to awaken for all beings is precious, and I am lucky to have it." I will let this feeling of appreciation guide my actions through the day.

*   **2. Seeing Others as a Source of My Merit:** When I meet a difficult person or face a frustrating situation, I will remind myself that working for the happiness of all beings — including this person — creates boundless goodness. I will use this understanding to replace irritation with patience.

*   **3. Turning Ordinary Kindness into Great Practice:** Each time I do something kind today — helping a colleague, listening to a friend, or simply smiling at a stranger — I will think: "Even this small act of wishing to benefit others is greater than any grand offering." I will use this thought to bring more care and intention into even my smallest actions.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Precious Jewel of the Mind (སེམས་ཀྱི་རིན་ཆེན་):** This term describes the wish to reach full awakening for the benefit of all beings. Just as a rare jewel is priceless and hard to find, this wish is extremely rare and of the highest spiritual value. The great teacher Śāntideva uses this image to show that no ordinary treasure can compare to it.

*   **Unprecedented (སྔ་ན་མེད་པ་):** This word means something that has never happened before — truly new and rare. In this context, it describes the extraordinary event of the wish to awaken arising in a practitioner's heart. It signals that this is not an ordinary occurrence but a remarkable spiritual milestone.

*   **Cause of Joy for All Wandering Beings (འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན་གྱི་དགའ་བའི་རྒྱུ་):** "Wandering beings" refers to all living beings who move through the cycle of existence driven by confusion. The wish to awaken is called their cause of joy because it leads to the conditions that bring them happiness — both in this life and ultimately in full liberation.

*   **Medicine That Cures Suffering (སེམས་ཅན་སྡུག་བསྔལ་རྩིར་གྱུར་པ་):** The Tibetan word here (*rtsi*) refers to a powerful healing elixir. The wish to awaken is compared to this medicine because it has the power to remove the deep suffering that comes from confusion, craving, and negative actions. It treats the root cause of suffering, not just its symptoms.

*   **Striving for the Happiness of All (བདེ་དོན་བརྩོན་པ་):** This phrase means actively working — with effort and dedication — to bring happiness to every single living being. It goes beyond a simple wish and points to the engaged, ongoing practice of a Hero of Enlightenment. The great teacher Śāntideva uses it to show that this active commitment is the highest of all spiritual actions.
