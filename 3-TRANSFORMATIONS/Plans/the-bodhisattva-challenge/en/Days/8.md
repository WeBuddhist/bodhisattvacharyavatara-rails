# Day 8 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: Benefits of Bodhicitta — Verses 1-22 to 1-24

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Seeing the Big Picture (Expanding Compassion):** Wishing to help every single being is much greater than helping just one person. When you see how vast and boundless this wish is, your compassion naturally grows bigger and stronger.

*   **Finding Inspiration in Rarity (Appreciating What Is Precious):** No ordinary person — not even a loving parent, a wise teacher, or a powerful god — has ever had a wish this great. Knowing this helps you see how rare and precious the wish for enlightenment really is.

*   **Overcoming Self-Centeredness (Letting Go of Ego):** Most beings have never even dreamed of working for the good of all. Recognizing this helps you understand how deep self-centeredness goes. It also helps you feel grateful when you turn your mind toward helping others.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-22**

> སེམས་ཅན་རེ་རེའི་མི་བདེ་བ། །
> དཔག་ཏུ་མེད་པ་བསལ་འདོད་ཅིང་། །
> རེ་རེའང་ཡོན་ཏན་དཔག་མེད་དུ། །
> བསྒྲུབ་པར་འདོད་པ་སྨོས་ཅི་དགོས། །

> *What need is there to mention wishing to remove the immeasurable pain of every being, and wishing to bring them immeasurable qualities?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-22**

If wishing to cure a simple headache for a few people already brings great merit, then the merit of wishing to remove all suffering from every single being — and bring each of them limitless good qualities — is completely beyond measure. This verse uses a powerful comparison: the small act of kindness and the unlimited wish of the wish for enlightenment are as different as a candle and the sun. The wish for enlightenment holds both compassion (wanting to remove all suffering) and love (wanting all beings to have every happiness and virtue), and together they make its merit truly boundless.

---

#### **Verse 1-23**

> ཕའམ་ཡང་ན་མ་ཡང་རུང་། །
> སུ་ལ་འདི་འདྲའི་ཕན་སེམས་ཡོད། །
> ལྷ་དང་དྲང་སྲོང་རྣམས་ཀྱང་རུང་། །
> ཚངས་པ་ལ་ཡང་འདི་ཡོད་དམ། །

> *Who among our fathers or mothers has such a beneficial wish? Do the gods or ancient sages possess it? Does even the creator god Brahma have it?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-23**

Even the most loving people in the world — parents, close friends, wise sages, and powerful gods — do not have a wish as great as the wish for enlightenment. Parents love their children deeply, but that love is limited to just a few people. Even Brahma, the highest god in Indian tradition, who meditates on love and care for all beings, still only wishes for people's temporary happiness. He does not wish to lead every single being all the way to perfect awakening. The wish for enlightenment is different from all of these because it is unlimited, universal, and aims at the highest possible good for everyone.

---

#### **Verse 1-24**

> སེམས་ཅན་དེ་དག་ཉིད་ལ་སྔོན། །
> རང་གི་དོན་དུ་འདི་འདྲའི་སེམས། །
> རྨི་ལམ་དུ་ཡང་མ་རྨིས་ན། །
> གཞན་གྱི་དོན་དུ་ག་ལ་སྐྱེ། །

> *In the past, those beings never dreamed of such a wish for their own sake, even in their dreams. How could it ever arise for the sake of others?*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-24**

Ordinary beings have never even wished for perfect awakening for themselves — not even in a dream, when the mind is more free and relaxed. If they have never had this wish for themselves, it is even more remarkable when such a wish arises for the sake of all other beings. This shows just how extraordinary and rare the wish for enlightenment truly is. It does not arise naturally from self-centered minds. When it does arise, it is a wonderful and completely new kind of event — something that has never happened before in the mind of that person.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Widen My Circle of Care:** Today, I will notice when I only think about helping my own family or friends. When that happens, I will try to extend my wish to include more people — strangers, people I find difficult, and eventually all living beings.

*   **2. Remember How Rare This Wish Is:** When I feel discouragement or boredom in my practice, I will remind myself that the wish to help all beings is rarer than finding a great treasure. This thought will help me feel grateful and motivated to keep going.

*   **3. Check My Dreams and Daily Thoughts:** I will notice whether my thoughts during the day — or even before sleep — are mainly about my own comfort and goals. Each time I catch myself being self-centered, I will gently turn my mind toward wishing happiness for someone else instead.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Wish for Enlightenment (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས།):** This is the heartfelt wish to reach full awakening in order to help all living beings. It is not just a good intention — it is a complete change in the direction of one's life, turning away from self-interest toward the good of everyone.

*   **All Sentient Beings (སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད།):** This means every living being who can feel pain and happiness — not just humans, but all beings without exception. The great teacher Śāntideva reminds us that the wish for enlightenment includes every single one of them equally.

*   **Boundless Qualities (ཡོན་ཏན་དཔག་མེད།):** These are the countless good qualities — wisdom, kindness, patience, courage — that come with full awakening. The wish for enlightenment is the wish to help all beings gain every one of these qualities completely.

*   **Dream State (རྨི་ལམ།):** In this context, the dream state is used to show how deeply self-centered the ordinary mind is. Even in dreams, where the mind is free, ordinary beings have never wished for perfect awakening for themselves — making the wish for enlightenment all the more extraordinary when it does arise.

*   **Creator God Brahma (ཚངས་པ།):** Brahma is the highest god in traditional Indian belief, known for meditating on love and care for all beings. Even so, his wish is limited to giving beings temporary happiness. He does not aim to lead all beings to perfect and complete awakening, which is what the wish for enlightenment does.
