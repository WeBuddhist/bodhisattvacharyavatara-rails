# Day 13 — Why a moment of anger can cost so much

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Catch one flash of ill-will before it grows
> **Detail:** A moment of anger at a good person carries real weight. Today, catch one early and let it go.

## Opening

Yesterday's verse showed how much good a bodhisattva does. Today's turns to the opposite side. Because such a person is so valuable, even a single angry thought aimed at them carries great weight, the verse says, long ages of suffering for each moment of anger. It is a warning to take our own minds seriously.

> If anyone harbors a negative mind toward such a child of the Buddha, they will dwell in hell for eons. The Sage said they will remain there for as many eons as there were moments of that negative mind.

## From the Tradition

**Gyaltsab Je**, a fifteenth-century teacher, explains why anger at such a person is treated as the gravest kind of harm. Every buddha, he says, was once a bodhisattva. So a bodhisattva is the very source from which buddhas come. To wish one harm is to strike at the root of all the good that would have grown from them, for countless beings. That is why the verse asks us to be so careful here.

## Today's Practice

Watch for the moment today when irritation or contempt rises toward someone. You don't have to fix it or feel bad about it. Just catch it early, before it builds, and let it pass.
