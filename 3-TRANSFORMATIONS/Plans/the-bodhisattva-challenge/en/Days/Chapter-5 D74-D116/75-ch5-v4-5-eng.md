## Today's Verse

> Tigers, lions, elephants, bears,
> snakes, and all enemies,
> the guardians of the hell realms,
> and likewise sorcerers and demons— ^5-4

> By binding this single mind,
> all those are bound.
> By taming this single mind,
> all those are tamed. ^5-5

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the fourth and fifth verses of the fifth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Let us look together at this amazing method: guarding just our own mind protects us from fearsome tigers and many evil spirits.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago, a bodhisattva who was a sea-captain went to sea to gather jewels. He lost his way and arrived at Simhala Island, a city of demonesses. The fearsome demonesses caught him and kept him there as a husband. A kind man secretly told him that this was, in truth, a city of demonesses. He said that the next day a horse would rise from the ocean. Anyone who wished to escape could ride on its back and reach the human world. The sea-captain rode that horse and escaped safely back to his own land.

This story shows us something important. If we guard our own mind from the afflictions and tame it, no fear will arise from tigers, lions, demonesses, or anything else.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when anger or fear arises in me, I will not look outward. Instead, I will tame only my own mind.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ We cannot tame every outer enemy and every fear one by one. If we tame only our own mind, every harm will calm down naturally. So taming our own mind is the best practice for the benefit of ourselves and others.
