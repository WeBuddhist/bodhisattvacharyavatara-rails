# Day 67 — Milk for a Snake: Why Serving the Afflictions Only Makes Them Stronger

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the three verses 32 to 34 from the fourth chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. Serving an outer enemy well can bring benefit, but the more we serve the inner enemy, the afflictions, the more harm it brings us again and again. This is the difference this lesson shows us clearly.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

For example, if a person wants to be kind to a snake and gives it milk, the snake will not become calm and gentle. The more milk it is given, the more its poison grows and gains strength. In the same way, if we follow our own anger and craving and satisfy their wishes, the afflictions do not calm down. The more we satisfy the wishes of the afflictions, the greater the harm they bring us.

Being kind to an ordinary enemy can turn them into a friend. But the more we serve the enemy of the afflictions, the more harm it does to us. This enemy, always dwelling in our own heart, takes away our happiness. So we must cut off the food of the afflictions. We must not feed them.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, the moment a thought of anger or craving arises in me, I will not feed it. I will stop it right away.

**Explanation:** *(Not feeding the afflictions)* Being kind to an outer enemy can turn them into a friend. But serving and following the afflictions makes them grow much larger and brings us suffering. This is like giving milk to a snake: its poison only grows. So we must not let a thought of affliction grow. We must cut it off right away.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains: in life after life before this one, attachment and aversion did nothing to any of us but bring harm, and in this life too they have done nothing but harm us. From beginningless time until now, wherever attachment and aversion have operated in someone's mind, they have never done anything except lead that person into suffering and into wrongdoing. It is also true that, because our grasping at a self is so powerful, self-cherishing can sometimes seem to help — as when, out of strong attachment to this self, the thought that "if I take life a bad result will ripen for me, but if I protect life I will live long" motivates some good behavior; on this basis it is even said that self-grasping can serve, in a sense, as a seed of buddhahood. An ordinary enemy in this life may have been a close friend in an earlier life — and not only across lifetimes: a friend in the first part of one life can become an enemy in its second half, and an enemy in the first half can become a friend by its end. Such things do happen. But the enemy that is the afflictions has, since beginningless time, existed purely and only as an enemy, and it is certain that in the future too it will only go on harming us and setting us toward suffering. So this affliction-enemy, at all times, without beginning or end, remains nothing but an enemy that harms us in every way — whereas even a fierce and powerful enemy of the teachings might, one day, travel the spiritual paths and grounds and become a true and noble holder of the teachings himself. No other enemy could ever go on harming us for so endlessly long a time the way this one does.

##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Considering how long-lasting the harm is: my enemy, the afflictions, has been harming me since beginningless cyclic existence right up to now, and if it is not destroyed by its antidote, it will go on harming me in every future life for as long as cyclic existence itself has no end. No other enemy — none of those who merely rob us of wealth and possessions and so on — can ever go on harming us that long. So we must make an effort at applying the antidote.

Here is a further danger: just as my enemy the afflictions endures for a very long time, without beginning or end, no other worldly enemy could ever go on causing harm for anywhere near that long. Meditating on the antidote to the afflictions for just one sitting accomplishes nothing at all, so we must make an effort at destroying the afflictions the way a river flows — without a break.

##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

My enemy the afflictions has, since beginningless cyclic existence, harmed me right up to the present, and if it is not destroyed by its antidote, it will go on harming me without end for as long as cyclic existence lasts. No other enemy — gods, demigods, or anyone else — could ever harm me for so long. So one should make an effort at applying the antidote for this reason too.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

If one wonders why one should hold such a grudge against this enemy: my enemy the afflictions is not only exceptionally powerful and exceptionally harmful — it has, since beginningless cyclic existence, done me great and unbroken harm right up to now, and unless it is abandoned through the power of its antidote, it is certain to go on harming me endlessly for as long as cyclic existence continues. Compare this to any other worldly enemy — human or non-human — none of them could ever harm us for so long; at most they can do so for a short span of years or months. So, in destroying this enemy too, one must apply oneself for a long time, like the constant flow of a river; meditating on the antidote for just one sitting accomplishes nothing.

For example, just as one or two applications of medicine cannot cure someone struck by a serious illness like leprosy, so too, since we have been struck by the grave sickness of the afflictions from beginningless time, it is not enough to practice the meaning of some instruction for only a session or two here and there — we must practice the entire path completely, for a long time. As Candragomin says:

> "Constantly deluded in mind, one nurses the illness for a long time; for a leper whose hands and feet have already fallen away, what use is medicine applied just once or twice?"

and:

> "The tree of the mind has existed since beginningless time, and is soaked through with the sap of the poison of the afflictions; just as it cannot be made into something wholesome and tasty, what good could a mere drop of the water of good qualities do?"

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-32]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

This means: as for how the harm is long-lasting — just as the afflictions in my own mind, such as craving and the rest, have done nothing but harm me from the beginningless time of cyclic existence right up to now, no other worldly enemy could ever harm us for so long — that is, from one birth through many lifetimes.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-32]]

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains: applying the skillful means used with an ordinary enemy, and drawing the comparison out further — take, for example, a person from twenty or thirty years ago whose sole occupation, without exception, was inflicting harm on his enemies; nowadays, whether his outlook has truly changed or not, he at least shows some small outward gesture that eases things for the moment. If one knows how to skillfully maintain such a relationship, an enemy can indeed become a friend, and there is even a way to turn misfortune itself into fortune. In this way, an ordinary enemy could, in every respect, become someone who benefits and brings happiness. But with the affliction-enemy, the more skillfully one tries to serve and befriend it, the more it only turns around and grows in strength — the afflictions gain power, their force increases, and the harm they do only becomes greater.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-33]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Consider how hard the afflictions are to placate: if one gives an ordinary enemy whatever wealth, food, and clothing they want, serving and attending to them in keeping with their wishes, they will constantly repay one with every kind of help and benefit in return. But the afflictions — craving, aversion, delusion, and the rest — when served however much the affliction itself demands, not only fail to bring benefit and happiness, but turn around and harm one with every kind of suffering, both in this life and in lives to come. So one must not rely on them, but abandon them.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-33]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

If one serves and attends to some other worldly enemy with food and the like, in keeping with their wishes, all of that becomes a source of benefit and happiness for oneself. But when one relies on the afflictions as one's enemies and lives in keeping with them, their strength only grows, and they turn around and harm one with suffering. So making the effort to destroy them is itself the means to one's own happiness.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-33]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->

If one serves and attends to them in keeping with their wishes, everyone else brings one benefit and happiness. But when one relies on the afflictions, they turn around and inflict great suffering and harm.

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If one takes hold of an ordinary enemy as a friend and serves and attends to them, in the great majority of cases they will repay one in kind with benefit and happiness. But the afflictions, when taken hold of as a friend and relied upon, are by nature untrustworthy — they turn around and give rise to the suffering of hell and the other realms, doing one fierce harm. So they should be abandoned.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

One might ask: if I serve or fall in with them in keeping with their wishes, will they not do me harm? To every other worldly enemy, one can offer food, clothing, wealth, and the like in keeping with their own wishes, honoring and serving them so as to please them; and, doing this to the very best of one's ability, all of them will, in return, become a support for one's own benefit and happiness. But the malicious affliction-enemies, when one falls in with their wishes and relies on them as though they were friends, only grow in strength and power the more one does so — just as a snake's poison grows the more milk one gives it — and turn around to give rise to intense suffering, doing great harm. So one should not befriend them, but instead make an effort, from now on, at destroying them, as the very means to one's own happiness.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-33]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

This means: as for how they are hard to serve — in the case of an ordinary worldly enemy, if one serves and attends to them in keeping with their wishes, or shows them a friendly face and flatters them, then eventually all of them may well become, in a sense, friends who bring one benefit and happiness in return. But the afflictions such as craving, however much one serves them, only turn around and harm one, through bodily and mental suffering — there is simply no possibility that they will ever bring benefit or happiness.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Serving an ordinary worldly enemy skillfully with food, clothing, and attention can turn them into a source of one's own benefit and happiness; but however one serves or follows the affliction-enemy, it only turns around and brings great suffering and harm, both in this life and in lives to come — so all the commentaries agree.

**Key points.**

- Serving an ordinary worldly enemy in keeping with their wishes can make them a source of one's own benefit and happiness.
- The dangers of relying on the affliction-enemies — craving, aversion, delusion, and the rest — grow in exact proportion to how much one serves and falls in with them, bringing on the intense suffering of hell and the other realms.
- Just as a snake's poison grows the more milk it is given, Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains, relying on the afflictions as a friend only makes their strength and the harm they do increase.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that an ordinary enemy can, through skillful means, become a friend, while the affliction-enemy only grows more harmful the more skillfully one tries to serve it.
- One should therefore abandon the affliction-enemy rather than rely on it, and make an effort to destroy it — this is itself the supreme means to one's own happiness.

> So if I let this constant enemy — the one true cause of all my growing troubles — settle permanently in my own heart, how could I ever feel safe or truly happy?

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains: this inner enemy, which does nothing since beginningless time but harm me continuously, and which is the sole cause of every kind of harm continuing to increase, is present in my own heart at this very moment. Given that, is there any way at all to remain joyfully unafraid of cyclic existence? There is not.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-34]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Just as explained above, this affliction-enemy — which, for even a single instant, without any reason at all, has by its own nature, like the heat that is fire's very nature, remained continuously my enemy since beginningless, endless time, and which is the sole cause of the ever-increasing mass of suffering in this life and lives to come — if it lodges and abides, as though installed, in my heart which is like its own temple, then whatever command of that enemy I carry out — a great one, a middling one, or a small one — brings a correspondingly great, middling, or small harm; and if I never carry out its commands at all, it never does me any harm at all. So if this affliction-enemy dwells lodged in my heart, then, given that the very root of all such harm is a cyclic existence whose nature is suffering — like a pit of fire, or a basket full of poisonous snakes — how could it possibly be right for me to feel no fear or dread of it, and instead take delight in it? It would not be right for me to become attached to and delighted by cyclic existence in that way. As for bodhisattvas: it is not that they take rebirth because they delight in and desire cyclic existence — rather, under the power of compassion, unwilling as they are for beings to suffer in cyclic existence, they take rebirth in order to work for the good of beings. If it were otherwise, we would have to say they also wish for beings themselves to remain in cyclic existence rather than be freed from it — which is not the case. Rather, unable to bear the suffering of sentient beings in cyclic existence, and setting their own suffering aside as unimportant, they do not abandon cyclic existence, for the sake of others.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-34]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

This enemy, which has in this way remained continuously my enemy since beginningless time, and which is the sole, unrivaled cause of the ever-increasing mass of every kind of suffering — if it comes to lodge and rely in my heart, how could there be any freedom from fear in cyclic existence, or any occasion for the mind to feel joy or happiness? There is none. So one should make an effort at destroying the afflictions.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-34]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

*(Ngulchu Thokme transcludes verses 4-34 and 4-35 together as one quoted block and comments on both at once; the explanation below covers both. In the current segmentation of this source, the quoted verse-pair is stamped as this file's own block ^4-71, and the explanation as ^4-73.)*

This enemy, which has in this way remained continuously my enemy since beginningless time without a break, and which is the sole cause of the ever-increasing mass of every kind of harm — this affliction-enemy — if it comes to lodge and abide firmly in my heart, then how could I possibly be free of fear in cyclic existence and feel joy? I could not. Moreover, since this enemy keeps me from escaping the prison of cyclic existence, these afflictions are like the guards and executioners who kill in hell and the other realms; and if they lodge in the place that is my mind — that is, if they dwell within the net of attachment to that place — then, one should reflect, how could there be any happiness for me at all?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^4-73]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

As just explained, this enemy, which has in this way remained continuously my enemy since beginningless time without end, and which is the sole cause of the ever-increasing mass of suffering and harm — if these afflictions come to lodge and abide firmly, inseparably, in my heart, then how could I possibly be free of fear in cyclic existence, or have any joy at all? I could not.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-34]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

This means: as explained above, these afflictions have, since the beginningless time of my past lives right up to now, remained continuously — without any break at all — an enemy that does nothing but harm me; they are the sole cause of every kind of harm to body and mind, that is, of all suffering, increasing more and more. If a self of that kind comes to lodge and abide, in every respect, within the great house that is my own heart, then within this very abode of the three realms of cyclic existence — a place like a pit of fire or a nest of wild animals — how could I possibly find joy through being utterly without fear or dread in my own mind? I could not — that is the meaning.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. If the Affliction-Enemy Lodges Permanently in My Heart as the Sole Cause of Ever-Increasing Harm Since Beginningless Time, How Could I Ever Be Free of Fear and Feel Joy in Cyclic Existence?

All the commentaries agree on this central point.

###### 2. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden Shows That the Degree of Harm Matches How Fully One Carries Out the Affliction's Command

Carrying out a great command brings great harm, a middling one brings middling harm, a small one brings small harm — and carrying out none at all brings no harm at all.

###### 3. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden Distinguishes Bodhisattvas, Who Take Rebirth Through the Power of Compassion, from Taking Delight in Cyclic Existence Itself

Because they cannot bear the suffering of sentient beings, bodhisattvas set their own suffering aside and do not abandon cyclic existence for others' sake — not because they delight in or desire it.

###### 4. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk Describes the Abode of the Three Realms as a Pit of Fire or a Nest of Wild Animals

While the affliction-enemy dwells in one's heart, there is no way to feel joy within such a place.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** Since beginningless time, this enemy has been the sole cause of every kind of harm continuing to increase; if it settles permanently in one's own heart, there is no way to be free of fear or to feel true joy in cyclic existence — all the commentaries reach this same conclusion together.

**Key points.**

- The afflictions are, since beginningless time, the sole cause of all harm to body and mind — all suffering — continuing to increase.
- Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that the degree of harm exactly matches how fully one carries out the affliction's command, so that carrying out none of its commands brings no harm at all.
- He further distinguishes that bodhisattvas take rebirth in cyclic existence through the power of compassion, unable to bear beings' suffering — not because they delight in or desire cyclic existence itself.
- Cyclic existence is like a pit of fire, a basket of poisonous snakes, or a nest of wild animals — so while the affliction-enemy dwells lodged in one's heart, it is simply not right to feel free of fear and take delight in it.
- Ngulchu Thogme explains that these afflictions, like guards and executioners who keep one from escaping the prison of cyclic existence, make it impossible to find any happiness at all.
