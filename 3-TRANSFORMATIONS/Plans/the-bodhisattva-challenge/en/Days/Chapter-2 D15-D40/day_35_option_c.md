# Day 35 — Refuge that faces forward

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Make one small, concrete pledge for tomorrow onward
> **Detail:** Today's verse turns regret into resolve: I take refuge, and now I will follow the guidance.

## Opening

Shantideva owns up to having broken the guidance before, and then takes refuge again. But the refuge does not stop at "rescue me from the consequences." It carries a turn toward the future. Having seen where ignoring the path led, he comes back not only to be protected, but to actually live by the guidance from here on. Refuge, in this verse, faces forward.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, reads one more thing into the plea than the verse spells out. When Shantideva says "I take refuge in you," Gyaltsab hears the rest of the sentence: and so, regarding what to take up and what to leave, let me now follow your guidance. The refuge is not only a request to be spared; it is a promise to change direction. This is worth holding onto, because regret on its own tends to fade by lunchtime. What gives it weight is letting it become a small, definite resolve about what you will actually do next. Gyaltsab's reading keeps the two halves together: you admit the past honestly, and you commit to the future concretely. Refuge that only looks backward is just relief. Refuge that also looks forward is the start of an actual change.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Think of one thing you keep getting wrong in the same way, and make a single, concrete pledge about it, small enough that you can actually keep it tomorrow: not "I'll be more patient," but "when my partner interrupts me, I'll take one breath before answering." Say it clearly, as a promise made under refuge, and let your regret turn into that one definite step.
