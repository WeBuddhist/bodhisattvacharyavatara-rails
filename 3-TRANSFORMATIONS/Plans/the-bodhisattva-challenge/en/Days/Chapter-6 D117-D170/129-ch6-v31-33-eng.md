## Today's Verse

> Thus, everything is dependent on others.
> Because of that dependence, it is not independent.
> Knowing this, you will not be angry
> at any phenomena, like a snake. ^6-31

> If you say, "What is to be averted by what?
> Averting is also illogical,"
> it is not illogical to desire that sufferings
> cease by relying on that. ^6-32

> Therefore, if you see someone, whether enemy or friend,
> doing something inappropriate,
> reflect, "This arose from such conditions,"
> and maintain your peace. ^6-33

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the thirty-first, thirty-second, and thirty-third verses of the sixth chapter. A friend or an enemy does something improper in front of us. Instead of feeling tight in the chest, can we say quietly, "This came from these conditions," and let our mind rest at ease?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging.

All things depend on other conditions and rely on earlier causes, so they are not free. They appear like an illusion, a dream, and something magically made. A bodhisattva who understands this is not afraid of happiness or suffering. Even taking birth for the sake of others is like going to a pleasure garden, with no suffering. If we see in this way, we do not get angry at those who harm us and who look like snakes.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when other people do something improper, I will think that it came from conditions, and I will keep my mind relaxed.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ When a friend or an enemy does something improper, we can think that it came from causes and conditions. Then anger does not arise. If we keep our own mind relaxed in this way, the stream of suffering is cut. Our mind becomes peaceful and happy.
