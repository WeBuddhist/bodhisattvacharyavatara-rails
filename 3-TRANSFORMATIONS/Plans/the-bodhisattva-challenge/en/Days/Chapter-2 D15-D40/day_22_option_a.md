# Day 22 — Give away the thing you envy

> **Notification**
> **Title:** When you envy something today, offer it in your mind
> **Detail:** Shantideva offers whole palaces to the buddhas. Today, give away the grand thing you'd want for yourself.

## Opening

Today the offering goes big. In his mind, Shantideva offers entire palaces, glowing with pearls and jewels, ringing with songs of praise, so bright they become ornaments of the sky. These are the kind of magnificent things people long for and compare themselves over. He hands them, freely, to the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

There is a small, sour pinch we all know: someone has the house, the success, the beautiful thing, and we feel ourselves shrink a little.

The verse offers palaces, the grandest things imaginable. Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, describes them glowing until they light up the whole sky, and notes who receives them: those whose very nature is compassion. The move is worth copying. When you want something you cannot have, wanting it closes the mind around a small, hard knot. Offering it does the opposite. You picture the fine thing, and instead of clutching, you give it away to the buddhas. The grip loosens, the knot opens, and what grows in its place is a steadier, more generous mind. The envy was only ever costing you.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

The next time you catch yourself envying something, a home, a possession, someone's good fortune, try offering it in your mind to the buddhas and bodhisattvas instead. Picture the thing clearly, then give it away. Notice whether the pinch of wanting eases when your hand is open rather than closed.
