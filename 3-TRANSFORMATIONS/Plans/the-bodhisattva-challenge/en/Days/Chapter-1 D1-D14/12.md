# Day 12 — Kindness that asks for nothing back

> **Notification**
> **Title:** After helping, notice what you hoped for
> **Detail:** The bodhisattva helps unasked, expecting nothing. Today, notice any wish for credit.

## Opening

We rightly admire someone who returns a kindness. Today's verses point past that. The bodhisattva helps people who never did anything for them, who never even asked. And what they give is not a meal or a favor, but lasting happiness, offered to everyone, with respect, for as long as the world lasts.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## Today's Verses

> ཕན་བཏགས་ལན་ལྡོན་གང་ཡིན་པ། །
> དེ་ཡང་རེ་ཞིག་བསྔགས་འོས་ན། །
> མ་བཅོལ་ལེགས་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཡི། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་སྨོས་ཅི་དགོས། །

> We praise those who merely repay a favor. What need is there to speak of the Hero of Enlightenment, who does good unsolicited?

> བརྙས་བཅས་ཉིན་ཕྱེད་འགྲངས་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཡང་། །
> དགེ་བ་བྱེད་པ་ཡིན་ཞེས་སྐྱེ་བོས་བཀུར། །
>
> People honor a person as a virtuous benefactor for giving a single meal to a few beings. They do this even when the food is given with disrespect, satisfying them for only half a day.

## From the Tradition

**Sazang Mati Panchen**, a fourteenth-century teacher, adds the part that is easy to miss. The bodhisattva expects nothing in return, not even the quiet sense that the good deed will come back to them later as merit. Most of our kindness keeps a small place for the self: a thank-you, a good feeling, a hope that it counts in our favor. This points past even that.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

After you do something kind today, holding a door, checking on a friend, helping before you're asked, pause and notice what you were hoping for. Not the big things, the small ones: a thank-you, a warm feeling, the sense of being a good person. You don't have to get rid of it. Just see it.
