### 1.7 Third, the reasons why those benefits arise has two parts: citing scriptures, and establishing through reasoning.

159. First: That this engaging mind possesses those benefits is explained, along with reasoning, by the Tathāgata himself in the *Subāhuparipṛcchā Sūtra*. ^1-7-1

160. For what purpose? For the sake of sentient beings who are inclined to the lower vehicle, where some of indefinite Śrāvaka lineage are terrified of accumulating vast collections and, having turned away from the Mahayana, seek the awakening of Śrāvakas. ^1-7-2

161. It was taught to turn back their inclination to the lower and establish them in the Mahayana. ^1-7-3

### 1.8 Second has two parts: the correctness of obtaining benefits from the aspiring mind, and the correctness of obtaining them from the engaging mind.

163. First has four parts: the benefits being great due to the infinite activity of accomplishing virtue, there being no other beneficial attitude superior to it, such a beneficial attitude never having been dreamed of even for their own sake by those very sentient beings in the past, and concluding with praise. ^1-8-1

#### First: Like the daughter of Captain Jñānavat, if one merely thinks of clearing just a headache of sentient beings, with a small focus and aspect, one still possesses a beneficial attitude toward others, and

165. is endowed with immeasurable merit. If so, what need is there to mention the infinite merit of wanting to dispel the boundless unhappiness of every single sentient being and wanting to establish every single sentient being in the boundless qualities of a Buddha? ^1-8-2

166. Second: Where in the world is there such a beneficial attitude? Even parents who wish to benefit their children, or any other person, do not have such a beneficial attitude that seeks to accomplish supreme bliss. Neither do gods, sages who speak truth, or even Brahma who abides in the four immeasurables possess such a beneficial attitude. ^1-8-3

#### Third: If those very sentient beings have never even dreamed of such a beneficial attitude for their own sake in the past, how could it ever arise in reality for the sake of others? It is unprecedented. ^1-8-4

168. Fourth: This precious, exceptional mind of the Bodhisattvas—the wish to attain buddhahood to accomplish the happiness and eliminate the suffering of all sentient beings, which does not arise in other sentient beings even for a single instant for their own sake—is an unprecedented, marvelous, and wondrous jewel of the mind. Others should also hold this as wondrous and make efforts toward it. ^1-8-5

### 1.9 Second has three parts: the actual explanation, its correctness, and clearing doubts.

#### First: Since the benefits of the aspiring mind are extremely great, the engaging mind conjoined with the practice of conduct is even more exceptional, because:

171. the merit of this mind, which is like a precious wish-fulfilling jewel, the cause of joy that leads all wandering beings to attain the happiness of high rebirth and liberation, and the alchemical elixir that overcomes the disease of suffering of all sentient beings—how can its measure be grasped? For it is infinite. In the *Questions of Vīradatta*, it is said: ^1-9-1

172. "If the merit of bodhicitta ^1-9-2

173. were to take physical form, it would fill the entire expanse of space and even exceed it." ^1-9-3

174. Thus it is taught. ^1-9-4

175. This should be understood in detail as explained in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya* by citing the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*. ^1-9-5

176. Second: If even the mere beneficial thought of "May I become a Buddha to protect all wandering beings" is taught in the *King of Samādhis Sūtra* to have merit far exceeding that of making offerings to the Buddhas in as many billions of Buddha-fields as there are, what need is there to mention the infinite merit of making great efforts in practicing and accomplishing the supreme happiness of every single sentient beings without exception? ^1-9-6

177. Third: If someone asks, "Since sentient beings themselves desire happiness and do not desire suffering, and by making efforts they are able to obtain happiness and avoid suffering, are they not themselves skilled in adopting happiness and discarding suffering? What need is there for others to strive for their sake?" ^1-9-7

178. It is definitely necessary to strive, because: ^1-9-8

179. although sentient beings have the wish to discard suffering, under the power of afflictions they accomplish only the causes of suffering, thereby running directly toward endless cyclic existence and the sufferings of bad migrations. Although they desire happiness, they are ignorant of its methods, and through their conjoining with delusion, they destroy their own happiness like an enemy, thus entering wrongly into the methods for happiness and suffering. For that reason, what other virtue is equal to this mind, whose activity satisfies those sentient beings who are ignorant of the methods of happiness and suffering, destitute of happiness, and endowed with abundant sufferings, with all the happiness of high rebirth and definitive goodness, cuts off all suffering, and dispels the delusion of ignorance regarding adoption and abandonment? None. ^1-9-9

180. Where else is there such a spiritual friend, who accomplishes all benefit and protects from all harm? ^1-9-10

181. Where else is there such merit, which unerringly teaches all points of adoption and abandonment? There is none, so one must make efforts to generate bodhicitta. ^1-9-11

182. Fourth has three parts: being worthy of praise because of acting as a friend drawn by great compassion even without being asked, being even more worthy of praise for accomplishing all benefit and happiness if those who accomplish minor benefits are praised, and being worthy of taking refuge because of being a supreme field of merit. ^1-9-12

#### First: The Bodhisattvas are indeed to be praised and lauded, because:

184. if someone in the world who repays a past favor is temporarily worthy of praise as a "grateful person," what need is there to mention how worthy of praise by gods and humans alike are the Bodhisattvas, who, without being asked, are drawn by great compassion to perfectly accomplish all benefit and happiness? ^1-9-13

185. Second: In the world, if someone who gives a minor thing like ordinary food to a few wandering beings—such as a hundred—for just a brief moment, with a low practice conjoined with disrespect and contempt, and of minor benefit such as satisfying them for half a day, is still honored by people as a "great benefactor who performs virtue"; then what need is there to mention how worthy of honor is the Bodhisattva, who, for a long duration throughout cyclic existence, gives to a vast field of infinite sentient beings the exceptional object of giving—the supreme, stainless bliss of the Sugatas—and ^1-9-14

186. who constantly gives with a practice conjoined with respect, completely fulfilling all the thoughts of benefit? Therefore, an intelligent person should be single-mindedly devoted to the Bodhisattvas. ^1-9-15

187. Third has three parts: the reasons why it is improper to disparage a Bodhisattva even slightly, the reasons why it is proper to cultivate faith, and why it is therefore proper to bow down and take refuge. ^1-9-16

#### First: Towards such a supreme benefactor, a Son of the Victorious, who gives all benefit and happiness to sentient beings,

189. if one should generate a harmful mind that wishes to disparage and harm them, that is the ultimate of negativity, because: ^1-9-17

190. if one generates a harmful mind, in the *Sūtra of the Miracle of Complete Pacification*, it is said: "O Mañjuśrī, for as long as a Bodhisattva generates a mind of anger and contempt toward another Bodhisattva, for that many aeons he must wear the armor of remaining in hell." ^1-9-18

191. Since it is taught by the Sage that one will remain in hell for as many aeons as the number of moments of generating that harmful mind, one must be extremely careful regarding this point. ^1-9-19

192. In the *Sūtra of the Mudra of Generating the Power of Faith*, it is said that generating a harmful mind toward a Bodhisattva who is inclined to the Mahayana produces far more countless negativity than destroying or burning as many stūpas as there are grains of sand in the River Ganges. The reason for this is explained to be that since Buddhas are born from Bodhisattvas, harming the cause of Buddhas is the ultimate of negativity, as is taught in many scriptures and commentaries. ^1-9-20

#### Second: On the other hand, if a person generates clear faith and devotion toward a Bodhisattva,

194. the fruit of that faith increases far more than the fruit of negativity explained before, ^1-9-21

195. and for far more countless aeons than the moments of clear faith, one will experience exceptional fruits, ^1-9-22

196. as is taught in the *Sūtra of the Mudra of Entering the Definite and Indefinite*. Third: Even if harm and suffering are produced toward the great Sons of the Victorious, they do not generate a negative mind of anger even at the cost of their lives; rather, their virtue increases naturally. According to the commentary, it is explained that because anger and so forth have no purpose in abiding in them, they do not arise in the continuum of the Bodhisattvas even under great pressure. ^1-9-23

197. The meaning is that even at the cost of their lives, the Bodhisattvas do not commit negativity, and their virtue increases naturally. ^1-9-24

198. For this reason, one bows down with devotion through the three doors to the body of anyone in whom this precious, supreme mind has arisen. As will be explained in the Chapter on Patience, and as in the *Jātaka of King Maitrībala* where the five yakṣas drank the blood of his body yet were ultimately established in the noble state, even if one harms these Bodhisattvas, they not only refrain from retaliating but produce only happiness. Thus, one takes refuge in this source of happiness. In brief, whether entering the path of Mantra or the Perfections, the very generation of the mind is the doorway for entering the Mahayana; therefore, one must make efforts to generate this mind through various methods. ^1-9-25

199. To generate it, as explained before, one must first meditate on the benefits of generating the mind so that one's joyful effort toward the benefits increases from the depths of one's heart, and one must practice the seven-limb worship along with taking refuge, as is taught in this supreme commentary showing the stages of the Bodhisattva path and in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*. ^1-9-26

200. The benefits thus taught can also be condensed into two: temporary benefits and ultimate benefits. ^1-9-27

201. The former also has two: not falling into bad migrations and being born in happy migrations. ^1-9-28

202. If this generation of the mind is born, many previously accumulated causes of bad migrations are purified, the future accumulation of them is cut off, previously accumulated causes of happy migrations are grasped by it and increase extensively, and newly accumulated ones are motivated by it and are endless. ^1-9-29

203. Ultimately, liberation and omniscience are also easily accomplished by relying on this mind; before that, if one lacks an uncontrived aspiration that seeks the temporary and ultimate benefits, then saying "one must make efforts to generate the mind because those benefits arise from generating the mind" becomes mere words. If one examines one's own continuum, this is extremely clear. ^1-9-30

204. To generate the desire to obtain the two benefits of high rebirth and definitive goodness, one must train the mind of lesser and medium persons, and then enter the meditation on bodhicitta which has love and compassion as its root. ^1-9-31

205. For those wise ones who have obtained a human body of leisure and fortune, diligently meditating on the two minds of awakening is the supreme way of taking the essence of the support of leisure and fortune; first, diligently meditate on the benefits of that mind! ^1-9-32

206. This is the summarizing verse. ^1-9-33

207. Second, the title of the chapter: From the *Commentary on Entering the Way of the Bodhisattvas, entitled 'The Gateway for the Sons of the Victorious'*, this is the commentary on Chapter One, entitled "Explaining the Benefits of Bodhicitta." ^1-9-34
