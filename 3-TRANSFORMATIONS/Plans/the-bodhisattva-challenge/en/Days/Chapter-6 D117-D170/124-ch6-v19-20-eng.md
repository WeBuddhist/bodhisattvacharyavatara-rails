## Today's Verse

> Even if suffering arises, the wise
> should not let their minds be agitated.
> When waging battle with defilements,
> there is much harm in battle. ^6-19

> Those who disregard all suffering
> and conquer enemies such as hatred
> are true heroes.
> The rest are merely killing corpses. ^6-20

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the nineteenth and twentieth verses of the sixth chapter. Today, we examine in our own mind whether the one who defeats their own anger, rather than an outer enemy, is the true hero.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable point, which is easy to understand and encouraging. An ordinary enemy will die naturally, even if we do not kill him. So killing him is not called heroic. But the afflictions are an enemy that will not be calmed unless an antidote defeats them. So the one who defeats them is a hero. We must recognize the afflictions in our own mind as the enemy. We must build the strength of patience toward the suffering of hunger, thirst, heat, cold, and so on. When we practice, many harms come because past bad karma surfaces. Even so, the wise do not let their mind be disturbed. If we complain, discomfort grows.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, even if suffering comes, I will keep my mind clear and unclouded, and stay peaceful.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ If our mind is disturbed when suffering comes, our discomfort becomes greater. If we know our own afflictions as the enemy and keep the mind peaceful, it is very helpful. If we keep the mind unclouded, the strength of patience grows.
