## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three classical commentators agree that Verse 1-24 employs a powerful *a fortiori* (argument from the lesser to the greater) logical structure to demonstrate the extraordinary rarity and unprecedented nature of the mind of enlightenment (Bodhicitta). They argue that since ordinary sentient beings have never conceived of this ultimate, supreme welfare even for their own self-interest—not even in their most imaginative dream states—it is a profound marvel that such a mind could ever arise for the sake of others in waking reality.

### 1. The Logical Argument (A Fortiori Reasoning)
*   **Consensus:** The commentators highlight the logical impossibility of a completely altruistic attitude arising naturally in ordinary beings. The argument is structured as follows: if a person has never generated the wish for supreme enlightenment (*Bodhicitta*) for their own benefit (*svārtha* / *rang don*), not even in a dream, then it is infinitely more difficult and unprecedented for them to generate this wish for the sake of all other sentient beings (*parārtha* / *gzhan don*) in reality.
*   **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen** frames this as the third key argument in his four-part division on the benefits of the aspiring mind of enlightenment. He notes that if those very sentient beings have never even dreamed of such an attitude for their own sake in the past, its actual arising in reality for the sake of others is completely "unprecedented" (*apūrva* / *snga na med pa*).
*   **Ngulchu Thokme** likewise emphasizes that since sentient beings have never dreamed of this attitude for their own self-interest, its arising in reality (*dngos su*) for others is a complete marvel that has never existed before.

### 2. The Contrast Between Dream and Reality
*   **Consensus:** The commentators leverage the distinction between the dream state (*svapna* / *rmi lam*) and waking reality (*dngos su* / waking state) to illustrate how deeply entrenched self-grasping and spiritual ignorance are.
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen** draws a sharp contrast between the dream state and the waking state (*gnyid sad pa'i dus*). He explains that in dreams, where ordinary mental constraints are loosened, beings still never dream of generating such an exceptional mind for themselves. Therefore, its arising in reality for the sake of other sentient beings during their waking state is "extremely difficult to arise" (*skye bar shin tu dka'*).
*   **Ngulchu Thokme** similarly points out that if the thought has never even been dreamed of (*rmi lam du'ang*) for self-interest, its actual arising in reality (*dngos su*) for the sake of others is truly extraordinary.

### 3. Citations and Doctrinal Connections
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen** supports his explanation by citing the *Mahāyānasūtrālaṅkāra* (*Ornament of the Great Vehicle Sūtras*):
    > *"Just as the heirs of the Victorious Ones remain here with a beneficial thought to ripen all wandering beings, in the same way, fathers, mothers, and relatives do not remain with such goodness toward their children and kin."*
    By utilizing this citation, Sazang reinforces that the absolute, unconditioned altruism of a Bodhisattva transcends even the most profound worldly love (such as parental love), making the arising of Bodhicitta a marvelous, jewel-like event in the mental continuum of a practitioner.
