## Today's Verse

> Having taken this body as "mine,"
> why do you guard it, mind?
> If you and this body are separate,
> what can it do for you? ^5-60

> Foolish mind, why do you not
> take a clean wooden image?
> What good is it to guard this machine
> of impure aggregates, which is decaying? ^5-61

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the sixtieth and sixty-first verses of the fifth chapter. When we say "my body," are the "I" who guards and the body that is guarded really one?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a valuable point. The _Sutra of Nanda's Entering the Womb_ says that eighty thousand worms live in the rotting waste of the food we eat, in the intestines. The sutra also says that the foul, disgusting smell of feces and urine comes from these worms. So this body is not clean, and it is not right to feel pride or attachment toward it.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, if attachment to my own body arises, I will remember that it is a collection of impure things, and I will do something that helps others.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ This body is a collection of impure things and the home of eighty thousand tiny worms. There is no reason to guard it out of attachment. Even so, we keep it alive in order to take the essence of the body, and we do this by helping others. In this way we tame our own mind.
