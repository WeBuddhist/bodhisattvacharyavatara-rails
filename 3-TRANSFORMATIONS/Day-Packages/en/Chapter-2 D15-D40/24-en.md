## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on verses 22 through 24 from the second chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. It teaches us how to follow the example of great Bodhisattvas like Manjushri and Samantabhadra by offering supreme mental worship, singing heartfelt praises to the ocean of spiritual qualities, and prostrating with bodies as numerous as all the dust particles in the universe. This practice inspires us to cultivate deep devotion, gratitude, and humility.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

These verses explain three key practices: supreme offerings through visualization, songs of praise, and respectful prostrations. Master Shantideva explains that just as great, highly realized Bodhisattvas like Manjushri and Samantabhadra use the power of their deep meditation to create vast clouds of offerings that fill the sky, we too can mentally imagine making these supreme offerings to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas.

Next, recognizing that the qualities of the Three Jewels (Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha) are as vast as an endless ocean, Shantideva offers melodic songs of praise to honor their wisdom and compassion. Finally, he bows down to all the Buddhas of the past, present, and future, as well as the Holy Dharma and the Sangha, visualizing his body multiplying into as many bodies as there are particles of dust in the entire universe. Spiritual teachers explain that praising and prostrating in this way crushes our inner pride and opens our minds to receive immense positive energy and wisdom.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, recalling the qualities of the Three Jewels, I will bow with deep devotion and show genuine respect and politeness to everyone around me.

**Explanation:** *(Doing Good)* Usually, due to our inner pride or self-centered habits, we forget to show respect to others and fail to appreciate good qualities in those around us. Today, following the teachings of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* to "bow with bodies as numerous as atoms in the world," I will reflect on the ocean-like virtues of the Three Jewels and bow to them with body, speech, and mind. Furthermore, I will drop my ego and treat the people around me with kindness, polite words, and sincere respect. This simple practice cleanses our pride and gathers a vast store of positive energy.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Just as Manjushri and the rest made offering to the victorious ones who appear in the form of the supreme emanation body, so too I will make offering to the Tathāgatas, the protectors, together with their heirs — with a vast motivation, for a vast purpose, with a clear and limpid mind, by way of measureless offerings emanated by the mind. Everything up to this point is the branch of offering.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-22]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Fourth, the unsurpassable offering. The bodhisattva Samantabhadra sends out from his heart light-rays of many different colours, a thousand million of them, equal in number to the atoms of countless buddha-fields. On the tip of each single ray he emanates a bodhisattva Samantabhadra just like himself; and from the heart of each of those, again, rays like the first are sent out, and so it continues on, countless and beyond conception. Each of those in turn makes offering to the victorious ones of the ten directions together with their heirs, with inconceivable kinds of offering-substances — like Samantabhadra's own clouds of offering. Just as Manjushri and Samantabhadra and the other bodhisattvas of the ten grounds make offering with boundless miraculously emanated clouds of offering to the perfect buddhas, the victorious ones of the ten directions — as the *Ratnolkā Dhāraṇī* says, "With flowers in abundance and canopies of flowers, sending out far the light-rays that arrayed flowers give off, and strewing flowers of every kind on every side, those great beings make offering to the victorious ones," and so on — so too one should form the devoted aspiration, thinking: I too will offer to the Tathāgatas, the protectors, together with their heirs. Furthermore, generating bodhicitta, meditating on compassion, and holding in mind the words and the meaning of the Dharma are also unsurpassable offerings, so one must do these oneself as well.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-22]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Just as Manjushri and Samantabhadra and the other bodhisattvas who have gained mastery emanate whatever offering-substances they wish, filling the whole of space, and make offering to the victorious ones, so too I will make offering to the Tathāgatas, the protectors of gods and humans, together with their heirs. As the *Ratnolkā Dhāraṇī* says: "With flowers in abundance and canopies of flowers, sending out far the light-rays that arrayed flowers give off, and strewing flowers of every kind on every side, those great beings make offering to the victorious ones." From here on — after the branch of offering — comes "second, paying homage," which has two parts: expressing praise with speech, and bowing with the body.

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

The way in which Manjushri and the other great bodhisattvas make offering to the victorious ones of the ten directions is as the *Ratnolkā Dhāraṇī* says: "With flowers in abundance and canopies of flowers, sending out far the light-rays that arrayed flowers give off, and strewing flowers of every kind on every side, those great beings make offering to the victorious ones." So too I devotedly aspire to make offering to the Tathāgatas, the protectors, together with their heirs.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^2-22]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

The way in which Manjushri and Samantabhadra and the other lords of the ten grounds make offering to the perfect buddhas, the victorious ones, with boundless miraculous display is as the *Ratnolkā Dhāraṇī* says: "With flowers in abundance and canopies of flowers, sending out far the light-rays that arrayed flowers give off, and strewing flowers of every kind on every side, those great beings make offering to the victorious ones," and again: "Just as they send inconceivable offerings from the palms of their hands to a single victorious one, so they do to all the victorious ones without exception: such is the miraculous display of the sages' concentration." Just as that is said, I too, with a mind of devoted aspiration, will offer the unsurpassable offering to the Tathāgatas, the protectors of beings, together with their heirs, the host of bodhisattvas.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^2-22]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Take the example: just as Manjushri and Samantabhadra and the other bodhisattvas who have gained mastery emanate, by the power of concentration, measureless offering-substances of whatever kind they wish, filling the whole of space, and make offering to the victorious ones of the ten directions with an unsurpassable, undefiled offering unstained by the taint of clinging to the three spheres as real — so too I will make offering to the Tathāgatas, the protectors of every being, together with their heirs, by merely aspiring with devotion to the way in which those great ones offer. As for how the lords of the ten grounds make offering to the buddhas with boundless miraculous display, the *Ratnolkā Dhāraṇī* says: "With flowers in abundance and canopies of flowers, sending out far the light-rays that arrayed flowers give off, and strewing flowers of every kind on every side, those great beings make offering to the victorious ones," and so on; and: "The concentration whose miraculous display is the offering to the victorious ones they accomplish manifestly toward all the victorious ones; filling the three thousand worlds entirely from the palms of their hands, they make offering to those great beings. In all ten directions, garlands of flowers, incense and perfumed ointments, and jewels most precious — these they scatter from the palms of their hands as offerings to the victorious ones seated beneath the tree of awakening. Robes and powders and every kind of precious thing, victory banners, flags and canopies of many sorts, streams of gold and jewelled awnings — all these they let fall as a rain from those palms"; and so it goes on.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-22]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

As for the unsurpassable offering of those who have attained the tenth bodhisattva ground and gained mastery over the ten powers and over the activity of the six clairvoyances: take the example of the venerable Manjushri, and the noble Avalokiteshvara, and the bodhisattva Sarvanivāraṇaviṣkambhin, and Samantabhadra, and Ākāśagarbha and the rest — just as they make offering with unsurpassable offering-substances to the victorious ones of the ten directions together with their heirs, so too a person at present should, in just that way, form the devoted aspiration of making offering rightly to those Tathāgatas of the ten directions, the protectors of the three realms, together with their heirs, the host of bodhisattvas. The unsurpassable offering means this: apart from merely aspiring with devotion to offer as those eight close heirs and the rest actually offer, I cannot make such an offering in fact.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-22]]

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **gyaltsab / tenzin-gyatso:** they explain that the branch of offering is brought to a close with this verse, and that the second branch, paying homage, begins directly after it. Gyaltsab writes the outline heading "second, paying homage, which has two parts" explicitly after this verse, and His Holiness the Dalai Lama says outright, "everything up to this point is the branch of offering."
- **minyak-kunzang-sonam / khenpo-kunga:** after this verse they write the outline heading "fifth, the offering of praise in song," so that the fifth subdivision of the branch of offering has not yet been reached and the branch of offering appears not yet to have been closed.

In the meaning of this verse on the unsurpassable offering there is no difference; but the commentaries differ over how many subdivisions the branch of offering has and at what point it is brought to a close, so this is recorded separately.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. This Verse Presents "the Unsurpassable Offering"

In Kunzang Pelden's system this is the fourth subdivision of the mind-emanated offering. The commentaries differ as to whether the branch of offering is brought to a close at the end of this verse (see the divergences).

###### 2. Since One Cannot Actually Make Such an Offering, One Makes It Through Devoted Aspiration

Many commentaries explain clearly that although we cannot really emanate measureless offering-substances as the bodhisattva lords of the ten grounds do, we must form the thought of aspiring to offer just as they offer.

###### 3. "Manjushri and the Rest" Means the Bodhisattvas Who Are Lords of the Ten Grounds

Manjushri, Samantabhadra and the others are explained to be the great bodhisattvas who have reached the tenth ground and gained mastery over the ten powers and over the activity of the six clairvoyances. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk adds Avalokiteshvara, Sarvanivāraṇaviṣkambhin and Ākāśagarbha to the list.

###### 4. The Scriptural Source — the *Ratnolkā Dhāraṇī*

Many commentaries cite the *Ratnolkā Dhāraṇī* as the authority for the way the lords of the ten grounds emanate measureless offering-substances by the power of concentration and offer them to the victorious ones.

###### 5. The Field of Offering — the Tathāgatas, the Protectors, Together With Their Heirs

All the commentaries agree in the end that the field of offering is the Tathāgatas together with their heirs, the host of bodhisattvas.

###### 6. Generating Bodhicitta and the Rest Are Also Included in the Unsurpassable Offering

Kunzang Pelden explains that generating bodhicitta, meditating on compassion, and holding the words and meaning of the Dharma in mind are likewise unsurpassable offerings, and so one must do these oneself.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse presents "the unsurpassable offering," a subdivision of the mind-emanated offering: just as Manjushri, Samantabhadra and the other bodhisattva lords of the ten grounds emanate measureless offering-substances and make offering to the victorious ones, so I too — though I cannot do this in fact — pledge to make offering, by way of devoted aspiration, to the Tathāgatas, the protectors, together with their heirs.

**Key points.**

- This is the fourth subdivision of the mind-emanated offering, "the unsurpassable offering." ⚑ In the system of Gyaltsab and His Holiness the Dalai Lama, the branch of offering is closed with this verse and the branch of paying homage begins; in the system of Minyak Kunzang Sonam and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk the fifth subdivision, the offering of praise in song, has not yet been reached, so the branch of offering is not closed here.
- Although the lords of the ten grounds — Manjushri, Samantabhadra and the rest — really do emanate measureless offering-substances and make offering with them, a person today cannot make such an offering in fact, and so offers by way of devoted aspiration.
- The field of offering is the Tathāgatas together with their heirs, the host of bodhisattvas.
- As the scriptural source for these kinds of offering, many commentaries cite the *Ratnolkā Dhāraṇī*, quoting its verses on the clouds of flower-offerings and on the offerings sent out from the palms of the hands.
- ⚑ The scope of the word "protector" is glossed differently: Sabzang has "the protector of beings," while Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk has "the protector of the three realms."
- Kunzang Pelden explains that generating bodhicitta, meditating on compassion, and holding the words and meaning of the Dharma in mind are also unsurpassable offerings, and so opens out the sense of offering more widely.

##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Fifth, the offering of praise in song. To the buddhas and bodhisattvas, who possess qualities of body, speech and mind deep and vast and inexhaustible as the ocean, I offer praise with branches of praise-melody that are themselves boundless as the ocean, expressing their boundless qualities. And so: may the boundless mass of clouds of offering that is the stream of those sweetly proclaimed songs of praise arise, just as I pray it should, before those victorious ones together with their heirs — in the field of their minds, or along the path of their hearing, or in their very presence — without fail, in every place and at every time, unbroken, and even at later times when I am not actually singing praise. As for "the branches of melody": the Great Commentary says that "melody" refers to the seven — the earth-holder and the rest — and "branches" to the kamoda and the rest; and since each of these has many branches, it is called an "ocean." The seven melodies are these:

> The middle, the sage, the earth-holder,
> the six-fold, the five-fold, the clear-minded,
> and the surrounding listener — seven in all.

So it is explained. And the *Dar ṭik* and other commentaries say: "melody" is the voice; "branches" are its causes, the tongue and the palate and so on; and "ocean" is a word for "many" — one praises while imagining measureless heads on each body and measureless tongues on each head. From the oral tradition it comes that "melody" means praising with the tones of the voice, and "branches" the many kinds — modulation, bending, raising and settling — and that it is for this reason that "ocean" is said. All this is like the *Aspiration Prayer of Excellent Conduct*, which says, "Their inexhaustible oceans of praise …" and so on. And it is for that same reason that some fold this verse into the branch of paying homage below, calling it "the homage of speech," and explain it in connection with what follows. This offering is also called the offering of honour, while the earlier ones are called the offering of things obtained.

The benefits of making offering to the Jewels in this way are taught in the *Jewel Lamp*, and taught in the *Kṣitigarbha* as well.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Offering of Praise in Song — Praising With the Voice the Ocean of Qualities of the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas

All the commentaries agree in the end that this verse states the pledge to praise the boundless, ocean-like qualities of the buddhas and bodhisattvas with an ocean of the branches of praise-melody.

###### 2. What "the Branches of Melody" Actually Means

Gyaltsab and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both explain clearly that "melody" is the voice, "branches" are its causes — the tongue, the palate and so on — and "ocean" is a word for "many": one praises while imagining measureless heads on each body and measureless tongues on each head.

###### 3. The Divisions of the Ocean of Qualities — Body, Speech and Mind

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk divides the ocean of qualities into the qualities of body, the major and minor marks; the qualities of speech, the sixty branches of Brahmā's voice; and the qualities of mind, the twenty-one classes of undefiled qualities, and so on.

###### 4. The Aspiration-Prayer That the Clouds of Sweet Praise-Melody Arise Without Interruption

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk identifies as the offering by the power of aspiration-prayer the prayer that boundless masses of clouds of sweet songs of praise arise along the path of the hearing of the victorious ones together with their heirs, at every time and in every form, without a break.

###### 5. The Presentation of the Seven Branches of Melody

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the Great Commentary for a sevenfold division of melody: the middle (བར་མ), the sage (དྲང་སྲོང), the earth-holder (ས་འཛིན), the six-fold (དྲུག་ལྡན), the five-fold (ལྔ་ལྡན), the clear-minded (བློ་གསལ་བ), and the surrounding listener (འཁོར་ཉན་པ).

###### 6. The Benefit of Offering and Praise to the Jewels — Swift Awakening, and the Cause of All Happiness

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the *Jewel Lamp* and the *Kṣitigarbha* to show that one who makes offering and praise to the Jewels passes beyond the path of the four Māras and swiftly attains unsurpassable awakening, and that all the happiness and ease of the world also arises from this.

###### 7. The Commentaries Place This Verse Differently in the Chapter's Outline

⚑ — Some count it as the fifth or the sixth subdivision of the branch of offering in the second chapter; some set "praise expressed in speech" apart as a branch of its own; and some fold it into the branch of paying homage — so their outlines do not agree.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** As one part of the branch of offering, this verse sets out the offering of praise in song: I praise the boundless, ocean-like qualities of body, speech and mind of the buddhas and bodhisattvas with an ocean of the branches of praise-melody, and pray that the sweet clouds of that praise arise without interruption along the path of the hearing of the victorious ones together with their heirs.

**Key points.**

- What is praised is the ocean-like good qualities of the buddhas and bodhisattvas; Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk divides these in detail into the qualities of body, of speech, and of mind.
- "The branches of praise-melody" means the causes of melody, that is, of the voice — the tongue, the palate and so on — or its many kinds, the modulation, bending, raising and settling of the voice; and since it is spoken of as "many," the word "ocean" is used.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the Great Commentary for a sevenfold division of melody: the middle, the sage, the earth-holder, the six-fold, the five-fold, the clear-minded, and the surrounding listener.
- Citing the scriptures of the *Jewel Lamp* and the *Kṣitigarbha*, he explains that one who makes offering and praise to the Jewels passes beyond the path of the four Māras and swiftly attains awakening, and that all the happiness and ease of the world also arises from this.
- ⚑ The commentaries do not agree on where this verse sits within the chapter's outline: Kunzang Pelden, Minyak Kunzang Sonam and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk make it the fifth subdivision of the branch of offering; Ngulchu Thogme makes it the sixth; Gyaltsab and Sabzang set it apart as a separate branch, "praise expressed in speech"; and His Holiness the Dalai Lama folds it into the branch of paying homage.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

By this: to all the buddhas who have gone and who will go in the three times; to the Dharma comprised of the truths of cessation and of the path; and to the supreme assembly, those bodhisattvas — emanating not merely one or two bodies of my own but measureless bodies equal in number to all the atoms of the fields, and bowing with reverence, out of a great faith and respect that recalls the qualities of body, speech and mind of the object of homage, I pay homage. Every posture in which the body bows is homage of the body. The "folded prostration" is the one in which the forehead, the two palms and the two knees touch the ground: it is called the prostration with five limbs, and those five limbs must definitely touch the ground — in particular, the two palms of the hands must definitely reach the ground. The "stretched prostration" is the one in which one falls like a tree trunk, or lays the whole body down on the ground; and one should stretch the fingertips out as far as one can. There is an oral tradition that pressing the palms perfectly flat together is the way of the non-Buddhists; it is said that they should be shaped like a jewel, hollow within. The outer jewel-shape symbolises the Form Body, and the hollow within, free of obstruction, symbolises the Truth Body. The joined palms are touched to the three places — the crown of the head, the throat and the heart — because these are the places where the channels of the brain and the vital essence of the body are; the throat is the root of where speech is uttered; and at the heart, in the middle of the chest between the two breasts, a single knot of channels falls, the indestructible essence, the root of samsara and nirvana, the place where mind resides. That is why the palms are touched to these three. Speaking songs of praise with the voice is homage of speech, and feeling devotion with the mind is homage of mind.

- **Palms joined like a flower about to open** (ཐལ་མོ་མེ་ཏོག་ཁ་འབུས་པ་ལྟ་བུ་) → the manner of the joined-palm gesture. It is explained that with the palms joined like a flower about to open and touched to the three places — the crown of the head, the throat and the heart — one pays homage to the body, the speech and the mind of the buddhas and bodhisattvas.
- **The stretched prostration, falling like a tree trunk** (བརྐྱང་ཕྱག་སྡོང་པོ་ལྟར་བསྒྱེལ་བ་) → the manner of prostration in which the whole body is laid on the ground. It is explained that the stretched prostration is the one in which one falls like a tree trunk, or lays the whole body on the ground, and that one should stretch out the fingertips as far as one can.
- **Palms shaped like a jewel** (ཐལ་མོ་ནོར་བུའི་དབྱིབས་ལྟར་) → a symbol of the two Bodies. It is explained that the palms, shaped like a jewel and hollow within, symbolise the two Bodies: the outer jewel-shape symbolises the Form Body, and the hollow within, being free of obstruction, symbolises the Truth Body.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. This Verse Begins the Branch of Paying Homage, One of the Seven Branches

Kunzang Pelden sets out clearly that the branch of offering in the second chapter came to its end at verse 2-23, and that from here the second branch, the branch of paying homage, begins.

###### 2. The Object of Homage Is the Three Jewels

All the commentaries agree in the end that homage is paid to the Three Jewels: all the buddhas who have gone in the three times; the sublime Dharma of scripture and realization; and the supreme assembly, the sangha of bodhisattvas.

###### 3. The Manner of Paying Homage — Emanating Bodies as Numerous as All the Atoms of the Fields, and Bowing

All the commentaries explain in agreement that from one's single body one emanates measureless bodies equal in number to all the subtle atoms of the infinite reach of the fields, and bows with reverence.

###### 4. Homage of the Three Doors — Body, Speech and Mind Must All Be Joined With Reverence

Bowing with the body, the joined palms touched to the three places; speaking the words of homage or songs of praise with the voice; recalling the qualities of the Three Jewels and feeling devotion with the mind: all three doors are said to be part of what makes homage.

###### 5. The Purpose of Paying Homage — Purifying One's Own and Others' Misdeeds and Obscurations

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk sets out clearly that the meaning of paying homage is to do so with the wish that all the misdeeds and obscurations gathered by the body, speech and mind of oneself and others be purified.

###### 6. The Kinds of Prostration — the Prostration With Five Limbs and the Stretched Prostration

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains in detail both the prostration with five limbs, in which the forehead, the two palms and the two knees touch the ground, and the stretched prostration, in which the whole body is laid on the ground.

###### 7. A Distinctive Point — on Each Emanated Body, Many Limbs Are Emanated for the Offering as Well

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that on each emanated body one must imagine emanating measureless heads and hands and tongues, so that each body bows with its five limbs and each tongue offers praise; and he sets this out as matching the words of the *Aspiration Prayer of Excellent Conduct*.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** With verse 2-24 the branch of offering is brought to a close and the branch of paying homage — one of the seven branches — begins: to all three of the Jewels, the buddhas who have gone in the three times, the sublime Dharma, and the supreme assembly, one states the pledge to pay homage by emanating one's own body in a number equal to all the atoms of the fields and bowing with reverence.

**Key points.**

- This is the beginning of the second branch of the second chapter, the branch of paying homage.
- The object of homage is the Three Jewels: the buddhas, the sublime Dharma, and the sangha of bodhisattvas.
- The manner of paying homage is to emanate, from one's single body, measureless bodies equal in number to the subtle atoms of all the fields, and to bow.
- All three doors must be joined with reverence in paying homage, and its purpose is to purify the misdeeds and obscurations gathered by the body, speech and mind of oneself and others.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains in detail the difference between the prostration with five limbs and the stretched prostration, and says that the joined-palm gesture, hollow within like the shape of a jewel, symbolises the two Bodies.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam sets out clearly that the sense of this matches the line of the *Aspiration Prayer of Excellent Conduct*, "bowing with bodies as numerous as the atoms of the fields"; and Sabzang too cites that very stanza as scripture at the end of this verse.
