# Day 38 — What did it actually leave you?

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Check the real exchange rate on one craving today
> **Detail:** Today's verse asks: what did past pleasures leave behind, and what did they cost you?

## Opening

Shantideva asks a simple, almost accounting question. All the pleasures you have already enjoyed and that are now gone, what do you have left of them? Nothing solid. And yet, to get them, you paid a real price, sometimes bending what you believed in, ignoring guidance you trusted. The verse is not against enjoying things. It is asking us to look honestly at the trade.

## From the Tradition

Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, reads the verse as a plain reckoning. Take any pleasure you chased hard in the past and now no longer have. What lasting thing did it leave you? Honestly, nothing that does not break. Then look at the other side of the ledger: what did getting it cost? Often something real, time you cannot get back, a value you set aside, guidance you knew was good and overrode anyway. Ngulchu's point is not that pleasure is bad or that you should feel guilty. It is that the trade is usually far worse than it looked at the time, because we count the gain at full price and forget to count the cost at all. Seeing the true exchange rate, even once, quietly changes what we are willing to pay next time.

## Today's Practice

When a strong craving pulls at you today, pause and run the honest sum: what will this actually leave me with afterward, and what does it cost to get it? Not to talk yourself out of every pleasure, just to see the real price tag before you pay. Sometimes you will still choose it; sometimes, seeing the rate clearly, you simply will not want to.
