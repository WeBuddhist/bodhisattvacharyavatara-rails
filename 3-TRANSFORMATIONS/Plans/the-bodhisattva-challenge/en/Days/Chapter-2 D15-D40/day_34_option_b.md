# Day 34 — You don't have to be good first

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Ask for help today with the part you're least proud of
> **Detail:** Today's verse calls out "protect me who have done wrong." Compassion does not turn away.

## Opening

Shantideva keeps calling out, and the words he uses are striking. He does not present himself as deserving. He cries to Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion, "protect me, who have done wrong." He asks for help precisely as the flawed person he is, not as someone who has cleaned himself up first. That is the heart of today.

## From the Tradition

Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, draws attention to one word in the verse: Avalokiteshvara's compassionate conduct is unerring. It does not misfire. It does not look at who is asking and decide they are too far gone. Notice how Shantideva approaches it. He does not wait until he is worthy; he comes as "one endowed with wrongs" and asks anyway. We often do the opposite. We feel we have to fix ourselves before we are allowed to ask for help, and so the ones who most need refuge are the ones who hold back. The verse cuts straight through that. Compassion that turned away from the flawed would not be great compassion at all; it is precisely for the person who has gone wrong and knows it. The only thing being asked of Shantideva is honesty about where he stands, and the willingness to call out from there.

## Today's Practice

Bring to mind the part of yourself you are least proud of, the thing you would rather not bring to practice. Today, bring exactly that, and ask for help with it, without first trying to explain it away or clean it up: "Help me with this; I can't do it alone." You do not have to deserve the help before you ask. Asking honestly is the practice.
