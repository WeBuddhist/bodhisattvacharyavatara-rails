## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the twenty-fourth and twenty-fifth verses of the fifth chapter. Here we can see a clear example: however much water we pour into a pot with a hole in its base, it does not stay, and simply leaks back out.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us an example that is easy to understand and encouraging.

If we pour water into a pot that has a hole in its base or side, in a short time the water leaks back out and does not remain. In the same way, however many good qualities a person without awareness gains from hearing, contemplating, and meditating, these will gradually be lost and forgotten.

Just as a sick person cannot do their work, without mindfulness and awareness, we have no power to do good deeds. So without awareness, the wisdom gained from hearing, contemplating, and meditating will not last. It will simply go to waste.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, I will look at the state of my own body and mind again and again. I will hold on to awareness and protect what I have learned from hearing and reflecting.

**Explanation:** *(Wisdom Practice)* If we do not have the awareness to continually check what our own body and mind are doing, the good qualities and wisdom that come from hearing, contemplating, and meditating will go to waste, like water leaking from a cracked pot. So if we look at our own mind again and again with awareness and guard mindfulness without distraction, all our training and good qualities can remain firm and stable in the mind. This brings great benefit.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden Clarifies That Even a Clear Mind Lacking Mindfulness and Awareness Has No Power for Virtuous Action

Just as someone disturbed by illness has no power for any task even while in the flush of youth and clear-minded, Khenpo Kunzang Pelden makes clear that a person whose mind is disturbed through lacking mindfulness and awareness likewise has no power to accomplish any virtuous action, however intelligent or sharp-witted that person may otherwise be.

###### 2. ⚑ Ngulchu Thogme Notes That the Detailed Exposition and the Great Commentary Diverge on Exactly Which Fault Is Taught

Following Ngulchu Thogme's citation of the two traditions, the Detailed Exposition explains this verse as teaching only the fault of lacking awareness, while the Great Commentary explains it as teaching the fault of lacking both mindfulness and awareness together.

###### 3. ⚑ Minyak Kunzang Sonam Resolves the Divergence by Distinguishing the Root Verse's Direct Statement from Its Intended Meaning

Taking up the disagreement noted above, Minyak Kunzang Sonam resolves it by proposing that, so far as the root verse directly teaches its subject, the fault is lacking awareness, while so far as the meaning actually intended is concerned, the fault is lacking both mindfulness and awareness.

###### 4. The Root Verse's Section Heading Is "The Faults of Lacking Awareness," Teaching That Without Mindfulness and Awareness No Virtuous Action Can Be Accomplished

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen identifies the root verse's own section heading as "the faults of lacking awareness," the chief point being that without mindfulness and awareness one is unable to accomplish any virtuous action at all.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse clearly teaches, together with an illustration, the fault of being without mindfulness and awareness. Just as a person whose body is afflicted by illness is unable to accomplish any worldly task, so too a person whose mind is disturbed by delusion and confusion, and who lacks mindfulness and awareness regarding what is to be adopted and discarded, becomes without power to accomplish any virtuous action at all.

**Key points.**

- **Matching the image to its meaning.** Just as a person whose body is disturbed by an illness of wind, bile, or phlegm becomes powerless to accomplish any task, so too a person whose mind is disturbed by delusion, and who lacks mindfulness and awareness regarding what is to be adopted and discarded, is said to be unable to accomplish even the slightest virtuous action. Even a person who is otherwise sharp-witted and clear-minded becomes utterly without power to accomplish virtue once mindfulness and awareness are lacking.
- **⚑ The commentaries' differing positions.** There is a difference among the great commentaries as to exactly which fault this verse chiefly teaches. As Ngulchu Thogme cites it, some who give a detailed explanation hold that it teaches only the fault of lacking awareness, while the Great Commentary and others hold that it teaches the fault of lacking both mindfulness and awareness together. As Minyak Kunzang Sonam clarifies on this point, so far as the root verse directly teaches its subject, it is the fault of lacking awareness; so far as the meaning actually intended is concerned, it applies to the fault of lacking both mindfulness and awareness — thus he brings the two positions together into one without contradiction.

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains that if one's mind lacks awareness, so that the force of mindfulness and awareness is absent and one falls under the sway of forgetfulness, then even though one has previously engaged in extensive hearing and reflection, these will not remain in memory without being forgotten — just as water leaks out of a pot with a hole in its base, whether at the bottom or the side.

##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

If one lacks awareness, wisdom itself is squandered. That is, for a person whose mind is distracted through lacking awareness, whatever wisdom of hearing has arisen from what was heard from the guru, whatever wisdom of reflection has arisen from examining and analyzing its meaning correctly and repeatedly, and whatever wisdom of meditation has arisen from becoming trained through one-pointed meditation on that very meaning — for such a person, just as water poured into a pot with a hole in its base gradually leaks out until none remains, these three wisdoms of hearing, reflecting, and meditating will not remain for long within mere memory, but will be forgotten; for once awareness is lacking, memory alone is unable to guard them.

If one is without awareness, an excellent wisdom does not arise. For a mind that lacks awareness, the wisdom of hearing, reflecting, and meditating — just as water poured into a leaky pot does not remain — likewise does not remain in memory: the meaning realized through them comes to be forgotten. This is according to the Detailed Exposition. ⚑ Acharya Gelha, however, says that it is not clearly experienced.

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

For a person whose mind is not held by the awareness that examines things well, even though the wisdoms arising from hearing, reflecting, and meditating have arisen just once, just as water poured into a pot with a hole in its base or side leaks out and does not remain for long, so too, in that person's memory, the meanings that arose from hearing and the rest do not remain for long, but decline.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

For a person whose mind is not held by the ally of awareness — which examines and correctly knows, one by one, the occasions for the three doors' actions and non-actions — even though there had previously arisen an excellent threefold wisdom (the wisdom of hearing, arisen from hearing the words and meaning of scripture from the mouth of a spiritual master upon whom one relies; the wisdom of reflection, arisen from correctly examining and analyzing the meaning of what was heard by means of the four kinds of reasoning; and the wisdom of meditation, arisen from meditating by resting one-pointedly and evenly on the meaning reflected upon), nevertheless, just as when water is first poured to fill a pot that has developed a hole at its base or side, the water does not remain long within it but leaks back out until it is exhausted, so too, in the memory of that person who lacks awareness, those three wisdoms that had previously existed will likewise not remain afterward; even an excellent wisdom will decline and be squandered.

##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

A person whose mind lacks the reflective consideration of awareness — which examines and analyzes, again and again, the state of one's own body and mind — such a person, whatever he does, whether it be hearing scriptural texts and treatises from a spiritual teacher and thereby gaining understanding, or cutting through exaggeration about their meaning through his own reflection and thereby coming to know it, or, having come to know it, taking it into experience and meditating upon it — whatever he does of these, all of it is like this: just as when water is now poured to fill a pot that has developed a hole at its base or side, the water leaks back out within a short time and does not remain inside, in just the same way, for a person who lacks awareness, everything he has heard, reflected upon, and meditated on will, without exception, be gradually lost, since memory alone is unable to hold it, and so carries the fault of not remaining in memory. He therefore teaches that one must diligently apply this awareness that examines and analyzes, again and again, at all times and in every way, the state of one's own body and mind.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Without Awareness, However Much Wisdom of Hearing, Reflecting, and Meditating Is Attained, It Is Squandered Like Water from a Leaky Pot

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that however much of the threefold wisdom of hearing, reflecting, and meditating one attains, if awareness is lacking, memory alone cannot guard it, and so it is squandered like water from a leaky pot.

###### 2. Minyak Kunzang Sonam Identifies the Three Wisdoms of Hearing, Reflecting, and Meditating Individually and Explains in Detail How Each Arises

Minyak Kunzang Sonam distinguishes the wisdom of hearing, the wisdom of reflection, and the wisdom of meditation as three separate attainments, explaining in detail how each specifically arises before showing how, together, they can still be lost.

###### 3. ⚑ Ngulchu Thogme Notes a Difference Between the Detailed Exposition's "Is Forgotten" and Acharya Gelha's "Is Not Clearly Experienced"

As Ngulchu Thogme lays out the point, the Detailed Exposition (Vibhūticandra's commentary) explains "it does not remain in memory" to mean that the meaning is forgotten outright, whereas Acharya Gelha explains it to mean instead that the meaning is simply not clearly experienced — that is, a different, more precise gloss under the same name.

###### 4. Attaining the Power of Hearing, Reflecting, and Meditating Is Not Enough — An Excellent Wisdom Requires the Awareness That Guards It

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen teaches that merely attaining the power of hearing, reflecting, and meditating is not sufficient; an excellent, lasting wisdom also requires the awareness that stands guard alongside it.

<!-- sub:divergences -->
#### Divergences Among Commentators

- ⚑ **Ngulchu Thogme, citing two prior authorities:** According to the Detailed Exposition (Vibhūticandra's commentary), the phrase "does not remain in memory" is explained to mean that, owing to the condition of lacking awareness, the meanings gained from hearing, reflecting, and meditating come to be forgotten outright — lost from the domain of memory. According to Acharya Gelha's position, however, this does not mean merely that the meanings are forgotten; rather, he explains it to mean that, within memory, those meanings are simply "not clearly experienced" — that is, not taken up as an experiential realization. There is thus a subtle difference between the meaning being forgotten and the meaning merely not being clearly experienced.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-25]]

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches, together with an illustration, how the fault of lacking awareness causes the wisdom arisen from hearing, reflecting, and meditating to fail to remain in memory and so to be lost. As the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* itself teaches, awareness is precisely that faculty which repeatedly examines the state of one's own mind; without it, however much effort one puts into hearing, reflecting, and meditating, none of it comes to rest firmly in one's own mind-stream — just like water leaking back out of a broken or leaky pot.

**Key points.**

- **The threefold wisdom of hearing, reflecting, and meditating.** As Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains in detail, the wisdom arisen from hearing, the wisdom arisen from reflecting, and the wisdom arisen from meditating are the very root of practice, yet if they are not caught by the hook of awareness, they cannot remain firmly grounded in memory, and so fall away.
- **The image of the leaky pot.** As is made clear in the commentaries of Khenpo Kunzang Pelden, Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk, and His Holiness the Dalai Lama, just as water poured into a broken or leaky pot leaks back out and does not remain, however much water is poured in, so too, however many good qualities of hearing, reflecting, and meditating a person lacking awareness accumulates, they cannot be held within the treasury of memory and become entirely lost.
