# Day 27 — Confession has more than one part

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Confess one wrong, then turn and ask for help
> **Detail:** Today's verses name the harm, confess it, and turn to the protectors for rescue, the parts that make confession whole.

## Opening

Saying sorry can feel like the whole of it. Today Shantideva names the harm done to the Three Jewels, his parents and his teachers, confesses the whole of it to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and then turns to them and asks to be rescued before death.

## From the Tradition

It is easy to think confession is just owning up, and that once you have admitted a thing you are done. Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, sets it out as several parts working together: facing the wrong and turning against it, leaning on the protectors as a support, applying something that counters the harm, and resolving to hold back from now on. Seen this way, the three verses do more than admit. Naming the harm to the Three Jewels, parents and teachers, and confessing the whole mass of it, is the facing. The plea in the last verse, rescue me, deliver me, is the leaning: you turn to someone with the strength to help, because remorse on its own does not lift the weight. Admitting opens the door; turning to the protectors is what carries you through it.

## Today's Practice

If one wrong is sitting on your mind, name it plainly to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, and then do the part that is easy to skip: ask them for help, the way you would lean on someone stronger than you. You do not have to carry it alone.
