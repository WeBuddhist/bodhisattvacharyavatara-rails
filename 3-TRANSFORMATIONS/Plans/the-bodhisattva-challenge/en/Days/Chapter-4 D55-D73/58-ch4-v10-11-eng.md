## Today's Verse

> If the happiness of a single being
> is destroyed, I myself will be harmed.
> Needless to say, if the happiness of countless
> beings, filling all of space, is destroyed! ^4-10

> Thus, if powerful downfalls
> and the powerful Mind of Awakening
> alternate in cyclic existence,
> it will take a long time to attain the stages. ^4-11

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the two verses 10 and 11 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. When two people pull a cart in opposite directions, it does not move forward. Let us think today about how making mistakes and then mending them, over and over by turns, works in just that way.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

The great adept Tilopa was close to reaching the supreme attainment within seven days. At that time he stole a handful of sesame that had not been given to him. He confessed that small mistake and it was made clean. Even so, the time it took him to reach the attainment was put off by seven months.

So even when we confess wrongdoing and make it clean, we still lose real time on the way up to the higher stages. It is far more important to give up downfalls from the start than to count on mending them later.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will not destroy the happiness of even one being, and I will do no harm at all to anyone.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ If we destroy the happiness of even one being, we ourselves are harmed. So if we do no harm to the happiness of others and bring up loving-kindness instead, it helps us and them alike. This is what makes our own mind clean.
