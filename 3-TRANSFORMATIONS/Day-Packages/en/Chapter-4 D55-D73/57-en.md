# Day 57 — Inconceivable karma, the gravest downfall, and the peril of hindering another's merit

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the three verses 7 to 9 from the fourth chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. Among the ordinary people around us, we do not know who is a bodhisattva. So we should respect even the small good that others do.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses tell us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago King Candraprabha generated bodhicitta, and he gave away whatever anyone asked of him. Mara took the form of a brahmin and asked the king for his hand. The king cut off his own right hand and offered it with his left hand.

Mara acted as if he were angry. He said, "This left hand of yours is dirty," and would not take it. He threw it on the ground. The king lost heart. Thinking that he could not satisfy a being like this, he gave up bodhicitta. That king was later born as Śāriputra, and by relying on the buddha's path of the hearers he reached the fruit of an arhat.

Even something like Śāriputra giving up bodhicitta cannot be judged by an ordinary mind. How karma ripens is known only by the all-knowing wisdom of a buddha. So we should take seriously the warning in the ninth verse against getting in the way of the good that others do.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today I will not get in the way of even the smallest good thing others do. I will support it instead.

**Explanation:** *(Avoiding wrongdoing)* If we obstruct or belittle the good that others do, we reduce their power to benefit beings. We do not know whether that other person is a bodhisattva, so getting in the way of their good is very great wrongdoing. But if we rejoice in even the small good that others do, and support it, our own mind is made clean. And great merit grows.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

If that is so, then someone might object: in a past life, the noble Śāriputra generated bodhicitta for the sake of unsurpassed, perfect enlightenment, engaged in the vast collections needed for that aim, and paid homage to a thousand buddhas — but then, provoked by a demon, he gave up bodhicitta. If that is so, how do we understand him going on to attain the enlightenment of a hearer and accomplish the welfare of beings? Even though a person gives up bodhicitta, the way that the beings he was to guide are still brought to liberation is not something that falls within the scope of ordinary perception — the working of karma there is inconceivable, and is a domain known only by the all-knowing one.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

This verse dispels an objection based on an apparent contradiction with another scriptural account. Long ago, Śāriputra generated the supreme resolve for enlightenment and was, at that time, a bodhisattva. Later, an evil being performed a magical display that provoked him, and, shaken by that circumstance, his resolve weakened, so that he fell away from bodhicitta in this manner. Yet even so, cared for by the compassion of the kind Teacher, he came to actualize the state of an arhat — and this is an inconceivable working of karma. Even considering that, from countless eons in the past, there existed some extraordinary karmic connection formed through virtuous deeds, it must also have been through the kind Teacher's compassionate resolve and the power of Śāriputra's own uncommon merit from past lives that he was able to actualize the state of an arhat once more. This is an inconceivable working of karma that only the all-knowing one knows — we are not able to explain it in full just as it is.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-7]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

In the *White Lotus Sūtra*, it is told that Śāriputra, having in a past life generated bodhicitta, paid homage to a thousand buddhas and engaged in the welfare of beings. At that time, King Vinaśin — as he then was — was approached by an evil demon disguised as a brahmin who asked for his hand. The king cut off his right hand and offered it with his left, but the demon, showing disrespect, said "I do not accept what is given without proper reverence" and threw it away. Grief-stricken, the king gave up bodhicitta, yet he still went on to attain personal liberation as an arhat and to accomplish the welfare of beings. If one objects that this contradicts the account in the *Golden-Hued One's Story*, where a person who had practiced the bodhisattva conduct for forty eons gave up bodhicitta and thereby became a solitary realizer — it does not, in fact, contradict it: that a person who gives up bodhicitta is not, thereby, cast down into a lower realm, but instead attains liberation himself and brings others to liberation as well, is, in each such case, a matter of that person's own particular past karma, and because the various workings of karma are inconceivable when viewed with ordinary sight, this cannot be resolved by reasoning alone. Just as the various causes behind the single dazzling pattern of a peacock's feather are known only to the all-knowing one, and to no one else — so too here, what is the use of wearying oneself with speculation?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-7]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

The noble Śāriputra, having in a past life generated bodhicitta and engaged in the conduct it requires, was approached by a demon who asked for his right hand; the demon, having received it cut off and offered with the left hand, spoke words of displeasure, and Śāriputra, grief-stricken, gave up bodhicitta — yet later, it is said, he attained the state of an arhat. If one objects that this and similar accounts are contradictory, it is not so: that a person such as the noble Śāriputra gives up bodhicitta and yet is brought to liberation is a working of karma that ordinary beings cannot fathom by reasoning, for it is known only by the all-knowing one, and by no one else.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-7]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

If one then asks: when the noble Śāriputra engaged in bodhisattva conduct, a demon asked for his right hand; having received it cut off and offered with the left hand, the demon spoke words of displeasure, so that Śāriputra, grief-stricken, gave up bodhicitta — yet he is said to have gone on to attain the state of an arhat, and Golden-Hued One's earlier practice of bodhisattva conduct for forty eons is said to have ended, immediately upon giving up bodhicitta, in his becoming a solitary realizer — are these accounts not contradictory? No: that a person gives up bodhicitta and yet is brought to liberation is a working of karma that ordinary people cannot fathom, for it is known only by the all-knowing one — this is stated, so, in the *Vairocana* and the *Nampar Shepa* [Vinayavastu-type sources]. It is also said that although Kṣemadeva was in a sense given up through skillful means, he was properly taken up again, and so beings were liberated. Vibhūticandra, for his part, says that while conventional bodhicitta was given up, ultimate bodhicitta was not given up — and this seems right to me: having given up the resolve to benefit others, he did not give up the wisdom realizing selflessness, and so he did not fall to a lower realm. Even so, it is inconceivable — so what is the use of wearying oneself with speculation?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^4-7]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If one then asks: when Śāriputra, having in a past life generated bodhicitta, paid homage to a thousand buddhas and engaged in the welfare of beings, an evil demon disguised as a brahmin asked for his hand; he cut off his right hand and offered it with his left, and the demon, saying the left hand was impure and so would not accept what was given, threw it away — Śāriputra, grief-stricken, gave up bodhicitta, yet is said to have attained liberation and to have accomplished the welfare of beings — does this not contradict such accounts? Such persons who, for a time, give up bodhicitta are not thereby cast down to a lower realm but instead attain liberation and bring others to liberation as well, because the various workings of karma, whether strong or weak, are inconceivable when viewed with ordinary sight. As it is said in the *Praise* and the *Autocommentary on the Treasury*: "The particular causes behind a single peacock feather are known by the all-knowing one alone — for it is not knowable except by all-knowing wisdom" — in just this way, the finer details are known only by the all-knowing one's wisdom, and ordinary beings cannot fathom them; to try would only bring confusion. The *Great Commentary* on this passage explains that there is no fault in this, because the power of the mind directed toward liberation was not given up even though bodhicitta itself was given up, so that liberation was attained and beings were brought to liberation. It is also explained that although the deity of virtue was, for a time, given up, it was later taken up again, and beings were thereby liberated.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-7]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

If one then asks: when the noble Śāriputra, once King Candraprabha, generated bodhicitta and trained in its conduct, he paid homage to a thousand buddhas and, while accomplishing the welfare of beings, an evil demon disguised as a brahmin asked for his right hand; he cut it off and offered it with his left, and the demon, displeased, said the left hand was impure and would not accept what was given, and threw it away in anger — Śāriputra, grief-stricken, thought he could not satisfy such a being and gave up bodhicitta, yet is later said to have attained the state of an arhat; and Golden-Hued One's earlier bodhisattva conduct practiced over forty eons is said to have ended, immediately upon giving up bodhicitta, in his becoming a solitary realizer — does this not contradict such accounts? The position of the *Vairocana* and the *Nampar Shepa* is that this is known by the all-knowing one, and that we ourselves do not know it — and this agrees with the root text and with the great majority of commentaries. The *Great Commentary* explains that there is no fault in the explanation that, although bodhicitta was given up, liberation was attained because the power of mind directed toward liberation was not given up; and it holds that although the deity of virtue was given up, it was later taken up again. Vibhūticandra explains that although conventional bodhicitta was given up, ultimate bodhicitta was not given up; and the bodhisattva Ngulchu Thogme regards this as fitting, though he also says: it is inconceivable, so what is the use of wearying oneself with speculation? Some say that bodhicitta was not, in fact, actually given up — and this is explained as contradicting the words "though bodhicitta was given up."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-7]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Even though a person such as Śāriputra gave up bodhicitta at the moment an evil demon provoked him, that such persons as Śāriputra are not born in a lower realm but instead attain the enlightenment of a hearer and are thereby liberated is itself Śāriputra's own past karma unfolding together with the buddha's enlightened activity guiding him — and this is inconceivable. In short, statements such as "the workings of beings' karma are inconceivable," "the manner of a buddha's enlightened activity is inconceivable," and "the power of mantra and medicine is inconceivable" show that such things fall within the domain known only to the all-knowing buddha, and not within the scope of others' perception. Alternatively: since Śāriputra is an emanated hearer, his giving up bodhicitta at the time the evil demon provoked him, during his earlier practice of bodhisattva conduct, was merely an enactment of the appearance of giving up bodhicitta, not an actual abandonment of its meaning; and this enactment of giving up bodhicitta was itself an enlightened activity bringing about the liberation of impure beings such as the demon — so that this manner of enlightened activity is itself inconceivable, known only by the all-knowing buddha. Either of these two ways of explaining it is admissible.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-7]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Even If Bodhicitta Is Given Up, an Inconceivable Working of Karma Can Still Bring Liberation

If a person gives up bodhicitta, the working of karma by which that person may still be brought to liberation is not something that falls within ordinary perception — it is inconceivable, and known only by the all-knowing one. All the commentaries agree on this.

###### 2. The Illustration for This Verse Is the Venerable Śāriputra

Having in a past life generated bodhicitta and paid homage to a thousand buddhas, Śāriputra was approached by an evil demon disguised as a brahmin who asked for his hand; he cut it off and offered it with his left, and, disrespected and cast aside, he gave up bodhicitta out of grief — yet later attained the state of an arhat.

###### 3. This Verse Dispels an Objection Based on an Apparent Contradiction with Other Scriptural Accounts

That someone such as Śāriputra gave up bodhicitta and yet later attained liberation, while Golden-Hued One's account teaches that giving up bodhicitta led immediately to becoming a solitary realizer, are explained as not being in contradiction.

###### 4. The Commentaries Diverge on How Exactly to Identify This Working of Karma

Some explain that only conventional bodhicitta was given up. Some explain that the power of mind directed toward liberation was not given up. Some explain that it was later properly taken up again. Some decline to settle the matter, explaining only that this working of karma is known by the all-knowing one, and that we do not know it. ⚑

###### 5. The Image of the Peacock Feather Shows That Only the All-Knowing One Knows Karma's Finer Details

The single dazzling pattern of one peacock feather has causes so varied that only the all-knowing one knows them; in the same way, the finer workings of karma are known only by all-knowing wisdom, and not by ordinary beings.

###### 6. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk Gives Two Ways of Explaining This

One way applies the general principle that the karma of beings and the enlightened activity of buddhas are alike inconceivable, citing Śāriputra's case as an example. The other explains that, since Śāriputra is an emanated hearer, his giving up bodhicitta was merely an enactment of that appearance, not an actual abandonment of its meaning.

###### 7. Śāriputra's Later Attainment of Arhatship Is Explained as Resting on the Teacher's Compassion and an Uncommon Karmic Connection

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that this rested on an extraordinary karmic connection formed from countless eons in the past together with the power of the kind Teacher's compassion, and states that we are not able to explain it in full just as it is.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that even if a person gives up bodhicitta, the working of karma by which that person may still be brought to liberation is inconceivable — known only by the all-knowing one. All the commentaries agree on this basic ground, and illustrate it with the story of the venerable Śāriputra, who generated bodhicitta in a past life, gave it up out of grief when a demon disrespected his offering, and yet went on to attain the state of an arhat.

**Key points.**

- Even if a person gives up bodhicitta, the working of karma by which that person, or those he was to guide, may still be brought to liberation is inconceivable, known only by the all-knowing one.
- The illustration for this verse is the venerable Śāriputra, who generated bodhicitta in a past life, was disrespected by a demon when offering his own hand, gave up bodhicitta out of grief, and yet later attained the state of an arhat.
- This verse resolves an apparent contradiction with the account of Golden-Hued One, whose giving up of bodhicitta led immediately to his becoming a solitary realizer.
- The commentaries diverge on how exactly to identify what was and was not given up — whether only conventional bodhicitta, or the power of mind directed toward liberation, or whether it was later taken up again — while several decline to settle the matter, holding that only the all-knowing one truly knows.
- The image of the peacock feather's many causes, known only to the all-knowing one, illustrates why ordinary beings cannot fathom the finer workings of karma.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

For a bodhisattva who has taken the bodhisattva vow, giving up bodhicitta is explained, among the eighteen root downfalls taught in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*, the *Bodhisattvabhūmi*, and elsewhere, to be the heaviest of all downfalls. At the very moment bodhicitta is given up, the bodhisattva vow degenerates immediately and irrecoverably, without needing the other conditions of full entanglement to be complete. *(By contrast, a root downfall such as praising oneself while disparaging others is specifically taught to require the full set of entangling conditions.)* The reason this downfall is so grave is that giving up the bodhicitta that was generated to accomplish the welfare of beings diminishes, or causes to degenerate, the very activity of accomplishing beings' welfare.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

It goes without saying that giving up bodhicitta itself is a great fault. Beyond that, one who obstructs a bodhisattva working to accomplish the welfare of beings — provoking or interrupting that bodhisattva's accomplishment of others' welfare, acting as a hindrance that cuts it off — harms the bodhisattva's ability to accomplish the vast welfare of beings; and as a result, one must experience boundless, endless lower realms. Because of this, he explains, it is an extremely grave fault. These points, he notes, are gathered from what is taught in the *Sūtra of the Miraculous Display Definitively Settling Utter Peace*.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-9]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Beyond that, if some other being becomes an obstacle or hindrance, even for the space of a mere instant — let alone a long time — to this bodhisattva's meritorious virtue, then, because that hindering being has thereby created karma that diminishes and degenerates this bodhisattva's ability to accomplish the welfare of all beings, the one who caused the hindrance will experience being born in the lower realms an endless, boundless number of times — this is settled by the scriptural citation drawn from the *Sūtra of the Miraculous Display Definitively Settling Utter Peace*. He also explains that "merit" here can be understood, in line with the previous verse's point about a bodhisattva's aspirational resolve being diminished by hindering others' welfare, as referring, in the same sense, to the root downfall of abandoning bodhicitta belonging to the lesser vehicle in reverse — that is, to that bodhisattva's own aspirational resolve, an interpretation not distinct from the fault of the preceding verse.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-9]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Furthermore, whoever else, even for a mere instant, obstructs and hinders this bodhisattva's merit, that is, virtue, causes that bodhisattva's capacity to accomplish the welfare of beings to become diminished; and because of this, the one who caused the hindrance takes rebirth in the lower realms an endless, boundless number of times. One should be careful about this point: because one does not know where a bodhisattva may be, this fault is very easy to fall into. If one is able to guard well against it, one is able to avoid most faults that depend upon a person.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-9]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Beyond that, if some other being becomes an obstacle and hindrance to this bodhisattva's merit — that is, to the generation of bodhicitta — then that bodhisattva becomes diminished with respect to the welfare of all beings, being rendered incapable of it; and so the one who caused the hindrance falls into boundless lower realms. The reason is: if destroying even a single being's happiness causes oneself to degenerate, then how much more so, needless to say, does hindering the virtue of a bodhisattva — one who would destroy the happiness of all embodied beings throughout the whole of space were his conduct interrupted — lead one to the lower realms. *(Note: this block, ^4-19, groups the explanation for verses 4-8, 4-9, and 4-10 together after their consecutive transclusions.)*

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Beyond the downfall being grave for oneself, if some other being becomes an obstacle or hindrance, even for the space of a mere instant — let alone a long time — to this bodhisattva's collection of merit that benefits others, then, because that hindering being has thereby created karma that diminishes and degenerates his capacity to accomplish the welfare of all beings, the one who caused the hindrance will experience being born in the lower realms an endless, boundless number of times.

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

In this way, giving up bodhicitta is itself a grave downfall for oneself; but beyond that, if some other being becomes an obstacle or hindrance, even for the duration of a mere instant — let alone a long time — to this bodhisattva's meritorious virtue that accomplishes others' benefit, then that bodhisattva, the object of the hindrance, becomes diminished and incapacitated in accomplishing the welfare of all beings; and because that being has, thereby, destroyed the welfare of all beings, the one who caused the hindrance will take rebirth in the lower realms — in hell and elsewhere — an endless, boundless number of times, without limit. Therefore, since one does not know where a bodhisattva may be, and this fault is very easy to fall into, one should be careful about this point; and it is also said that if one can guard against it, one can block off many faults that depend upon a person.

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

If, on some occasion, another person obstructs or hinders, even for the duration of a mere instant, this bodhisattva's merit — in short, if one obstructs or hinders even something like giving a mere mouthful of food and drink to a single animal — then, because the bodhisattva who is the object of that hindrance thereby becomes weakened to that extent in accomplishing the welfare of beings, the fault belonging to the person who caused the hindrance produces a future rebirth going to a destiny among the lower realms of a kind that is utterly without limit or bound, immeasurable in nature.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Obstructing a Bodhisattva's Merit Even for a Single Instant Leads to Boundless Lower Realms

If another being obstructs, even for a mere instant, this bodhisattva's merit, then, because that bodhisattva's capacity to accomplish the welfare of all beings is thereby diminished, the one who caused the obstruction takes rebirth in the lower realms an endless, boundless number of times. All the commentaries agree on this.

###### 2. The Measure of This Fault Is Established by the Sūtra of the Miraculous Display Definitively Settling Utter Peace

Obstructing a bodhisattva's virtue, even to the extent of giving merely a single mouthful of food to an animal, is taught in this sūtra to generate immeasurably more wrongdoing than killing every being in Jambudvīpa and seizing all their possessions, because it obstructs the very root of virtue that gives rise to the appearance of a buddha.

###### 3. This Verse Continues the Preceding One (4-8) as the Second Part of the Explanation of the Fault of Degenerated Bodhicitta

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen marks this with the outline heading "Second." Ngulchu Thogme, likewise, groups verses 4-8, 4-9, and 4-10 into a single continuous explanation, showing that the fault of a degenerated bodhicitta is grave not only for oneself but also, extremely so, for anyone who obstructs it in another.

###### 4. Since One Cannot Know Where a Bodhisattva May Be, One Should Be Respectful and Careful in All Circumstances Toward Every Person

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both advise that, because one does not know where a bodhisattva may be, this fault is very easy to fall into, so one should be careful about this point; and if one is able to guard well against it, one is able to avoid most faults that depend upon a person.

###### 5. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden Notes That "Merit" Here Could Also Be Read as Connecting Back to the Fault of the Preceding Verse (4-8)

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that "merit" in this verse can also be understood, consistent with the point of the preceding verse, as referring to that bodhisattva's own aspirational resolve — an interpretation not distinct from the fault taught there.
