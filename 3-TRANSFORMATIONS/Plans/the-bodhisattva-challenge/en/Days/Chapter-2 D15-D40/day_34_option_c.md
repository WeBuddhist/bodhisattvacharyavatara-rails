# Day 34 — Not shouting into an empty room

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Call out today as if you are surrounded by help
> **Detail:** Today's verse names not one protector but many. You are not alone in the dark.

## Opening

Shantideva does not call a single name. He calls many: Akashagarbha, whose name means the treasury of space, Ksitigarbha, the treasury of the earth, and all the protectors of great compassion. And he calls from the heart, not the lips. The picture is no longer one frightened person alone in the dark. It is someone surrounded on every side by help, finally crying out to it.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, fills in the list the verse only begins. After Akashagarbha and Ksitigarbha he adds Maitreya, Sarvanivaranavishkambhin, and all the protectors of great compassion. The naming matters. A few verses ago Shantideva was searching the four directions and finding no one; now he names protector after protector, and the room that felt empty turns out to be crowded with help. Sazang also keeps the verse's own phrase: the cry comes from the heart, not just the mouth. That is the difference between reciting a list of holy names and actually reaching for them. The fortitude these verses promise does not come from convincing yourself you are safe. It comes from realising you were never as alone as the fear told you, and then letting yourself call out for real.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Once today, if you feel alone with something heavy, pause and picture yourself surrounded, not by an empty room but by the compassionate ones, many of them, turned toward you. Then call out from the heart, in your own plain words, not a tidy prayer but a real one: "I'm here, please help me." Let yourself mean it, and notice that the asking itself already loosens the aloneness.
