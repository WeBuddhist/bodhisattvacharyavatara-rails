## Today's Verse

> In the past, I inflicted
> similar harm on sentient beings.
> Therefore, it is appropriate that this harm
> comes to me from those who harm sentient beings. ^6-42

> The weapon and my body
> are both causes of suffering.
> If I remove my body from the weapon,
> at whom should I be angry? ^6-43

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the forty-second and forty-third verses of the sixth chapter. When a weapon wounds our body, the enemy's weapon and our own body have both come together. So why are we angry at only one of them?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging.

In the past, I harmed others myself. So the harm that comes to me today is right, like paying back a debt. Cause and effect does not deceive. It is not possible to experience the result of bad karma that we did not do ourselves. So this harm surely comes from an earlier cause. Also, a wound comes when the enemy's weapon and my own body both meet. If one of them is missing, no wound comes. So if we must be angry, we must be angry at both. It is not right to be angry only at the other one and not at our own body.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when others harm me, I will think that my own past bad karma is ripening, and I will not get angry.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ In the past I harmed others, so harm comes to me today. This is the result of karma. Cause and effect does not deceive, so it is not possible to experience the result of karma we did not do ourselves. If we remember that this harm is our own karma ripening, we have no need to be angry at others.
