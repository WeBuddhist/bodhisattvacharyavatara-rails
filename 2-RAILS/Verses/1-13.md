**Variants**
⚑ The Sanskrit concludes *nāśrīyate tatkathama**jñasattvaiḥ*** — "why would not even **ignorant** beings (*ajñasattvaiḥ*) rely on it?" The Tibetan reads **བག་ཅན་རྣམས་** (bag can rnams) — "**vigilant/careful** beings." These are semantically opposite: Sanskrit names the least qualified (*ajña* = ignorant, unwise), while Tibetan names the most motivated (bag can = those who are circumspect, who take care). MKS notes the Sanskrit explicitly, explaining that the great commentary (*'grel chen*) reads: "even ignorant beings (*mi shes kyi sems can*) bereft of wisdom — why would they not rely?" — i.e. even the most unqualified should rely; *a fortiori* the wise.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-13)

## Traditional Interpretation

### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Palden (Tibetan)

KKP reads verse 1-13 as the fourth benefit of bodhicitta. Even those who have committed the gravest sins — severing the Dharma, harming the Three Jewels, the five anantarya crimes — if they generate bodhicitta, are like a person who murdered his own father yet still, by relying on a brave, well-armed escort traveling through dangerous territory, escapes all the revenge-seeking enemies' danger and danger of the lower realms (*pha bsad pa'i nyes can gyi mi zhig skyel ma dpa' bo la brten nas bza' len rag gi dgra'i gnod pa'am 'jigs pa chen po las yud tsam gys sgrol ba ltar*). By relying on bodhicitta the fruition of those grave sins in the hells is averted; they experience only a small token (*dar gyi pho long bsdabs pa tsam las mi myong bar thar bar 'gyur ba*) and are freed from the lower realms. Therefore vigilant beings who fear sin should certainly rely on it.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-13)

### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Tibetan)

GDR labels this the fourth benefit (*bzhi pa ni*): bodhicitta is the cause for eliminating the realm of non-virtue (*mi dge ba'i phyogs zad par byed pa'i rgyu yang yin par bstan pa ni*). Even extremely grave sins — harming the Three Jewels, the five anantarya — once one relies on the capable antidote-force of bodhicitta, the danger of the lower realms (*ngan song gi 'jigs pa*) is escaped in an instant, just as a brave, powerful escort protects one from the great dangers of enemies on the road. The verse thus confirms: bodhicitta can override even definitely-matured karma (*myon nges kyi las kyi nus pa 'joms par byed nus*); if it can do that, *a fortiori* indefinite karma (*ma nges pa*). Those who take care in what to adopt and avoid (*blang dor la bzod pa lhur len pa'i bag can rnams*) therefore have every reason to rely on it.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-13)

### ngulchu-thogmed — Ngulchu Thokme Sangpo (Tibetan)

NTS reads the verse as teaching that bodhicitta is the cause for freeing one even from the ripening of extremely grave sins. Even if one has harmed the Three Jewels, committed anantarya sins and the like — by relying on bodhicitta one is freed from the ripening of even those great sins in an instant (*sdig chen de'i rnam smin las yud tsam gyis dgrol bar 'gyur ba*), just as by relying on a brave escort one is freed from the great danger of enemies on the road. NTS cites the Aksayamati sūtra: "Just as relying on a brave man, one fears no enemy — so too the bodhisattva who properly relies on bodhicitta, the brave one who has generated bodhicitta, fears no enemy of misconduct."
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-13)

### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Mi Nyag Kunzang Sönam (Tibetan)

MKS gives the most detailed analysis. The referent of the simile's "brave one" (*dpa' bo*) is bodhicitta itself as a powerful antidote force (*gnyen po stobs dang ldan pa'i byang chub kyi sems*). Even someone who harmed the Three Jewels or committed the five anantarya crimes — before having generated bodhicitta — if they rely on bodhicitta, it overrides (*zil gyis mnan nas nus pa nyams par byas pa*) those great sins, depriving them of the capacity to produce their fruit (*'bras bu skyed pa'i nus pa med par byas pa de bsregs pa nyid yin*). The result: that person may experience a small token amount in the lower realms — like a hero wrapped in a drop of blood — and then is freed. MKS explicitly notes the textual variant: in the Sanskrit the verse ends "why would not **ignorant beings** (*mi shes skye bos*) rely?" meaning even those bereft of wisdom should rely — *a fortiori* the wise. The great commentary (*'grel chen*) also follows this Sanskrit reading: "even ignorant beings, those bereft of wisdom, why would they not rely? — meaning even the most unqualified should; how much more the discerning." MKS cites both the Aksayamati sūtra and the Gaṇḍavyūha.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-13)

### khenpo-kunga — Ngakwang Kunga Wangchuk (Tibetan)

NKW reads the verse as presenting the fourth benefit through the brave escort simile. Someone who has committed very grave sins — killing parents or a pratyekabuddha, murder, theft, misconduct — sins that will certainly ripen in the lower realms (*ngan song du skye nges can gyi rang bzhin te de 'dra'i sdig pa... byas na yang*) — if they generate bodhicitta, this is like traveling through dangerous territory (where thieves, predators, and enemies lurk) with a brave escort fully armed and armored (*skyes bu dpa' bo go mtson lag cha dang ldan pa la brten nas song na gcan gzan dang dgra rkun la sogs pa'i 'jigs pa chen po las skyob par byed pa'i dpe de bzhin du*): relying on bodhicitta, even those sins that are certain to ripen in the lower realms (*ngan song du myong ba cung zad ma nges pa'i las ngan pa*) are eliminated in an instant (*dus skad cig ma gcig gis nges par sreg par byed par or rtsad drung nas 'byin te 'bras bu rnam par smin pa myong mi dgos par sbyong bar byed pa*) — one can experience just a small token and then be freed. Therefore any practitioner who has disaffection for suffering and cares about avoiding karma should rely on bodhicitta.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-13)

### Divergences

⚑ **Sanskrit vs. Tibetan final phrase**: As noted under Variants, the Sanskrit concludes with "ignorant beings (*ajñasattvaiḥ*)" while the Tibetan concludes with "vigilant beings (*bag can rnams*)." The Tibetan commentaries naturally follow the Tibetan reading — "vigilant beings, who take care with regard to adoption and abandonment, why would they not rely?" This makes the rhetorical structure a statement about the most motivated group. The Sanskrit reading makes the rhetorical structure *a fortiori* from the least motivated: "even ignorant beings should rely — how much more the wise." MKS records both readings as valid; the great Indian commentary (*Prajñākaramati*) follows the Sanskrit. No doctrinal incompatibility, but the rhetorical direction differs.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-13)

⚑ **Scope of "sins" suppressed**: GDR specifies that verse 1-13 overrides the *definitely-experienced* sins (*myon nges kyi las kyi nus pa*) — bodhicitta overrides (*zil gyis gnon pa*) their ripening; verse 1-14 by contrast eliminates *indefinite* sins from the root (*ma nges pa'i sdig pa rtsad nas 'byin pa*). MKS nuances this: some scholars say 1-13 treats indefinite sins and 1-14 treats definitely-experienced ones; however MKS prefers the reading that the force (*nus pa*) of bodhicitta differs, with GDR's reading that 1-13 shows overriding power and 1-14 shows root-eradication.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-13) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-13)

- **སྡིག་པ་ཤིན་ཏུ་མི་བཟད་** — "extremely grave / unbearable sins": KKP specifies: "ཆོས་སྤང་གི་ལས་སོགས་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་ལ་གནོད་པ་དང་མཚམས་མེད་པ་ལ་སོགས་པ་" — severing the Dharma, harming the Three Jewels, the five anantarya crimes, and similar. The qualifier *shin tu* (extremely) signals these are the gravest category of sin, those that most certainly lead to the lower realms.

  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-13)

- **བག་ཅན་རྣམས་** (Tibetan) / *ajñasattvaiḥ* (Sanskrit) — see Variants above. ⚑ Tibetan: "the vigilant / those who take care (*bag can rnams*)." Sanskrit: "ignorant beings (*ajñasattvaiḥ*)." The meaning in context is rhetorical: given bodhicitta's power to purify even the gravest sins, who would not rely on it?

## Key Concepts (ཆོས་ཀྱི་གནད་ཚིག)

### ཚིགས་བཅད་ནང་གི་གནད་ཚིག

- **མཚམས་མེད་པའི་ལས་** (anantarya crimes) — "the immediately-retributed sins" (those producing inevitable hell-rebirth in the very next life). Mentioned by KKP as the canonical example of the gravest category. All Tibetan commentaries agree these are the reference-class of "extremely grave sins" in this verse.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-13) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/མཚམས་མེད་པ་_(anantarya).md]]

### འགྲེལ་པ་ནས་འབྱུང་བའི་གནད་ཚིག

- **རྣམ་སྨིན་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་** (overriding karmic ripening) — The mode of purification in verse 1-13 as identified by GDR and MKS: bodhicitta *zil gyis gnon pa* = overrides/suppresses the karmic capacity to ripen; this is different from root-eradication (*rtsad nas 'byin pa*) which is the mode in 1-14.

## Quotations (ལུང་།)

> རིགས་ཀྱི་བུ་འདི་ལྟ་སྟེ་དཔེར་ན་སྐྱེས་བུ་དཔའ་བོ་ལ་བསྟེན་པ་ནི་དགྲ་བོ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་མི་འཇིགས་སོ། །དེ་བཞིན་དུ་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཏུ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་པའི་སྐྱེས་བུ་དཔའ་བོ་ཡང་དག་པར་བསྟེན་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ནི་ཉེས་པར་སྤྱོད་པའི་དགྲ་བོ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་མི་འཇིགས་སོ།
> — བློ་གྲོས་མི་ཟད་པས་བསྟན་པ་ལས། (Aksayamati sūtra)
> (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-13)

> རིགས་ཀྱི་བུ་འདི་ལྟ་སྟེ་དཔེར་ན་དཔའ་བོ་ལ་རྟེན་པའི་མིང་ནི་དགྲ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་མི་འཇིགས་སོ། །དེ་བཞིན་དུ་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ཉིད་དུ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་པ་དཔའ་བོ་ལ་རྟེན་པ་ཡང་སྤྱོད་པ་ངན་པའི་དགྲ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་མི་འཇིགས་སོ།
> — སྡོང་པོ་བཀོད་པ་ལས། (Gaṇḍavyūha sūtra)

## Disambiguated Restatement (Tibetan)

སྔར་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་པའི་སྔོན་རོལ་དུ་མཚམས་མེད་ལྔ་ལྟ་བུའི་སྡིག་པ་ཤིན་ཏུ་མི་བཟད་པ་ (= ངན་སོང་གི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་མི་བཟད་པ་སྐྱེད་པར་ངེས་པའི་རྣམ་སྨིན་གྱི་རྒྱུ་ཆགས་ཟིན་པ་) བྱས་ཡོད་ཀྱང་། དཔེར་ན་གོ་མཚོན་དང་ལྡན་པའི་དཔའ་བོའི་སྐྱེལ་མ་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་ལམ་དགྲ་ལས་ལྷག་མེད་གྲོལ་བ་ལྟར། བྱང་ཆུབ་མཆོག་ཏུ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་གང་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་སྡིག་ཆེན་དེ་དག་གི་འབྲས་བུ་རྣམ་སྨིན་སྐྱེད་པའི་ནུས་པ་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་ནས་ (= ས་བོན་རུལ་བ་ལྟར་འབྲས་བུ་སྐྱེད་ནུས་མེད་པར་བྱས་ནས་) ངན་སོང་གི་འཇིགས་པ་ལས་ཡུད་ཙམ་གྱིས་སྒྲོལ་བར་འགྱུར་བས། བོད་འགྱུར་གྱི་དོན་ལྟར་ན་འཁོར་བ་དང་ངན་སོང་གི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ལ་བག་ཡོད་པ་ཅན་ལ། རྒྱ་གཞུང་གི་དོན་ལྟར་ན་མི་ཤེས་པ་ཡང་རུང་། རྒྱུ་མཚན་ཅིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་མི་བསྟེན་ཏེ་ཅི་ནས་ཀྱང་བསྟེན་རིགས་སོ།
