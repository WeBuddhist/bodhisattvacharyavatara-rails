# Day 21 — Offer a light, grow a light

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Offer a light you switch on today
> **Detail:** Shantideva offers jewelled lamps to the buddhas. Today, offer a light and wish for clear seeing.

## Opening

After the food and incense, Shantideva offers light. In his mind he sets radiant, jewel-bright lamps in rows of golden lotuses and offers them to the buddhas. A lamp is a small thing, but it is the one offering that meets darkness directly and changes it.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

We carry a lot of darkness we do not call darkness: the parts of ourselves we cannot see clearly, and the people we have decided we already understand.

The verse offers lamps, and Ngulchu Thokme, a fourteenth-century teacher, describes them carefully, jewel-light set in rows of golden flowers. He treats the lamp as more than decoration. A lamp does one thing: it ends darkness wherever it reaches. When you offer light in your mind, you are practising for the kind of light that clears not-knowing, first the blind spots about yourself, then the quick, wrong conclusions you draw about others. The benefit lands close to home. Your own seeing grows a little clearer, and the people you had filed away get a fairer look.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Some time today, as you switch on a light or light a candle, pause for a second and offer that light in your mind to the buddhas and bodhisattvas. As you do, quietly wish to see one thing more clearly: a habit of your own, or one person you may have judged too quickly.
