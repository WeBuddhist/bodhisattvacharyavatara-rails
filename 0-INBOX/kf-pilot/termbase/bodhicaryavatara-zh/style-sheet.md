## Policies

1. Script and punctuation: Traditional characters in standard (正體) forms with Buddhist terms of art in CBETA spelling (眾, 說, 迴向, 剎土), full-width punctuation (，。、；：？！), 「」 for quotation, 《》 for the title of the work and 〈〉 for chapter titles inside running text, and Chinese numerals inside terms (三寶, 十方, 第四品); the registers fix Traditional script and its readers use it.
2. A glossary entry is the bare term: no aspect particles (了, 著), no measure words, no sentence punctuation, and no Latin letters except the Sanskrit title line (policy 13); a children's phrase may carry 的 only as a noun phrase; this keeps entries pasteable into any line of a verse translation, where one or two characters per term matter.
3. Academic register, order of authority: (a) the terms listed in registers-zh.md; (b) the established Chinese rendering of the same Sanskrit term in the Chinese canon and in Tibetan-derived Chinese translations of this work; (c) current Mandarin usage in Traditional-character Buddhist publications; (d) the Gemini zero-shot line only as evidence of readability or as a tie-breaker, because the lines drift from verse to verse and have no term discipline.
4. Gemini forms seen in the examples that must not be copied: 佛 for རྒྱལ་བ (1-10, 6-113; use 勝者), 佛法 for སངས་རྒྱས་ཆོས (6-113; use 佛功德), 善法 for དགེ་བ (1-6; use 善), 智慧 for ཤེས་རབ in the chapter 9 heading (9-0; its own colophon 9-a has 般若波羅蜜; use 般若), 波羅蜜多 (5-83; use 波羅蜜), 眾生 for སེམས་ཅན (4-9; use 有情); each is a different term or an inconsistency inside Gemini itself.
5. Beings: སེམས་ཅན (sentient beings) is 有情 and འགྲོ་བ / འགྲོ (living beings of the world) is 眾生, so the English split survives and Gemini's own main habit (有情 for སེམས་ཅན, 眾生 for འགྲོ་བ) is kept; children's text uses 眾生 for both because the English children's glossary does not split them and 有情 is opaque to a child; compounds follow (སེམས་ཅན་དམྱལ་བ is not a term, see policy 22).
6. ཆོས has two senses in this text and each gets its own word: the Buddha's teaching (16 of 17 occurrences) is 佛法 in both registers, and things as such (9-105) is 諸法 in the plural and 法 as a head form (children 事物); a third sense, quality of a Buddha, occurs only in the compound སངས་རྒྱས་ཆོས and is 佛功德, so 佛法 is never read as "qualities" and 法 never as "teaching"; the tags are those of the Ratnaguṇasaṃcayagāthā glossary, not the Diamond Sutra's 教法, so that the later Prajñāpāramitā-line texts agree.
7. Established compounds with 法 keep it and are listed so no agent "corrects" them: 法身 (ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ, named in registers-zh.md), 正法 (དམ་པའི་ཆོས, དམ་ཆོས; saddharma), 三法衣 (ཆོས་གོས་གསུམ), 說法, 聞法; other compounds of the teaching sense are built as 佛法 plus the partner (ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྒྲོན་མ 佛法之燈, ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྒྲ 佛法之聲).
8. Where the English glossary splits a sense that established Chinese does not, keep the established Chinese word for both and let the partner disambiguate: 功德 for ཡོན་ཏན (guṇa) and, inside སངས་རྒྱས་ཆོས, for the quality sense; 導師 for every Tibetan "Guide"; 心 for སེམས and 意 for ཡིད only in 意門 / 身語意; the reverse case also holds, 福德 (བསོད་ནམས, puṇya) is never 功德 and 善 (དགེ་བ, kuśala) is never 福德, because the Tibetan keeps those apart.
9. Epithets of the Buddha get one fixed Chinese word per Tibetan epithet, never collapsed into 佛 or into each other: སངས་རྒྱས 佛, རྒྱལ་བ / རྒྱལ 勝者, བདེ་གཤེགས / བདེ་བར་གཤེགས 善逝, དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས(་པ) 如來, འདྲེན་པ 導師, མགོན(་པོ) 怙主, ཐུབ(་པ) 牟尼, སྐྱོབ་པ 救護者, བཅོམ་ལྡན(་འདས) 世尊, རྫོགས་སངས་རྒྱས 正等覺者; the epithet is what Śāntideva chose to say and the commentaries read the choice (these follow the Diamond Sutra and Ratnaguṇa glossaries where they share the term).
10. "Child of" titles: སྲས and སངས་རྒྱས་སྲས are 佛子 (registers-zh.md), བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས is 善逝之子, རྒྱལ་སྲས is 勝者之子, so the owner chosen by the Tibetan stays visible in the 〈owner〉之子 shape; children's text uses 的孩子 (佛陀的孩子, 得勝者的孩子).
11. Names take the established Chinese name, not a fresh transliteration, in both registers unless noted: 寂天 (Śāntideva), 文殊師利 (འཇམ་དབྱངས / འཇམ་པའི་དབྱངས; children 文殊), 彌勒 (བྱམས་མགོན 彌勒怙主), 觀世音 (སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས, འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་ཕྱུག; children 觀音), 普賢 (ཀུན་ཏུ་བཟང), 虛空藏 (ནམ་མཁའི་སྙིང་པོ), 地藏 (ས་ཡི་སྙིང་པོ), 須彌山 (རི་རབ), 贍部洲 (འཛམ་གླིང), 無間獄 (མནར་མེད), 閻羅 (གཤིན་རྗེ), 死主 (འཆི་བདག), 梵天 (ཚངས་པ), 自在天 (དབང་ཕྱུག), 魔 (བདུད), 阿修羅 (ལྷ་མིན); a name with no known Chinese form gets a conventional transliteration marked ⚑, because the registers ask for common names and an invented one would be unrecognisable.
12. The six perfections use the registers' set 布施, 持戒, 安忍, 精進, 靜慮, 般若, each followed by 波羅蜜 when the Tibetan has ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན; ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ, ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན, ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱེན and ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ are one term, as are all spelling variants, truncated and misspelled forms in the overview (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔ, བྱང་ཆུང་, སྔག་བསྔལ, སྡིག for སྡིག་པ), and 波羅蜜 (registers-zh.md) is used without 多, which departs from the Ratnaguṇasaṃcayagāthā glossary's 波羅蜜多 on the ground that the registers file of this text lists 波羅蜜.
13. Title of the work: 《入菩薩行論》 for བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ་འཇུག་པ, the established Chinese title (入 + 菩薩行 + 論, the genre word 論 being the Chinese counterpart of the śāstra that the Tibetan title leaves implicit) and the form Gemini uses throughout; the Sanskrit title line བོ་དྷི་སཏྭ་ཙརྱ་ཨ་བ་ཏཱ་ར is kept in IAST (Bodhisattvacaryāvatāra), not transliterated into Chinese characters, as in the two other glossaries.
14. Chapter titles are written 第N品 plus the title with no punctuation, fixed as: 第一品 菩提心利益, 第二品 懺悔罪業, 第三品 受持菩提心, 第四品 不放逸, 第五品 護正知, 第六品 安忍, 第七品 精進, 第八品 靜慮, 第九品 般若波羅蜜, 第十品 迴向; the chapters on the perfections reuse the perfection words of policy 12, and Gemini's 第九品 智慧 is refused (policy 4); the children's titles are 第一品 覺悟之心的好處, 第二品 坦承有害的行為, 第三品 持守覺悟之心, 第四品 謹慎, 第五品 保持警覺, 第六品 耐心, 第七品 歡喜的努力, 第八品 平靜專注, 第九品 圓滿的智慧, 第十品 分享好事, built from the children's phrases of the core table.
15. Mind terms are kept apart: སེམས 心, ཡིད 意, བློ 智, བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས 菩提心 (also its variants བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས, བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ཀྱི་, ཐུགས་བསྐྱེད 發心), with the Tibetan's own pair of aspiring and engaging bodhicitta written 願菩提心 (བྱང་ཆུབ་སྨོན་པའི་སེམས) and 行菩提心 (བྱང་ཆུབ་འཇུག་པའི་སེམས / བྱང་ཆུབ་འཇུག་པ), so that the two stay separate wherever the text names them; in the metrical short form (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས standing for the bodhisattva) the rendering is 菩薩.
16. Two truths and the path vocabulary use the established pair registers-zh.md names: ཀུན་རྫོབ 世俗諦 and དོན་དམ 勝義諦 when "truth" is meant (the bare adjective, as in "conventionally", is 世俗 and 勝義), སྟོང་པ་ཉིད / སྟོང་ཉིད 空性, སྡུག་བསྔལ 苦, བདེ་བ 樂, བདེ་སྡུག 苦樂, ཚོགས as accumulation 資糧; this follows the established usage in Tibetan-derived Chinese and the term sets already fixed for the Ratnaguṇasaṃcayagāthā.
17. Privative and negated terms are built from the base term: མི་ / མེད is 不 / 無 in the academic register (མི་རྟག 無常, བདག་མེད 無我, མི་དགེ 不善, མི་གཙང 不淨, བག་མེད 放逸) and 不 or 沒有 in children's phrases; the base term is never replaced by a synonym just because the negated form has a familiar Chinese word.
18. Compounds take their parts' renderings in the Tibetan order (བྱང་ཆུབ་ཡོན་ཏན 菩提功德, ཡོན་ཏན་རྒྱ་མཚོ 功德海, ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན 持戒波羅蜜, སེམས་ཅན་གྱི་དོན 有情之利), and an established Chinese compound (菩提心, 法身, 暇滿, 三寶, 三界, 惡趣, 善趣, 輪迴, 迴向) is used only when its parts agree with the core table; where they do not, the table wins and the glossary note records the established form (e.g. 苦樂 for བདེ་སྡུག uses 樂, which is the core rendering of བདེ་བ).
19. Children's register: a technical term becomes a short everyday phrase naming the same thing, never a different idea (空性 stays 空性, not "nothing"); Sanskrit-derived forms are replaced by native words (波羅蜜 becomes 圓滿, 菩提 becomes 覺悟, 功德 becomes 美好的品質); transparent or already everyday words stay (菩薩, 如來, 佛法, 導師, 世尊, 輪迴, 三寶, 大乘, 地獄); and the same child-phrase is used every time so that a young reader meets one word per idea, with the English children's glossary ("awakening hero", "calm focus") serving as the sense anchor but not forcing a Chinese word that is less natural than the everyday one (菩薩 is kept for "awakening hero", as in the other two glossaries).
20. Names of the Buddha and the three jewels for children: 佛陀 for སངས་རྒྱས, 得勝者 for རྒྱལ་བ, 走向安樂的人 for བདེ་གཤེགས, 如來 for དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ, 守護者 for མགོན, 聖者 for ཐུབ་པ, 世尊 for བཅོམ་ལྡན; no transliterated technical Sanskrit (not 阿耨多羅三藐三菩提 or 三摩地; say the idea in plain words, e.g. 深度安定 for ཏིང་འཛིན).
21. A rendering that departs from the other two glossaries for a shared term says so in the Reason column and names the sense that needs it (so far: 波羅蜜 versus 波羅蜜多, 覺悟之心 for the general བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས versus the Ratnaguṇasaṃcayagāthā's 想要覺悟的心願 which is the aspiring sense, 好事 for དགེ་བ, 分享好事 for བསྔོ་བ); where the English glossaries are the same, the earlier children's phrase is reused word for word.
22. Pipeline overrides and non-terms are respected: སེམས་ཅན་དམྱལ (T463) is a mis-cut span and not a term, and ཉོན་མོངས at 4-37 means "wretched" and is not 煩惱 (overrides.json); བདག as the first-person pronoun is not a term (only བདག as "self" is, see the core table); an agent that must depart from this sheet, or meets a term not covered for which no established Chinese term is known, writes the best candidate and marks the entry ⚑ with the reason, and never changes a core-term rendering silently, because the core terms are the stems of the compounds.
