# Day 29 — A guest who travels light

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Let one push of wanting or anger go today
> **Detail:** Today's verses call us passing guests. Hold your time lightly and loosen one grip.

## Opening

Shantideva looks at his own life and calls himself a kind of guest: he arrived having left everything behind, and he will leave the same way. What he enjoyed has already turned to memory, like a dream on waking. He sees too that death can come without warning. He is not trying to frighten himself. He is trying to live the brief, uncertain time he has more honestly.

## Renewing the Bodhisattva Vow

> **Mind Training** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> May all beings enjoy happiness and the causes of happiness.
> May all beings be free from suffering and the causes of suffering.
> May all beings never be separated from supreme happiness, free from suffering.
> May all beings rest in great equanimity, free from attachment and aversion to those near and far.
>
> **Refuge** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Until reaching the heart of buddhahood,
> I take refuge in the Buddha,
> I take refuge in the dharma,
> And in the sangha of bodhisattvas.
>
> **Taking the Bodhisattva Vow** *(recite 3 times)*
>
> Just as the buddhas of the past gave rise to bodhicitta
> And engaged step by step in the training of the bodhisattvas,
> So I too, for the benefit of all beings, will give rise to bodhicitta
> And engage step by step in that same training.

## From the Tradition

Sazang Mati Panchen, a fourteenth-century teacher, gives the last verse a plain image: we are each a sudden guest. We came into this life having left everything from before behind, and we will leave it the same way, carrying nothing. The fighting with enemies, the time with friends, the eating and the saving up, all of it becomes only "I once did such and such," memory and nothing more. His point is about what forgetting this does to us. When we lose sight of how brief and uncertain our stay is, the three poisons take over: confusion about what is worth doing, grasping for our own side, pushing against the other. That is the engine behind most of the harm we cause. Remembering that we are only passing through takes the fuel out of it.

## Aspiration and Dedication

> May the precious bodhicitta
> Arise in those who don't have it yet;
> And in those who already have it,
> May it not decline, but grow ever further.
>
> By the merit of practicing
> The Bodhicharyavatara today
> May all living beings come to engage
> In the conduct of the bodhisattvas.

## Today's Practice

Once today, when you feel yourself pulled hard toward wanting something or pushing against someone, pause and remember that you are only passing through, here a short while. See if that lets you loosen your hold by just a little, and turn the freed-up moment toward something kinder.
