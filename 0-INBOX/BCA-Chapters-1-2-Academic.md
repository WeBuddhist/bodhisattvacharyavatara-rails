# Bodhisattvacaryāvatāra
### A Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life
*Translated from the Sanskrit of Śāntideva — Academic Edition*

---

## Chapter 1: Bodhicittānuśaṃsaḥ — The Benefits of the Awakening Mind

With heartfelt reverence I bow down to the *Sugatas* (the Blissfully Gone), to their heirs, to their *Dharmakāyas* (Truth Bodies), and to all others worthy of veneration. Following scripture and in brief, I shall explain the discipline of entering the path of the *Sugatas*' heirs. ^1-1

There is nothing new here that has not already been said, and I possess no skill in literary composition. Therefore I have no concern for the benefit of others — this work has been composed solely for the purpose of cultivating my own mind. ^1-2

Through this composition my faith in virtuous practice grows strong and increases. If others of modest intelligence encounter these words and derive some benefit, that too is a fitting result. ^1-3

This precious human life — endowed with leisure and the full range of favourable conditions (*kṣaṇa-sampad*) — is exceedingly difficult to obtain. Since one has now obtained it, one must accomplish that which is genuinely and lastingly beneficial. ^1-4

Like a flash of lightning on a dark night that briefly illuminates everything, so too through the power of the Buddhas a virtuous thought arises only momentarily in the mind of beings. Thus virtue is extremely feeble, while the power of wrongdoing (*pāpa*) is enormous and overwhelming. ^1-5

Apart from this awakening mind (*bodhicitta*) — the mind that aspires to perfect enlightenment for the sake of all beings — what other complete and supreme remedy could there be for the afflictions of *saṃsāra* (the cycle of conditioned existence)? The Buddhas who have contemplated this through immeasurable eons declare that *bodhicitta* is exceedingly beneficial. ^1-6

By means of this precious *bodhicitta*, beings who are immeasurable in number are led to the supreme state of happiness and bliss. Its benefits were explained in detail by the Lord *Maitreya* to the wise student Sudhana, and they surpass all other virtues. ^1-7

Those who wish to overcome the manifold sufferings of existence, to dispel the unhappiness of beings, and to enjoy the many different kinds of joy — let them never abandon *bodhicitta*, the supreme awakening mind. ^1-8

The very moment one gives rise to this awakening mind, a miserable being who is bound within the prison of conditioned existence becomes a "child of the Victorious Ones" (*jinaputra*), a *bodhisattva*, worthy of veneration by gods and humans alike throughout the world. ^1-9

Like the supreme alchemical transmutation (*rasāyana*), which transforms the base metal of an impure body into the infinitely precious substance of a Buddha-body — for this reason that which is called *bodhicitta* must be firmly and resolutely held. ^1-10

Since the sole captain of wandering beings, the omniscient one, has fathomed *bodhicitta*'s value with immeasurable wisdom and declared it priceless, those who wish to pass from existence toward transcendence (*nirvāṇa*) must firmly seize this jewel of the awakening mind. ^1-11

All other virtues are like the plantain tree: having borne fruit, they are exhausted and come to an end. But the tree of *bodhicitta* always bears fruit — it does not diminish; it is inexhaustible and forever increasing. ^1-12

Like the great conflagration (*mahāpralaya*) that arises at the end of an eon, *bodhicitta* in a single instant burns away tremendous accumulations of *pāpa* (misdeeds). Its inconceivable benefits were declared to the wise student Sudhana by the protector *Maitreya*. ^1-13

In brief, the awakening mind (*bodhicitta*) is to be understood as twofold in nature: the aspiring awakening mind (*bodhipraṇidhicitta*), which wishes to attain full awakening, and the engaging awakening mind (*bodhiprasthānacitta*), which actively enters into the practices that lead to it. ^1-14

Just as one clearly understands the difference between a person who wishes to go somewhere and one who is actually travelling there, so too the learned understand these two minds — the aspiring and the engaging — as distinct in their order and function. ^1-15

The aspiring awakening mind (*bodhipraṇidhi*) yields great and excellent fruit while one is still within the cycle of existence (*saṃsāra*). Nevertheless, it does not produce a continuous and uninterrupted stream of merit (*puṇya*) in the way that the engaging awakening mind does. ^1-16

From the moment one genuinely takes up the engaging awakening mind — wholeheartedly aspiring to liberate all beings without exception — a continuous stream of merit equal in measure to the number of those infinite beings flows forth in every moment, even during sleep and even during states of distraction. ^1-17

This principle was declared by the Tathāgata himself, supported by valid reasoning, for the sake of those inclined toward lesser vehicles, in the *Subāhuparipṛcchā Sūtra*. ^1-18

If even the mere thought of benefiting beings is more exalted and meritorious than making lavish offerings to the Buddhas, then what need is there to speak of the immeasurable fruit of striving wholeheartedly for the happiness and liberation of all sentient beings? ^1-19

People flee from suffering while simultaneously running toward its very causes. They yearn for happiness yet in their ignorance (*avidyā*) destroy happiness as though it were an enemy. How pitiable is the condition of beings who remain entangled in this confusion. ^1-20

For those deprived of happiness, for those afflicted with manifold sufferings, for those who are sated with the fleeting pleasures of existence — *bodhicitta* is the supreme nourishment and the unsurpassable medicine. May it never be exhausted! ^1-21

This precious *bodhicitta* has now arisen. May all beings be made joyful! May those who are suffering be freed from suffering! May those gripped by fear be freed from fear! May those weighed down by grief swiftly find joy! ^1-22

As long as beings remain afflicted with misery, as long as the world remains enshrouded in darkness, as long as the Tathāgatas have passed beyond into *nirvāṇa* — may this *bodhicitta* endure to dispel the darkness and suffering of existence without remainder. ^1-23

I offer protection to all beings throughout the entirety of existence. May their suffering be completely dispelled! In whatever way the wise speak and however they instruct, may I always act in precise accordance with that instruction. ^1-24

May physicians be found everywhere and in abundance; may all illnesses and diseases be completely cured. May all beings who are afflicted with suffering swiftly obtain lasting happiness and well-being. ^1-25

As long as the sky endures and as long as this world endures, so too may I remain — taking birth again and again — in order to eliminate the suffering of the world without leaving any being behind. ^1-26

May all the suffering of wandering beings (*sattva*) ripen upon me alone. Through the virtue of the *bodhisattva* community, may all wandering beings find happiness and abide in joy. ^1-27

May the sacred Dharma — which is the sole medicine for the suffering of the world and the fountainhead of all happiness and bliss — endure for a very long time without decline, and may it be supported with provisions and veneration. ^1-28

I bow with joined palms to the awakening mind (*bodhicitta*)! I bow to the happiness of beings! I bow to the extraordinary beings in the world who, out of compassion, give happiness even to those who have harmed them. ^1-29

I rejoice with pure joy at the virtue accumulated through the three times by all beings everywhere — wandering or liberated — and I rejoice in the virtue of all *bodhisattvas* as though it were my own. ^1-30

I rejoice in the ocean of virtue that is the attainment of enlightenment, and I rejoice in the levels (*bhūmi*) that bring liberation for sentient beings. I rejoice with delight in the minds of those who have awakened and who carry all beings across the ocean of suffering. ^1-31

With joined palms I beseech the Buddhas of all directions: please kindle the lamp of Dharma for those beings who are wandering in darkness and suffering through the pain of delusion. ^1-32

With joined palms I implore the Victorious Ones who are inclined toward *parinirvāṇa*: please remain for countless eons and do not leave this world in blindness and darkness. ^1-33

Through whatever merit I have accumulated by all these practices of veneration — bowing, rejoicing, requesting, and imploring — may all suffering beings be freed from every form of pain. ^1-34

May I be medicine for the sick as long as they are unhealed. May I be food and drink for those consumed by hunger and thirst. May I become an inexhaustible treasure for those who are destitute and poor. ^1-35

I bow with reverence to these physical forms of beings in whom this supreme jewel of the awakening mind has arisen. To those whose very wrongdoing (*apakāra*) leads to happiness — to these sources of happiness I bow. ^1-36

*Thus ends Chapter 1: "Bodhicittānuśaṃsaḥ — The Benefits of the Awakening Mind."*

## Chapter 2: Pāpadeśanā — The Confession of Misdeeds

In order to properly receive this jewel of the awakening mind (*bodhicitta*), I shall now make offerings (*pūjā*) to the Tathāgatas, to the stainless jewel of the true Dharma, and to the vast ocean of *bodhisattvas*. ^2-1

Whatever flowers and fruits exist, whatever medicinal substances and precious jewels are found in the world, whatever clear and pure waters flow — all of these I mentally gather and offer. ^2-2

Whatever mountains of jewels exist, whatever forest groves are pleasant and peaceful, whatever trees adorned with blossoms and fruit, whatever lakes and lotus pools, whatever cries of wild geese resound — all of these I offer. ^2-3

Whatever cultivated and uncultivated lands exist, whatever fragrances and incenses, wish-fulfilling trees (*kalpataru*), jewel trees, lotus ponds, wild ducks and geese — all of these too I offer. ^2-4

Whatever rare and precious substances exist throughout the world, whatever things that give pleasure and delight, whatever is extensive, beautiful and supreme — all of these I take up mentally and offer to the supreme *Muni* (Sage) and to his heirs. ^2-5

Having no resources of my own with which to make offerings, I therefore offer, out of compassion for me, what the protectors receive as my mental offering. May these incomparably compassionate ones accept my offering! ^2-6

I offer my body completely to the Victorious Ones and their heirs — I offer myself as their servant, without any reservation. Having surrendered myself entirely, please take me under your protection, O Victorious Ones! I shall serve you without fear. ^2-7

By dedicating myself in this way, having completely given myself over, I shall benefit beings and go beyond all previous misdeeds (*pāpa*). I shall act with wisdom and restraint, recalling former actions and avoiding them henceforth. ^2-8

Bathed in the fragrant perfume of the bathing houses — perfectly clean, golden, and scented — in vessels filled with incomparable flowers, I mentally bathe the Tathāgatas and their heirs. ^2-9

I dry them with incomparably clean and well-perfumed cloths and then offer them garments of supreme fragrance and colour — beautiful, well-made, and most excellent. ^2-10

I adorn the noble and supreme *Samantabhadra*, *Mañjughoṣa*, *Avalokiteśvara*, and all *bodhisattvas* with various ornaments of the finest and most exquisite kind. ^2-11

With the finest perfumes pervading the three-thousand great-thousandfold world system (*trisāhasramahāsāhasraloka*), I anoint the bodies of all Victorious Ones — which shine like burnished and polished pure gold. ^2-12

I offer all the divine flowers — *mandāra*, blue lotus (*utpala*), jasmine (*campaka*) and all others — along with precious garlands beautifully woven and arranged, to the most excellent of sages. ^2-13

I send forth clouds of incense whose sweet fragrances enchant and pervade all directions. I offer a variety of divine foods and delicacies as extensive as the finest ritual repasts. ^2-14

I offer lamps of jewels set in rows of golden lotuses, and I scatter fragrant flowers upon the surface of the level ground, beautifully anointed and adorned. ^2-15

I also offer celestial palaces resounding with hymns of praise (*stotra*) and adorned with hanging garlands of pearls and jewels, worthy gifts for the protectors of the world who are the supreme objects of merit. ^2-16

I offer beautiful parasols with golden handles studded with jewels, standing upright and perfectly proportioned, adorned with jewelled rims and auspicious designs. ^2-17

May clouds of offerings remain continuously, with music and song resounding in all directions — sweet, gentle, and unceasing — bringing joy and pleasure to all beings. ^2-18

Upon all the Dharma jewels and the *stūpas* containing relics, upon the images of the Buddhas and upon the *bodhisattvas*, I scatter a continuous rain of flowers, jewels, and precious things. ^2-19

Just as *Mañjughoṣa* and other *bodhisattvas* make offerings to the Victorious Ones, so too do I make offerings to the Tathāgatas, the Dharma, and the supreme community (*saṃgha*). ^2-20

I praise those who are oceans of virtue with an ocean of melodious verses. May this cloud-like praise never fail to rise and offer itself to these most excellent ones. ^2-21

With prostrations with my body, words, and mind (*trikāya*), I bow before all the Buddhas of the three times, before the Dharma, before the supreme community, and before all *bodhisattvas*. ^2-22

I go for refuge to all the Buddhas who are the protectors, who have applied themselves to awakening (*bodhi*) and who are sources of refuge for beings throughout the world. I go for refuge to the Dharma and to the assembly of *bodhisattvas*. ^2-23

With joined palms I implore the Awakened Ones (*sambuddha*) in all directions: may they kindle the torch of Dharma for beings who are floundering in suffering through the torrential downpour of their misdeeds. ^2-24

With joined palms I supplicate the Victorious Ones who are inclined toward *parinirvāṇa*: please remain and do not pass away, do not leave this world wandering blind in the darkness of ignorance! ^2-25

Through all of this may I accumulate merit. May I eliminate all misdeeds without exception. May I thereby be liberated from the ocean of existence (*saṃsāra*) without remainder! ^2-26

I confess all *pāpa* (misdeeds) and wrongdoing that I have committed with my body, speech, and mind (*trikāya*) — whether in this life or in former lives — in the presence of all the Buddhas: ^2-27

With joined palms and bowed head, in terror of suffering, I bow again and again and confess all the *pāpa* committed since beginningless time in the cycle of existence. ^2-28

Whatever misdeeds I have committed against the Three Jewels (*triratna*) — the Buddha, Dharma, and *Saṃgha* — and against my father and mother, and against other holy objects — through my body, speech, and mind, born of delusion (*moha*): ^2-29

Whatever heavy wrongdoing (*pāpa*) has been done by me — a corrupt being (*pāpaka*) — beset by many faults and deficiencies, committing misdeeds through the influence of afflictive emotions (*kleśa*) — all of this I confess. ^2-30

O Masters (*nātha*), how can I remain at ease when I have committed these misdeeds? How can I rest without anxiety? Please rescue me quickly — do not let time pass! ^2-31

Death is not to be relied upon. Life is impermanent. Existence is uncertain. This is the nature of the body, which is like a rented house, and of life, which passes swiftly by. ^2-32

The Lord of Death (*Mṛtyu*) does not wait for things to be done or undone. Whether one is healthy or ill, the moment of death is uncertain. There is no basis for confidence in this life. ^2-33

In the past I abandoned the Dharma and sought other worthless things. Having understood this clearly now, I see that I have run after what is harmful and abandoned what would benefit me. ^2-34

Knowing that at the moment of death one is completely alone — bereft of relatives and possessions — and that whatever *karma* (action) one has created goes with one: having understood this reality, how can one who has not secured the Dharma live without anxiety? ^2-35

Just as a man suddenly seized by a severe illness cannot eat anything, so too someone seized by the messengers of death (*mṛtyudūta*) — what will they do with all their possessions, relatives, and friends? ^2-36

When the mind is beset by the overwhelming power of the afflictive emotions, what relative or friend can offer protection at that moment? Who is there who can help then? ^2-37

Therefore, from this very day, I go for refuge to the great protectors — the Victorious Ones (*jina*) who have completely conquered fear in the world and who vow to protect all beings. ^2-38

I go for refuge wholeheartedly to the Dharma which they have realised — which removes the fears of *saṃsāra* — and to the community of *bodhisattvas*. ^2-39

Terrified and trembling, I offer myself completely to *Samantabhadra*. I also offer my body, without reservation, to *Mañjughoṣa*. ^2-40

To the protector *Avalokiteśvara* whose conduct is born of compassion — crying out, I call with a distressed mind: "Please protect this wrongdoing being!" ^2-41

And to the noble *Ākāśagarbha*, to *Kṣitigarbha*, and to all the compassionate protectors — I call out with earnest entreaty: "Please protect me!" ^2-42

To the *Vajrapāṇi*, the terrifier of all misdeeds — upon seeing whom even the messengers of death (*yamadūta*) and all malevolent forces flee in all directions — I go for refuge. ^2-43

Through the power of my former actions (*karma*), when I have violated these precepts and commitments and when the Lord of Death (*Yama*) comes before me in terrible form — what shall I do then? What refuge shall I have? ^2-44

I go for refuge today to the Victorious Ones who are the protectors, who apply themselves to protecting beings and whose power eliminates all fear. I shall strive to fulfil the Dharma with all my strength. ^2-45

Whatever misdeeds I have previously committed — born of not knowing what is to be done and what is not to be done — I confess everything before the Victorious Ones and what has been done I accept as a misdeed. ^2-46

May the masters (*nātha*) accept my confession of these misdeeds! Since this is not beneficial, O Protectors, I shall never commit such acts again in the future. ^2-47

The precepts (*śīla*) are the basis for all good qualities. Just as the earth is the basis for all moving and stationary things, so too the precepts, which generate all virtue and all good qualities, are the most important of all. ^2-48

I have failed in my precepts and commitments by not maintaining them properly through carelessness (*pramāda*) and negligence. Having understood this clearly, I confess these violations before the Victorious Ones. ^2-49

In this way, with joined palms, having gone for refuge to the Buddhas and *bodhisattvas*, I confess before the Victorious Ones all misdeeds arising from the three doors of body, speech, and mind since beginningless time. ^2-50

All the *pāpa* (misdeeds) accumulated throughout countless eons (*kalpa*) — through this very confession may they be completely purified. Through confession, through restraint, through application to virtue — may the *pāpa* of beings be purified. ^2-51

Whatever good I have done — in this or any other existence — through body, speech, or mind — and whatever virtue (*puṇya*) has been accumulated — I rejoice in all of that without any trace of jealousy. ^2-52

I rejoice in the virtue of those who have relieved the suffering of the lower realms; I rejoice in the virtue of all beings who have attained the happiness of a fortunate existence. ^2-53

I rejoice in the Dharma which is the great vehicle, the cause of the awakening of the Awakened Ones; I rejoice in the ocean of *bodhicitta* of the *bodhisattvas*, which brings liberation and joy to all beings. ^2-54

With joined palms I beseech the Awakened Ones (*buddha*) of all directions: please kindle the lamp of Dharma for those who, through the darkness of delusion, have fallen into suffering. ^2-55

With joined palms I implore all the Victorious Ones who are inclined toward *parinirvāṇa*: please remain for countless eons — do not leave this world in darkness! ^2-56

Through whatever virtue (*puṇya*) I have obtained through these practices — bowing, offering, rejoicing, beseeching, and imploring — may all beings obtain full enlightenment. ^2-57

May all beings obtain happiness. May all beings be freed from suffering. May no being remain without joy. May no being remain in a state of suffering and pain. ^2-58

As long as beings remain in the cycle of existence (*saṃsāra*), may I serve as medicine, as food, as a source of every kind of well-being, and as a protector — in whatever form is needed in each situation. ^2-59

Just as the earth, water, fire, wind, and space — the great elements — serve the welfare of innumerable beings in all directions, so too may I serve the welfare of beings throughout space, in every world and every realm, in all ways. ^2-60

Until all beings of the realm of space attain *nirvāṇa*, may I remain without weariness, serving their welfare in whatever way is needed in every moment. ^2-61

Just as the Tathāgatas of the past generated the awakening mind (*bodhicitta*) and progressively trained in the practices of the *bodhisattva*, so too I generate the awakening mind for the welfare of all beings and will progressively train in those same practices. ^2-62

In order to give rise to this awakening mind joyfully and wisely, I make offerings to the Buddhas; so too the wise, for the sake of the Dharma and the welfare of beings, offer their body, speech, and mind. ^2-63

Having offered the finest of offerings to the Victorious Ones and having generated this precious *bodhicitta* — the mind that aspires to supreme and complete enlightenment for the welfare of all beings — all sentient beings should be made to rejoice. ^2-64

O Masters (*nātha*), please accept my transgression (*ataya*) as a transgression. Since this is not beneficial, O Protectors (*nātha*), I shall not act this way again. ^2-65

*Thus ends Chapter 2: "Pāpadeśanā — The Confession of Misdeeds."*
