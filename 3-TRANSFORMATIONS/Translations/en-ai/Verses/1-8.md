## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three classical commentators agree that Verse 1-8 presents the mind of awakening (bodhicitta) as the ultimate and indispensable method for fulfilling all spiritual aspirations. Whether one's focus is personal liberation, the welfare of others, or the attainment of supreme happiness, bodhicitta is the singular vehicle that can accomplish these goals, and therefore, it must never be abandoned or discarded at any time.

### 1. The Tripartite Aspirations and the Scopes of the Path
The commentators align on the three primary motivations outlined in the verse: wanting to overcome personal suffering, wanting to dispel the suffering of others, and wanting to experience limitless joy.
- **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen's Lamrim Structuring:** Gyaltsab Je provides a highly structured analysis of these three aspirations, mapping them directly onto the traditional scopes of the path (*lamrim*):
  - *Overcoming personal suffering:* Corresponds to training the mind of a **medium person** (the middling scope), who seeks to destroy the hundreds of sufferings of cyclic existence within their own continuum.
  - *Dispelling the suffering of others:* Corresponds to training the mind of a **great person** (the great scope), who seeks to clear the unhappiness and misery of all other sentient beings.
  - *Enjoying limitless joy:* Encompasses both temporary and ultimate happiness, which can only be fully realized through the supreme method of bodhicitta.
- **Consensus on Inseparability:** Both Gyaltsab Je and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo emphasize that because there is "no other supreme method" to fulfill these three goals, an intelligent practitioner has no choice but to generate and permanently maintain bodhicitta.

### 2. The Nature of Sufferings to be Destroyed
- **Sazang Mati Panchen's Elaboration:** Sazang Mati Panchen expands on the specific types of suffering that bodhicitta overcomes. He distinguishes between:
  - *Sufferings of cyclic existence:* Such as the fierce, unendurable pains of the hell realms, which the intelligent seek to destroy by engaging in their direct remedies.
  - *Miseries of sentient beings:* Such as the universal sufferings of birth, old age, sickness, and death, which the Bodhisattva seeks to clear out of compassion.
- **Thokme Zangpo's Concise Framing:** Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo presents this under the structural heading of "fulfilling wishes" (the third of the common benefits of bodhicitta), summarizing that those who wish to overcome samsaric suffering and remove others' misery must never turn away from this precious mind.

### 3. Fulfilling Joys (Temporary and Ultimate)
- **High Rebirth and Definitive Goodness:** All three commentators agree that the "hundreds of joys" mentioned in the verse refer to both temporary and ultimate happiness:
  - *Temporary happiness:* The excellent fortunes of high rebirth (*abhyudaya*), such as birth in the higher realms of gods and humans.
  - *Ultimate happiness:* The definitive goodness (*niḥśreyasa*) of complete liberation and the boundless qualities of a Buddha.
- **Absolute Necessity of Bodhicitta:** Sazang Mati Panchen concludes that since bodhicitta is the definitive method to accomplish these dual states of happiness "for themselves and all others," one must hold onto it "firmly and unshakeably" and "never discard it at any time."
