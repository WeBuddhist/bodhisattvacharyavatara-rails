# Day 37 — Nobody signed the guarantee

> **Notification**
> **Title:** Notice one thing you trust to keep you safe, and look again
> **Detail:** Today's verse asks: who promised you you'd be fine? Move a little trust to what doesn't expire.

## Opening

Shantideva asks a blunt, almost startling question: who gave me freedom from fear? Who guaranteed I will be fine? The honest answer is no one. We move through our days as if someone, somewhere, had signed off on our safety, but nobody has. The verse is not meant to leave us anxious. It is meant to move our trust off the things that cannot actually carry it, and onto something that can.

## From the Tradition

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, a fifteenth-century teacher, reads the question as a search for a signature that does not exist. No one, he says, has handed us the gift of "you will not die." There is no guarantee, and no exit either; we will not escape this by ignoring it. But he is careful about what conclusion to draw. The ease he criticises is not calm; it is a kind of laziness, resting our weight on a promise no one made, the quiet assumption that things will hold. The verse asks us to see that clearly and then redirect our trust. The point of admitting there is no outside guarantee is not to live afraid. It is to stop leaning on what expires, our health, our savings, our plans, and to put some of our weight where it can actually rest, on the practice and refuge that do not run out.

## Today's Practice

Notice one thing you quietly trust to keep you safe: money in the bank, your health, a plan that you are sure will work out. Without throwing it away or pretending it is worthless, just acknowledge that it cannot finally guarantee anything. Then move a small piece of that trust somewhere steadier: a moment of refuge, a line of practice. Lean a little less on what expires, and a little more on what does not.
