These are the circumstantial fortunes. Possessing these ten fortunes and being free from the eight non-leisures is 'leisure and fortune' (dal 'byor). ^1-3-3

Such a human body endowed with these eighteen qualities is extremely difficult to find. ^1-3-4

As is said in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya* citing the *Biography of Noble Maitreya*: 'To be free from the eight non-leisures is extremely difficult to find, and to obtain pure leisure and fortune is also extremely difficult to find.' ^1-3-5

Thus it is said. ^1-3-6

Having obtained this human body of leisure and fortune, which has the capacity to accomplish the goals of a person—namely high rebirth and definitive goodness—if one does not accomplish benefit for future lives on this support, how could one perfectly obtain such a leisure and fortune again in the future? ^1-3-7

For we mostly reside in states of non-leisure, and from there, accomplishing the causes of happy migrations is extremely rare. ^1-3-8

Second, the difficulty of generating the support of the mind (the virtuous mind) is as follows: Just as on a dark, cloudy night, a brief flash of lightning reveals forms for a single instant, likewise, by the power of the Buddha, a virtuous mind that wishes to perform merit arises only occasionally in worldly beings, but does not arise continuously. For this reason, since we constantly abide in weak virtue, and the powerful negativity that produces birth in bad migrations is extremely difficult to overcome, one must make efforts to generate the virtuous mind. ^1-3-14

### 1.6 First, overcoming negativity: Since the support and the virtuous mind are extremely difficult to find and of short duration, the virtuous mind is extremely weak and of short duration. ^1-6-0

In contrast, the causes of non-virtue are easily gathered, so extremely fierce negativity arises continuously. To overcome it, we must generate a virtuous mind. As is said in the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*: 'Just as the eyes of a cat instantly overcome mice so they cannot endure, likewise, the eyes of the precious mind of awakening also instantly overcome the mice of karma and afflictions so they cannot endure.' ^1-6-2

Thus it is said. ^1-6-3

Second, accomplishing benefit and happiness: The Kings of Sages, who contemplated and thought deeply about the benefit and methods for sentient beings for many countless aeons, saw that this very generation of bodhicitta is what benefits sentient beings. Third, fulfilling wishes: Those who wish to overcome hundreds of sufferings of cyclic existence in their own continuum, those who wish to dispel the unhappiness of all other sentient beings, and those who wish to enjoy hundreds of happinesses of high rebirth and definitive goodness, must never abandon this mind of perfect awakening. ^1-6-5

Fourth, transforming name and meaning: Wretched beings bound by the noose of karma and afflictions in the prison of cyclic existence, the moment this precious mind of perfect awakening is born in them, are called by the exceptional name 'Sons of the Sugatas,' and they obtain the exceptional meaning of that name, becoming objects of homage for worldly beings, including gods and humans. ^1-6-6

### 1.7 First, the analogy of the gold-making elixir: Just as a single ounce of the gold-making elixir can transform a thousand ounces of iron into gold, likewise, this mind transforms this impure body that we have taken into the priceless, precious body of a Buddha. Thus it is said. ^1-7-2

Second, the analogy of the wish-fulfilling jewel: The captain of wandering beings, the Buddha alone, who possesses immeasurable wisdom, thoroughly investigated the supreme method and saw that this very bodhicitta is of great value—being both rare and of immense benefit, like a precious wish-fulfilling jewel. As is said in the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*: 'Just as the sovereign king of wish-fulfilling jewels outshines all other ornaments, likewise, the precious mind of perfect awakening outshines all the qualities of Śrāvakas and Pratyekabuddhas, does not lose the name of a Bodhisattva, and dispels all the poverty of cyclic existence.' ^1-7-4

Third, the analogy of the wish-granting tree: All other virtues not grasped by bodhicitta are like a banana tree; once they bear fruit, their capacity to bear fruit again is exhausted, whereas the tree of bodhicitta, like a wish-granting tree, always bears fruit, is never exhausted, and increases more and more. ^1-7-6

As is said in the *Sūtra Taught by Aksayamati*: 'Just as a drop of water that falls into the great ocean is not exhausted until the ocean itself is exhausted, likewise, virtue dedicated to awakening is not exhausted until one reaches the heart of awakening.' ^1-7-7

As is said in the *Sūtra Taught by Aksayamati*: 'Just as relying on a powerful hero protects one from all enemies, likewise, relying on the mind of perfect awakening protects one from all the enemies of negative actions.' ^1-7-10

As is said in the *Questions of Subāhu Sūtra*: 'If a Bodhisattva generates the mind of perfect awakening with a vast resolve to benefit all sentient beings, even when sleeping, awake, or unmindful, the force of merit always increases and develops.' ^1-8-7

This precious, exceptional mind of the Bodhisattvas—the wish to attain buddhahood to accomplish the happiness and eliminate the suffering of all sentient beings, which does not arise in other sentient beings even for a single instant for their own sake—is an unprecedented, marvelous, and wondrous jewel of the mind. ^1-9-4
