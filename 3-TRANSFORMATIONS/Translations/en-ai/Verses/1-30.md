## Combined Commentary Summaries
The three classical Tibetan commentators—Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme—express absolute consensus on the profound import of Verse 1-30. They interpret this verse as the climax of the praise of conventional bodhicitta's unmatched qualities. Worldly beings are trapped in a tragic paradox where they desire happiness but actively destroy its causes out of sheer ignorance; bodhicitta is the only force in existence that can cure this existential blindness and guide them to ultimate liberation.

### 1. Dispelling Ignorance and Delusion (*sammoha* / *གཏི་མུག*)
All three commentators define the core function of bodhicitta as the eradication of the ignorance that plagues sentient beings.
*   **The Ignorance of Cause and Effect:** **Gyaltsab Je** and **Ngulchu Thokme** explain that worldly beings are fundamentally ignorant of the methods and causes of happiness and suffering. While they wish to avoid suffering, they blindly run toward non-virtue (its cause). While they desire happiness, they abandon virtue (its cause), thereby "destroying their own happiness like an enemy."
*   **The Remedy of Wisdom:** **Ngulchu Thokme** and **Gyaltsab Je** emphasize that the mind of enlightenment dispels this ignorance through "great wisdom" (*ཤེས་རབ་ཆེན་པོ* / *mahā-prajñā*). **Sazang Mati Panchen** adds that this wisdom clears their confusion specifically by "showing them the way of adoption (of virtue) and avoidance (of non-virtue)."

### 2. The Incomparability of Bodhicitta's Virtue
The commentators raise a series of rhetorical questions to show that no other virtue can match the mind of enlightenment.
*   **No Equal Virtue:** **Sazang Mati Panchen** asks, "what other virtue of equal strength is there to this motivation of the mind of enlightenment...?" and answers, "None exists." **Gyaltsab Je** similarly asks, "what other virtue is equal to this mind?" and **Ngulchu Thokme** asks, "where is there a virtue equal to this mind?"
*   **The Threefold Synthesis:** **Ngulchu Thokme** explains that this virtue is incomparable because it perfectly synthesizes the three great qualities: it satisfies beings with all happiness through great love, cuts off all suffering through great compassion, and dispels ignorance through great wisdom.

### 3. The Ultimate Spiritual Friend (*kalyāṇamitra*)
The commentators highlight that the Bodhisattva is the supreme companion and protector in cyclic existence.
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen** asks, "What other trustworthy spiritual friend or companion equal to this is there?" and declares, "None exists."
*   **Gyaltsab Je** defines this supreme spiritual friend as one "who accomplishes all benefit and protects from all harm."
*   **Ngulchu Thokme** echoes that there is no other helpful spiritual friend who can compare to the Bodhisattva, who acts as an unsolicited savior to helpless wandering beings.

### 4. The Ultimate Merit
Finally, the commentators discuss the unmatched merit generated by generating and maintaining this mind.
*   **Unerring Guidance:** **Gyaltsab Je** explains that this merit is supreme because it "unerringly teaches all points of adoption and abandonment," which is the only way to guide beings out of cyclic existence. He concludes that because such merit is unmatched, "one must make efforts to generate the mind of enlightenment."
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen** asks, "What other accumulation of merit equal to this that benefits sentient beings is there? There is none equal to it."
*   **Ngulchu Thokme** similarly asks, "Where else is there such merit generated by any other virtue?" and notes that the merit of this mind is limitless, filling and exceeding the expanse of space itself.
