## Per-Commentary Summaries

### [[en-AI-generated-commentary-gyaltsab(old)|Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen]] Summary
Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen structures the explanation of the homage into three parts: the purpose, the condensed meaning, and the meaning of the words.
- **Purpose:** Paying homage and taking refuge pacifies obstacles to composition, recognizes the author as a holy being, and leads to definitive goodness for both the author and disciples.
- **Condensed Meaning:** Paying homage to the Three Jewels and other worthy objects of respect.
- **Meaning of the Words:**
  - **Sugata (Gone to Bliss):** Explained in terms of both perfect abandonment and perfect realization.
    - *Perfect Abandonment:* Comprises (a) "leaving beautifully" (graceful abandonment of afflictions without remainder); (b) "leaving in a way that one does not return" to cyclic existence under the power of afflictions; and (c) "leaving without exception" (abandoning non-afflictive ignorance). These three distinguish him respectively from non-Buddhist ascetics free from attachment, Stream-enterers/Once-returners, and Arhats of the lesser vehicle.
    - *Perfect Realization:* Directly knowing the reality of the two types of selflessness, knowing firmly, and knowing without exception. These distinguish him respectively from non-Buddhist ascetics, Hinayana disciples, and Hinayana solitary realizers.
  - **The Three Jewels:**
    - *Buddha Jewel:* The Sugata who possesses the Dharmakaya.
    - *Dharma Jewel:* The naturally pure sphere (dhatu) free from all stains (the Dharmakaya), and the Mahayana Dharma.
    - *Sangha Jewel:* The "Sons" who are the noble Bodhisattvas.
  - **Homage:** Paid with devotion through body, speech, and mind to the Three Jewels and others worthy of respect (preceptors, masters).

### [[en-AI-generated-commentary-sazang(old)|Sazang Mati Panchen]] Summary
Sazang Mati Panchen Lodro Gyaltsen details the opening homage by analyzing its purpose, the person paying homage, the object, and the manner of homage.
- **Purpose:** Relying on clear faith in the qualities of the Buddhas to engage in the path and text, thereby obtaining higher rebirth and definite goodness. He cites the *Sūtra of Making Many Buddhas* as textual support.
- **Person:** The author along with his followers.
- **Object (The Three Jewels):**
  - **Buddha Jewel:** The one who has abandoned cyclic suffering and attained great, untainted happiness (self-benefit), and who guides others with great compassion to eliminate their suffering (other-benefit). He cites the *Uttaratantra* to describe Buddhahood's two benefits.
  - **Dharma Jewel:** The direct realization of emptiness (Dharmakaya), the cessation of desire/karma, and the clear light of wisdom. He cites the *Uttaratantra* definition of the Dharma Jewel.
  - **Sangha Jewel:** The "supreme sons" born from the Buddha's mind (Bodhisattvas) who possess direct realization of both the dharmadhatu and conventional reality. He cites the *Uttaratantra* and details why they are called "Jewels" (rare, stainless, powerful, ornament of the world, supreme, changeless).
  - **Other Objects:** Homage is also paid to Śrāvakas, Pratyekabuddhas, preceptors, stupas, etc.
- **Manner:** Bowing with the body, praising with speech, and recollecting qualities with the mind.

### [[en-AI-generated-commentary-thokme(old)|Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo]] Summary
Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo explains the homage in terms of the terms defined, the nature of the Three Jewels, and the ultimate purpose of the worship.
- **Homage to Sugatas:** He cites Locana's explanation of *Sugata* in terms of abandonment: (a) "gone beautifully" (abandoning afflictions); (b) "gone in a way that he does not return" (eliminating ignorance and afflictions); and (c) "gone completely without exception" (eliminating all habit-energies). He also cites Kṣemadeva's explanation of *Sugata* in terms of perfect realization. He cites the *Bodhisattva-bhūmi* on *Sugata* as "gone in an exceptionally distinguished way, without returning."
- **Object (The Three Jewels):**
  - **Buddha Jewel:** The Sugata.
  - **Dharma Jewel:** The realized Dharmakaya (inseparable from the pure dharmadhatu and stainless primordial wisdom) and the teaching of the Dharma (profound and diverse aspects taught by way of the two realities). He cites the *Uttaratantra*'s twofold division of the Dharmakaya.
  - **Sangha Jewel:** The "Sons of the Sages" (Bodhisattvas) who are born in the family of the Sugatas and inherit their lineage. He cites the *Uttaratantra* description of the Sons (faith as seed, wisdom as mother, concentration as womb, compassion as wet-nurse).
- **Homage and Purpose:** Paid with devotion through body, speech, and mind to the Three Jewels and preceptors/masters. He cites the *Sūtra Praising the Qualities of the Buddha* to show that paying homage accomplishes temporary and ultimate happiness.

## Combined Commentary Synthesis

### Consensus
All three commentators agree that Verse 1-1 constitutes the opening homage (worship) to the Three Jewels—the Buddhas (Sugatas), the Dharma (Dharmakaya), and the Sangha (Sons of the Sugatas)—and other worthy spiritual teachers (preceptors, masters) to clear obstacles to the composition and accumulate merit for temporary and ultimate happiness. 

They agree on the core definitions of the Three Jewels:
- **Buddha Jewel:** Identified as the *Sugata* (Gone to Bliss). Both Gyaltsab and Thokme explicitly cite classical sources (Locana, Kṣemadeva) to explain *Sugata* in terms of **abandonment** (abandoning afflictions beautifully, without returning, and without exception) and **realization** (directly knowing the selflessness of persons and phenomena).
- **Dharma Jewel:** Defined as the *Dharmakaya* (the naturally pure sphere or stainless dharmadhatu/wisdom, and the teachings).
- **Sangha Jewel:** Identified as the *Sons* (the noble Bodhisattvas who inherit the Buddha's lineage).
- **Maitreyan Literature:** All three commentators rely heavily on the *Uttaratantra* (Ratnagotravibhāga) to define the qualities of the Three Jewels and the spiritual sonship of the Bodhisattvas, showing a shared lineage of scholastic reference.

### Divergences and Unique Insights
- **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen** provides a highly structured, scholastic distinction showing how each aspect of *Sugata*'s abandonment and realization distinguishes the Buddha from non-Buddhist ascetics, Hinayana Stream-enterers, Once-returners, and Arhats/Solitary Realizers.
- **Sazang Mati Panchen** focuses on the dual aspects of self-benefit (perfect realization and abandonment) and other-benefit (compassionate guidance) of the Buddha Jewel. He also lists specific reasons why the Three Jewels are called "Jewels" (rare, stainless, powerful, ornament of the world, supreme, changeless).
- **Ngulchu Thokme** provides a highly poetic and verbatim citation of the *Uttaratantra*'s metaphor for the "Sons of the Sages" (faith as seed, wisdom as mother, concentration as womb, compassion as wet-nurse) and references the *Bodhisattva-bhūmi* for the definition of *Sugata*.
