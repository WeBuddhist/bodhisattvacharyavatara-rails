## Combined Commentary Summaries
The three commentators provide a highly unified and structurally consistent analysis of Verse 1-7, which praises the mind of enlightenment (Bodhicitta) as the supreme discovery of the Buddhas, capable of bringing countless sentient beings to the highest happiness easily and without hardship.

### 1. Structural Outlines and Contextualization
Each commentator places this verse under a specific section of their outline that highlights the ability of Bodhicitta to accomplish supreme benefit and happiness:
* **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen** explains this under section **癸二、能成勝樂** (Able to accomplish supreme happiness), which is a subdivision of **壬一、能摧一切罪成一切善** (Able to destroy all negativity and accomplish all virtue).
* **Sazang Mati Panchen** treats this under section **1.1** (holding the mind-generation firmly), specifically "Second, holding it firmly because it accomplishes benefit and happiness."
* **Ngulchu Thokme** analyzes this under section **1.7** (five common benefits of Bodhicitta), specifically "Second, accomplishing benefit and happiness."

### 2. The Exhaustive Search of the Sages
The commentators agree that the supreme benefit of Bodhicitta is not a casual or superficial finding, but the result of the Buddhas' deep, multi-eon contemplation:
* **Gyaltsab Je** notes that the "Kings of Sages" (the Buddhas) contemplated and thought deeply about the methods to benefit sentient beings for "many countless eons" and ultimately saw that this very generation of Bodhicitta is what truly benefits them.
* **Sazang Mati Panchen** similarly emphasizes that the Buddhas spent "many countless ages (such as three countless eons)" contemplating only the methods to benefit sentient beings, discovering that Bodhicitta is the unique vehicle that accomplishes all purposes of benefit and happiness.
* **Ngulchu Thokme** echoes this, stating that the Sages analyzed these methods over "three countless eons" and recognized Bodhicitta as the ultimate source of welfare for wandering beings.

### 3. The Joyful and Easy Path ("Without Hardships")
A major point of consensus among all three commentators is that Bodhicitta offers an exceptionally joyful and easy path to supreme enlightenment, contrasting sharply with other arduous spiritual paths:
* **Gyaltsab Je** introduces an agricultural metaphor, describing Bodhicitta as a "seed" that enables infinite assemblies of sentient beings to easily attain supreme bliss through a joyful path, "without relying on hardships such as tearing out one's hair" (referring to severe ascetic practices). He quotes the classic verse: *"Going from bliss to bliss, what intelligent person would be lazy?"* to illustrate that the Bodhisattva's path proceeds from happiness to even greater happiness.
* **Sazang Mati Panchen** likewise explains that Bodhicitta allows countless assemblies of beings to "easily obtain the supreme happiness of higher rebirth and definite goodness without depending on fierce hardships or arduous, mediocre efforts."
* **Ngulchu Thokme** summarizes this consensus by stating that through Bodhicitta, "infinite hosts of wandering beings can easily attain the supreme happiness of perfect enlightenment" smoothly and effortlessly.
