## Today's Verse

> Day and night, without ever stopping, my life is running out. No extra time is ever added back in. So how could I possibly avoid dying one day? ^2-39

> When I lie there dying, even if everyone I love is gathered around me, I will be completely alone in feeling that pain — no one else can feel it for me. ^2-40

> When Yama's messengers finally come for me, what good will family do? What good will friends do? Only merit can protect me then — and I have built so little of it. ^2-41

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on verses 39 through 41 from the second chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. Our life is being spent without a pause, day and night, and nothing is being added back. When we finally lie on the deathbed, however many relatives and friends are in the room, the experience itself is ours alone to go through. At that point nothing protects us except the merit we have already gathered. These verses are a serious push not to spend our days on nothing.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries compare this life to a pond whose feeding spring has been cut. Water drains out at the same rate as always and nothing flows in, so the level only falls. That is the force of the first verse today. It is not simply that life shortens. There is no mechanism anywhere by which anything could be added back.

His Holiness the Dalai Lama describes the last bed plainly. The person the illness has taken is like an old tree coming down: he tries to turn himself over and cannot. The room is full — relatives, friends, doctors, someone sitting up with him — and it changes nothing. Medicine and prayers do not change it either. Not the smallest part of what he is feeling can be handed to anyone else.

Then the verses say what does help, and it is more specific than merit in general: having gone for refuge, and clean conduct. That is what stays with us there. The commentaries reduce the whole thing to one line — the regret is for not having done the virtue. Which makes it a statement about ordinary days rather than about the last one.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today I will not let the time go to waste. I will help one person in need or one animal, and gather merit that way.

**Explanation:** _(Doing good)_ The reason we postpone virtue is that we assume there is plenty of time to get to it, and the reason we waste days is that no single wasted day feels expensive. These verses take away both assumptions: the time is draining without pause, and at the end only merit is any use to us. So today I will act on that instead of agreeing with it. I will look for one real chance to help, and it can be small. Carrying something for someone who is struggling, giving food to a street animal, sitting with a person who is unwell. Starting small is the point, because a habit of virtue that actually holds is built out of ordinary days.
