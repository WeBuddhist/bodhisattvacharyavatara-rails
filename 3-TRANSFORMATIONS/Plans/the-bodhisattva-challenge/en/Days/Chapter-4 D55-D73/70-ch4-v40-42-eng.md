## Today's Verse

> If fishermen, hunters, farmers, and so forth,
> who only think of their own livelihood,
> endure the harm of cold, heat, and so forth,
> why should I not endure it for the happiness of beings? ^4-40

> When I vowed to liberate
> all beings, filling the ten directions of space,
> from defilements,
> I myself was not yet free from defilements. ^4-41

> How is it not madness
> to speak without knowing my own measure?
> Therefore, I must always
> be unwavering in conquering defilements. ^4-42

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the three verses 40 to 42 from the fourth chapter of the _Bodhicaryāvatāra_. We vowed to free others, yet we ourselves are not yet free from the afflictions. Feeling ashamed of this, let us build up the strength of mind to conquer the afflictions.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Fishermen, hunters, farmers, and people in trades like these bear great hardship for the sake of a small livelihood for themselves and their families. They work day and night, not shrinking from many hardships such as cold and heat.

Ordinary people in this world endure such hardship for the sake of a small livelihood. We should endure hardship for the sake of the happiness and freedom of all beings. It is not right to be unable to endure hardship for the sake of this vast purpose.

No source commentary could be found for verses 41 and 42, so this explanation relies only on verse 40.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when I face some small hardship while helping others, I will not complain about it. I will accept it.

**Explanation:** _(Patience for the sake of others)_ People of this world take on great hardship for a small purpose of their own. So we should be able to endure a small hardship for the great purpose of helping others. This is a good path for bringing about the happiness of all beings.
