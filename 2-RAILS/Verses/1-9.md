## Traditional Interpretation

### kunpal — Khenpo Kunzang Palden (Tibetan)

Verse 1-9 describes the instant benefit of bodhicitta arising in one's continuum. The moment precious bodhicitta is born (*skad cig gis* — in an instant, the very moment it arises), two simultaneous transformations occur in even the most wretched being bound in samsara's prison (*'khor ba'i btson rar bsdams pa'i ñam thag pa*): (1) **name-change** (*ming gnas 'gyur*): the being is now called "son of the Sugatas" (*bde gshegs rnams kyi sras*) — a bodhisattva; (2) **essence-change** (*don gnas 'gyur*): the being becomes worthy of homage (*phyag bya*) from gods, humans, and non-humans of the world (*'jig rten lha mir bcas pas*). KKP adds: not only do ordinary beings honour bodhisattvas — the buddhas themselves honour bodhisattvas because bodhicitta is the teacher (*bla ma*) of all buddhas.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^1-9)

### gyaltsab — Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Tibetan)

GDR reads verse 1-9 as describing the second benefit of bodhicitta: the name-and-essence change (*ming don gnas 'gyur*). GDR specifies that this applies universally — regardless of sex (male/female), age (old/young), or social status (high/low birth). The moment (*skyes ma thag pa'i skad cig de ñid la*) precious bodhicitta arises in one's continuum, the being who was formerly bound in samsara's prison by the iron chains of karma and kleshas (*las dang ñon mongs pa'i lcags sgrog gis bsdams*) undergoes both changes: (1) name-change — they are called "son of Sugatas, bodhisattva," crowned with this name; (2) essence-change — gods and humans venerate and pay homage. Moreover, even the perfect buddhas honour bodhisattvas, since bodhicitta is the teacher (*bla ma*) of all buddhas.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-9)

### ngulchu-thogmed — Ngulchu Thokme Sangpo (Tibetan)

NTS reads the verse as showing the immediate, transformative power of bodhicitta arising. Even the most wretched (*ñam thag pa*) samsaric being, bound in the prison of cyclic existence, undergoes the instant transformation: they become called "son of Sugatas" and are bowed to by all gods and humans the very moment bodhicitta is born in their continuum. NTS notes this does not require one to be an ārya-bodhisattva or to have any special attainment — even an ordinary (*so so'i skye bo*) person who generates bodhicitta experiences this change immediately (*skad cig gis*). This is the verse's primary practical teaching: bodhicitta's benefit is not deferred to some future liberation but begins now, in this instant.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^1-9)

### minyak-kunzang-sonam — Mi Nyag Kunzang Sönam (Tibetan)

MKS gives the richest analysis. The "wretched beings bound in samsara's prison" are explicitly glossed: those bound by the iron chains of desire, hatred, and ignorance — the kleshas — in the prison of the three realms (*khams gsum gyi 'khor ba'i btson ra*). The moment either aspiring or engaging bodhicitta (*smon sems ñam 'jug sems gang rung*) arises, two complete transformations occur. (1) Name-change: one's very name changes — one is now "son of the Sugatas" (*bde gshegs rnams kyi sras*), a bodhisattva. (2) Essence-change: the inner reality changes — gods, humans, and non-humans (*lha dang lha min dang mir bcas pa*) all bow to one. MKS cites the Kāśyapaparivarta (*O srung gis zhus pa*): "Even a king's son born of a servant-woman gains the crown prince's title the moment of birth — similarly, the moment bodhicitta arises, one is 'son of the Sugatas' regardless of prior status." MKS also cites Śāntideva (*Zhi ba lha*) from a related text noting that even bodhisattvas who have taken rebirth as animals are worshipped, showing the name-change transcends bodily form.
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^1-9)

### khenpo-kunga — Ngakwang Kunga Wangchuk (Tibetan)

NKW reads the verse as demonstrating the most immediate benefit of bodhicitta — the transformation that happens in this very instant (*skad cig de ñid la*). When either aspiring or engaging bodhicitta arises in the continuum of even the most wretched samsaric being bound by karma and kleshas, that being instantly: (1) is called "son of the Sugatas" (name-change); (2) is bowed to by all the world including gods and humans (essence-change). NKW cites a general praise: "We bow to that one in whose continuum this precious mind has arisen." The point: the transformation is not gradual — it is as instantaneous as a lightning-flash, mirroring the verse's "skad cig gis."
(1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^1-9)

### Divergences

**བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་** vs. **བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་སྐྱེས་**: GDR and NTS read "bskyed" (actively generated); KKP, MKS, NKW, and KS read "skyes" (arisen/born). ⚑ The Tibetan root-text block at `bo-བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ།.md#^1-9` will be authoritative; the Disambiguated Restatement follows the KS/KKP reading ("skyes") as the dominant commentary reading.

**Aspiring vs. engaging bodhicitta**: MKS specifies "smon sems ñam 'jug sems gang rung" (either form). Other commentaries do not specify, implying both are included but not making it explicit. ⚑ Not a divergence on substance — a difference in explicitness.

## Word-by-word Disambiguation (ཚིག་དོན་གསལ་བཤད།)

- **འཁོར་བའི་བཙོན་ར་** ('khor ba'i btson ra) — "prison of samsara / samsara's jail": btson ra = prison, dungeon. The metaphor: samsara is a prison in which beings are incarcerated; the chains (*sgrog*) are kleshas. KS: "ādog chags la sogs pas bsdams" — bound by desire and the rest of the kleshas in this prison.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-9)

- **སྐད་ཅིག་གཅིག་གིས་** (skad cig gcig gis) — "in a single instant": the temporal qualifier that gives the verse its dramatic power. KS: "skyes par gyur na skad cig gcig gis" — the very instant it arises. This is not a gradual process but an instantaneous transformation.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-9)

- **མིང་གནས་འགྱུར་** (ming gnas 'gyur) — "name-position changes": a technical pair with **དོན་གནས་འགྱུར་** (don gnas 'gyur = essence/reality-position changes). KS supplies both explicitly. "Ming" refers to nominal designation — what one is called; "don" refers to the actual reality — what one is in essence. The verse itself only describes these; KS and the commentaries name the framework.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KS_bo.md#^1-9) · (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-9)

- **བདེ་བར་གཤེགས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་སྲས་** — "son of the Sugatas": "Sugatas" (bde gshegs = those who have gone to bliss, i.e., the buddhas) use the plural, indicating the being is now the spiritual child of *all* buddhas, not of one particular lineage. GDR: this is the crown of names (*ming gi cod pan*) placed upon one.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-9)

## Key Concepts (ཆོས་ཀྱི་གནད་ཚིག)

### ཚིགས་བཅད་ནང་གི་གནད་ཚིག

- **འཁོར་བའི་བཙོན་ར་** ('khor ba'i btson ra = prison of samsara) — སྲིད་པའི་བཙོན་རར་ལས་དང་ཉོན་མོངས་ཀྱི་ལྕགས་སྒྲོག་གིས་བསྡམས་ཏེ་གཡོ་མི་ནུས་པར་གནས་པའི་སྟངས་འཛིན། "btson ra" ལ་ "lcags sgrog" (GDR) = iron chains = karma and kleshas as the binding mechanism.
  (1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^1-9) · [[2-RAILS/Local-Wiki/འཁོར་བའི་བཙོན་ར་_(samsara-prison).md]]

- **བདེ་གཤེགས་ཀྱི་སྲས་** (bde gshegs kyi sras = son of the Sugatas) — བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་མིང་ (ming). GDR: "ming gi cod pan dpyang" = the crown of names placed upon one. Spiritual sonship to all buddhas, not birth-sonship. The term establishes the being's new lineage (*rigs*) as Mahāyāna.

### འགྲེལ་པ་ནས་འབྱུང་བའི་གནད་ཚིག

- **མིང་གནས་འགྱུར་** and **དོན་གནས་འགྱུར་** (name-change and essence-change) — GDR, KKP, KS གསུམ་གྱིས་གསལ། མིང་གནས་འགྱུར་ = བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཞེས་བརྗོད་བྱར་གྱུར་བ། དོན་གནས་འགྱུར་ = འཇིག་རྟེན་ལྷ་མིར་བཅས་པས་ཕྱག་བྱར་གྱུར་ལ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང་བཀུར་སྟི་བྱ་རུང་། དོན་གནས་འགྱུར་ = inner ontological change in status, not merely a renaming.

## Quotations (ལུང་།)

> རིགས་ཀྱི་བུ་དཔེར་ན་རྒྱལ་པོའི་ཕྲུ་གུ་བླ་མ་བྲན་མོའི་ལྟ་བུར་བཙས་ཤིང་སྐྱེས་པ་དེར་རྒྱལ་རིགས་ཀྱི་མིང་གི་ཅོད་པཎ་དེ་མ་ཐག་ཏུ་དབུར་མི་ཕུལ་བའམ། དེ་བཞིན་དུ་སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་སྲིད་པའི་བཙོན་རར་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་སྐྱེས་མ་ཐག་པར་བདེ་གཤེགས་ཀྱི་སྲས་ཞེས་བྱ་བའི་མིང་གི་ཅོད་པཎ་དབུར་ཕུལ་བར་འགྱུར་རོ།

## Disambiguated Restatement (Tibetan)

བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་ (སྨོན་སེམས་གང་ཡང་རུང་) རང་གི་རྒྱུད་ལ་སྐྱེས་པར་གྱུར་ན། སྔར་འཁོར་བའི་བཙོན་རར་ (= ཁམས་གསུམ་གྱི་འཁོར་བར) འདོད་ཆགས་ལ་སོགས་པའི་ཉོན་མོངས་ (= ལྕགས་སྒྲོག) གིས་བསྡམས་ཉམ་ཐག་པའི་སེམས་ཅན་གང་ཡིན་ (= ལུས་ལ་ཕོ་མོ། ན་ཚོད་ལ་རྒན་གཞོན། རིགས་ལ་བཟང་ངན་མེད་པར) ཡང་། (①) མིང་གནས་འགྱུར་ཏེ་ (= བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་སྐྱེས་མ་ཐག་གི་སྐད་ཅིག་གཅིག་གིས་) བདེ་བར་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་སྲས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཞེས་མིང་གི་ཅོད་པཎ་དབུར་ཕུལ་བར་བརྗོད་བྱར་འགྱུར། (②) དོན་གནས་འགྱུར་ཏེ་ (= ངོ་བོ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་གནས་འགྱུར་ཏེ་) འཇིག་རྟེན་གྱི་ལྷ་དང་ལྷ་མིན་དང་མིར་བཅས་པས་ཕྱག་བྱར་གྱུར་ལ། དེར་མ་ཟད་རྫོགས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང་བཀུར་སྟི་བྱ་རུང་གི་གནས་སུ་འགྱུར་རོ། །
