## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the two verses 28 and 29 from the third chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. A traveller worn out after a long road finds a cool tree to rest under at ease. In the same way, you should look closely today at the meaning of these two verses.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses tell us a story that is easy to understand and encouraging.

Long ago in Varanasi there was a king called Wangchen De. The king of the southern land gathered an army and made war on Varanasi. So that his own people would not commit wrongdoing, King Wangchen De gave up the kingdom and went to the forest. There he lived on roots and fruit.

At that time a poor brahmin heard that the king gave away whatever was asked of him. So he came to find him. The king had himself bound and carried before the king of the southern land, and told the brahmin to ask for whatever he wanted.

The king of the southern land was amazed. He gave the kingdom back and returned to his own country. The brahmin too was satisfied with wealth, and everyone was set in the ten good actions. The Buddha said that he himself was King Wangchen De at that time.

The king gave his body and his kingdom without holding anything back, and so removed the brahmin's poverty. In the same way, this shows that bodhicitta is the treasure that never runs out, which removes the poverty of all beings.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today I will comfort one friend who is weighed down by worry, and give them some rest.

**Explanation:** *(Generosity Practice)* Many beings are worn out on the road of samsara and worn down by the illness of suffering. Bodhicitta is like the medicine that calms that illness, and like the tree that gives them rest. If today we help and comfort someone with love, we accomplish the supreme cause of bodhicitta.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

By teaching the Dharma to sentient beings in a way that fits each one's own inclination, bodhisattvas destroy at the very root the demon of the Lord of Death for limitless beings without exception, and bring them to attain great awakening, the deathless peace. What has thus become the supreme nectar is this very bodhicitta; and this agrees with the scriptural authority of the *Uttaratantra*.

For the time being too, it is the nectar that turns back untimely death.

With the word "too," he shows that it is supreme even over the accomplished nectar of the awareness-holders among gods, nāgas and humans, which can keep them from dying for many hundreds of years.

And on the eighth ground, when mastery over the necessities of life has been gained, it removes the poverty of wandering beings without exception. What has thus become the great inexhaustible treasure — the wealth of the sky-treasury — is this very bodhicitta as well.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-28]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

The supreme nectar that destroys the Lord of Death for beings, who die with no freedom in the matter, is this generation of bodhicitta — because it establishes them on the level where there is no more aging and death.

And since it gives inexhaustible enjoyment of both material things and Dharma, the inexhaustible treasure that removes the poverty of beings is this generation of the mind too.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-28]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Since it destroys the Lord of Death for beings and so makes them deathless, the supreme nectar too is this bodhicitta; and the inexhaustible treasure that removes all the poverty of beings is this as well.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-28]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Because it destroys at the very root the demon of the Lord of Death for beings without exception, and brings them to attain the awakening in which there is no aging and death, the supreme deathless nectar too is this very bodhicitta.

And because, once mastery over the necessities of life is gained, it removes the whole of the poverty of every being without exception, what has become the great treasure of inexhaustible wealth is this very bodhicitta as well.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-28]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

The supreme deathless nectar, the finest of medicines, which destroys whatever demon of the Lord of Death there is for other wandering beings, is this very generation of bodhicitta.

And the inexhaustible treasure — the treasury of precious things that never runs out and yields whatever is needed and wished for, which clears away the poverty of wandering beings, their being in want and destitute of food, clothing, bedding and the necessities of life — is this very generation of bodhicitta too.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-28]]

#### Stories and Illustrations

<!-- story:wangchuk-rinchen -->
##### The Story of King Wangchen De

Long ago in Varanasi, while King Wangchen De was governing a realm perfectly prosperous both in Dharma and in worldly affairs, the king of the southern land drew up his forces and made war on Varanasi. Rather than let his own people commit wrongdoing merely to stay alive, Wangchen De had them pay homage to the king of the southern land, while he himself went off into the forest and lived on roots and fruit. At that time a poor brahmin heard that Wangchen De would give whatever was asked of him, and came to find him. The king had himself bound and carried before the king of the southern land, and told the brahmin to ask for whatever he wanted. The king of the southern land was amazed; he begged the king's pardon and bore it patiently, gave the kingdom back, and returned to his own country. The brahmin too was satisfied with wealth, and everyone was established in the ten virtuous actions. The Teacher said that he himself had been King Wangchen De at that time. This connection is applied to the words "inexhaustible treasure": it is given as an illustration of how one becomes oneself an inexhaustible treasure of giving that removes the poverty of others.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Bodhicitta Itself Is the Supreme Nectar That Destroys the Lord of Death for Beings

All the commentaries agree in the end that this very generation of bodhicitta destroys the demon of the Lord of Death for beings at its root, and brings them to attain great awakening, the deathless peace in which there is no aging and death.

###### 2. Bodhicitta Itself Is the Inexhaustible Treasure That Removes the Poverty of Beings

All the commentaries agree in the end that this generation of the mind has become the great inexhaustible treasure that removes every kind of poverty beings have — their want of food, clothing, bedding and the rest.

###### 3. The Nectar of Bodhicitta Is Supreme Even Over the Nectar of the Awareness-Holders Among Gods, Nāgas and Humans, Which Only Postpones Death

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that with the word "too" bodhicitta is shown to be supreme even over the nectar that can keep those awareness-holders from dying for many hundreds of years.

###### 4. When Mastery Over the Necessities of Life Is Gained on the Eighth Ground, Bodhicitta Becomes a Great Inexhaustible Treasure Like the Sky-Treasury

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden and Sabzang Mati Panchen both explain that at the time when mastery over the necessities of life is gained, the poverty of wandering beings is removed without exception.

###### 5. The Generation of the Mind Can Destroy the Lord of Death Because It Establishes Beings on the Level Where There Is No Aging and Death

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen gives this as the reason why the generation of the mind is able to destroy the Lord of Death.

###### 6. The Story of Giving Away Even Oneself Shows What "Inexhaustible Treasure" Means

Wangchuk Rinchen applies the story of King Wangchen De — who gave away not only his body but his very self to a brahmin — as an illustration of how an inexhaustible treasure removes the poverty of others.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows bodhicitta in two ways. First, it is the supreme nectar that destroys the demon of the Lord of Death for beings at its very root and brings them to the deathless peace in which there is no aging and death. Second, it is the great inexhaustible treasure that removes beings' poverty of food, clothing and the rest. All the commentaries agree on this.

**Key points.**

- Bodhicitta is the supreme nectar that destroys the Lord of Death for beings.
- This generation of the mind can destroy the Lord of Death because it establishes beings on the level where there is no aging and death.
- Bodhicitta is supreme even over the nectar of the awareness-holders among gods, nāgas and humans, which only postpones death.
- Bodhicitta is the inexhaustible treasure that removes beings' poverty of food, clothing, bedding and the rest.
- When mastery over the necessities of life is gained on the eighth ground, it becomes a great inexhaustible treasure, like the inexhaustible wealth of the sky-treasury.
- The story of King Wangchen De illustrates the inexhaustible treasure of one who gives away even himself.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

The words "the supreme medicine that perfectly calms the illnesses of beings is this too" he explains in this way: the supreme nectar that destroys the Lord of Death for beings and bestows the unsurpassed awakening in which there is no aging and death — that too is this.

Or again: whatever measure of the wish to help others a person has, to that measure he becomes happier in mind, more at ease, less frightened and more courageous. Because of that his mind can find peace and well-being, his body has less illness, and his life grows longer. For this reason too, he says, bodhicitta is the supreme medicine of nectar.

The supreme medicine that perfectly calms every illness of beings — those that come suddenly and those they are born with alike — is this bodhicitta. So he teaches.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-29]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The illnesses of the afflictions are attachment, aversion, delusion and the rest, and their result is the illness of suffering — the disorders of wind, bile and phlegm and so on. By teaching the Dharma of the unattractive aspect, of loving kindness, of dependent arising and of selflessness, bodhisattvas perfectly calm every illness of beings, both cause and result. What has thus become the very finest of all good medicines is this bodhicitta.

As the Perfection of Wisdom says, bodhisattvas come to know the paths of the three vehicles and the rest. To those beings of the shravaka and pratyekabuddha family who are persons of middling capacity, who have wandered long on this road of samsaric existence and are worn out by suffering and tormented by it, they teach the path of the shravakas, and so calm the suffering of samsaric existence. In this way even the attainment of the shravaka and pratyekabuddha fruit — the resting place that eases the weariness of suffering, like the cool shade of a fine tree — comes about through the power of this very bodhicitta.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-29]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-zhengah -->

In the phrase "the road of existence," he sets out the identification given by the base commentary: existence is divided into three — existence as birth, existence as death, and the intermediate-state kind of existence.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Bodhicitta Is the Supreme Medicine That Perfectly Calms Every Illness of Beings

Many commentaries say the same thing: it calms both the illnesses of the body (wind, bile, phlegm and the rest) and the illnesses of the mind (the afflictions).

###### 2. Bodhicitta Is the Resting Tree for Beings Who Have Wandered Long on the Road of Existence and Are Worn Out

Many commentaries explain that for the travellers among beings, worn out by suffering, it has become a resting place, like a tree with cool shade.

###### 3. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden Explains That Bodhisattvas Calm Beings' Illnesses of Cause and Result by Teaching the Unattractive Aspect, Loving Kindness, Dependent Arising and Selflessness

He identifies these four teachings as the remedies that clear away both the illness of the afflictions and the illness of suffering that results from them.

###### 4. Even the Fruit Attained by Those of the Shravaka and Pratyekabuddha Family Comes About Through the Power of Bodhicitta

Citing the Perfection of Wisdom, Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that it is attained through the power of bodhisattvas teaching the shravaka path and thereby calming the suffering of samsaric existence.

###### 5. "The Road of Existence" Is Identified as Three: Existence as Birth, Existence as Death, and the Existence of the Intermediate State

Zhenphen Jampé Gocha, following the base commentary, divides the road of existence into these three and identifies it accordingly.

###### 6. "Illness" Means the Eighty-Four Thousand Afflictions in the Minds of Beings

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk gives the actual number of the illnesses, and explains that bodhicitta calms the weariness and exhaustion of circling and wandering again and again in the three realms of samsaric existence.

###### 7. Bodhicitta Is Also the Supreme Nectar That Destroys the Lord of Death and Bestows the Unsurpassed Awakening in Which There Is No Aging and Death

His Holiness the Dalai Lama gives two ways of taking the phrase "the supreme medicine." On the first, it is the nectar that destroys the Lord of Death. On the second, he has in mind the benefit that even a passing wish to help others brings — the mind grows peaceful and content, the body has less illness, and life grows longer — and on that basis calls it the supreme medicine of nectar.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse applies two images to bodhicitta: it is the supreme medicine that perfectly calms every illness of beings' bodies and minds, and it is the resting tree for beings who have wandered long on the road of existence and are worn out. Many commentaries explain both images in the same way, and in the end what they show is that bodhicitta has the power to clear away both beings' afflictions and the suffering those afflictions produce.

**Key points.**

- Bodhicitta is the supreme medicine that perfectly calms both the illnesses of the body (wind, bile and phlegm) and the illnesses of the mind (the afflictions).
- For the travellers among beings, who have wandered long on the road of existence and are worn out by suffering, bodhicitta has become a resting place, like a tree with cool shade.
- As the Perfection of Wisdom says, even the fruit attained by those of the shravaka and pratyekabuddha family comes about through the power of the bodhicitta of the bodhisattvas who teach them the path.
- ⚑ The commentaries identify "the road of existence" differently: Zhenphen Jampé Gocha divides it into the three existences of birth, death and the intermediate state, while Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains it as circling again and again in the three realms of samsara.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama gives two ways of taking "the supreme medicine": as the nectar that destroys the Lord of Death, and as the benefit by which even a passing wish to help others brings peace of mind, less bodily illness and a longer life.
