## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on verses 18 and 19 from the second chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. It invites us to use the power of our imagination to offer boundless, jewel-filled palaces and beautiful golden-handled umbrellas (parasols) to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas. This practice teaches us how to take an ordinary object from daily life — like an umbrella or shade — and transform it into a powerful practice of generosity and mental offering.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

These verses conclude the teachings on the twelve types of mental offering by focusing on celestial palaces and precious umbrellas. Master Shantideva imagines presenting immeasurable, jewel-built palaces where delightful songs of praise and music echo through the halls. These palaces are adorned with glowing pearl pendants and shining gemstones, filling the entire sky like a magnificent ornament. He joyfully offers all of this to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas, who are the very embodiment of great compassion.

Next, recalling how the ancient gods Brahma and Indra once offered umbrellas to the Buddha, Shantideva mentally presents magnificent precious parasols with solid gold handles. These umbrellas are beautifully shaped and decorated around the edges with strings of pearls and jewels. He vows to continually offer these protective canopies to the awakened beings.

Spiritual teachers explain that offering these umbrellas carries a deep spiritual wish. By mentally offering parasols that protect from the burning sun, we plant the seeds of positive energy (merit) so that all living beings may be protected from the burning heat of worldly suffering and pain. We pray that through this gift, everyone may find safe, cool refuge under the protective shade of the Three Jewels (Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha).

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when I see an umbrella or shade, I will offer it in my mind to the Three Jewels as a special umbrella protecting everyone from pain.

**Explanation:** *(Generosity Practice)* Usually, when we use an umbrella or look for shade from the sun or rain, we only think about keeping ourselves comfortable. Today, following the teachings, when I see an umbrella or cool shade, I will picture it as a beautiful "special umbrella" with a gold handle and covered in jewels. I will happily offer it in my mind to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas. As I make this offering, I will strongly wish that its cool shade protects me and all living beings from the hot fire of anger and pain, bringing everyone into the safe and peaceful care of the Three Jewels.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

I offer celestial palaces. First, the ground where the palace stands is arrayed — that is, swept clean and made pure — and, anointed well with anointing-perfume so that the place is pervaded with sweet fragrance, one adorns it with pleasing, beautifully strewn flowers, and scatters loose blossoms over the ground: this too is offered. And such a precious palace, immeasurable in size and value; and, further, in every direction and quarter, singers and dancers making the offering of song, pleasing, who chant songs of praise; hung with clusters of precious ornaments such as pearls; beautiful; blazing with light; endless and boundless — that is, immeasurable to the mind — and, pervading all that space can reach, become an ornament of the sky: these too I offer to you whose very nature is compassion.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-18]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Palaces immeasurable to the mind in extent and design, formed of various precious substances — five-storied, five-hundred-storied and the like — within which, and along all the eaves, offering-goddesses, with instruments such as the lute, chant pleasing, sweet songs of praise. From the outer skirts and the inner pillars and the rest hang red pearls and clusters of various-jeweled ornaments — nets and half-nets and the like — from the mouths of lions, water-monsters and so on, beautiful and pleasing, blazing with light and rays; the ends of the nets and half-nets adorned with yak-tail whisks, bells, and mirrors, the surface of the mirrors and the sound of the bells teaching the conduct of the bodhisattvas. From sandalwood spouts, streams of sweet-scented water fall, so that bathing-pools gather in which various water-birds sing sweet songs and play. In the inner chambers, in some there are various garments, in some various food and drink; day-thrones and night-resting-couches with variegated cushions and pillows of divine cloth and mosaic-work, of the seven kinds of precious substance; by the light of lamps of various precious substances every direction is bright, day and night. Immeasurable to the mind and boundless, pervading all that the expanse of space can reach, so that they become like an ornament of the sky — these too I offer to the buddhas, the Blessed Ones, whose very nature is compassion, together with their host of bodhisattva heirs. And one should think, "may all beings, oneself and others, attain the great city of liberation."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-18]]

##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Within palaces — fine mansions formed of various precious substances, endowed with measureless qualities of beautiful shape and color and the like — the goddesses abiding there chant many sweet, pleasing songs of praise and eulogy to the buddhas together with their heirs; and, inside and out, made beautiful with dangling clusters of pearl-necklaces and various ornaments of great-value gems, blazing with light; with canopies, victory-banners, pennants and the like, lovely in shape and color and radiating splendor — offering-substances measureless and boundless — so that, with a great cloud-mass of them, they fill the whole expanse of space and make it beautiful, and so have become an ornament of the sky: these too I offer to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, whose very nature is great compassion that wishes only the benefit and happiness of all beings. And some, gathering "the ground well arrayed, anointed with scented water and strewn with flowers" into the offering of the celestial mansion, explain the arrayed-ground offering as part of the offering of the immeasurable mansion.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^2-18]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

A palace — the pleasing design of a celestial palace-city, by nature formed of various precious substances — in which beautiful, pleasing goddesses, taking as their theme the qualities of the body, speech, and mind of the buddhas and bodhisattvas, chant sweet, pleasing songs of praise and eulogy; from every direction of that fine mansion, throughout the sky, hang pendants of a pearl-luster nature and various ornaments of precious substances such as gold, silver, cat's-eye, coral, camphor-crystal and the like, beautiful, blazing with light and rays; immeasurable to the mind — hence "boundless beyond measure" — and become an ornament of the vast expanse of space: all such pleasing celestial-mansion designs one emanates with the mind and offers to the buddhas and bodhisattvas, whose very nature is a compassion without any focal object. By the merit of offering such a celestial mansion, one should pray, "may all beings, oneself and others, in the future be able to enter the unsurpassed city of liberation." And [likewise] the requisites fit for a great one — precious parasols, lion-thrones with footrests, cooling fans with jeweled handles and the like — are treated as offerings.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^2-18]]

<!-- div:divergences -->
##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **gyaltsab / tenzin-gyatso:** they gather the offering of the arrayed ground of verse 2-17 (ground anointed with incense and strewn with loose blossoms) into the explanation of the celestial-palace offering, treating the two together.
- **minyak-kunzang-sonam:** he himself explains the arrayed-ground offering and the celestial-palace offering separately, but notes that some commentaries gather the two together.
- **khenpo-kunga:** he adds the qualification that the field of offering, those whose nature is compassion, has "a compassion without any focal object" — a specification not found in the other commentaries.

The meaning does not conflict, but since the way the text is divided, and the way the field of offering is qualified, differ, it is noted separately.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Mind-Emanated Offering of the Celestial Palace

All the commentaries explain on a shared basis that one emanates with the mind, and offers, a palace immeasurable to the mind in extent and design, formed of various precious substances.

###### 2. The Songs of Praise of the Goddesses

Within the celestial palace the goddesses chant sweet, pleasing songs of praise; Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk makes clear that these are praises taking as their theme the qualities of the body, speech, and mind of the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

###### 3. The Offering-Substances, Hung With Pearl and Jewel Ornaments, Become an Ornament of the Sky

Many commentaries explain in one voice that the dangling clusters of pearl and various-jewel ornaments, blazing with light, are so measureless and boundless that they pervade the whole expanse of space and thus become the very ornament of the sky.

###### 4. The Field of Offering — Those Whose Nature Is Compassion, the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas

The field is settled as the buddhas and bodhisattvas whose very nature is compassion; and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk in particular explains that their compassion is by nature without a focal object.

###### 5. The Tradition of Gathering the Arrayed-Ground Offering Into the Celestial-Palace Offering

Minyak Kunzang Sonam sets out separately that some commentaries gather the incense-anointed, flower-strewn arrayed-ground offering of verse 2-17 into the offering of the celestial palace.

###### 6. An Aspiration-Prayer Accompanies This Mind-Emanated Offering

Kunzang Pelden explains that, at the close of offering the celestial palace in this way, one links the aspiration-prayer, "may all beings, oneself and others, attain the great city of liberation."

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse presents the mind-emanated offering of a celestial palace: an immeasurable palace of precious substances in which goddesses chant songs of praise, hung with pearl and jewel ornaments and blazing with light so that — measureless and boundless — it becomes an ornament of the sky, offered to the buddhas and bodhisattvas whose very nature is compassion. All the commentaries explain it in agreement.

**Key points.**

- The offering-substance is a precious celestial palace emanated with the mind.
- Within the palace, goddesses chant sweet songs of praise resting on the qualities of the buddhas and bodhisattvas.
- The dangling clusters of pearl and jewel ornaments, blazing with light, are so measureless that they pervade all space and become the ornament of the sky.
- The field of offering is the buddhas and bodhisattvas whose nature is compassion; ⚑ Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk specifies that it is a compassion without any focal object.
- ⚑ Gyaltsab and Tenzin Gyatso gather the arrayed-ground offering of verse 2-17 into the celestial-palace offering, treating them together, as Minyak Kunzang Sonam notes is done by "some commentaries."

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Beautiful precious parasols with golden handles, which give shelter with cool shade from the touch of scorching heat; adorned all around with a pleasing design of ornaments; so well-shaped and lovely to behold — these too, raised aloft and held up, I offer always to the Lords of Sages. With this, he has taught some twelve offerings up to this point.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^2-19]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Beautiful parasols of divine silk and various precious substances, with golden handles; their rim — the skirt — adorned with pleasing ornament-forms of pearls and the like: endless-knots, nets, half-nets and the rest; well-shaped and lovely to behold, raised aloft — that is, held up on high — held by offering-goddesses and by well-trained horses, elephants and so on. Just as, in the past, to the compassionate Teacher, Brahmā from the right offered five hundred parasols of sapphire and Indra from the left offered five hundred parasols of Jambū-river gold, so I offer them, always, to the Lords of Sages together with their heirs.

Offering thus, one should think, "may all beings, oneself and others, be sheltered from the burning torment of samsara and the lower realms, and be gathered under the cool shade-refuge of the Three Jewels."

Thus, since one's own mind cannot pervade the billionfold universe and the rest, one must imagine these twelve kinds of mind-emanated offering as pervading in that way. Because Shantideva was a yogi of utter non-elaboration, he did not speak of vast material offerings, but [taught] that these — a supreme means of perfectly completing a vast accumulation of merit without difficulty — are the skillful-means offering of the bodhisattvas. Therefore one should not disregard these kinds of offering, but cultivate them again and again. And this need not be a continuous meditation like deity-yoga: merely by their appearing to the mind for a moment, an instant at a time, without difficulty or fatigue, a vast accumulation of merit is gathered, so that the accumulations are completed, the obscurations purified, bodhicitta arises in the mind-stream, and all the qualities — understanding and not forgetting what one has heard, and the rest — arise. Since great aims are accomplished with small effort, if one reflects on and offers these kinds of offering again and again, it is said to be a vaster and purer offering than however much one might offer of offerings produced through wrong livelihood and wrongdoing, which turn into pride and display.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^2-19]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Parasols formed of precious substances, to be held up over the head — rain-parasols that give shelter from rain, sun-parasols that give shelter from heat — utterly beautiful and pleasing, with handles made of gold, hence "golden-handled"; the whole rim of that parasol adorned, in a pleasing nature, with various pearls and various ornamental designs of gold, silver and the like, of fine workmanship and quality; utterly well-shaped — plump and full, and not too flat or thin — so that they are lovely to behold and pleasing; and many, so many that they fill the whole vast expanse of space like a forest, all raised aloft: all of these too I offer, held up over the heads of the Teacher, the Lord of Sages, and his heirs the bodhisattvas, always. By the merit of offering such supreme parasols, one should pray, "may all beings, oneself and others, come under the refuge of the Three Jewels."

#### Metaphors and Examples

- **Brahmā and Indra each offering five hundred parasols to the Teacher** (ཚངས་པ་དང་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱིས་སྟོན་པ་ལ་གདུགས་ལྔ་བརྒྱ་རེ་ཕུལ་བ་) → a precedent-story set beside the present way of offering. — Kunzang Pelden gives the precedent that, in the past, to the compassionate Teacher, Brahmā from the right offered five hundred parasols of sapphire and Indra from the left offered five hundred parasols of Jambū-river gold — so, likewise, now one offers parasols always to the Lords of Sages.
- **The rain-parasol and the sun-parasol** (ཆར་གདུགས་དང་ཚ་གདུགས་) → the sheltering power that is the parasol-offering's nature. — Like a rain-parasol that shelters from rain and a sun-parasol that shelters from heat, the offering-parasol too is shown to have the power to shelter from harm.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Last of the Twelve Kinds of Mind-Emanated Offering — Offering the Precious Parasol

This verse presents the last of the twelve kinds of mind-emanated offering, stating that one offers always, to the Lords of Sages together with their heirs, a beautiful parasol with a golden handle.

###### 2. The Material and Design of the Parasol

All the commentaries explain in one voice that it is a beautiful parasol formed of various precious substances, with a golden handle, its rim adorned with many precious ornament-forms such as pearls, well-shaped and lovely to behold.

###### 3. The Power of the Parasol — Its Nature of Sheltering From Burning Heat

Sabzang Mati and Minyak Kunzang Sonam explain clearly that the parasol gives shelter from the burning torment of the sun's rays and the like; Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk gives the image of the rain-parasol and the sun-parasol; and Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso explains it as giving shelter with cool shade from the touch of scorching heat.

###### 4. The Past Precedent — Brahmā and Indra Offering Parasols to the Teacher

Kunzang Pelden gives the precedent that in the past Brahmā offered five hundred sapphire parasols and Indra five hundred golden parasols to the compassionate Teacher, so that now too one offers the parasol.

###### 5. The Fruit of the Offering — the Aspiration-Prayer That All Beings Come Under the Refuge of the Three Jewels

Kunzang Pelden explains that by offering the parasol one should think, "may all beings, oneself and others, be sheltered from the burning torment of samsara and the lower realms, and be gathered under the cool shade-refuge of the Three Jewels"; and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains the aspiration-prayer in agreement.

###### 6. The Distinctive Quality of the Mind-Emanated Offering — Perfecting a Great Accumulation Without Difficulty

Kunzang Pelden explains clearly that, because Shantideva was a yogi of utter non-elaboration, he did not speak of vast material offerings; rather, these mind-emanated offerings are a supreme means of perfectly completing a vast accumulation of merit without difficulty, and that even by their appearing to the mind for a moment the accumulations are completed and the obscurations purified.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse, the last of the twelve kinds of mind-emanated offering, states that one offers always, to the Lords of Sages together with their heirs, a beautiful parasol with a golden handle. All the commentaries explain it in agreement on the parasol's material (various precious substances, a golden handle) and design (the rim adorned with pearls and the like, well-shaped).

**Key points.**

- This is the last, the culmination, of the twelve kinds of mind-emanated offering, as both Kunzang Pelden and Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso state.
- The parasol has the power to shelter from harm, like a rain-parasol and a sun-parasol, as Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk gives by image and Sabzang Mati and Minyak Kunzang Sonam by explaining it as sheltering from the sun's burning heat.
- Kunzang Pelden gives the precedent-story of Brahmā and Indra each offering five hundred parasols to the Teacher, set beside the present offering.
- The aspiration-prayer of the parasol-offering — that all beings, oneself and others, be sheltered from the burning torment of samsara and the lower realms and come under the refuge of the Three Jewels — is explained in agreement by Kunzang Pelden and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk.
- The distinctive quality of the mind-emanated offering is that it is a supreme means of perfectly completing a vast accumulation of merit without difficulty — so that even a momentary appearance to the mind completes the accumulations and purifies the obscurations, as Kunzang Pelden explains.
