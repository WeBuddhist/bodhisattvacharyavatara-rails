## 8. Chapter Eight: Teaching on Meditation ^8-0

When diligence has thus been generated, ^8-1
the mind should be settled in meditation.
A person whose mind is distracted ^8-1
remains in the grip of troubling emotions. ^8-1

By being in solitude, in body and mind, ^8-2
distraction will not arise.
Therefore, abandon worldly things ^8-2
and completely give up discursive thoughts. ^8-2

Due to attachment and craving for gain and so forth, ^8-3
one does not abandon worldly things.
Therefore, completely give up these things, ^8-3
and the wise should examine them in this way. ^8-3

Knowing that insight, perfectly endowed with calm, ^8-4
completely destroys troubling emotions,
one should first seek calm, and that too ^8-4
is accomplished by delight in non-attachment to worldly things. ^8-4

Who among the impermanent ^8-5
would truly become attached to the impermanent?
For a thousand eons, ^8-5
one will never see what is pleasant. ^8-5

If one does not see it, one will not rejoice. ^8-6
The mind will not be settled in equipoise.
Even if one sees it, one will not be satisfied, ^8-6
and one will be tormented by craving as before. ^8-6

If one becomes attached to beings, ^8-7
one is completely obscured from the truth.
One also destroys the mind of weariness, ^8-7
and ultimately one will be tormented by sorrow. ^8-7

Merely by thinking of them, ^8-8
this life will pass meaninglessly.
By impermanent friends, ^8-8
the eternal Dharma will also be destroyed. ^8-8

If one associates with foolish people, ^8-9
one will certainly go to the lower realms.
If one leads them to what is not equal, ^8-9
what is the use of relying on foolish people? ^8-9

In a single instant, one becomes a friend, ^8-10
and in a moment, one also becomes an enemy.
Becoming angry at the object of joy, ^8-10
it is hard to please ordinary people. ^8-10

If one speaks beneficently, they become angry. ^8-11
One also turns away from benefit.
If one does not listen to their words, ^8-11
one will go to the lower realms due to anger. ^8-11

They envy the high, compete with equals, ^8-12
are arrogant when praised by the low,
and become angry when unpleasant things are said. ^8-12
When will one receive benefit from foolish people? ^8-12

If one associates with foolish people, ^8-13
one will certainly generate unwholesome things,
such as praising oneself and criticizing others, ^8-13
and talk that delights in cyclic existence. ^8-13

Thus, relying on oneself and others ^8-14
will only lead to ruin.
One's own purpose has not been accomplished by them, ^8-14
and one will not accomplish their purpose. ^8-14

One should keep far away from foolish people. ^8-15
If one meets them, one should please them with joy.
One should act well, merely as a stranger, ^8-15
without becoming too intimate. ^8-15

Like a bee taking honey from a flower, ^8-16
one should take only the essence of the Dharma.
One should dwell without intimacy with everyone, ^8-16
as if one had never seen them before. ^8-16

If one harbors such pride as, ^8-17
"I have much gain and respect,
and many people are fond of me," ^8-17
fear will arise after death. ^8-17

Therefore, whatever the deluded mind ^8-18
is attached to,
that, multiplied a thousandfold, ^8-18
will become suffering and arise. ^8-18

Therefore, the wise should not be attached. ^8-19
Fear arises from attachment.
Since these things are by nature impermanent, ^8-19
be steadfast and truly understand. ^8-19

Even if much gain arises, ^8-20
and fame and renown arise,
where will one go ^8-20
with the collection of gain and fame, which is uncertain? ^8-20

If others criticize me, ^8-21
what joy is there in praise for me?
If others praise me, ^8-21
what unhappiness is there in criticism? ^8-21

If the buddhas themselves ^8-22
cannot satisfy the various inclinations of beings,
what need is there to speak of a lowly person like me? ^8-22
Therefore, abandon worldly concerns. ^8-22

Beings criticize those who have no gain. ^8-23

How can joy arise ^8-23
from those whose nature is difficult-to-please? ^8-23

Because foolish people's own purposes ^8-24
do not arise without joy,
the buddhas taught ^8-24
that no foolish person is a friend. ^8-24
