## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three commentators agree on the core meaning of Verse 1-8: it presents three supreme motivations (overcoming one's own samsaric suffering, removing the suffering of all other sentient beings, and experiencing/attaining supreme happiness for self and others) which can only be accomplished by generating and never abandoning the mind of enlightenment (bodhicitta).

### 1. The Three Core Aspirations of the Verse
- **Overcoming Samsaric Sufferings:** All three commentators interpret "overcoming the hundreds of sufferings of existence" (*bhavaduḥkhaśatāni tartukāmaiḥ*) as wishing to transcend the sufferings of cyclic existence (samsara). Sazang specifically mentions "sufferings of the hell realms" as an example of what is to be destroyed, while Thokme and Gyaltsab Je specify overcoming sufferings in "their own continuum" or "their own cycle of existence."
- **Removing Others' Misery:** All three interpret "removing the unhappiness/adversities of sentient beings" (*sattvavyasanāni hartukāmaiḥ*) as the compassionate wish to dispel the sufferings of all other living beings. Sazang illustrates this with specific samsaric miseries like "birth, old age, sickness, and death."
- **Experiencing Limitless Joys:** All three interpret "experiencing a myriad of/hundreds of joys" (*bahusaukhyaśatāni bhoktukāmaiḥ*) as wanting to enjoy both temporary and ultimate happiness. Thokme and Gyaltsab Je explicitly define these joys as "high rebirth / happy migrations" (temporary, *abhyudaya*) and "definitive goodness" (ultimate, *niḥśreyasa*), while Sazang similarly calls them "temporary and ultimate happinesses for themselves and all others."

### 2. Bodhicitta as the Sole and Certain Method
- **Consensus:** All three commentators agree that the mind of enlightenment (*bodhicitta*) is the supreme, certain, and indeed *only* method to accomplish these three goals. Consequently, an intelligent practitioner must "never abandon" or "never discard" it at any time. Thokme Zangpo notes that it is "certain to accomplish all these goals," while Sazang explains that since "this very mind of enlightenment is the method to definitely accomplish these, they should never discard it at any time."

### 3. Structural Divisions and Unique Scholastic Frameworks
- **Gyaltsab Je's Lamrim Scopes:** Gyaltsab Je provides a distinct Lamrim-based structural alignment. He maps the first aspiration ("overcoming sufferings of cyclic existence") to the training of the **medium person** (middle scope, seeking personal liberation), and the second aspiration ("dispelling the unhappiness of all sentient beings") to the training of the **great person** (great scope, seeking universal liberation). This is a unique scholastic classification not explicitly mentioned in Sazang or Thokme's explanations of this verse.
- **Thokme's Fulfilling Wishes Heading:** Thokme Zangpo places this discussion under the structural heading of "Third, fulfilling wishes," treating the verse as a logical proof that bodhicitta fulfills all temporary and ultimate aspirations of practitioners.
- **Sazang's Focus on Intelligence:** Sazang introduces this section under "holding it firmly because it accomplishes one's aspirations," emphasizing that "anyone possessing intelligence" who harbors these wishes must recognize bodhicitta as the indispensable tool. Sazang also explicitly lists the specific sufferings of hell and of the human condition (birth, aging, sickness, death) to ground the abstract terms of the verse in concrete samsaric realities.
