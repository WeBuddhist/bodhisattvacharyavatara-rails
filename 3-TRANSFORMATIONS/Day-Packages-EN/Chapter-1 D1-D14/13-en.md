## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:notification -->
### Notification

**Title:** Catch one flash of ill-will before it grows  
**Detail:** A moment of anger at a good person carries real weight. Today, catch one early and let it go.

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Yesterday's verse showed how much good a bodhisattva does. Today's turns to the other side. Because such a person is so precious, even a single angry thought toward them carries enormous weight. The verse puts it starkly: long ages of suffering for each moment of anger. It is a warning to take our own minds seriously.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

These two verses show the bodhisattva's magnificent generosity, and also how serious it is to feel ill will toward them.

If everyone honours even an ordinary worldly giver who offers just a little food to a few beings, then the bodhisattva is truly the holy one worthy of our homage and honour — for the bodhisattva always gives the highest happiness of the Buddhas to countless beings, until samsara itself is empty.

But if someone feels anger and ill will toward such a great giver, the Buddha said they will suffer in hell for as many ages as there were moments of that ill will. There is a story that makes this clear: long ago a monk, out of jealousy, spoke badly of a bodhisattva. Because of this he was reborn in hell for many ages and went through endless suffering. This shows plainly how serious it is to hold ill will toward others.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, I will feel genuine gratitude for the people who help me, and I will let go of any urge to criticise them.

**Explanation:** Today's verse offers a strong warning: if you hold malicious or bad thoughts toward a Bodhisattva, you will experience suffering for a very long time. By choosing to feel deep thankfulness for those who support you instead of harbouring critical thoughts, you are practising true patience and protecting yourself from harmful karma.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

The bodhisattva's giving is the very opposite of that [ordinary giver of 1-32]: to **beings countless and boundless** in number, for a **long time** — until they reach the heart of enlightenment — with a special benefit that **brings to complete fulfillment every wish held in mind**, namely the very **unsurpassed bliss** of the **Blissful Ones**. Since one who **always gives** in this way is [so] worthy of honor — **what need is there even to speak of it?**

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

In terms of the field, the giving is to beings countless and boundless; in terms of time, for a long time, for as long as space endures; and in terms of the very substance given, it is the specially exalted one — the intention to establish beings, all at once, in the state of the limitless, boundless qualities of the body, speech, and mind of the Blissful Ones. It is endowed with a benefit — a purpose — that brings to complete fulfillment every wish held in mind; carried out through a fine manner that is loving and respectful, and with gentle, pleasing speech. What need, then, is there even to speak of always giving in this way? For one who, from the depths of the heart, wishes to give beings such a state of buddhahood — where every desire is inexhaustibly fulfilled — the benefit that arises is inconceivable; what need is there to mention it?

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^1-33]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The bodhisattvas, in this way, give — not to a lowly object, but to the countless realms of beings, equal to the reach of space; not for a lowly span of time, but for a long time, until samsara is emptied; not a lowly gift, but the unsurpassed bliss of the Blissful Ones; not in a lowly manner, but peacefully and with respect; and not a lowly benefit, but a special one — bringing to complete fulfillment, without exception, whatever each and every being holds in mind. Since bodhisattvas who bestow such a great, unceasing, constant gift are patrons of giving fit to be praised and honored, what need is there even to say so?

Bodhisattvas — persons of good intention whose mind-stream is possessed of bodhicitta — are the patrons of giving for all the beings of the three realms. #### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Point of the Great Patron's Five Distinctions

Verse 1-33 completes the "what need is there even to speak of it?" statement begun in 1-32, showing that the bodhisattva is a great patron of giving. Whereas the small [ordinary] giver is lowly in all five respects, the bodhisattva is specially exalted in all five: the object (beings countless as the reach of space), the time (until the heart of enlightenment), the gift (the unsurpassed bliss of the Blissful Ones), the manner (peaceful and respectful), and the benefit (bringing to complete fulfillment every wish held in mind). KKP's commentary sets out these five distinctions explicitly.

###### 2. The Point of the Four Kinds of Giving (NKW)

NKW explains clearly that the bodhisattva gives, unceasingly, all four kinds of giving (སྦྱིན་པ་རྣམ་པ་བཞི་): the giving of Dharma (ཆོས་), the giving of material goods (ཟང་ཟིང་), the giving of fearlessness / protection (མི་འཇིགས་པའི་སྐྱབས་), and the giving of loving-kindness (བྱམས་པ་) — four in all. These four kinds of giving encompass every form of benefit completely.

###### 3. The Point of the Unsurpassed Bliss of the Blissful Ones

The bodhisattva's gift is by its very nature without equal: it is not ordinary material goods such as food, but the very "unsurpassed bliss of the Blissful Ones" (བདེ་གཤེགས་ཀྱི་བདེ་བ་བླ་ན་མེད་). All four of KS, KKP, SMPLG, and NKW explain this distinction of the gift as the chief point that sets it apart from the ordinary giving of food in 1-32.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse carries forward the "what need is there even to speak of it?" that continues from verse 1-32. It makes the bodhisattva, the great patron of giving, superior (not lowly) in all five respects — object / time / gift / manner / benefit — teaching that they always give, to beings beyond number and until samsara is emptied, the unsurpassed bliss of the Blissful Ones, fulfilling completely every wish held in mind.

**Key points.**
- Object / time / gift / manner / benefit — being superior (not lowly) in all five, the bodhisattva patron surpasses the lowly giver of 1-32.
- NKW explains that the bodhisattva gives all four kinds of giving (Dharma, material goods, fearlessness / protection, loving-kindness) unceasingly.
- In GDR / NTS / SMPLG / MKS, following their shared doctrinal basis, the instruction ends by teaching that one should show respect toward the bodhisattva. —

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

**Whoever**, toward **such heirs of the victors, the masters of giving**, **should give rise to a negative mind** — that is, a mind of evil — **that person**, for **as many eons** as the **number** of moments in which the **negative mind was aroused**, **will dwell in hell, so the Sage has said.** — Here the ground for elaboration is set alongside [the verse], in accordance with the cited scripture.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

As for such a bodhisattva — one who has given rise to such a good heart and works to bring beings to benefit and happiness — what need is there even to speak of it if someone, confused about what to adopt and what to reject, under the sway of attachment and aversion commits an offense against them by body or speech? Even if only a negative mind arises, for as many eons as the number of moments it arose, one will dwell in the boundless states of the lower realms — so the *Praśāntaviniścayaprātihārya Sūtra* says; and for this reason the danger is grave. However, since a person like me — or anyone like me — cannot take the measure of another person, and since we do not know where a bodhisattva may be, we must cultivate pure perception toward everyone. As the Eight Verses of Mind Training by Langri Thangpa says, "Whenever I am in the company of anyone, may I regard myself as the lowest of all"; and as Dromtönpa, the Dharma King, said, "Even if everyone holds you as authoritative, keep the pride that thinks 'I' small" — accordingly, we ourselves must take the low place. If we hold everyone else above us, crowning them at the top of our heads, and take the low place, many faults are averted; we must abide in just such a way. But if one gives rise to a negative mind directed at a bodhisattva, that is a great fault.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^1-34]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

If some foolish being gives rise to a negative mind toward one who has become such a great heir of the victors, a patron of giving — harboring anger or a contemptuous, scornful attitude — then for as many eons as the number of moments in which that negative mind was aroused, that evil-doer will experience and dwell in the hells, such as Avīci, the Hell of Unrelenting Torment. As it is said: "Mañjuśrī, a bodhisattva should put on the armor [of resolve] to dwell in the hells of beings for as many eons as the measure of angry and contemptuous thoughts directed toward a bodhisattva."

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

To disparage such an ultimate heir of the victors, a patron of giving — a being who gives all benefit and happiness — and to wish to harm them, giving rise to a negative mind, is the very worst of misdeeds. If one gives rise to a negative mind, then — as taught in the *Praśāntaviniścayaprātihārya Sūtra*, the Sutra of the Miraculous Ascertainment of Perfect Peace (⚑ Sanskrit title reconstructed from the Tibetan `རབ་ཏུ་ཞི་བ་རྣམ་པར་ངེས་པ་ཆོ་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་མདོ།`; confirm) — for as many eons as the number of moments of that negative-minded thought, one will dwell in the hells; so the Sage has said. One should be careful in this matter. The *Śraddhābalādhānāvatāramudrā Sūtra*, the Sutra of the Seal of Entering the Development of the Power of Faith (⚑ Sanskrit title reconstructed from the Tibetan `དད་པའི་སྟོབས་བསྐྱེད་པ་ལ་འཇུག་པའི་ཕྱག་རྒྱའི་མདོ།`; confirm), teaches that giving rise to malice and the like toward a bodhisattva who aspires to the Great Vehicle produces vastly more countless misdeeds than destroying or burning stupas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges. As proof of this, since it is taught that buddhas are born from bodhisattvas, harming the cause of buddhahood is the very worst of misdeeds — as taught in many scriptures together with their commentaries.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

If anyone gives rise to a negative mind toward such an heir of the victors, a patron of giving, then for as many eons as the number of moments in which that person aroused the negative mind, they will dwell in the hells — so the Sage said in the *Praśāntaviniścayaprātihārya Sūtra*: "Mañjuśrī, a bodhisattva should put on the armor [of resolve] to dwell in the hells of beings for as many eons as the measure of angry and contemptuous thoughts toward a bodhisattva."

But if, on the other hand, someone's mind turns bright and full of glad faith toward such a bodhisattva, the fruit of that far exceeds [the harm of] a negative mind. As the *Niyatāniyatagatimudrāvatāra Sūtra*, the Sutra of the Seal of Entering the Determinate and the Indeterminate (⚑ Sanskrit title reconstructed from the Tibetan `ངེས་པ་དང་མ་ངེས་པར་འཇུག་པའི་ཕྱག་རྒྱའི་མདོ།`; confirm), says: "Mañjuśrī, suppose someone deliberately put out the eyes of all the beings in all the world-realms of the ten directions, and suppose another son or daughter of good family deliberately restored the eyes of all those beings with a mind of loving-kindness. Mañjuśrī, if yet another son or daughter of good family should look upon a bodhisattva who aspires to the Great Vehicle with a mind of faith, this produces merit far greater — countless times greater — than that."

*Note: The NTS section "Third (that [the bodhisattva] is a special field)" (གསུམ་པ་ ཞིང་ཁྱད་པར་ཅན་ཡིན་པ་) covers 1-34 together with the first two lines of 1-35 as a negative/positive pair. Sources: [[2-RAILS/Verses/1-35-summary.md]] [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

If someone gives rise to a negative mind toward the bodhisattva, the heir of the victors who has become such a great patron of giving, then for as many eons as the number of moments of that negative mind, they will dwell in the hells — as the *Praśāntaviniścayaprātihārya Sūtra* says: "Mañjuśrī, for as long as a bodhisattva gives rise to a mind of anger and contempt toward [another] bodhisattva, for that long he should put on the armor [of resolve], thinking, 'By this I shall dwell in the hells for eons.'"

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

For a foolish being who gives rise to a negative mind toward such an heir of the victors, a patron of giving, for as many eons as the number of moments [of that mind], that evil-doer will experience and dwell in the hells, such as Avīci. This is taught in the *Praśāntaviniścayaprātihārya Sūtra*; and the Sutra of Entering the Development of the Power of Faith also teaches that harming a bodhisattva, who is the basis [of buddhahood], produces vastly more misdeeds — for since the bodhisattva is the cause of buddhahood, [harming them] is like harming a buddha.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

This shows the reason why it is unfitting to feel contempt or anger toward those heirs of the victors, the bodhisattvas, who have become the patrons of giving for all the beings of the three realms. If a person of wrong conduct gives rise to a negative mind — such as harboring anger, or a contemptuous, scornful attitude — toward one who has become such a great patron of giving, one who at all times and in every way satisfies all beings with food, clothing, bedding, provisions, and so on, or through the giving of Dharma and material goods, then — as the Teacher, the Lord of Sages, ascertained in the *Praśāntaviniścayaprātihārya Sūtra* — for each and every moment in which the negative mind was aroused, from the next life onward, that person will be born in the hell realms for eon after eon and will experience the sufferings of heat and cold. Therefore the meaning is: it is unfitting, at any time or in any way, to let an evil thought of anger or contempt arise toward that very bodhisattva, the great patron of giving for all beings of the three realms.

#### Stories and Illustrations

<!-- story:BCACXX_WR_bo -->
##### BCACXX_WR_bo — The Monk's Story

A story given to illustrate this verse's line, **"they will dwell in hell, so the Sage has said."** When Maudgalyāyana went to the hell realms, he saw a being undergoing fierce suffering upon a burning iron ground, and asked the Teacher the reason. In a past age, at a time when [a former buddha's] way had not yet declined, a monk who knew the Three Baskets (Tripiṭaka) had become the officiating priest in a king's palace. When an arhat monk who had mastered the Three Baskets arrived, together with a retinue of five hundred, and all the offerings and honor came to be directed to him, the former monk, out of jealousy, disparaged the arhat, saying he was loose in his discipline. Because of that deed, from that time until now the being has been reborn again and again, from one hell into another. It was prophesied that in the future, in the time of the Buddha Lama (⚑ buddha name uncertain — Tibetan `སངས་རྒྱས་བླ་མ`; confirm), he would attain a human body, go forth into ordination, and though he would attain arhatship, he would still undergo much disparagement, and in the end, out of weariness [with samsara], would pass into nirvana without remainder.

#### Scriptural Quotations

> "Mañjuśrī, for as long as a bodhisattva gives rise to a mind of anger and contempt toward [another] bodhisattva, for that long he should put on the armor [of resolve], thinking, 'By this I shall dwell in the hells for eons.'"
> — From the *Praśāntaviniścayaprātihārya Sūtra*. This is the scriptural source directly cited by the Sage — the root scriptural basis of this verse.

> From the *Śraddhābalādhānāvatāramudrā Sūtra*, the Sutra of the Seal of Entering the Development of the Power of Faith: it is taught that giving rise to malice and the like toward a bodhisattva who aspires to the Great Vehicle produces far more countless misdeeds than destroying or burning stupas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges.
> — A scripture cited by Gyaltsab (GDR), showing that harming a bodhisattva is a far greater misdeed even than destroying stupas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges.

> "Whenever I am in the company of anyone, may I regard myself as the lowest of all."
> — The Eight Verses of Mind Training by Langri Thangpa. > "Even if everyone holds you as authoritative, keep the pride that thinks 'I' small."
> — Dromtönpa, the Dharma King. An instruction that, even if everyone holds you as an authority, one should keep one's pride small — cited by His Holiness the Dalai Lama, alongside the mind-training verse, as a safeguard.

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. The Point of Measuring Each Moment by an Eon

This verse definitively shows a result in which each single moment of a negative mind aroused toward a bodhisattva equals one eon in the hells. This is not a mere estimate or a rhetorical warning, but a profound point in which the number of moments equals the number of eons — established as a scriptural fact by all the commentaries (KS, KKP, GDR, NTS, SMPLG, MKS, NKW), which directly cite the *Praśāntaviniścayaprātihārya Sūtra*.

###### 2. The Point of the Ground — the Heir of the Victors, the Patron of Giving

What all the commentaries teach as the chief point is that the reason the fault of anger here is so extremely great depends on its ground: it is directed at a bodhisattva who has become the great patron of giving for all beings of the three realms. NKW distinguishes three kinds of wrong attitude — anger, contempt, and wrong faith. GDR too cites the Sutra of [Developing] Faith, [showing] that harming a bodhisattva is a far greater misdeed than destroying stupas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges.

###### 3. The Point That NTS Pairs [1-34] With Its Opposite

NTS (and KS likewise) explains verse 1-34 together with the positive aspect at the start of 1-35 ("if one's mind turns bright and glad"), under a single outline heading, "that [the bodhisattva] is a special field," presenting the good and the bad as a linked pair: toward that very special ground, giving rise to a negative mind brings an extremely great fault, while giving rise to faith and respect brings extremely great merit.

###### 4. The Point That One Must Cultivate Pure Perception (His Holiness the Dalai Lama)

Since we cannot know for certain whether bodhicitta is present in any given being's mind-stream (we do not know where a bodhisattva may be), TG draws out the practical upshot that we must cultivate pure perception toward everyone. Citing the Eight Verses of Mind Training by Langri Thangpa and the words of Dromtönpa, he explains that taking the lowest place oneself is a safeguard against generating, in advance, any contemptuous attitude toward a hidden bodhisattva.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse shows how the misdeed incurred by giving rise to a negative mind toward an heir of the victors, a patron of giving, mounts up at the rate of one eon for each moment. Because of the greatness of the ground, it teaches that when one commits a fault against that ground, the magnitude of the result is not to be taken lightly. It draws its scriptural authority from the *Praśāntaviniścayaprātihārya Sūtra*.

**Key points.**
- One eon of result for each moment — the point that the number of eons of result matches exactly the number of negative-minded moments.
- The ground — the heir of the victors, the patron of giving — is the patron for all beings of the three realms, and so the emphasis falls on the greatness of the ground.
- GDR draws on the Sutra of [Developing] Faith to show that giving rise to a negative mind toward a bodhisattva is a far greater misdeed than destroying stupas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains that, since we do not know where a bodhisattva may be, we must cultivate pure perception toward everyone — citing the Eight Verses of Mind Training by Langri Thangpa and the instruction of Dromtönpa.
