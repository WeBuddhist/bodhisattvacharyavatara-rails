## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the two verses 30 and 31 from the third chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. Just as a cool moon rises in the mind and clears away its fever, let us look closely today at these words.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

When a lot of milk is churned again and again, butter comes from it. When that butter is melted, the essence of butter is drawn out, the purest part of what was already pure.

It is the same with the great ocean of the Buddha's holy teaching. If we churn it again and again with the churning stick of the wisdom of hearing and reflecting, then bodhicitta comes out of it. Bodhicitta is the finest refined essence of the heart of the Dharma.

This mind holds the power to produce all benefit and happiness, and it holds the finest taste. So we should listen to the profound Dharma and reflect on it again and again. Once we have found bodhicitta itself, we should hold it always with great joy.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when anger arises in me, I will calm it with cool loving-kindness.

**Explanation:** *(Taming the mind)* Bodhicitta is like the moon that clears away the fever of the afflictions in our own mind. When anger or desire arises, if we meditate on loving-kindness, the burning of the mind is calmed. In that way we make our own mind peaceful and tame.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Beings are called "wandering beings" because they keep going on from one birth and death to the next along the road of samsaric existence, the fearful round of birth and death.

And what is like the common platform, or the bridge, that delivers them from the lower realms and the states of misery is bodhicitta too.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-30]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

To all those beings who are persons of small capacity, bodhisattvas teach the virtuous path that accords with merit — what is to be taken up and what left aside, virtue and wrongdoing. In this way they deliver them from the place of the three lower realms and set them in, and keep them in, the happy result of the higher realms as gods and humans. That is why it is called a "common platform," like a stepping-block. And since it delivers beings from the lower realms it is a common platform; and since all sentient beings, if they are to be established in the definite goodness of the three vehicles, must first be established in high status, it is also called the platform of the path shared by all.

Having in this way established beings in high status and in the definite goodness of the lesser vehicle, bodhisattvas then teach the Dharma of the Great Vehicle to those of the Mahayana family, and in the end to all of them together — for establishing beings in the Great Vehicle is the ultimate intention of the bodhisattvas. And so this supreme state of mind, bodhicitta, which clears away without exception the burning heat of the afflictive obscurations — attachment and the rest — that are the chief thing standing in the way of liberation in the minds of limitless beings, is like the rising of a moon that is cool by nature. As for the afflictive obscurations here, Lord Maitreya says: "Whatever conceptual thoughts there are of miserliness and the rest — those are held to be the afflictive obscurations." That is, they are all the coarse factors incompatible with the six perfections.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-30]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

The common platform that delivers all beings from the lower realms — the bridge for everyone — is this generation of the mind too.

Setting out the second part under two headings, clearing away the afflictive obscurations and clearing away the cognitive obscurations, he explains: this generation of the mind is the moon of the mind that has risen and clears away the burning torment of beings' afflictions, because it is able to destroy the afflictive obscurations entirely.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-30]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

It is also what has become like the common — that is, the shared — platform or bridge that delivers all beings from the lower realms; and it is the moon of the mind that has risen and clears away the torment of beings' afflictive obscurations.

*(Note: this commentary's prose treats this verse's two similes together with the neighbouring similes of the elixir, treasure, medicine, tree, sun, butter-quintessence and wishing-tree of bodhicitta in one continuous run — only the platform and moon material is drawn on here as pertaining to 3-30.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-30]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Because it delivers all those beings who act wrongly from the river, or the swamp, of falling wrongly into the lower realms and the states of misery, what has become like the common platform or bridge is this bodhicitta too.

*(Under the heading "generating joy at the purification of the two obscurations.")* Because it clears away the whole burning torment of the afflictive obscurations, which stand in the way of every being's attaining liberation, this bodhicitta is like the rising of the moon with its cool light.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-30]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

All those beings who pile up the great wrongdoing that is the cause hurling them into the lower realms — because it delivers them from the great lake, or the thick swamp, of falling wrongly into the lower realms and the states of misery, and brings them to the dry land of the higher realms and of liberation, what has become the common platform, the platform or bridge shared by everyone alike, good and bad, high and low, is this bodhicitta too.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-30]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Whoever a being may be — high, low or in between, old or young, man or woman — if they all take up the practice of generating bodhicitta they can be delivered from the lower realms. So what delivers them from the lower migrations, the hells and the rest, and brings them to the higher realms and to liberation — the common platform, a supporting thing like a stepping-stool — is this very generation of bodhicitta.

And the moon of the mind that has risen, which clears away or removes the burning torment of beings' afflictions, desire and the rest, is this too. The moon out there in the sky is made of the water-crystal jewel, and by the power of that it is cool to the touch and so dispels heat; but the comparison with it holds only in a partial way. For unlike the moon in the outer sky, the generation of bodhicitta is like a moon that has risen from within the mind itself, and since it is able to clear away all the burning heat of the afflictions present in the mind-stream, it is also a moon that has risen from the very centre of the mind. So he says.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^3-30]]

#### Metaphors and Examples

- **The common platform / bridge** (སྤྱི་སྟེགས) → bodhicitta, which liberates all beings from the lower realms and establishes them in higher rebirth and liberation. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains it as a platform like a stepping-block: it delivers beings from the place of the three lower realms and sets them in the happy result of the higher realms as gods and humans, and in the end it is also called the platform of the common path, which establishes those of the Mahayana family in the Great Vehicle. Sabzang explains it as the bridge that delivers beings from the river or the swamp of falling into the lower realms. Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains it as the platform or bridge shared by everyone alike, good and bad, high and low, which delivers beings from the great lake or thick swamp of falling into the lower realms and brings them to the dry land of the higher realms and of liberation. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains it as a supporting thing, like a stepping-stool: whoever practises it — high, low or in between, old or young, man or woman — can be delivered from the lower realms by it. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and His Holiness the Dalai Lama both explain it as a bridge.
- **The moon of the mind** (སེམས་ཀྱི་ཟླ་བ) → bodhicitta, which dispels the burning heat of the afflictive obscurations. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains it as the rising of a moon that is cool by nature, and identifies the afflictive obscurations as all the coarse factors incompatible with the six perfections. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen explains that it is the moon rising because it is able to destroy the afflictive obscurations entirely. Sabzang explains that, because it clears away the whole burning torment of the afflictive obscurations that stand in the way of attaining liberation, it is the rising of the moon with its cool light. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that although the moon in the outer sky, being made of the water-crystal jewel, dispels heat by the power of its coolness to the touch, that is only a partial comparison; unlike the outer moon, bodhicitta is a moon that rises from within the mind itself, and so, as he explains in particular, it is able to clear away all the burning heat of the afflictions present in the mind-stream.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Bodhicitta Is Shown Here by Two Images: The Common Platform and the Moon

This verse applies two images to bodhicitta — the common platform that delivers all beings from the lower realms, and the moon of the mind that has risen and clears away the torment of beings' afflictions. All the commentaries agree on this in the end.

###### 2. The Moon Here Shows the Clearing Away of the Afflictive Obscurations, While the Next Verse Is Applied to the Cognitive Obscurations

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen divides the passage into two headings, clearing away the afflictive obscurations and clearing away the cognitive obscurations, and explains the moon here as being able to destroy the afflictive obscurations entirely. Sabzang Mati Panchen, under the heading of generating joy at the purification of the two obscurations, likewise explains this verse as clearing away the burning torment of the afflictive obscurations. The two appear to agree.

###### 3. The Image of the Common Platform Covers Everything from the Path of the Person of Small Capacity Up to Establishing Beings in the Great Vehicle

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains that "common platform" means first delivering beings from the lower realms and setting them in the happy result of high status, and in the end also being the platform of the common path that establishes those of the Mahayana family in the Great Vehicle. This, he says, is the ultimate intention of the bodhisattvas.

###### 4. The Image of the Common Platform Is Shared by Everyone Alike

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that whoever a being may be — high, low or in between, old or young, man or woman — if they take up the practice of generating bodhicitta they can be delivered from the lower realms.

###### 5. The Fall into the Lower Realms Is Described as a River or a Swamp, and Bodhicitta Brings Beings to Dry Land

Sabzang Mati Panchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both describe the state of falling into the lower realms as a river or a thick swamp, and explain that bodhicitta delivers beings from it to the dry land of the higher realms and of liberation.

###### 6. The Cool Moon of the Outer Sky Is Only a Partial Comparison; the Moon That Rises Within the Mind Is Superior

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that the outer moon is cool only by the power of being made of the water-crystal jewel, whereas the generation of bodhicitta is a moon that rises from within the mind itself, and so is able to clear away all the burning heat of the afflictions present in the mind-stream.

###### 7. "Afflictive Obscurations" Is Established from Lord Maitreya's Words as All the Coarse Factors Incompatible with the Six Perfections

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden cites Lord Maitreya's words and explains that whatever conceptual thoughts there are of miserliness and the rest are the afflictive obscurations.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse applies two images to bodhicitta: it is the common platform that delivers all beings from the lower realms, and it is the moon of the mind that has risen and clears away the burning torment of beings' afflictions. All the commentaries are agreed on this basic reading.

**Key points.**

- The image of the common platform shows bodhicitta delivering beings from the river or swamp of the three lower realms and setting them on the dry land of the higher realms and of liberation; and it is shared by everyone alike.
- The image of the common platform reaches from the graded path of the person of small capacity all the way to establishing those of the Mahayana family in the Great Vehicle — which is the ultimate intention of the bodhisattvas.
- The moon here shows the clearing away of the afflictive obscurations, while the following verse is applied to the clearing away of the cognitive obscurations; Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Sabzang Mati Panchen appear to agree on this division.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that the cool moon of the outer sky is only a partial comparison, whereas the moon that rises from within the mind itself is superior, since it can clear away all the burning heat of the afflictions present in the mind-stream.
- Khenpo Kunzang Pelden establishes from Lord Maitreya's words that "the afflictive obscurations" means all the coarse factors incompatible with the six perfections.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

So it is that what is like the moon of the mind clearing away the burning torment of the afflictions is precious bodhicitta.

And the great sun that utterly draws out — that finally removes — the cataracts of not knowing in the minds of beings, all the stains of the cognitive obscurations that stand in the way of knowing what is to be known, is this bodhicitta too.

And what is like the butter-essence — the way the vital essence of the heart of it all is gathered together out of the sacred Dharma taught by the Blessed One, that Dharma which is able to accomplish every benefit and happiness and is as vast and boundless as an ocean of milk — is this bodhicitta as well.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^3-31]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

This very bodhicitta uproots from the minds of beings all the cognitive obscurations — the cataracts of not knowing the suchness of things, which are the chief thing standing in the way of omniscience, the knowledge of all aspects. So it is like the great sun that clears away the darkness of a whole three-thousandfold universe all at once. As for the cognitive obscurations, Lord Maitreya says of them: "Whatever conceptualising there is of the three spheres — that is held to be the cognitive obscuration." That is, if one clings to the three spheres as truly existent and focuses on a single object, one is caught there and cannot see all the other knowable things at once; and for that reason it is held to be an obscuration.

And this bodhicitta, the refined quintessence of the heart of the sacred Dharma of the Buddha's word, the three turnings of the wheel, is like what is drawn from churning a great deal of milk — first the essence, butter, and then what is called the butter-essence, the essence of the essence, drawn out by melting the butter down. It has thus become supreme.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^3-31]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

It is also the great sun that has risen and uproots from the very base beings' not knowing that is not afflicted — the cataracts of the cognitive obscurations — because the wisdom that realises emptiness, adorned by the vast accumulations, exhausts the seeds of the cognitive obscurations.

And this generation of the mind is the essence drawn out when the great ocean of milk that is the sacred Dharma of the scriptures has been well churned with the churning stick of the wisdom of hearing and reflecting. Since it is the butter-essence that has been drawn out, endowed with the taste and the power to produce every benefit, everyone should make an effort at it.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^3-31]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

It is the great sun that utterly draws out beings' not knowing — that is, the cataracts of the cognitive obscurations. And it is the butter-essence drawn out from churning all the milk of the sacred Dharma.

*(Note: this commentary's prose treats this verse's two similes together with the neighbouring similes of the bridge and moon of 3-30 and the guest-satisfaction of 3-32 in one continuous run — only the sun-and-butter material pertaining to 3-31 is drawn on here.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^3-31]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Because it uproots from the very depths and clears away all the cognitive obscurations of every being without exception — the not knowing, the delusion that is like a cataract, like darkness, which stands in the way of attaining omniscience — this bodhicitta is like a great sun freed from the clouds.

And just as the essence of butter is obtained by churning a great deal of milk, so, from churning and gathering together what is within the vast sacred Dharma that is like milk, this profound and ultimate bodhicitta has been found, like the butter-essence drawn out, as the supreme essence. Therefore it is right to hold it with joy.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^3-31]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Because it clears away all the burning torment of the afflictive obscurations, which stand in the way of beings attaining even mere liberation for their own sake, this moon of bodhicitta with its cool rays has risen in one's own mind-stream. And because it uproots — draws out from the very depths — the cataracts of the darkness of the cognitive obscurations, the unafflicted kind of not knowing that stands in the way of attaining omniscience for the sake of others, this great sun of bodhicitta has risen in one's own mind-stream. That is so because the wisdom that realises subtle suchness, held by the vast side of method — bodhicitta and the rest — is able to abandon the seeds of the cognitive obscurations.

Just as the butter-essence, endowed with the taste and the power to help certain illnesses, is drawn out by churning well, with a churning stick like the wisdom of hearing and reflecting, the great ocean of milk that is the scriptures of the sacred Dharma gathered in the three baskets — so this very bodhicitta is the essence drawn out, which has become still more essential than the very heart of the sacred Dharma. It calms all the illnesses of karma and afflictions in every being, and it is endowed with the perfect potency to satisfy them by giving them every benefit and happiness. Since we have found it this time, we should give rise to joy and make an effort at the means of guarding it and increasing it.

*(Note: this commentary quotes the Gaṇḍavyūha's list of bodhicitta-similes between its treatment of the sun and the butter-essence, and its explanation of the butter-essence continues, without a new heading, into the same prose run as its explanation of ^3-32's guest-simile — only the material pertaining to 3-31 is drawn on here.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^3-31]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

The sun outside, dwelling in the sky, clears away only the darkness that is outside; it cannot clear away the darkness within. But what uproots — draws out from the very root — and clears away all the cataracts, the darkness, of the delusion and ignorance in the minds of beings who do not know the points of what is to be taken up and what left aside, is a sun unlike the ordinary sun of the sky. It is "the great sun," the sun that clears away the darkness of the mind. That is the meaning.

And as for the words "from the churning of the sacred Dharma — the scriptures, the baskets of the perfect Buddha's word — carried out in the proper way by the wisdom of hearing, reflecting and meditating": just as milk is churned by a churning stick, so, with the pure discipline of a Dharma person as the vessel, the wisdom of hearing, reflecting and meditating does the churning. Relying on taking it to heart again and again, the wisdom of the path of seeing, which sees the true nature of things directly, and the rest, arises in one's mind-stream. That is this very generation of bodhicitta, which is like the butter-essence drawn out.

##### ⚑ Divergences (where the commentaries differ)

- **Khenpo Kunzang Pelden, Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Ngulchu Thogme, Sabzang Mati Panchen, Minyak Kunzang Sonam and His Holiness the Dalai Lama:** they all name the darkness dispelled by the image of the sun explicitly as the cognitive obscurations (ཤེས་སྒྲིབ, jñeyāvaraṇa) — completing the pair with the moon image of 3-30, which they associate with the afflictive obscurations (ཉོན་སྒྲིབ, kleśāvaraṇa). ⚑

#### Metaphors and Examples

- **The great sun** (ཉི་མ་ཆེན་པོ) → bodhicitta, which uproots from beings' minds the cataract-like darkness of not knowing (the cognitive obscurations, or basic unknowing). Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains it as the great sun that clears away the darkness of a whole three-thousandfold universe all at once, and establishes the cognitive obscurations as the conceptualising of the three spheres. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen explains it as the great sun that uproots the cataracts of the cognitive obscurations from the very base. Sabzang explains that, since it clears away the darkness of the cognitive obscurations, it is like a great sun freed from the clouds. Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains it as the great sun that clears away the cataracts of the darkness of the cognitive obscurations, which stand in the way of attaining omniscience for the sake of others. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains it as a sun of a special kind: unlike the sun in the outer sky, which cannot clear away the darkness within, this one can clear away the darkness of the mind. His Holiness the Dalai Lama likewise explains it as the great sun that utterly draws out all the stains of the cognitive obscurations.
- **The butter-essence** (མར་གྱི་ཉིང་ཁུ) → bodhicitta as the ultimate quintessence drawn out by churning the milk of the sacred Dharma with the wisdom of hearing and reflecting. Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains it as the refined quintessence of the heart of the Buddha's word, the three turnings of the wheel — like the essence of the essence drawn out from churned milk. Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen explains it as the essence drawn out when the great ocean of milk that is the sacred Dharma of the scriptures is churned with the churning stick of the wisdom of hearing and reflecting. Sabzang explains that, since the profound and ultimate supreme essence is found from churning and gathering together what is within the vast sacred Dharma that is like milk, it is right to hold it with joy. Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that from churning the great ocean of milk that is the scriptures of the sacred Dharma in the three baskets, with the wisdom of hearing and reflecting, the essence drawn out is still more essential than the very heart of the sacred Dharma. Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that when the baskets are churned by the wisdom of hearing, reflecting and meditating, the wisdom of the path of seeing arising in one's mind-stream is the generation of bodhicitta that is like the butter-essence drawn out.

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Bodhicitta Is Shown Here by Two Images: The Great Sun and the Butter-Essence

This verse applies two images to bodhicitta — the great sun that utterly draws out beings' cataracts of not knowing, and the butter-essence that has come from churning the milk of the sacred Dharma. All the commentaries agree on this in the end.

###### 2. The Image of the Sun Is Applied to the Clearing Away of the Cognitive Obscurations

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen explains it as the great sun because the wisdom realising emptiness, adorned by the vast accumulations, exhausts the seeds of the cognitive obscurations. This is the division that pairs it with the moon of 3-30, which shows the clearing away of the afflictive obscurations.

###### 3. "Cognitive Obscurations" Is Established from Lord Maitreya's Words as the Conceptualising of the Three Spheres

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden cites Lord Maitreya's words and explains it as the obscuration by which, clinging to the three spheres as truly existent and focusing on a single object, one cannot see all the other knowable things at once.

###### 4. The Image of the Butter-Essence Shows That Bodhicitta Is the Supreme Essence, Finer Even Than the Vital Essence of the Sacred Dharma

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden explains it as the refined quintessence of the heart of the three turnings of the wheel of the Buddha's word. Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains it as still more essential than the very heart of the sacred Dharma. Sabzang explains that, since the profound and ultimate supreme essence has been found, it is right to hold it with joy.

###### 5. The Butter-Essence Comes About as the Fruit of Churning the Sacred Dharma with the Wisdom of Hearing, Reflecting and Meditating

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that when the milk of the sacred Dharma is churned again and again with a churning stick like the wisdom of hearing, reflecting and meditating, the wisdom of the path of seeing arises in one's mind-stream, and that this is the generation of bodhicitta which is like the butter-essence drawn out.

###### 6. Unlike the Outer Sun, Which Cannot Clear Away the Darkness Within, Bodhicitta Clears Away the Darkness of the Mind Itself

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that although the sun in the outer sky can clear away only outer darkness and cannot clear away the darkness within, bodhicitta is called the great sun in a special sense, because it is able to clear away the very darkness of not knowing what to take up and what to leave aside that is present in the mind-stream. ⚑

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse applies two images to bodhicitta: the great sun that utterly draws out beings' cataracts of not knowing, and the butter-essence that has come from churning the milk of the sacred Dharma. Most of the commentaries apply the image of the sun to the clearing away of the cognitive obscurations, pairing it with the moon of 3-30, which clears away the afflictive obscurations, and they agree on this as the basic reading.

**Key points.**

- The image of the great sun shows the uprooting of the cataracts of the cognitive obscurations, and the image of the butter-essence shows bodhicitta as the supreme essence, finer even than the heart of the sacred Dharma.
- Khenpo Kunzang Pelden establishes from Lord Maitreya's words that "the cognitive obscurations" means the conceptualising of the three spheres.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that the butter-essence is the wisdom of the path of seeing arising in one's mind-stream as the fruit of churning the sacred Dharma again and again with the wisdom of hearing, reflecting and meditating.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the Gaṇḍavyūha, where bodhicitta is shown by many images — nectar, an inexhaustible treasure and the rest.
- ⚑ Most of the commentaries say the darkness dispelled by the sun is the cognitive obscurations, but Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains it only as the darkness of the delusion of not knowing what to take up and what to leave aside. There is a difference here.
