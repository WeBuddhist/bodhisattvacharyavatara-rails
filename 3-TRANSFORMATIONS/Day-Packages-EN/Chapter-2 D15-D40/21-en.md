## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on verses 16 and 17 from the second chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. It invites us to use the power of our mind to offer clouds of sweet-smelling incense that fill the sky, delicious heavenly food, radiant jewel lamps that conquer darkness, and fragrant, flower-strewn floors to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas. This practice teaches us how to take ordinary experiences — like seeing a light or smelling a fragrance — and turn them into a powerful practice of generosity and mental offering.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

These verses explain four special types of mental offering: incense, divine food, lamps, and beautiful grounds. Master Shantideva imagines burning the finest incense, like pure sandalwood, creating thick, fragrant clouds that billow across the entire sky. He also presents a rich variety of heavenly foods and drinks — delightful, divine meals fit for the gods — offering them with deep devotion to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas.

Next, he imagines arranging radiant jewel lamps inside beautiful, golden, lotus-shaped bowls to light up and scatter the darkness of the world. Finally, he visualizes a smooth, even ground painted with sweet-scented sandalwood and camphor water, beautifully scattering fresh, delightful flowers all over it as an offering to the holy beings.

Spiritual teachers explain that each of these mental offerings brings wonderful spiritual benefits. Offering sweet incense plants the seed for all beings to attain the pleasant fragrance of good moral conduct. Offering divine food nourishes our spiritual capacity for deep meditation. Offering bright lamps clears away the mental darkness of ignorance, bringing the bright light of profound wisdom. And offering smooth, fragrant ground helps our hearts become as stable, strong, and reliable as the earth itself in practicing compassion.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when I see electric lights or lamps at night, I will offer them in my mind to the Three Jewels as lamps of wisdom taking away confusion.

**Explanation:** *(Generosity)* Usually, when we turn on lights at night, see street lamps, or look at the stars, we just see them as normal things to make the dark bright. Today, following the teachings, when I see any light shining in the dark, I will picture it as a very bright "jewel lamp" sitting in a golden bowl. I will happily offer this light in my mind to the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas. As I make this offering, I will wish that its bright light clears away all the fog and confusion in my mind and in the minds of all living beings, replacing it with the bright, clear light of wisdom and understanding.

To the supreme fields of offering, the Lords of Sages together with their heirs, I also make offering with pleasing flowers and garlands — these being offered chiefly for the flourishing of the faculty of the eye. ##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Within the Mind-Emanated Offering — the Offering of Incense and the Offering of Food

All the commentaries agree that, after the flower-offering, this verse presents two: the offering of incense and the offering of food.

###### 2. The Offering of Incense — Banks of Sweet-Scented Smoke Pervading in All Directions

It is explained that the finest incense, such as sandalwood and aloeswood, is burned so that its smoke masses like banks of cloud and pervades everywhere, and is offered respectfully to the victors together with their heirs.

###### 3. The Offering of Food — Divine Offering-Cakes Endowed With a Hundred Flavors

All the commentaries explain in one voice that things to eat, to chew, and to drink, perfect in color, fragrance and flavor and of many kinds — divine offering-cakes formed of divine substances — are offered to the fields of offering.

###### 4. Aspiration-Prayers Linked to Each Offering — the Sweet Fragrance of Discipline and the Food of Meditative Concentration

Kunzang Pelden, Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk, and Minyak Kunzang Sonam all pray that, by the merit of offering incense, the sweet fragrance of the discipline that delights the victors may arise in the mind-streams of all beings, and that, by the merit of offering food, they may gain the supreme food of meditative concentration; the other commentaries do not make the aspiration-prayer explicit here.

###### 5. The Division of Offerings by Faculty — Incense Offered for the Faculty of the Nose

Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso distinguishes by faculty, explaining that flowers are offered chiefly for the flourishing of the faculty of the eye, and incense for the flourishing of the faculty of the nose.

###### 6. The Meaning of "Divine Offering-Cake" (Lha-Shö) — Food Fit to Be Presented to a King, or to a Great One

Kunzang Pelden glosses "god" as "king," so "food fit to be eaten in the presence of a king"; while Ngulchu Thogme and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk gloss "god" as "great or excellent," so "an offering-cake fit to be presented upward" — both agreeing on the sense of food fit to be offered to a supreme field of offering.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse presents two offerings within the mind-emanated offering: the offering of incense — banks of sweet-scented smoke, from the finest incense such as sandalwood and aloeswood, pervading in all directions — and the offering of food — divine offering-cakes endowed with a hundred flavors, of many kinds. Both are offered to the fields of offering, the buddhas and bodhisattvas. All the commentaries explain it in agreement.

**Key points.**

- After the flower-offering, this verse presents the offering of incense and the offering of food.
- The incense is the finest, such as sandalwood and aloeswood, burned so that its smoke masses like banks of cloud and pervades in all directions.
- The food is divine offering-cakes, perfect in color, fragrance and flavor and endowed with a hundred flavors.
- ⚑ Kunzang Pelden, Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk, and Minyak Kunzang Sonam all link aspiration-prayers to each offering — the sweet fragrance of discipline and the food of meditative concentration — while the other commentaries do not make the aspiration-prayer explicit here.
- ⚑ Gyalwa Tenzin Gyatso divides the offerings by faculty (flowers for the eye, incense for the nose), a framework the other commentaries do not set out directly.

##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

(The offering of light:) the many golden vessels formed of the nature of gold, beautiful in shape like a lotus with open petals, arranged in rows as a garland; within or upon them, lamps formed of various precious wish-fulfilling gems of great value, so bright and radiant with light that at all times, day and night, they pervade every direction with great illumination. ##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Lotuses of the nature of gold — that is, having the form or shape of a lotus, their petals utterly well-formed, clean and pleasing — arranged one after another in rows; set out well, and within them the jeweled lamps, the pleasing lights of a precious nature. By the merit of offering such a ground, one should pray, "may the earth-like generation of bodhicitta arise in the mind-streams of oneself and other beings."

#### Metaphors and Examples

- **The lamp-bowl as the billionfold universe, the wick as Mount Meru, the oil as a great ocean** (ཀོང་བུ་སྟོང་གསུམ། སྡོང་བུ་རི་རབ། མར་ཁུ་རྒྱ་མཚོ་ཆེན་པོ) → the vast measure of offering a lamp in its every part. — Kunzang Pelden explains emanating and offering the lamp with the mind: its bowl as the great billionfold world-system, its wick as Mount Meru, king of mountains, and its oil as a great ocean, its five-colored light pervading all the realms of space.
- **Flower-heaps arrayed as Mount Meru, the four continents, the sun and moon** (མེ་ཏོག་གི་ཚོམ་བུ་རི་རབ་གླིང་བཞི་ཉི་ཟླར་བཀོད་པ) → resembling the offering of a buddha-field. — Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that if the flower-heaps are arrayed in the form of Mount Meru, the four continents, and the sun and moon, it becomes like the maṇḍala offering of a buddha-field — "the ground anointed with scented water and strewn with flowers; adorned with Mount Meru, the four continents, the sun and moon."

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Within the Mind-Emanated Offering — the Offering of Light (Lamps) and the Offering of the Arrayed Ground

All the commentaries agree that, after the offering of food, this verse presents two offerings: the offering of light and the offering of the arrayed ground; Sabzang Mati identifies them as the eighth and ninth in the fixed count.

###### 2. The Offering of Light — Precious Lamps Offered Within Golden Lotuses Arranged in Rows

All the commentaries explain in one voice that priceless lamps of a precious nature, which dispel the darkness of the world, are placed within lotus-shaped golden vessels arranged in rows and offered.

###### 3. The Additional Gloss on the Lamp — the Sun, Moon, Stars and Other Luminous Things Are Gathered in as Well

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and Minyak Kunzang Sonam, resting on the word "too" in "the lamps too," explain that the great light-makers such as the sun and moon, and other luminous things, are also offered to the fields of offering.

###### 4. The Offering of the Arrayed Ground — Scattering Flowers as Loose Blossoms Upon Incense-Anointed Ground

Many commentaries explain that, upon the pleasing ground anointed with the scent of sandalwood, saffron, camphor and the like, one scatters many stemless flower-blossoms knee-deep and offers this to the Three Jewels, or to the buddhas and bodhisattvas.

###### 5. Aspiration-Prayers Linked to Each Offering

Kunzang Pelden and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explain that, by the merit of offering the lamp, one prays that the light of the all-knowing wisdom (the pure eye of wisdom) may be attained; and by the merit of offering the ground, one prays that the earth-like generation of bodhicitta may be attained.

###### 6. If the Flower-Heaps Are Arrayed as Mount Meru and the Four Continents, It Resembles the Offering of a Buddha-Field

Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that arraying the flower-heaps in the form of Mount Meru, the four continents, and the sun and moon is like the maṇḍala offering of a buddha-field.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse presents two offerings within the mind-emanated offering: the offering of light — precious lamps that dispel the world's darkness, offered within golden lotuses arranged in rows — and the offering of the arrayed ground — scattering pleasing flowers as loose blossoms upon ground anointed with sweet fragrance.

**Key points.**

- For the offering of light, precious lamps of a jewel nature that dispel the world's darkness are offered within lotus-shaped golden vessels arranged in rows; all the commentaries explain this in one voice.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk and Minyak Kunzang Sonam explain that the sun, moon and other great light-makers are also offered to the fields of offering.
- For the offering of the arrayed ground, one scatters many flower-blossoms upon ground anointed with incense and offers it to the Three Jewels.
- Kunzang Pelden and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk link aspiration-prayers to each offering: the light of all-knowing wisdom by the lamp, and the earth-like generation of bodhicitta by the ground.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that arraying the flower-heaps as Mount Meru and the four continents is like the offering of a buddha-field.
