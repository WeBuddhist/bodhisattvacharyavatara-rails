# Day 1 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: The Benefits of Bodhicitta — Verses 1-1 to 1-3

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> 
> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

#### **2. Bodhicitta (སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)**
> ཇི་ལྟར་སྔོན་གྱི་བདེ་གཤེགས་ཀྱིས། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་ཐུགས་ནི་བསྐྱེད་པ་དང་། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་བསླབ་པ་ལ། །
> དེ་དག་རིམ་བཞིན་གནས་པ་ལྟར། །
> 
> དེ་བཞིན་འགྲོ་ལ་ཕན་དོན་དུ། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ནི་བསྐྱེད་བགྱི་ཞིང་། །
> དེ་བཞིན་དུ་ནི་བསླབ་པ་ལའང་། །
> རིམ་པ་བཞིན་དུ་བསླབ་པར་བགྱི། །
> 
> *Just as the Sugatas of former times*
> *Generated the mind of awakening,*
> *And just as they sequentially dwelt*
> *In the training of the Bodhisattvas,*
> 
> *So too, for the benefit of all beings,*
> *Do I generate the mind of awakening,*
> *And so too will I sequentially*
> *Train in those trainings.*

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Clearing Obstacles and Accumulating Merit (Homage):** Paying homage and taking refuge in the Three Jewels (Sugatas, Dharmakaya, and Bodhisattvas) serves to pacify inner and outer obstacles to our spiritual practice. It purifies pride, establishes a foundation of deep reverence, and accumulates the vast merit necessary for our spiritual journey toward definitive goodness.
*   **Familiarizing the Mind and Preventing Spiritual Decline (Humility & Commitment):** Actively cultivating and contemplating these teachings is a powerful method of mental familiarization (*svamano vāsayituṃ*). This practice helps prevent spiritual decline, stabilizes our baseline of virtue, and gradually increases key qualities such as faith, diligence, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom.
*   **Opening the Gateway to Mutual and Altruistic Benefit (For Equal Fortune):** Expressing genuine humility and setting down these realizations creates a gateway of entry for other practitioners of equal fortune (similar disposition and capacity). This mutual connection fosters shared joy, deepens our aspiration for the Bodhisattva conduct, and ensures that our practice of the Dharma directly contributes to the temporary and ultimate happiness of both ourselves and others.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-1**
> བདེ་གཤེགས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་མངའ་སྲས་བཅས་དང་། །
> ཕྱག་འོས་ཀུན་ལའང་གུས་པར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏེ། །
> བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་ནི། །
> ལུང་བཞིན་མདོར་བསྡུས་ནས་ནི་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ། །
> 
> *Paying homage with reverence to the Sugatas — the Dharmakāya and their heirs — and to all who are worthy of veneration, I shall now briefly describe, in accordance with scripture, the practice of entering the discipline of the Sugatas' heirs.*

##### **Commentary Explanations for Verse 1-1**
*   **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen:** Explains that paying homage pacifies obstacles to spiritual practice and leads to definitive goodness. He defines **Sugata** (Gone to Bliss) in terms of both perfect abandonment (abandoning afflictions beautifully, without return, and without exception) and perfect realization (directly knowing the reality of the two types of selflessness).
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen:** Focuses on the dual benefits of Buddhahood: self-benefit (perfect realization and abandonment) and other-benefit (compassionate guidance). He cites the *Uttaratantra* to define the Three Jewels and explains that bowing with the body, praising with speech, and recollecting qualities with the mind purifies our state and brings temporary and ultimate happiness.
*   **Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo:** Explains that the homage is performed to clear obstacles and generate merit. He defines the **Dharma Jewel** as the realized Dharmakaya (inseparable from the pure dharmadhatu and stainless wisdom) and the **Sangha Jewel** as the "Sons of the Sages" (Bodhisattvas) who inherit the Buddha's lineage.

#### **Verse 1-2**
> སྔོན་ཆད་མ་བྱུང་བ་ཡང་འདིར་བརྗོད་མེད། །
> སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་མཁས་པའང་བདག་ལ་ཡོད་མིན་ཏེ། །
> དེ་ཕྱིར་གཞན་དོན་བསམ་པ་བདག་ལ་མེད། །
> རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་ངས་འདི་བརྩམས། །
> 
> *What is written here has not been said before, nor do I possess any skill in composition. Therefore I have no thought of benefiting others — I composed this solely to cultivate it in my own mind.*

##### **Commentary Explanations for Verse 1-2**
*   **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen:** Explains that this expression of humility serves to eliminate pride. The primary purpose of writing is self-benefit: to familiarize (*vāsayituṃ*) his own mind with what has already been understood, preventing spiritual decline and promoting the increase of faith, wisdom, and compassion.
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen:** Notes that Śāntideva abandons pride by asserting that there is nothing new in this text that does not already exist in the pure Mahayana scriptures, and that he lacks literary scholarship. By setting these realizations down, he seeks to cultivate and practice the aspiring mind of bodhicitta and the six perfections in his own mind.
*   **Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo:** Explains that stating humility shows the author is entirely free from pride, which pre-emptively fosters sympathy and asks for forgiveness for any faults. The writing is a tool to familiarize his own mind with bodhicitta, leading to the gradual increase of diligence, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom.

---

#### **Verse 1-3**
> དགེ་བ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་བདག་གི་དད་པའི་ཤུགས་། །
> འདི་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་རེ་ཞིག་འཕེལ་འགྱུར་ལ། །
> བདག་དང་སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཀྱང་། །
> ཅི་སྟེ་འདི་དག་མཐོང་ན་དོན་ཡོད་འགྱུར། །
> 
> *Through these writings, my faith in virtue may grow stronger for a while, and should another of equal fortune encounter this, perhaps it may also be of benefit to them.*

##### **Commentary Explanations for Verse 1-3**
*   **Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen:** Explains that although the text is written primarily for self-benefit, it will also benefit others of equal fortune (similar disposition and capacity). Showing this relationship and purpose serves as a "branch of entry" to encourage other fortunate individuals to study and engage with the treatise.
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen:** Outlines how the secondary purpose of the treatise is to benefit others. If other individuals of equal fortune (in terms of spiritual family, qualities, and aspiration) read, understand, and practice the meaning of these verses, they will receive immense temporary and ultimate benefits.
*   **Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo:** Citing Kṣemadeva and Vairocana, Thokme explains that the word "temporarily" (*re zhig*) in the preceding verse establishes the transition from self-benefit to this secondary purpose of benefiting others. If other unbiased Mahayana practitioners seeking the Bodhisattva conduct encounter these formulations, it may prove meaningful and beneficial for them.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Cultivating Reverence and Humility:** Begin your day or any major task by cultivating a mind of respect and gratitude. Acknowledge your teachers, spiritual guides, and beneficial influences. This simple act of paying homage helps dissolve pride, centers your mind, and establishes a harmonious foundation for your daily activities.
*   **2. Shifting Focus to Inner Transformation:** When engaging in study, work, or spiritual practices, realign your motivation. Like Śāntideva, focus primarily on familiarizing your own mind with virtue rather than seeking external validation, praise, or renown. Ask yourself: *"How can this activity help me cultivate patience, wisdom, or compassion in my own continuum?"*
*   **3. Sharing with Gentle Openness:** When sharing insights or helping others, do so with a gentle, non-presumptuous attitude. Instead of assuming the role of an expert, share your experiences with the thought: *"Perhaps this might be of benefit to someone of equal fortune."* This humble approach fosters genuine connection, mutual support, and deepens shared learning.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**
> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །
> 
> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །
> 
> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Sugata (བདེ་གཤེགས།):** Literally "One who has gone to bliss" or "The Well-Gone One." In the context of Verse 1-1, it refers to the Buddha. The commentaries define it in terms of perfect abandonment (having permanently left all afflictions and ignorance behind) and perfect realization (directly knowing the ultimate nature of reality).
*   **Dharmakāya (ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ།):** The "Truth Body" of the Buddha. In Verse 1-1, it refers to the ultimate nature of the Buddha's mind, which is inseparable from the naturally pure, stainless sphere of reality (*dharmadhātu*) and primordial wisdom. It represents the ultimate Dharma Jewel.
*   **Bodhicitta (བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས།):** The "Mind of Enlightenment." The altruistic aspiration to attain complete Buddhahood for the sake of liberating all sentient beings. It is the central theme of the entire treatise and is introduced in Verse 1-1 as the "discipline of the Sugatas' heirs."
*   **Familiarization / Habituation (བསྒོམ་པ། / *Vāsanā*):** In Verse 1-2, Śāntideva states his primary purpose is to "cultivate" or "habituate" (*svamano vāsayituṃ*) his own mind. In Buddhist psychology, this refers to the process of repeatedly training the mind in virtuous thoughts and realizations until they become natural, stable, and effortless.
