## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the sixth and seventh verses of the fifth chapter. Sharp questions like these bring fresh energy to today's practice: who made the weapons of hell, and where did its fires come from?

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a clear and encouraging teaching.

The *Sutra Distinguishing Rebirths* teaches that the sufferings of hell, its weapons and fires, are not made by any outside maker. They arise from the mind of the person who did wrong. It says:

> The iron ground is vast and hot. Blazing tongues of fire spread everywhere. Sharp iron blades cut a single body into a hundred pieces. All of this arises from the minds of those who did wrong in body, speech, and mind.

So, all the suffering of hell comes from the mind of the person who did the wrong.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today, when suffering or fear arises, I will not blame the outside world. Instead, I will examine my own mind.

**Explanation:** *(Taming the mind)* The Teacher, the Buddha, said that all fear and suffering do not come from outside. They come from our own mind. So when we face difficulty, we should not blame the outside world. Instead, we should look at our own mind and work to tame it. If we tame our own mind, all fear and suffering will calm down naturally.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains that suffering and happiness come from whether or not the mind is tamed, as the Tathāgata taught in the *Smṛtyupasthāna Sūtra* and elsewhere.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-6]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

As proof of this by scriptural authority: here, all these fears in this life and beyond, and the immeasurable suffering of body and mind as well, arise from having fallen under the sway of one's own distracted and deluded mind and having committed unwholesome, non-virtuous actions. That this is authentic — the single, unmistaken truth — has been taught by the Buddha himself, who is free of falsehood. As the *Ratnamegha Sūtra* says: "If the mind itself is brought under control, all phenomena come under control." And as the *Smṛtyupasthāna Sūtra* says: "The mind is one's enemy, one's great enemy; there is no enemy other than the mind. Just as a fire-drill's stick burns itself, so the mind burns itself by its own mind," and so forth.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-6]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

The fears and suffering that arise for oneself are made by one's own mind: all fears in this life and beyond, and immeasurable suffering as well, arise from one's own sinful mind. He explains that the Sage himself — whose teaching on all objects of knowledge is authentic and unmistaken — taught this in the *Ratnamegha Sūtra*.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-6]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Thus, all fears and the immeasurable suffering of this life and the next arise from one's own mistaken mind — so the Truthful Speaker himself has taught, as clarified above by passages from the *Ratnamegha Sūtra* and the *Dharmasaṃgīti Sūtra*.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-6]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

The reason that harm is pacified when the mind is guarded is this: all fears of this life and the next, and the immeasurable suffering of body and mind as well, arise from the mind having become afflicted. This — the true nature of all phenomena — has been taught by the Buddha himself, as clarified above by the passage from the *Smṛtyupasthāna Sūtra*.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-6]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Proof by trustworthy scriptural authority: however many fears there are in this life and beyond, and however much immeasurable suffering of body and mind and mental unease there is, all of it arises through the pull of one's own past bad karma; and since that karma is itself the mental intention, together with the bodily and verbal actions it initiates, it arises from the mind. This unmistaken account of the true situation of all knowable phenomena has been well taught by the Omniscient Teacher — so he says, clarified above by numerous scriptural passages from the *Gaṇḍavyūha Sūtra*, the *Abhidharmakośa*, various sūtras, and the *Ratnamegha Sūtra*.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-6]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

The reason, as explained below, is this: all these fears in this life and future lives, and the immeasurable suffering of body and mind as well — all of it arises in dependence on this mind alone. This true teaching of reality has been taught by the perfectly complete Buddha, the Bhagavān; and he further explains that it is also taught that if the mind itself is brought under control, all phenomena come under control.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-6]]

All the commentaries agree on this point, and it is the central teaching of this verse.

<!-- sub:synthesis -->
#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** All these fears in this life and beyond, and the immeasurable suffering of body and mind, arise from one's own mind having fallen under affliction and delusion; the perfectly complete Buddha himself has authentically taught that the mind is the chief factor among all things — a point on which all the commentators agree.

**Key points.**

- **The cause of all fear and suffering.** All fears in this life and beyond, and the immeasurable suffering, are taught by the Buddha himself to arise from one's own mind having fallen under distraction and afflicted delusion.
- **The Ratnamegha Sūtra's teaching.** If the mind itself is brought under control, all phenomena come under control — therefore the mind itself is the chief factor.
- **The root of karma.** All virtuous and non-virtuous karma is accumulated by the mind, and all phenomena depend on the mind, as taught in the *Ratnamegha Sūtra* and the *Dharmasaṃgīti Sūtra*.
- **The mind as its own great enemy.** Just as a fire-drill stick burns itself, the mind is its own great enemy and burns itself by its own mind, as taught in the *Smṛtyupasthāna Sūtra*.
- **The manifold world also arises from karma.** The world of sentient beings and their environment alike arise from karma, and that karma is itself mental intention and the bodily and verbal actions it produces.
- **The benefit of a tamed mind.** Happiness and suffering come from whether the mind is tamed or not; by taming the mind, one attains buddhahood.

<!-- sub:interlinear -->
#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

Thus, in order to prove decisively that these fears are nothing other than products of the mind, he asks: the weapons, swords, and forests of sword-leaves and the like belonging to the guardians of the hell-beings — who intentionally made these? Who, too, made that ground whose very nature is burning iron? From what cause did those masses of fire appearing above and below the Śālmalī tree arise?

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He explains that the manifold arrangements of the world's environments — such as, for example, the various countries of Europe — are not made by a creator god or any other maker, but are established through the collective karma of the sentient beings who inhabit and use that environment; on this basis he poses, in the form of a question, who made the hell-beings' weapons, the burning iron ground, and the throng of females.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^5-7]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Proof by reasoning: as for the hell-beings' cutting weapons — swords, blades, forests of sword-leaves, and the like — and the grinding implements such as mills, who intentionally made these things for the purpose of cutting and so forth? There is no maker other than the mind. Likewise, who made the burning-iron-natured ground, blazing with fire, and for what purpose? There is no other maker. And the appearances, above and below the Śālmalī tree, of the throng of females — to whom one was formerly attached and through whom one wrongly indulged — calling out: from what other maker did these arise? They arose from no other source than the mind. So he explains.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^5-7]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

By posing the questions — who intentionally made the hell-beings' weapons? Who made the burning iron ground? From what did that throng of females arise? — he shows that there is no maker apart from the mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^5-7]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

By such questions as "Who intentionally made the hell-beings' weapons? Who made the burning iron ground?" he clarifies, as above, that there is no fear apart from the mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^5-7]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

As for the weapons that torment hell-beings — blades, short spears, grinding poles, forests of sword-leaves, and the like — who intentionally made these, and for what purpose? There is no other maker, such as Īśvara. Likewise, who made the burning-iron-natured ground, ablaze with fire? There is no other maker; and the appearances of the throng of men and women calling out above and below the Śālmalī tree, whom one formerly wrongly indulged — from what did these arise? He explains that they arose from nothing else than the mind.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^5-7]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

If the fears and suffering experienced in hell and elsewhere did not arise from one's own past sinful mind, then one might ask: the weapons that inflict harm on hell-beings — short spears, blades, grinding poles, forests of swords, and the like — who intentionally strove to make these, and for what purpose? There is no other maker. And the ground of blazing fire, whose very nature is burning iron — who made this? Neither Īśvara, Brahmā, nor any other maker made it. As for the throng of females — the women climbing up and down the Śālmalī tree — all of them arose from nothing other than the sinful mind generated by one's own former wrongful indulgence; he explains that it is entirely unreasonable to suppose they arose from any other cause, or from no cause at all.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^5-7]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

In future rebirths, the weapons held in the hands of the hell-beings — swords, axes, blades, and the like, blazing with fire — appear to the great sinner, yet no person intentionally sought out or made them; no one made them at all. Likewise, in the hot hells, every feature of that external environment, including the burning iron ground, was made by no one; no one made it at all. And the appearances, above and below the mountain of the Śālmalī tree, of the throng of females calling out — whom one formerly wrongly indulged and engaged through one's own attachment — all of these too, he explains, arose from no other maker whatsoever.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^5-7]]

#### Divergences Among Commentators

- ⚑ **Minyak Kunzang Sonam:** The Sanskrit source and Sanskrit commentaries give the root verse's final phrase as "from what did those women arise," and the translator Pang Lotsāwa likewise states it this way; he therefore holds that the reading "heaps of fire" is a corrupted reading.
- ⚑ **His Holiness the Dalai Lama:** Although some commonly cited editions read "heaps of fire," he holds this to be a corrupted reading; since the Sanskrit source reads "throng of females," he considers this the correct reading.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** The fears such as the hell-beings' weapons and the burning iron ground are not made by Īśvara or any other maker; through a series of questions, the commentators consistently establish that there is no fear apart from the mind.

**Key points.**

- **No maker other than the mind.** The hell-beings' weapons, the burning iron ground, and the throng of females all arise from no maker other than the mind, as the questions in the verse establish.
- **The cause of hell's appearances.** All the fearful and painful appearances of hell arise from one's own past sinful mind, not without a cause.
- **The true status of the hells.** Hell-beings have no inherently established existence; they arise from the conceptual thought of ordinary, childish beings.
- **The cause of the manifold physical world.** The manifold environment of the world is established through the collective karma of sentient beings, not made by a creator god.
- ⚑ **A textual divergence in the root verse's closing phrase.** The reading "heaps of fire" is held to be a corrupted reading; the Sanskrit source supports "throng of females" as correct.
