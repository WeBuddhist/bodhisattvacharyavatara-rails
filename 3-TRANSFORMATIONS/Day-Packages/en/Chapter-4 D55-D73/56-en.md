# Day 56 — From a withheld crumb to an abandoned world: the weight of a broken pledge

**Date:** Aug 30  
**Chapter:** 4  
**Verses covered:** 4-4 to 4-6

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the three verses 4 to 6 from the fourth chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. There is a difference between promising to give a little food and inviting all beings as guests. That difference makes us take our everyday promises seriously too.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

> Sagaramati, it is like this. Suppose a king, or a king's chief minister, invites all the people of the city and says, "Tomorrow I will give you a meal." Then he lets it slip and sets out no food or drink. In that case he has deceived that whole crowd of people.

This comparison shows the meaning of the sixth verse clearly. We have invited all beings, saying that we will bring them to the happiness of a buddha. If we do not practise, it is the same as the king breaking his word. Then we have deceived every being we invited. If we act like that, we ourselves have no fortune of gaining a good rebirth.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** Today I will actually give even the small amount of food I have thought of giving to someone else.

**Explanation:** *(Generosity Practice)* Suppose we think in our heart of giving a little food to someone, and then do not actually give it. That becomes a cause of being born as a hungry ghost. But if today we actually carry out even a small promise to give, our own mind is made clean. And we are freed from the fear of the lower realms.

#### Interlinear Gloss (Khenpo Zhenga's annotation commentary)

If, having vowed in this way — "I will free those not yet freed, liberate those not yet liberated, give breath to those without breath, and bring those not yet fully passed beyond suffering to their full passing beyond suffering" — one then fails to accomplish this through actual deeds, then all those beings, together with the buddhas, bodhisattvas, and gods, will have been deceived. As the deceiver, what then will my destiny be? <!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Having Vowed and Then Failed to Accomplish It Through Deeds, One Deceives All Beings and Has No Destiny but the Lower Realms

If, having vowed to free all beings, one fails to accomplish this through the deeds of body, speech, and mind, then all the beings one invited as guests are deceived, in contradiction to one's own pledge — so that one's destiny can only be the lower realms. All the commentaries explain this in agreement.

###### 2. The Content of the Vow Is the Fourfold Pledge: Freeing, Liberating, and the Rest

Both Khenpo Zhenga's interlinear gloss and Minyak Kunzang Sonam show the essence of the vow as the fourfold formula: "I will free those not yet freed, liberate those not yet liberated, give breath to those without breath, and bring those not yet fully passed beyond suffering to their full passing beyond." Minyak Kunzang Sonam explains that this is also confirmed by the words of the sūtra cited in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*.

###### 3. The Sūtra of the Essence of the Earth Shows That Claiming to Follow the Great Vehicle While Not Practicing the Training Is Lying Before the Buddhas

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the *Sūtra of the Essence of the Earth* to explain that someone who claims to be a follower of the Great Vehicle while not keeping even a single path of virtuous action is engaging in the height of pretense, deceiving the world before all the buddhas.

###### 4. This Verse Falls at the Start of the Section on "the Faults of Abandoning [Bodhicitta]"

Ngulchu Thogme places this verse at the start of the outline section called "The Faults of Abandoning [Bodhicitta]."

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that if, having vowed to free all beings from the lower realms and bring them to full liberation, one fails to accomplish this through actual practice, then all those beings one took as guests will have been deceived, so that one's own destiny can be nothing but the lower realms. All the commentaries agree on this basic ground.

**Key points.**

- The essence of the vow is the fourfold pledge: freeing those not yet freed, liberating those not yet liberated, giving breath to those without breath, and bringing those not yet fully passed beyond suffering to their full passing beyond.
- Having made this vow, if one fails to accomplish it through the practice of application, all the beings taken as guests are deceived.
- Because of this fault, Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains, one's future destiny can be nothing but the lower realms.
- Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the *Sūtra of the Essence of the Earth*: claiming to follow the Great Vehicle while not keeping even a single path of virtue is the height of pretense — lying before all the buddhas.
- Ngulchu Thogme places this verse at the start of the section on "the faults of abandoning [bodhicitta]."

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains this verse together with the following one (4-6). Having decided in one's mind to give some small, ordinary thing and then, for the time being, not actually carrying this through into practice and simply not giving it — this too, he explains, is taught to result later in rebirth as a hungry ghost in a lower realm. And having, most sincerely, taken all beings as guests to the supreme, unsurpassed happiness, if one then fails to strive through actual practice to bring about their welfare and happiness, this comes to resemble inviting an honored guest and then, once the invitation has been made, leaving them with nothing — a fault he explains to be extremely grave.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-5]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

The reason is this: even for a very small, ordinary thing — a bit of food or drink — if someone resolves in their mind, "I will give this to this person," and then does not actually give it, the *Dharmasaṃgīti Sūtra* teaches that such a person will be reborn as a hungry ghost. The *Sūtra of the Applications of Mindfulness* likewise teaches that failing to give some small thing one has resolved to give results in rebirth as a hungry ghost, while failing to give something one has formally vowed to give results in rebirth in the hell realms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-5]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen treats this verse together with the following one (4-6) under a single outline heading, "Second." He explains that even for an ordinary object to be given — as little as a single mouthful of food — someone who resolves in their mind to give it and then does not is taught, in both the *Sūtra of the Applications of Mindfulness* and the *Sūtra Distinguishing the Destinies*, to have created a cause for rebirth as a hungry ghost.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-5]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Ngulchu Thogme likewise treats this verse together with the following one (4-6) under a single outline heading, "Second, the reason for that." He explains that the *Saddharma-smṛtyupasthāna* teaches that someone who resolves in their mind to give even a small, ordinary thing such as food, and then does not go on to give it, creates thereby a cause for rebirth as a hungry ghost.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^4-13]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

The reason for this, too — to say nothing of a fine possession — is that even for a small, ordinary thing such as food or clothing, someone who resolves in their mind, "I will give this to this person," and then does not actually give it, is taught in the *Dharmasaṃgīti Sūtra* to become a hungry ghost because of it.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-5]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

Minyak Kunzang Sonam likewise explains this verse together with the following one (4-6) as "the reason, or grounds." To say nothing of a great and abundant possession — even for something as small as a single morsel, an ordinary thing, someone who resolves in their mind, "I will give this," and then does not actually give it, is taught to be reborn as a hungry ghost. And, he explains, the word "again" in the sūtra shows that if, having formally vowed to give, one fails to hand it over, one is reborn in hell. He then details, at length, the vow and the manner of taking beings as one's guests, drawing on many scriptural citations from the *Smṛtyupasthāna*, the *Dharmasaṃgīti*, and the *Sāgaramatiparipṛcchā*, as cited in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-5]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that this verse serves as an illustration of the fault taught in the previous verse. Even for a small, ordinary thing — a bit of rice porridge or the like — someone who resolves in their mind, "I will give this to the crows," and then does not actually give it is taught in the *Sūtra of the Applications of Mindfulness* to be reborn in the realm of hungry ghosts.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-5]]

<!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Even a Small, Ordinary Thing Resolved to Be Given, but Not Given, Results in Rebirth as a Hungry Ghost

Resolving in one's mind to give a small, ordinary thing such as food or clothing, and then not actually giving it, results — all the commentaries agree — in rebirth as a hungry ghost.

###### 2. This Point Is Established by Scripture

The fault taught in this verse is confirmed, in agreement, by Khenpo Kunzang Pelden, Gyaltsab, Sabzang, Minyak Kunzang Sonam, and Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk, citing several scriptures such as the *Applications of Mindfulness* (Smṛtyupasthāna), the *Dharmasaṃgīti*, and the *Sūtra Distinguishing the Destinies*.

###### 3. This Verse and the Following One (4-6) Are Explained Together, Showing the Reason for the Preceding Verse's (4-4) Claim That Failing to Accomplish a Vow Deceives Beings

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Ngulchu Thogme, Minyak Kunzang Sonam, and His Holiness the Dalai Lama all explain these two verses together, in one continuous passage.

###### 4. The Small Example Illustrates the Greater Point

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains this verse as an illustration of the preceding fault: if not giving even a small thing like a bit of food results in rebirth as a hungry ghost, then how much graver the fault of vowing to accomplish the welfare of all beings and then failing to do so.

###### 5. The Image of Inviting a Guest and Leaving Them with Nothing Makes Clear the Fault of Not Fulfilling One's Pledge

His Holiness the Dalai Lama and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both explain that failing to accomplish the welfare of all beings, once one has taken them as guests, is equivalent to the fault of failing to serve food to an invited guest.

###### 6. Not Breaking One's Vow Is the Bodhisattva's Truthfulness

Minyak Kunzang Sonam cites the *Dharmasaṃgīti* to explain that generating bodhicitta and not turning against beings is itself the bodhisattva's truthfulness.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches, drawing on sūtra citations, that even for a small, ordinary thing such as food or clothing, resolving in one's mind to give it and then not actually giving it results in rebirth as a hungry ghost — directly supplying the scriptural grounds for the point made in the previous verse (4-4), that failing to accomplish a vow deceives beings. **Key points.**

- Resolving in one's mind to give a small, ordinary thing such as food or clothing, and then not actually giving it, results in rebirth as a hungry ghost — the chief point of this verse.
- This is a scripturally grounded position, attested by several commentators citing the *Applications of Mindfulness*, the *Dharmasaṃgīti*, and the *Sūtra Distinguishing the Destinies*.
- Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Ngulchu Thogme, Minyak Kunzang Sonam, and His Holiness the Dalai Lama all treat this verse together with the following one (4-6), explaining together the reason for the fault of an unaccomplished vow taught in the preceding verse (4-4).
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama and Minyak Kunzang Sonam illustrate the fault of an unfulfilled pledge with the image of inviting a guest and then leaving them with nothing, or of a king who promises the whole city a feast and then fails to provide it.

<!-- sub:commentary -->
#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

Having most sincerely taken all beings as guests to the supreme, unsurpassed happiness, if one then fails to strive through actual practice to accomplish the welfare and happiness of all beings, then — even though we say with our mouths, "I take all beings as my guests; I will act out the delightful, supreme conduct of enlightenment," inviting them as guests to my own kind of happiness — we must actually carry into practice the delightful bodhisattva conduct that benefits them both now and ultimately, and bring about their welfare. If we fail to do this, it is like inviting an honored guest and then, once the invitation has been made, leaving them with nothing — an unseemly and extremely grave fault. And if, having generated the excellent intention for the sake of all beings, one does not carry it into actual practice as intended, one turns one's back on all the buddhas and deceives all beings — and, he says, there is no fault graver than that.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-6]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

Having, not merely with words but with genuine, wholehearted intention, invited or called upon all the beings pervading the whole of space as guests to the great, unsurpassed happiness of buddhahood, if I fail to accomplish, or fail to bestow, that state of buddhahood through actual practice, exactly as promised — then I will have deceived, with falsehood, all those beings, together with the victorious ones and their heirs, and the whole world of gods and demigods. Since I am the one who has deceived them, how could I possibly go to a happy realm? I could not — that is all.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-6]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

Though this is taught to be a cause for rebirth as a hungry ghost, if — not merely with words but with genuine, wholehearted, respectful intention, proclaimed aloud — I have invited all beings as guests to the unsurpassed happiness and to the temporary happiness of gods and humans, and then, deferring it, deceive all those beings, how could I possibly go to a happy realm? I could not.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-6]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Having, with genuine, wholehearted intention, taken all beings as guests to unsurpassed buddhahood and to temporary happiness, if I then deceive all beings, how could I possibly go to a happy realm? I could not. *(Note: this commentary transcludes 4-5 and 4-6 consecutively without intervening prose and explains both verses in one continuous block; only the portion pertaining to 4-6's "supreme bliss / inviting as guest / deceiving all migrators" is drawn on above.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^4-13]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Though it is taught that this results in rebirth as a hungry ghost, if — having sincerely and wholeheartedly invited beings as guests to the great, unsurpassed happiness of buddhahood, and then failed to accomplish it as promised — I deceive all beings, gods and demigods alike, then, as the one who did this, how could I possibly go to a happy realm? I could not — that is all.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-6]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

To say nothing of something great and abundant — even for a small, ordinary thing such as a single morsel, resolving in one's mind to give it and then not actually giving it results in rebirth as a hungry ghost; and the sūtra's use of the word "again" shows that if, having formally vowed and not delivered, one is reborn in hell. If that is so, then how much more so when — with nothing greater to offer, and not merely with words but with genuine, wholehearted, respectful intention, proclaimed aloud — one has invited and called all beings as guests to the supreme, unsurpassed happiness of buddhahood, and, incidentally, also to the happiness of gods and humans, and then fails to accomplish that very purpose as one has taken it upon oneself to do, thereby deceiving all beings — it is certain that one cannot possibly go to a happy realm. The vow and the manner of taking beings as guests are statements such as: "I will free beings not yet freed," and so on, and, "Today, I take upon myself the protection of all," and the like.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-6]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Having, with heartfelt and wholehearted intention, invited or taken as guests all beings — gods and the rest — to the unsurpassed happiness of enlightenment, or the very happiness of buddhahood, if one then fails to accomplish it afterward as one promised, or fails to accomplish it properly, then one has deceived all those beings, gods and the rest, with false words. Because of this fault, how could one possibly go, in a future life, even so much as to a fortunate rebirth among gods and humans? One could not go there at all — he teaches that there is nothing left for such a person but to be reborn in the lower realms.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-6]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Having Sincerely Vowed to Establish All Beings in Unsurpassed Happiness and Taken Them as Guests, Failing to Accomplish This Through Practice Deceives All Beings

The main point of this verse: having taken all beings as guests to the great happiness of buddhahood, if one fails to accomplish this as promised through the practice of application, all beings are deceived by falsehood — all the commentaries agree on this to the end.

###### 2. "Taking as Guests" Refers to the Very Words of the Bodhicitta Vow

"Sincerely taking as guests" refers to the words of bodhicitta by which one vows to protect all beings — to be understood in the sense of pledges such as "I will free beings not yet freed" and "I take all beings as my guests."

###### 3. The Fault of Not Giving Even a Small, Ordinary Thing Is Far Exceeded by This Graver Fault

This verse's main point is explained, with parallel reasoning: if not giving even a small, ordinary thing results in rebirth as a hungry ghost, then the fault of taking all beings as guests to the supreme, unsurpassed happiness of buddhahood and then deceiving them is far graver still.

###### 4. The Fault of Deceiving Beings Makes It Certain One Cannot Go Even to a Fortunate Rebirth

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that, because of the fault of deceiving beings, one cannot go, even in a future life, so much as to a fortunate rebirth among gods and humans, but only to the lower realms.

###### 5. The Fault of Deceiving Beings Is Illustrated by the Image of Inviting a Guest and Not Providing for Them

His Holiness the Dalai Lama and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both illustrate the fault of deceiving beings with the image of inviting a guest and leaving them with nothing, or of a king promising a city a feast and failing to provide it.
