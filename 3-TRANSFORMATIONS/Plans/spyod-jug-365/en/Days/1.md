# Day 1 — Bodhisattvacharyavatara Practice Plan

## Chapter 1: Benefits of the Mind of Enlightenment — Verses 1-1 to 1-3

---

### 1. Refuge & Bodhicitta (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།)

#### **1. Refuge (སྐྱབས་འགྲོ།)**

> *Until I reach the heart of awakening,*
> *I take refuge in the Buddhas.*
> *Likewise, I take refuge in the Dharma*
> *And in the assembly of Bodhisattvas.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྙིང་པོར་མཆིས་ཀྱི་བར། །
> སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །
> ཆོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡི། །
> ཚོགས་ལའང་དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི། །

---

### 2. Benefit (ཕན་ཡོན།)

Based on the traditional commentaries of Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Sazang Mati Panchen, and Ngulchu Thokme Zangpo, practicing and reflecting on today's verses yields the following profound benefits:

*   **Releasing Pride (Humility):** The great teacher Śāntideva says he has no special skill or new ideas to share. This shows that letting go of pride makes it easier to learn and grow. When we are humble, we stop being arrogant, and this helps us move forward on our spiritual path.

*   **Building Stronger Faith (Spiritual Growth):** Writing down teachings is a powerful way to strengthen your own faith and practice. When you record what you have learned, your understanding and motivation become clearer and stronger. This helps your spiritual qualities develop in a steady and lasting way.

*   **Helping Others of Equal Heart (Shared Benefit):** Even when someone writes only to train their own mind, others who are sincere and open-hearted can also benefit. When one person shares their honest practice with others, it encourages those with similar goals to study and practice as well.

### 3. Today's Root Verses & Commentaries (དེ་རིང་གི་སྤྱོད་འཇུག་རྩ་ཚིག་དངོས།)

#### **Verse 1-1**

> བདེ་གཤེགས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་མངའ་སྲས་བཅས་དང་། །
> ཕྱག་འོས་ཀུན་ལའང་གུས་པར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏེ། །
> བདེ་གཤེགས་སྲས་ཀྱི་སྡོམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་ནི། །
> ལུང་བཞིན་མདོར་བསྡུས་ནས་ནི་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ། །
>
> *I pay respectful homage to the Blissful Ones who possess the Truth Body, and to their heirs. I also bow to everyone who is worthy of honor. I shall now briefly describe how to enter the commitments of the Buddha's heirs, in accordance with the scriptures.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-1**

The great teacher Śāntideva opens the text with an act of deep respect, bowing to the Buddhas who have fully let go of all faults and realized all good qualities, to the teachings they gave, and to the great Heroes of Enlightenment who follow their path. This opening act of homage is not just a formality — it clears away inner obstacles, generates great positive energy, and prepares both the author and the reader to engage fully with the path of enlightenment. By honoring these supreme objects, a sincere practitioner creates the right conditions for the teachings that follow to truly take root in the heart.

---

#### **Verse 1-2**

> སྔོན་ཆད་མ་བྱུང་བ་ཡང་འདིར་བརྗོད་མེད། །
> སྡེབ་སྦྱོར་མཁས་པའང་བདག་ལ་ཡོད་མིན་ཏེ། །
> དེ་ཕྱིར་གཞན་དོན་བསམ་པ་བདག་ལ་མེད། །
> རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་ངས་འདི་བརྩམས། །
>
> *I have nothing new to say here, and I do not possess any skill in writing poetry. Therefore, I have no expectation of benefiting others. I wrote this solely to train and cultivate my own mind.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-2**

The great teacher Śāntideva humbly states two things: his text contains no new teachings beyond what is already in the scriptures, and he has no special skill in beautiful writing. This honest declaration removes any trace of intellectual pride. His only real purpose is to train and strengthen his own mind through repeated reflection on the teachings. Writing down these reflections is more than just notes — it acts as a powerful support that keeps his wisdom and compassion from fading and helps them grow stronger over time.

---

#### **Verse 1-3**

> དགེ་བ་བསྒོམ་ཕྱིར་བདག་གི་དད་པའི་ཤུགས། །
> འདི་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་རེ་ཞིག་འཕེལ་འགྱུར་ལ། །
> བདག་དང་སྐལ་བ་མཉམ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ཀྱང་། །
> ཅི་སྟེ་འདི་དག་མཐོང་ན་དོན་ཡོད་འགྱུར། །
>
> *By writing this, the strength of my faith to cultivate virtue will temporarily increase. If anyone of equal fortune should see these words, perhaps they will also find them useful.*

##### **Combined Commentary Summary for Verse 1-3**

The great teacher Śāntideva explains that writing down his practice increases three kinds of faith within him: trust in the law of cause and effect, admiration for the qualities of the Buddhas and Heroes of Enlightenment, and confidence in his own ability to practice. As this faith grows, it naturally supports the development of diligence, mindfulness, and wisdom on the path. At the same time, those who share the same sincere wish to practice and are free from bias may also find great benefit in reading these words, making this text a gift that quietly serves both the author and those of like heart.

### 4. Daily Life Application (ཉིན་རེའི་འཚོ་བའི་ནང་ཉམས་སུ་ལེན་ཚུལ།)

*   **1. Starting the Day with Homage:** Each morning today, I will pause before beginning any activity and call to mind the Buddhas, the teachings, and the great Heroes of Enlightenment. I will bow, even briefly and inwardly, as a way of acknowledging that my day has a deeper purpose than just getting things done.

*   **2. Practicing Humility in Conversation:** When I speak with others today, I will notice any moment where I feel the urge to show off my knowledge or correct someone. Each time I catch this impulse, I will take a breath and remember the great teacher Śāntideva's example — that true practice begins with letting go of the need to be seen as skilled or wise.

*   **3. Writing to Strengthen My Faith:** At some point today, I will write down one teaching or insight that has helped me on my path. I will do this not to share it with others, but only to help my own mind stay connected to what matters most. I will notice whether this small act of writing makes my motivation feel clearer and stronger.

### 5. Dedication & Aspiration (བསྔོ་བ་དང་སྨོན་ལམ།)

#### **1. Dedication (བསྔོ་བ།)**

> *By the virtue accumulated*
> *Through composing this entry into the Bodhisattva conduct,*
> *May all wandering beings without exception*
> *Engage in the Bodhisattva conduct.*

> བདག་གིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་པ་ལ། །
> འཇུག་པ་རྣམ་པར་བརྩམས་པ་ཡི། །
> དགེ་བ་གང་དེས་འགྲོ་བ་ཀུན། །
> བྱང་ཆུབ་སྤྱོད་ལ་འཇུག་པར་ཤོག །

#### **2. Aspiration (སྨོན་ལམ།)**

> *May the precious and supreme mind of awakening,*
> *Where it has not arisen, arise;*
> *Where it has arisen, may it not decline,*
> *But increase further and further.*

> བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ། །
> མ་སྐྱེས་པ་རྣམས་སྐྱེ་གྱུར་ཅིག །
> སྐྱེས་པ་ཉམས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། །
> གོང་ནས་གོང་དུ་འཕེལ་བར་ཤོག །

### 6. Key Terms Glossary (གནད་ཚིག་ཁག་གི་འགྲེལ་བཤད།)

*   **Blissful One (བདེ་གཤེགས།):** This is a title for a fully awakened Buddha, meaning one who has "gone to bliss." It points to two things: the Buddha has completely left behind all mental faults and suffering, and has fully realized the deepest truth of reality. This quality sets a Buddha apart from all other spiritual figures and makes him worthy of the highest respect.

*   **Truth Body (ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ།):** The Truth Body is the deepest nature of a Buddha's mind — the pure, open awareness that is completely free from all obscurations. In this verse, it is described as the heart of what makes a Buddha a Buddha. It is not a physical form but the wisdom and purity that a Buddha embodies completely.

*   **Heroes of Enlightenment (བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་སྲས།):** These are the noble spiritual companions who follow the path of a Buddha and dedicate themselves to freeing all beings from suffering. In this verse, they are called the "heirs" or "sons" of the Buddhas because they are born from the Buddha's wisdom and carry that wisdom forward for the benefit of the world.

*   **Commitment to the Conduct (སྡོམ་ལ་འཇུག་པ།):** This refers to formally entering and training in the way of life of the Heroes of Enlightenment. It means taking on a set of ethical commitments and practices designed to cultivate the mind of enlightenment. The entire text is a guide to understanding and living out this conduct fully.

*   **Training the Mind (རང་གི་ཡིད་ལ་བསྒོམ་པ།):** This phrase refers to the process of familiarizing the mind with the teachings through repeated reflection and practice. It is not about gaining new information, but about letting the teachings become a natural part of how one thinks, feels, and acts. This kind of training is presented as the honest and humble foundation of the whole text.
