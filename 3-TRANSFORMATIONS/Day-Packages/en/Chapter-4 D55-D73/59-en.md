# Day 59 — Kept promises without outer supports: why the mind alone must be tamed

## 1. Today's Challenge (from the practice-plan track)

<!-- challenge:opening -->
### Opening

Today's practice is based on the two verses 12 and 13 from the fourth chapter of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*. Many buddhas have come in the past, but if we are not ready on our own side it does us no good. So from today onwards we have to make the effort ourselves.

<!-- challenge:tradition -->
### From the Tradition

The commentaries on these verses give us a comparison that is easy to understand and encouraging.

His Holiness the Dalai Lama has said that the holy Dharma is a means of taming our own mind. It is not something brought about by outer supports: images, texts and stupas and the like.

Jetsun Milarepa had no thangkas and no statues of gold or copper. He went about in the manner of a poor beggar, and yet he reached the level of a buddha in a single life. In the same way, Khedrub Norzang Gyatso had no wealth and no possessions at all, and practised alone in the mountains.

No matter how many outer supports of the Dharma we have, they are of no use if our own mind is not tamed. So real practice does not count on outer supports. From today, from this very moment, we should work year by year. We should make the conduct of our body, speech and mind better.

<!-- challenge:practice -->
### Today's Practice

**Practice:** From today onwards I will not put off good practice. I will make the effort straight away.

**Explanation:** *(Diligence Practice)* If from today we do not make effort but put things off, we will fall step by step into the lower realms. But if from today we work hard at the good practices we have promised, we will be freed from the suffering of the lower realms and gain the fruit of doing good.

#### Commentary Explanations

<!-- cm:tenzin-gyatso -->
##### His Holiness the Dalai Lama (Teaching on Entering the Bodhisattva's Way of Life)

He counsels that this excellent opportunity — having given rise to a good intention in the very presence of the buddhas and their heirs — arises only once, if that, throughout beginningless saṃsāra, and so one must guard it with the utmost care, in proportion to one's own capacity. He teaches that what is called "Dharma" is a means of taming one's own mindstream, and is not accomplished through outer supports such as images, speech, and mind representations. He gives the example of Jetsun Milarepa, who had no thangka or image of gold or copper whatsoever and lived as a destitute beggar, yet attained the state of buddhahood in a single lifetime; and likewise Khedrub Norzang Gyatso, who had no wealth or possessions whatsoever and practiced alone, free from worldly activity, in the mountains. Having shown that however many outer monuments one has, they are of no benefit unless one's own mindstream is tamed, he counsels that it is of great importance to strive, year after year, to bring the conduct of one's own three doors — body, speech, and mind — to ever finer quality.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_TG_bo.toc.md#^4-12]]

<!-- cm:kunpal -->
##### Khenpo Kunzang Pelden (Nectar Drops)

"The reason" or "since this is so" means: since this is so, then, just as I accepted and vowed at the time of generating bodhicitta, I will accomplish the trainings with respect, through the gate of carefulness. Second: if, from now on — from today — I do not strive at what is to be trained in, at what is to be adopted or discarded, then, through the power of downfalls, I will go from here through the animal realm and so forth, from one lower realm to a lower realm still, going on and on in a continuous stream of births in ever lower states, and I will experience suffering.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_KKP_bo_segmented.md#^4-12]]

<!-- cm:gyaltsab -->
##### Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen (Gateway for Bodhisattvas)

In that way, just as I have vowed, I will respectfully accomplish, for the sake of beings, the trainings that establish buddhahood. After having generated bodhicitta and taken the vow, if from now on I do not strive at the trainings, then, through the force of a powerful downfall, I will go on and on, from one lower realm to a lower realm still, in a continuous stream of rebirth.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_GDR_bo_segmented.md#^4-12]]

<!-- cm:ngulchu-thogmed -->
##### Ngulchu Thogme (Ocean of Good Explanations)

Since this is so, then, just as I vowed at the time of generating bodhicitta, I must accomplish it with respect; if from now on I do not strive, then afterward I will go from one lower realm to a lower realm still. *(Note: this commentary groups the root verses and explanation for 4-12, 4-13, and 4-14 together after their consecutive transclusions — the block ^4-25 covers all three verses; only the material specific to 4-12's admonition is drawn on here.)*

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_NTS_bo_segmented.md#^4-25]]

<!-- cm:sabzang -->
##### Sabzang Mati Panchen (Clear Illumination)

Since there is great harm in letting bodhicitta degenerate, then, just as I vowed above, I will make it my very essence to strive diligently, through the gate of carefulness, in taking up the vast trainings with respect — for the *Gayāśīrṣa Sūtra* says, "For bodhisattvas who make the practice their essence, there is enlightenment; there is no turning back from it."

Now that I have obtained the freedoms and endowments and taken up bodhicitta, if from now on I do not strive to take up the trainings in practice, then, through the force of a powerful downfall, I will go on and on, from one lower realm to a lower realm still, in a continuous stream of the three lower realms, and I will experience suffering.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC14_SMPLG_bo_segmented.md#^4-12]]

<!-- cm:minyak-kunzang-sonam -->
##### Minyak Kunzang Sonam (Inexhaustible Treasury Vase)

As for the point that if I do not accomplish it just as I have vowed, I must go on and on through the lower realms — one might ask, given that this is so, what is now fitting to do. Since there is great fault in not taking up the practice just as I have vowed, therefore, or since this is so, just as I vowed at the time of generating bodhicitta that I would liberate all beings, I must accomplish, with respect, through the gate of carefulness and diligence, the vast trainings that are the cause of accomplishing great enlightenment for the sake of others — gladly taking them up and carrying out the meaning of the teaching.

Now that I have generated bodhicitta and taken the vow, if from now on I do not, without slackening, diligently and earnestly take up the meaning of what I have vowed — the trainings — in practice, and thereby let it degenerate, then, through the force of a serious downfall, I will afterward go from one lower realm to a lower realm still, in a continuous stream of rebirth, and I will experience unceasing suffering as well.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC19_MKS_bo_segmented.md#^4-12]]

<!-- cm:khenpo-kunga -->
##### Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk (Commentary on the Bodhicaryāvatāra)

Since it is not fitting, once the trainings of generating bodhicitta have degenerated, to go anywhere but the lower realms, therefore he teaches: just as I first vowed or promised, at the time I took the vow of generating bodhicitta or generated the supreme aspiration to enlightenment, I myself must show respect and accomplish it properly through fierce diligence. Second, regarding not letting the application of diligence degenerate: he says that if I do not strive from now on, I will go from one lower realm to a lower realm still.

Sources: [[1-SOURCES/Commentaries/Transcluded/BCAC20_NKW_bo_segmented.md#^4-12]]

#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. One Must Accomplish the Trainings with Respect, Just as Vowed at the Time of Generating Bodhicitta

All the commentaries agree that, just as one vowed and promised at the time of generating bodhicitta, one must accomplish the trainings with respect and through the gate of carefulness.

###### 2. Failing to Strive from Now On Leads, Through the Power of Downfalls, from One Lower Realm to a Lower Realm Still

All the commentaries agree in explaining that if one does not strive at the trainings to be adopted or discarded, then, through the force of a powerful downfall, one goes on and on in a continuous stream of rebirth from one lower realm to a lower realm still, experiencing unceasing suffering.

###### 3. The Sūtra's Teaching That a Bodhisattva Who Makes the Practice Essential Never Turns Back from Enlightenment

Sabzang Mati Panchen cites the *Gayāśīrṣa Sūtra* to clearly explain the benefit of taking up the trainings earnestly in practice.

###### 4. True Dharma Is a Means of Taming the Mindstream, Not Something Accomplished by Outer Clothing

His Holiness the Dalai Lama gives the examples of Jetsun Milarepa and Khedrub Norzang Gyatso, counseling that one must strive year after year to bring the conduct of one's three doors to ever finer quality.

###### 5. This Verse Marks the Opening of the Section "Not Letting the Application of Diligence Degenerate"

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk identifies the two lines beginning "if I do not strive from now on" as marking the section on not letting the application of diligence degenerate.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that, just as one vowed at the time of generating bodhicitta, one must accomplish the trainings with respect; if one does not strive from now on, then, through the force of a downfall, one goes on and on from one lower realm to a lower realm still. All the commentaries agree on this basic ground.

**Key points.**

- All commentaries agree that just as one vowed at the time of generating bodhicitta, one must strive earnestly and with respect to accomplish the trainings.
- If one does not strive from now on, one goes, through the force of a downfall, from one lower realm to a lower realm still, in a continuous stream of rebirth.
- Sabzang Mati Panchen cites the *Gayāśīrṣa Sūtra* to show that a bodhisattva who makes the practice essential never turns back from enlightenment.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama, through the examples of Milarepa and Khedrub Norzang Gyatso, counsels that one must strive year after year to bring the conduct of body, speech, and mind to ever finer quality.

He teaches that if, from today — from this very day I generated the supreme aspiration to enlightenment — I do not, with delight and in the proper way, strive at or accomplish the trainings I am to undertake, then, through the fault of letting what I have vowed degenerate, after death I will go on and take birth from one lower realm to a lower realm still — from the animal realm to the realm of hungry ghosts, and from hungry ghosts to the hells and so forth. <!-- sub:teaching-points -->
#### Main Teaching Points

###### 1. Though Countless Buddhas Have Passed, One's Own Fault Placed One Outside Their Healing Sphere

All the commentaries explain, in agreement, that although countless buddhas who benefit all beings have arisen and passed away before now, I, through my own fault of letting bodhicitta and the trainings degenerate, was not within the sphere of benefit of those buddhas.

###### 2. Whether the Buddhas Can Protect One Depends on One's Own Karmic Fortune

Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both cite the *Abhisamayālaṃkāra*, explaining that just as an unfit seed does not sprout even when the rain of the gods falls, so one without the karmic fortune does not experience goodness even when a buddha has arisen.

###### 3. Even Meeting a Future Buddha and Generating Bodhicitta Again Would Be Extremely Difficult

Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that although it might seem possible to attain higher rebirth or liberation by meeting a future buddha and generating bodhicitta again, this too is extremely difficult, since one's own past evil karma carries one from one lower rebirth to another, experiencing suffering.

###### 4. An Illustration of a Being Falling into the Lower Realms Even in the Teacher's Own Presence

Khenpo Kunzang Pelden gives the example of Devadatta falling visibly into hell in the Buddha's own presence, clearly explaining that whether the buddhas can protect a being depends on that individual's own abandonment of faults.

###### 5. The Interlinear Gloss Sets Out the Same Point

The mchan-'grel likewise answers the doubt of whether meeting even a single tathāgata could remove every fault, explaining that although countless perfect buddhas have passed away, one's own fault placed one outside their healing sphere.

#### Verse Synthesis (overview)

**Brief introduction.** This verse teaches that although countless buddhas who benefit all beings have arisen and passed away before now, I, through my own fault of letting bodhicitta and the trainings degenerate, was not within the sphere of those buddhas' benefit. All the commentaries agree on this basic ground.

**Key points.**

- Whether the buddhas can protect a being depends on that individual's own karmic fortune, as shown by the analogy of an incurable patient whom even a skilled physician cannot benefit.
- Per the *Abhisamayālaṃkāra*, just as an unfit seed does not sprout even when the rain of the gods falls, one without the karmic fortune does not experience goodness even when buddhas have arisen — as Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen and Minyak Kunzang Sonam both explain.
- Khenpo Kunzang Pelden clarifies, through the example of Devadatta falling visibly into hell, that the mere arising of a buddha cannot save a being.
- Khenpo Kunga Wangchuk explains that even meeting a future buddha and generating bodhicitta again would be extremely difficult, given the force of one's own past evil karma.
- His Holiness the Dalai Lama explains this in terms of not having had the chance to come within reach of the buddhas and their heirs or to hear their teaching, and so not partaking of that excellent festivity.
