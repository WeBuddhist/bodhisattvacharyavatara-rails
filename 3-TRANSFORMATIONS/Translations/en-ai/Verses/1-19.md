## Combined Commentary Summaries
All three commentators maintain an absolute consensus on the core meaning, mechanism, and profound doctrinal implications of Verse 1-19, which describes the continuous, uninterrupted arising of merit once the engaging mind of enlightenment (*bodhi-prasthāna-citta*) is correctly adopted.

### 1. Functional Superiority: Engaging Mind vs. Aspiring Mind
The commentators focus heavily on explaining why the engaging mind produces an uninterrupted stream of merit, whereas the aspiring mind (*bodhi-praṇidhi-citta*) does not:
*   **Cause vs. Fruit:** **Gyaltsab Je** explains that the aspiring mind is merely an attitude that *seeks the fruit* (the state of a Buddha), whereas the engaging mind is conjoined with *accomplishing the complete causes* of Buddhahood (the active practice of the perfections). Thus, only the engaging mind can sustain a continuous flow of merit.
*   **Conjoining with Practice and Vows:** **Sazang Mati Panchen** notes that while the aspiring mind brings great fruits—such as the splendor of gods and humans within cyclic existence—it is not conjoined with the active practice of perfections like generosity. The engaging mind, however, is conjoined with the *three kinds of moral discipline* (restraining non-virtue, gathering virtue, and benefiting sentient beings), which generates an uninterrupted force of virtue.

### 2. The Scope of the Resolve
Each commentator defines the vast scope of the resolve that triggers this continuous merit, focusing on the liberation of infinite sentient beings:
*   **Gyaltsab Je** defines the resolve as intending to "liberate the infinite realms of sentient beings from the cycle of existence and establish them on the stage of a Buddha throughout the cycle of existence."
*   **Sazang Mati Panchen** describes it as intending to "completely liberate the infinite, boundless realms of sentient beings from the cycle of existence characterized by the three afflictions (desire, anger, and delusion)."
*   **Ngulchu Thokme** describes it as intending to "liberate the infinite realms of sentient beings from the noose of karma and afflictions and the prison of cyclic existence as long as cyclic existence remains."

### 3. Continuous Arising in Careless States
A defining characteristic of the merit produced by the engaging mind is that its flow is completely independent of the practitioner's active mental focus or physical posture:
*   **Mental States:** All three commentators agree that the force of merit continues to arise even when the practitioner is asleep, distracted, unmindful, or intoxicated. Sazang Mati Panchen adds "faints" to this list of non-deliberate states.
*   **Physical Postures:** Sazang Mati Panchen explicitly notes that this stream of merit flows constantly regardless of physical activity, continuing whether the practitioner is walking, standing, sitting, or lying down.
*   **Vastness:** All commentators affirm that this uninterrupted stream of merit is immeasurable and "equal to the expanse of space" (`nabhaḥsamāḥ`).

### 4. Ngulchu Thokme's Essential Caveat
*   **The Condition of Non-Degeneration:** **Ngulchu Thokme** introduces a critical qualification: this powerful stream of merit arises continuously *if the mind of enlightenment does not degenerate* (`དེ་མ་ཉམས་ན་`). This highlights that the continuous flow of merit is not automatic or unconditional; it is strictly contingent upon the practitioner maintaining the integrity of their Bodhisattva vows and preventing their resolve from decaying.
